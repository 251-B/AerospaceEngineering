Draw: grey i1 (to the right) and k1 (up) from the origin, 2 units each, labels at the tips. Orange e_r at lambda = 40 degrees from i1 and orange e_n at 40 degrees from k1 (towards the upper left), 2 units each, labels "e_r (local vertical)" and "e_n (towards the north)". Counterclockwise arcs from i1 to e_r and from k1 to e_n, each labelled "lambda". Shaded right triangles at unit scale (1.4 drawing units): for e_r, leg "cos lambda" along i1 and leg "sin lambda" dashed vertical up to the tip, right-angle mark; for e_n, leg "cos lambda" along k1 and leg "sin lambda" dashed horizontal towards minus i1, label "-sin lambda on i1", right-angle mark. Matrix box at the lower right: "[1R2] = [[cos lambda, -sin lambda, 0], [0, 0, -1], [sin lambda, cos lambda, 0]] (columns e_r, e_n, -j1)".
Values to draw: lambda = 40 degrees.
Alt: Meridian view of the local basis e_r, e_n turned by the latitude from i1, k1, with the two unit right triangles.
Caption: Rotation by $\lambda$ in the meridian plane ($-\mathbf j_1$ out of the paper). The triangle of $\mathbf e_n$ has its opposite leg along $-\mathbf i_1$: the minus sign of $\mathbf e_n = -\sin\lambda\,\mathbf i_1 + \cos\lambda\,\mathbf k_1$.

## p21-fbd
View: the meridian plane, i1 to the right, k1 up (polar axis), j1 into the paper (⊗); the planet as a light grey disc of radius R = 1.6 units centred at O (small cross, label "O"), the polar axis as a thin dash-dot vertical line with a curved arrow at its top labelled "Omega".
Draw: two bodies, each a small filled square on the surface. Body at the north pole (label "pole"): pink "-m g_p e_r" down towards O, length 0.8; pink "N_pole = m g_p k1" up, length 0.8, drawn beside it with a small sideways offset so the two arrows do not overlap; note "on the axis: F_C = 0". Body at the equator on the right (label "equator"): pink "-m g_p i1" towards O (to the left), length 0.8; pink dashed "F_C = m Omega^2 R i1 (inertia)" to the right, length 0.4 (half of the attraction, as in the statement); pink "N_eq = m (g_p - Omega^2 R) i1" to the right, length 0.4, drawn above the line of the other two with a small offset. Text near the equator body: "W_eq = W_pole / 2 when Omega^2 R = g_p / 2". Note at the bottom: "body at rest in S1: no Coriolis force; F_A = F_E = 0".
Values to draw: R = 1.6 units; attraction arrows 0.8 units; centrifugal and contact arrows at the equator 0.4 units.
Alt: Free-body diagrams of a body at rest at the north pole and at the equator of the spinning planet, with the attraction, the contact force and the centrifugal force.
Caption: At the pole the body is on the spin axis: the contact force balances the attraction alone, $W_{pole} = mg_p$. At the equator the centrifugal force $m\Omega^2R$ takes away part of it, $W_{eq} = m(g_p - \Omega^2R)$; the ratio 2 of the statement means $\Omega^2R = g_p/2$.
