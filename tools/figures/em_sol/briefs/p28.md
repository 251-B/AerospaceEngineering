## p28-fbd
View: the vertical plane through Az' and the cord (the meridian plane of p28-meridian), u to the right, k up; the unit vector e_theta of that plane points INTO the paper (⊗ near A, label "e_θ"), since e_theta = k x u and k up, u to the right.
Draw: the fixed point O at the bottom and the support A above it on the vertical axis (A at the height a sin omega t, a small double arrow along the axis at A labelled "A oscillates: a sin ωt"). The cord from A to P at phi = 30 degrees from the upward vertical (P above and to the right of A; filled dot, label "P"). Forces at P, each a separate arrow with its label placed off the lines and the other arrows: pink "−T e_r" from P towards A along the cord, 0.6 units (drawn for T positive, with the note "a cord can only pull: T ≥ 0"); pink "−m g k" straight down, 0.8 units; pink dashed "F_A = m a ω² sin(ωt) k (inertia)" straight up, 0.4 units, offset sideways by 0.08 units so it does not overlap the weight (drawn for sin omega t positive). Orange e_r (along AP, outwards) and e_phi (perpendicular to the cord, down and to the right) at P, 0.3 units. A small box at the upper right with the text "weight + inertia force = −m g_e k, g_e = g − a ω² sin ωt".
Values to draw: phi = 30 degrees; L = 1 (unit 180 px); a sin omega t = 0.25 units.
Alt: Free-body diagram of the particle on the cord in the translating frame of the support: tension, weight and the inertia force of the moving support.
Caption: Forces on $P$ in the translating frame $Ax'y'z'$: the tension $-T\,\mathbf e_r$, the weight $-mg\,\mathbf k$ and the inertia force of the origin $ma\omega^2\sin\omega t\,\mathbf k$. The last two add up to an effective weight $-mg_e\,\mathbf k$ with $g_e = g - a\omega^2\sin\omega t$.
