## p50-plane
View: the plane Oxy of the figure, i to the right, j up, k OUT of the paper (⊙ at O, label "k").
Draw: grey axes x and y through O (x 1.5 units each way, y 1 unit up). Dashed vertical lines downwards from O and from P. Thick black bar O-P of length l = 1.6 at theta = 25 degrees from the downward vertical (P to the right of the vertical), label "l"; thick black bar P-Q of length 1.6 at phi = 45 degrees from the downward vertical, label "l"; filled dots P and Q with labels. Angle arcs: at O between the downward vertical and OP, label "theta"; at P between the downward vertical and PQ, label "phi". Right triangle of OP shaded light yellow: O, F_1 = (0, -l cos theta), P; vertical leg dashed labelled "l cos theta" (adjacent), horizontal leg dashed labelled "l sin theta" (opposite). Right triangle of PQ shaded light yellow: P, F_2 = P + (0, -l cos phi), Q; legs labelled "l cos phi" and "l sin phi". Blue i_1 at P along OP outward (0.6 units) and blue j_1 perpendicular to it in the sense of increasing theta (up and to the right); green i_2 at Q along PQ outward and green j_2 perpendicular to it in the sense of increasing phi; labels at the tips. A dashed horizontal line through O labelled "axis Ox: y_Q = 0 here".
Values to draw: l = 1.6; theta = 25 degrees; phi = 45 degrees.
Alt: Double pendulum with the bars OP and PQ, the right triangles of legs l sin and l cos of each angle and the bases of the two bars.
Caption: Plane of the motion, $\mathbf k$ out of the paper. The triangles give $\mathbf r_0^P = \ell\sin\theta\,\mathbf i - \ell\cos\theta\,\mathbf j$ and $\mathbf r_0^Q = \ell(\sin\theta + \sin\phi)\,\mathbf i - \ell(\cos\theta + \cos\phi)\,\mathbf j$; $Q$ reaches the axis $Ox$ only if $\cos\theta + \cos\phi$ vanishes.

Values to draw: theta = 25 degrees; phi = 45 degrees.
Alt: Two views along k of the bases of the bars, with the angles measured from the downward vertical and the polar angles theta - pi/2 and phi - pi/2.
Caption: Rotations about $\mathbf k$ (out of the paper). An angle from the downward vertical is the polar angle plus $\pi/2$; with $\cos(\theta - \pi/2) = \sin\theta$ and $\sin(\theta - \pi/2) = -\cos\theta$ the triangle gives $\mathbf i_1 = \sin\theta\,\mathbf i - \cos\theta\,\mathbf j$.
