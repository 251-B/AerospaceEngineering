kind: plane / meridian section
view: the plane of the motion; k0 points OUT of the paper (⊙ at O); i0 to the right, j0 up.
draw: a = 1 (drawing radius about 3 units), drawing configuration phi = 30 deg, so P = (cos^3 30, sin^3 30) = (0.650, 0.125).
      - Grey axes i0 and j0 through O, from -1.2 to 1.2.
      - The whole astroid x = cos^3 phi, y = sin^3 phi (phi from 0 to 360 deg) thin grey, with its four cusps (1,0), (0,1), (-1,0), (0,-1) marked by small dots.
      - The first-quadrant arc (phi from 0 to 90 deg) drawn thick dark blue, with an arrow along it showing the sense of motion from (1, 0) to (0, 1).
      - Start cusp (1, 0) labelled "phi = 0, t = 0, at rest"; end cusp (0, 1) labelled "phi = pi/2, t = sqrt(3a/k)".
      - Black arrow O->P labelled "r_0^P".
      - Shaded right triangle O, (0.650, 0), P: horizontal leg on i0 (dashed) labelled "a cos^3(phi)", vertical leg (dashed) labelled "a sin^3(phi)", hypotenuse OP.
      - At P: orange unit vector e_t = (-cos30, sin30) = (-0.866, 0.5) (pointing up-left, tangent to the arc); green unit vector e_n = (sin30, cos30) = (0.5, 0.866) (pointing up-right, away from O). Draw them at about 0.35 length units.
      - Small symbol ⊗ near P labelled "e_b = -k0".
      - Centre of curvature C = P + rho e_n with rho = 3 sin30 cos30 = 1.299: C = (0.650 + 0.650, 0.125 + 1.125) = (1.300, 1.250); draw a faint dashed segment P->C labelled "rho = 3a sin(phi) cos(phi)" and a faint dashed arc of the osculating circle near P (if C is outside the frame, extend the frame to 1.4 or clip the segment with an arrow "toward C").

alt: Astroid with the first-quadrant smooth arc highlighted, the position vector of P with its two projections and the intrinsic vectors at P.
caption: The particle runs along the smooth arc between the cusps (a, 0) and (0, a). The position vector has projections a cos^3(phi) and a sin^3(phi); the normal e_n points away from O, toward the centre of curvature outside the arc.

## p03-parameter-angle
kind: other (auxiliary circle: parameter phi versus polar angle)
view: same plane, k0 OUT of the paper (⊙); i0 right, j0 up.
draw: a = 1, phi = 30 deg.
      - Grey axes i0, j0; first quadrant only (from -0.1 to 1.15 in both).
      - Grey dashed quarter circle of radius a (auxiliary circle).
      - Point Q on the circle at phi = 30 deg: Q = (0.866, 0.5); grey dashed segment O->Q; angle arc phi from i0 to OQ, labelled "phi".
      - The first-quadrant astroid arc, thick dark blue.
      - Point P = (0.650, 0.125) on the arc; black segment O->P; angle arc from i0 to OP labelled "vartheta" (polar angle, about 10.9 deg: tan(vartheta) = tan^3(30 deg) = 0.192).
      - Dashed vertical line from Q down to the i0 axis at x = cos(phi) = 0.866, and a dashed line from P to the i0 axis at x = cos^3(phi) = 0.650, to show the cubes of the coordinates of Q.
      - Mark the point at phi = 45 deg on the arc (0.354, 0.354) with a small open circle labelled "phi = vartheta = pi/4".
labels: "O", "Q (a cos(phi), a sin(phi))", "P (a cos^3(phi), a sin^3(phi))", "phi", "vartheta", "tan(vartheta) = tan^3(phi)", "a".
alt: Auxiliary circle showing that the parameter phi is the angle of the circle point Q, while the particle P is seen from O at the smaller polar angle vartheta.
caption: The parameter phi is the angle of the auxiliary point Q, not the polar angle of P. The two angles coincide only at 0, pi/4 and pi/2.

## p03-arc-element
kind: fold-down triangle
view: a magnified detail of the arc near P in the plane of the motion, k0 OUT of the paper (⊙); i0 right, j0 up.
draw: phi = 30 deg; draw the arc from P to a nearby point P' (phi + d phi), magnified so that the triangle is about 3 cm across.
      - The arc segment P->P' (thick dark blue), going up and to the left (dx negative, dy positive). At phi = 30 deg the ratio |dy|/|dx| equals tan(phi) = 0.577, so the chord rises at 30 deg above the -i0 direction.
      - Shaded right triangle: P, the point P + (dx, 0) (to the left of P), and P'. Horizontal leg from P to the left, labelled "|dx| = 3a cos^2(phi) sin(phi) d phi"; vertical leg up to P', labelled "dy = 3a sin^2(phi) cos(phi) d phi"; hypotenuse PP' labelled "ds = 3a sin(phi) cos(phi) d phi".
      - Angle arc phi at P between the -i0 direction (the horizontal leg) and the hypotenuse, labelled "phi".
      - Orange arrow e_t along the hypotenuse from P.
      - A small grey reference arrow "-i0" along the horizontal leg.
labels: "P", "P'", "dx (negative)", "dy", "ds", "phi", "e_t", "ds^2 = dx^2 + dy^2".
alt: Infinitesimal right triangle at P with legs dx and dy and hypotenuse ds, the tangent rising at angle phi above the negative x direction.
caption: The hint of the statement is Pythagoras on the infinitesimal triangle: the legs are the changes of x and y, the hypotenuse is the arc element ds = 3a sin(phi) cos(phi) d phi.

alt: View along k0 with the tangent at angle phi above the negative x direction and the normal at angle phi from j0, turned clockwise from the tangent so the binormal points into the paper.
caption: The tangent makes the angle phi with -i0 (hence the minus sign of its i0 component) and the normal makes phi with j0. The turn from e_t to e_n is clockwise in this view, so e_b = -k0 points into the paper.
