## rb02-triangle-opa
View: the plane Oxy seen from above: k points out of the paper (⊙ at O), i to the right, j up. Origin O at the lower left.
Draw: thin grey axes x (i, to the right) and y (j, up) from O, ⊙ symbol for k at O. Point A on the x axis at distance a from O; point P on the y axis at height a tan(theta). Right triangle O-P-A with a light translucent blue fill and a right-angle mark at O. Leg OA labelled "a" (below it), leg OP labelled "a tan θ" (left of it), hypotenuse PA labelled "a / cos θ" (above-right of its midpoint, off the line). Angle arc theta at vertex A between the segment A->O (towards negative x) and the segment A->P, labelled θ. The line through P and A extended a little beyond A, labelled "Px_B". Thick blue unit vector i_B (= i_1) drawn from P along PA (length 0.35 a), labelled i_B. A second small angle arc theta at P between a dashed horizontal line through P (parallel to Ox) and the segment PA, labelled θ.
Values to draw: theta = 35 deg, a = 1 (P at y = 0.70).
Alt: Right triangle formed by O, the fixed point A on Ox and the point P on Oy, with the angle theta at A.
Caption: Plane $Oxy$. The axis $Px_B$ is the hypotenuse $PA$ of the right triangle $OPA$; with the angle $\theta$ at $A$, the opposite leg gives the position of $P$ and the hypotenuse has length $a/\cos\theta$.

## rb02-rotation-k
View: looking down the k axis: k points out of the paper (⊙ at the centre), i to the right, j up.
Draw: thin grey axes i (right) and j (up) from the centre, labelled i and j. Thick blue unit vectors i_1 = cos θ i − sin θ j (turned clockwise from i, below the i axis) and j_1 = sin θ i + cos θ j (turned clockwise from j, to the right of the j axis), labelled i_1 and j_1. Clockwise angle arcs with arrowheads from i to i_1 and from j to j_1, each labelled θ, and a small note "rotation −θ about k" near the ⊙. Dashed projections of the tip of i_1 onto i (length cos θ) and onto −j (length sin θ), with a light translucent orange right triangle (hypotenuse 1 along i_1, adjacent leg cos θ along i, opposite leg sin θ along −j) and the leg labels.
Values to draw: theta = 35 deg, unit vectors of length 1.
Alt: View along k showing the first rotation, clockwise by theta, that takes i and j to i_1 and j_1.
Caption: First rotation, viewed with $\mathbf k$ out of the paper. The axis $Px_B$ turns clockwise from $\mathbf i$ by $\theta$, the negative sense about $\mathbf k$, so $\mathbf i_1$ has the component $-\sin\theta$ along $\mathbf j$.

## rb02-rotation-i1
View: looking down the i_1 axis: i_1 points out of the paper (⊙ at the centre), j_1 to the right, k_1 (= k) up. The horizontal line through the centre is the trace of the plane Oxy.
Draw: thin grey axes j_1 (right) and k_1 (up), labelled j_1 and k_1; a thin dashed grey horizontal line labelled "plane Oxy". Thick blue unit vectors j_B = cos θ j_1 + sin θ k_1 (above the right axis) and k_B = −sin θ j_1 + cos θ k_1 (to the left of the up axis), labelled j_B and k_B. Counterclockwise angle arcs with arrowheads from j_1 to j_B and from k_1 to k_B, each labelled θ. Dashed projection of the tip of j_B down to the j_1 axis, with a light translucent orange right triangle (hypotenuse 1 along j_B, adjacent leg cos θ along j_1, opposite leg sin θ vertical) and the leg labels; the opposite leg also labelled "lift of Py_B above Oxy".
Values to draw: theta = 35 deg, unit vectors of length 1.
Alt: View along i_1 showing the second rotation, counterclockwise by theta, that lifts j_B above the plane Oxy.
Caption: Second rotation, viewed with $\mathbf i_1$ out of the paper. Turning about $\mathbf i_1$ keeps $Px_B$ in the plane $Oxy$ and lifts $Py_B$ above that plane by the angle $\theta$, as the third condition requires.
