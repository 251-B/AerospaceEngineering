## rb15-rot-psi
View: from above, k1 out of the paper (⊙ at G), i1 to the right, j1 up.
Draw: grey axes i1 and j1. Coloured (blue) i and j' turned counterclockwise by psi. Arcs of psi (counterclockwise, arrowheads) from i1 to i and from j1 to j', labelled psi. Light translucent triangles: i with adjacent leg cos psi along i1 and dashed opposite leg sin psi along j1; j' with adjacent leg cos psi along j1 and dashed opposite leg sin psi towards negative i1. Label i also as "Gx (line of nodes, horizontal)".
Values to draw: psi = 35 deg.
Alt: Precession: the horizontal axis Gx turned by psi from O1x1 about the vertical.
Caption: Precession about $\mathbf k_1$ (⊙) by $\psi$; $\mathbf i$ is the horizontal axis $Gx$ of the statement.

## rb15-rot-theta
View: i out of the paper (⊙ at G), j' to the right, k1 up.
Draw: grey axes j' and k1. Blue j and k turned counterclockwise by theta. Arcs of theta (counterclockwise, arrowheads) from j' to j and from k1 to k, labelled theta. Light translucent triangles: j with adjacent leg cos theta along j' and dashed opposite leg sin theta along k1; k with adjacent leg cos theta along k1 and dashed opposite leg sin theta towards negative j'. Label k as "Gz (axis of revolution, towards the rim)".
Values to draw: theta = 35 deg.
Alt: Nutation: the axis of revolution Gz tilted by theta from the vertical about the horizontal axis Gx.
Caption: Nutation about $\mathbf i$ (⊙) by $\theta$. The tip of $\mathbf k_1$ moves towards negative $\mathbf j'$, which gives the minus sign in $\mathbf k = -\sin\theta\,\mathbf j' + \cos\theta\,\mathbf k_1$.

## rb15-rot-phi
View: k out of the paper (⊙ at G), i to the right, j up.
Draw: grey axes i and j (the frame Gxyz). Body vectors i_b and j_b (green) turned counterclockwise by phi, with arcs of phi from i to i_b and from j to j_b and the light triangles (cos phi adjacent, sin phi opposite; the leg of j_b towards negative i).
Values to draw: phi = 50 deg.
Alt: Spin: the body axes turned by phi about the axis of revolution.
Caption: Spin about $\mathbf k$ (⊙) by $\varphi$. The frame $Gxyz$ does not follow this rotation; the axisymmetric tensor keeps the same components in both bases.

## rb15-meridian
View: the vertical plane that contains k1 and k (the meridian section); i points out of the paper (⊙), j' to the right, k1 up.
Draw: hatched horizontal plane line. The section of the hemispherical shell: a half circle of radius a centred at O, tilted so that its axis k (from the pole to O, continuing beyond the rim) makes the angle theta with the vertical; the rim is the diameter normal to k through O. Contact point P on the plane straight below O (dot, label P), dashed vertical OP labelled a. Pole on the axis at a from O. G (dot) on the axis between O and the pole, at a/2 from O. Light translucent orange right triangle O, foot of G on the vertical through O, G: hypotenuse a/2, vertical leg (a/2) cos theta (downwards from O), horizontal leg (a/2) sin theta (along j'). Arc of theta at O between the upward vertical and k. Blue k from G; grey k1. Dashed arm from G to P. Height z_G = a - (a/2) cos theta marked from the plane to G.
Values to draw: a = 3, theta = 35 deg.
Alt: Meridian section of the tilted hemispherical shell with its centre O at height a above the contact point and G halfway between O and the pole.
Caption: Meridian section. $O$ stays at the height $a$ above the contact point $P$; $G$ is at $a/2$ from $O$ along $-\mathbf k$, so its height is $a - \tfrac a2\cos\theta$.

## rb15-shell-element
View: oblique 3D view of the shell in body axes from O; k up (the pole at the bottom, the rim circle at the top, through O).
Draw: hemispherical shell (light translucent grey) below the rim circle of radius a. Polar angle beta measured from the downward axis (the pole) to the radius of a point; azimuth alpha from i_b in the rim plane. A small surface element at (beta, alpha), labelled dA = a^2 sin beta d beta d alpha, with dashed radius a from O and the arcs of beta and alpha. G on the axis at a/2 below O (dot, label G).
Values to draw: a = 3, beta = 55 deg, alpha = 40 deg.
Alt: Spherical surface element of the hemispherical shell and the position of its centre of mass.
Caption: Surface element $a^2\sin\beta\,d\beta\,d\alpha$ of the shell. Every point is at the distance $a$ from $O$, which gives the isotropic tensor $\tfrac23 ma^2[U]$ about $O$; $G$ lies at $a/2$ below $O$.

## rb15-fbd
View: meridian section as in rb15-meridian (i out of the paper).
Draw: the shell section outline, plane faint grey. At G: weight -mg k1 (red, down), label mg. At P: normal reaction N k1 (green, up, its line passing through O), label N. Dashed arm from G to P. Note on the figure: "smooth plane: no friction". The moment of N about G drawn as a small curved arrow about the axis i (sense: minus i, as M_G = -(a/2) N sin theta i).
Values to draw: as in rb15-meridian.
Alt: Free-body diagram of the shell: weight at G and vertical normal reaction at the contact point, whose line passes through O.
Caption: Free-body diagram. Both forces are vertical, so the horizontal momentum is conserved; the reaction passes through $O$ on the axis of revolution and has no moment about $Gz$ nor about the vertical through $G$.
