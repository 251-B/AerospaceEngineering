# Briefs of problem 40 (particle, Topic 5): figures and simulator

The module `tools/figures/em_sol/p40.py` holds `p40-top-view` and `p40-ring`. `p40-top-view` still fits the new solution and is kept unchanged. `p40-ring` keeps its name but must gain the label of the point F (the text now names the triangle DFQ); its full brief is below. One figure is new: `p40-fbd`. Colour code (as in the module): fixed axes grey; frame S1 blue; ring basis orange; ring outline dark grey; actual forces (weights, spring, reactions) pink solid; inertia forces pink dashed with the word "inertia" in their label.

## p40-ring
View: the plane of the ring seen from above, k1 OUT of the paper (⊙, label "k1" near the top right corner), i1 to the right, j1 up; O at the origin; the ring is fixed in S1.
Draw: thin grey axes x1 (horizontal through O) and y1 (vertical through O); the ring, a circle of centre D = (0, l) and radius R, dark grey; D as a filled dot labelled "D"; O labelled "O"; the dimension "ℓ" between O and D on the right of the y1 axis. Q on the ring at the angle phi measured at D from the +j1 direction counterclockwise (towards −i1), filled dot labelled "Q". The radius DQ (dark grey solid) with the label "R" beside its middle. The point F = foot of the perpendicular from Q to the y1 axis (same height as Q, on the line through D along j1): a small dot labelled "F" placed to the right of the axis, off the lines. Shaded right triangle D F Q (light translucent fill) with the right-angle mark at F; leg DF labelled "R cos φ" (along +j1), leg FQ dashed and labelled "R sin φ" (along −i1); arc of phi at D from the +j1 direction to DQ, counterclockwise, labelled "φ". At Q the orange unit vectors e_r (along DQ, outwards) and e_phi (tangent, in the sense of growing phi, i.e. e_r turned 90 deg counterclockwise), labels "e_r" and "e_φ" off the ring. Small blue i1 and j1 at O.
Values to draw: l = 1.6, R = 1, phi = 50 deg; unit 125 px.
Alt: Plane of the ring from above: centre D on the j1 axis at distance l from O, particle Q at angle phi from j1, right triangle DFQ with legs R cos phi and R sin phi, ring basis at Q.
Caption: Plane of the ring, fixed in $S_1$, seen from above ($\mathbf k_1$ out of the paper). $\overrightarrow{OQ} = \ell\,\mathbf j_1 + R\,\mathbf e_r$; in the shaded right triangle $DFQ$ (right angle at $F$) the hypotenuse is $R$, the leg along $\mathbf j_1$ is $R\cos\phi$ and the leg along $-\mathbf i_1$ is $R\sin\phi$. $\mathbf i_1, \mathbf j_1$ in blue, $\mathbf e_r, \mathbf e_\phi$ in orange.

## p40-fbd
Two panels side by side.
Left panel. View: the vertical plane that contains the axis and Q, seen from the side; k1 up, the horizontal direction from the axis towards Q to the right (label "towards Q (horizontal)"). Draw: the vertical axis as a thick grey line labelled "k1"; P on it at height zeta above O (filled dot "P", O labelled "O" below); Q to the right at the level of O (filled dot "Q"); the spring as a zigzag from P to Q, label "k". Forces on P (each a separate arrow, labels off the lines): pink "−mg k1" down; pink "k PQ" along the spring towards Q; pink "N1 i1 + N2 j1" horizontal, pointing away from Q, with the note "(normal to the axis)". Forces on Q: pink "−mg k1" down; pink "−k PQ" along the spring towards P; pink "N4 k1" up. A note under P: "no inertia force on the axis".
Right panel. View: from above, k1 OUT of the paper (⊙ at O, label "k1"), i1 to the right, j1 up; the ring of centre D = (0, l), radius R, Q at phi = 50 deg as in p40-ring. Forces on Q in the horizontal plane: pink "N3 e_r" along e_r (outwards from D); pink "horizontal part of −k PQ" from Q towards O; pink dashed "F_C = m Ω² r1^Q (inertia)" from Q along the direction O to Q, away from the axis; pink dashed "F_COR = 2 m Ω R φ̇ e_r (inertia)" along e_r, drawn for a positive phi rate, offset sideways by 0.08 units so it does not overlap N3 e_r, with the note "(drawn for φ̇ > 0)". Orange e_r and e_phi at Q (short). A counterclockwise curved arrow about O labelled "Ω".
Values to draw: zeta = 0.8, l = 1.6, R = 1, phi = 50 deg; unit 100 px left, 90 px right.
Alt: Free-body diagrams: P on the vertical axis with weight, spring force and the horizontal reaction; Q on the ring with weight, spring force, reactions, and the centrifugal and Coriolis inertia forces seen from above.
Caption: Forces seen from $S_1$. On $P$: weight, spring $k\,\overrightarrow{PQ}$ and the reaction of the axis, normal to it; no inertia force, since $P$ is on the axis. On $Q$: weight, spring $-k\,\overrightarrow{PQ}$, reaction $N_3\,\mathbf e_r + N_4\,\mathbf k_1$, and the centrifugal and Coriolis forces (dashed), both horizontal.
