## 1. Core concepts

| Pair | Where it is recorded | Key distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Income vs. collection | Income statement vs. cash flow table | Income is earned when goods or services are delivered. Collection is cash received. |
| Expense vs. payment | Income statement vs. cash flow table | Expense is value consumed in the period. Payment is cash paid out. |
| Investment vs. depreciation | Balance sheet vs. income statement | Buying a long-term asset is an exchange of cash for an asset. Depreciation spreads its cost over its useful life. |

Definitions consistent with the course background sheet (*Background for Practice Financial Statements*, p. 1–2):

- **EBITDA** = income − expenses (before interest, depreciation and tax).
- **EBIT** = EBITDA − depreciation.
- **EBT** (net income before tax) = EBIT − interest.
- **Net income** = EBT − corporate tax.

Accrual principle: income and expenses are recognised when value is created or consumed, not when cash moves.

## 2. Video-by-video summary

### Video 1. Framework and three-step procedure
- Three formats: balance sheet, income statement, cash flow table.
- For each transaction, ask in order: (1) Is there a cash inflow or outflow? → cash flow table. (2) Does wealth increase or decrease? → income statement. (3) Does a balance sheet item change? → balance sheet.
- Example opening balances are zero for every account on 1 January 2016.
- Balance sheet identity: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Closing balance = opening balance + period variations.

### Video 2. Initial capital
- Shareholders pay €100,000 in cash for shares.
- Cash inflow (collection) +€100,000; income €0; expense €0.
- Paid-in capital (equity) +€100,000.
- Capital contribution is financing, not income.

### Video 3. Inventory purchases on 30-day credit
- Summary: 2,000 units per month at €15/unit; "40,000 units/year"; supplier paid 30 days after purchase.
- Cash paid in 2016 = Jan–Nov purchases × €15.
- Inventory asset = purchases × €15; accounts payable = December invoice.
- Purchase of inventory is not an expense. Cost becomes COGS only when the goods are sold.
- **Not verified, arithmetic inconsistency in the summary.** 2,000 units/month × 12 months = 24,000 units/year, not 40,000. With 2,000 units/month, the figures would be: Jan–Nov cash paid = 22,000 × €15 = **€330,000**, inventory = 24,000 × €15 = **€360,000**, payable = €30,000. The summary's figures (€570,000 and €600,000) use 38,000 and 40,000 units. Check the video itself for which number is correct.

### Video 4. Labour and general expenses
- Labour: €10,000 per month, paid in the same month. Total 2016 = €120,000.
- General expenses: €3,000 per month, paid 60 days later. Total 2016 expense = €36,000.
- Cash paid in 2016 = €120,000 + 10 × €3,000 = **€150,000** ✓.
- Expense in 2016 = €156,000 ✓.
- Unpaid at year end (Nov–Dec general expenses) = **€6,000** payable ✓.

### Video 5. Bank loan with accrued interest
- Loan of €50,000 received 31 March 2016 at 5% per year. Principal due 1 April 2017.
- Interest for 9 months (April–December) = €50,000 × 5% × 9/12 = **€1,875** ✓.
- Interest is paid on 31 December 2016 and is a financial expense.
- Loan is a current liability at year end because it is due within 12 months ✓.

### Video 6. Equipment and straight-line depreciation
- Machine bought 1 April 2016 for €60,000 cash, 10-year life.
- Annual depreciation = €60,000 / 10 = €6,000 ✓.
- 2016 depreciation (9 months) = €6,000 × 9/12 = **€4,500** ✓.
- Net book value at 31 December 2016 = €60,000 − €4,500 = **€55,500** ✓.

### Video 7. Credit sales and COGS
- Revenue 2016 = €900,000. Collected Jan–Oct (10/12) = €750,000 ✓; receivable Nov–Dec = €150,000 ✓.
- COGS is taken from inventory at €15/unit.
- **Not verified, incomplete.** The summary does not give the number of units sold, so COGS, EBITDA and EBIT are not computable from it.

### Video 8. Corporate tax and net income
- Tax base = EBT. Tax expense = EBT × tax rate. Tax is paid in the following year, so it creates a current liability.
- **Not verified, incomplete.** The summary gives no EBT or tax amount, because COGS is missing (see Video 7).

### Video 9. Profit allocation and balance sheet
- 40% of 2016 net income paid as dividend on 31 December 2016 (cash outflow, not an expense). 60% retained earnings.
- Closing cash = opening cash (€0) + net cash flow 2016.
- Balance sheet check: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
- **Not verified, incomplete.** Closing cash and the balance check are described but not computed in the summary.
