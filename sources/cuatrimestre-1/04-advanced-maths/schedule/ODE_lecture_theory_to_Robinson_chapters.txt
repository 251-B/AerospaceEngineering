======================================================================
NOTES ON DIFFERENCES BETWEEN LECTURE AND BOOK
======================================================================

- Order of presentation: the lecture runs ahead of the book in places.
  The mass-spring example (Sep 22) is in Ch. 13, after the Ch. 12
  solution methods, and logistic growth is in Ch. 8 although it was
  seen together with the Ch. 7 phase portraits.

- Terminology: the book never says "Picard's theorem" or "well-posed".
  It states the result as Theorem 6.2 and discusses existence and
  uniqueness in Sec. 6.1. The book calls the method "undetermined
  coefficients".

- Free fall: the book derives F = ma in Sec. 5.3 but treats free fall
  only as Exercise 5.6 (an apple falling from height h), so the
  derivation seen in class comes from Sec. 5.3.

- Variation of constants (Oct 1): Ch. 18 is a starred chapter, which
  the book marks as optional material. It assumes you already know two
  independent solutions of the homogeneous equation, so it builds on
  Ch. 11 and Ch. 17 (reduction of order). Ch. 18 has no numbered
  sections.

- Systems of ODEs (Sep 8 and Oct 6): the book only mentions them in
  passing in Ch. 3. Their treatment is Part V (Ch. 25 onwards), which
  the lecture reached on Oct 6.

- Systems (Oct 6): the lecture develops the theory of linear systems
  (Wronskian, fundamental set, homogeneous + particular) in a general
  way, which the book does not do. Robinson is concerned with two
  equations only (n = 2) and moves quickly to explicit solutions and
  phase portraits. Ch. 25 is starred (optional in the book), and
  Ch. 26 has no numbered sections.

- Order of the two solution methods: the book presents "convert to a
  second order ODE" first (Ch. 26) and the eigenvalue method after it
  (Ch. 27-28), the same order as the lecture. Ch. 27 only sets up the
  eigenvalue problem; the explicit solutions by eigenvectors are in
  Ch. 28 (distinct real), Ch. 29 (complex) and Ch. 30 (repeated).

- Eigenvalues and phase portraits (Oct 8): the lecture has reached
  Case 1 (distinct real eigenvalues, Ch. 28). Still ahead are the
  complex case (Ch. 29), the repeated eigenvalue (Ch. 30), the summary
  of phase portraits (Ch. 31) and, within Ch. 28, Sec. 28.5 on stable
  and unstable manifolds (p. 281).

- Terminology (Oct 8): the book never says "diagonalizable" or
  "triangular". It describes the same ideas as "changing coordinates"
  (Sec. 28.2) and as matrices of the form [lambda1 b; 0 lambda2] (the
  upper-triangular case, Example 27.3). It writes the new variable as
  x~ (x with a tilde) where the lecture uses z, so z = P^(-1) x
  corresponds to x~ = P^(-1) x, Eq. (28.5).

- "Saddle node": the book only says "saddle point" or "saddle". The
  word "node" is reserved for stable and unstable nodes (Sec. 28.3),
  so "saddle node" in the lecture notes is probably "saddle".

- Linear independence (Oct 8): the book proves independence of the
  eigenvectors, not of the vector solutions e^(lambda t) v directly,
  and it does not use the Wronskian for systems (see the Oct 6 note
  above).
