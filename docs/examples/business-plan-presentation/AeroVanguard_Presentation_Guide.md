## Defence cheat sheet (about 15 seconds each)

Questions 1 to 10 are the classic traps; the rest are the next most likely. Every member has at least one.

**1. Why is NPV negative without residual value?** (CFO)

Because in Year 5 we are still growing: one point five million goes into working capital that year. But the company doesn't close. It keeps four point nine five million of assets and working capital, at book value only. With it, NPV is two point two six million.

**2. Why a cost of equity of 18%?** (CFO)

VCs quote thirty-one to fifty percent because they put failure risk inside the rate (Gompers, 2020). Following Damodaran, we put that risk in the cash flows and the scenarios. Even at thirty percent, NPV with residual value stays positive, about zero point seven five million.

**3. How do you certify for Defence versus civil customers?** (CTO)

Civil: under twenty-five kilos we fly in the EASA specific category, with a SORA authorisation from AESA. Defence aircraft are State aircraft, certified under Royal Decree 866 of 2015 by the Defence authority in DGAM, with INTA. That campaign is budgeted from Year 2.

**4. Why 5.2 M€ if the CAPEX is only 1.35 M€?** (CFO)

Because we certify for two years and must finance stock and receivables: three point eight five million is cash for that. Even so, cash falls to zero point seven eight million in Year 3, so the round is not too big. Later CAPEX, one point three five million, comes from our own cash.

**5. What happens in the pessimistic scenario?** (CFO)

Units down twenty-five percent from Year 2 and price down five. We still make a profit from Year 4, but cash would go one point three five million negative. A one-point-five-million credit line, negotiated in Year 2, covers it, and we slow hiring.

**6. Why an S.L., and why a 300,000 € ENISA loan?** (COO)

S.L.: limited liability, and the partners decide who comes in, which matters under security screening; investors join by capital increase, and we can become an S.A. later. ENISA is a participative loan with no personal guarantees, next to a bank loan, at a blended six percent.

**7. Why should we believe 45 systems in Year 3?** (CMO)

Forty-five systems is about two orders the size of Romania's twenty-two Vectors, and thirty percent of units go to private operators, who decide in three to six months.

**8. What stops Quantum, Indra or DJI from copying you?** (CTO)

Our VRIO is honest: the supply chain and the design can be copied in two or three years. The on-board autonomy is hard to copy: it is our own software, and it will improve with every hour flown. DJI is distrusted at sensitive sites, and with Indra we want to be a subcontractor, not a rival.

**9. Is 160,000 € the right price?** (CMO)

It is value-based: the five-year cost is ninety-two point seven percent below a helicopter. It sits above a Skydio, about twenty thousand euros, and far below a Vector. Variable cost is about ninety-five thousand, so each aircraft contributes about sixty-five thousand.

**10. Why does the TCO include a pilot if the system is autonomous?** (CMO)

Flights beyond visual line of sight still need a remote pilot to supervise. We count a quarter of a full-time pilot: about fifty-eight thousand euros over five years. It's the customer's cost, not ours, and we call the comparison illustrative.

**11. Is 3.7 M€ for 40% a fair valuation?** (CFO)

Post-money is nine point two five million: investors pay one euro of nominal plus seventeen euros and fifty cents of premium per participation. By our own rough measure that is under two times Year 5 net income, and founders keep sixty percent with four-year vesting.

**12. Your NPV is 2.26 M€, but you value the company at 9.25 M€. Why?** (CFO)

NPV is not the company's value: it is the value created after paying back every euro invested at fourteen point nine percent. The share price is agreed with investors, and by our own rough measure it is under two times Year 5 net income.

**13. Will you need another round?** (CFO)

The base case needs none: Year 5 ends with six point eight one million in cash and no debt. Only the pessimistic case needs the one-and-a-half-million credit line.

**14. How exactly do you compute break-even?** (CFO)

All fixed charges, including depreciation and interest, are three point eight nine million in Year 3. Divided by a forty-six point four percent contribution margin, that is eight point three seven million of revenue: thirty-nine point one full systems, each with its share of docks and licences. Bare aircraft alone would need about sixty.

**15. You make a profit in Year 3, so why does cash fall?** (CFO)

Revenue more than doubles, so receivables and stock absorb zero point eight five million of extra working capital. We also invest half a million in equipment and repay three hundred thousand of debt. Profit is not cash.

**16. Why a 25% tax shield in the WACC if you pay 0 to 15%?** (CFO)

The WACC is a long-run rate, and after the two new-entity years we pay the general twenty-five percent. We pay fifteen only in Years 4 and 5, the first two years with a positive tax base after offsetting the launch losses.

**17. Why take debt at all if you raise equity?** (COO)

It costs six percent, interest is tax-deductible, and ENISA asks for no personal guarantees. From Year 3 return on assets is about sixteen percent, well above the six percent cost of debt, so the loans raise the return to shareholders while we repay them by Year 5.

**18. Do you need an external audit?** (COO)

Yes, from Year 2. By size alone it would start in Year 4, but public contracts above six hundred thousand euros and over half of turnover bring it forward under the Audit Act. It is budgeted.

**19. What does VanguardOS do, and why would 90% keep paying?** (Head of AI)

It commands the fleet, plans routes and runs the detection models we retrain and update. Ninety percent is our estimate. Without licences we would still be profitable in Years 4 and 5, but their margin is what makes break-even possible in Year 3.

**20. How does it fly without GPS?** (Head of AI)

It navigates with its cameras when GPS is blocked. We test jamming only at authorised ranges, because jamming needs a spectrum permit.

**21. What about the AI Act, data and cyberattacks?** (Head of AI)

Our civil software follows the AI Act. VanguardOS runs on European cloud servers or, as signed packages, on the customer's own defence network. Links are encrypted, and we pay for external security audits.

**22. Your TCO counts one dock per aircraft, but you sell one dock for every two.** (CMO)

Right: the TCO shows one aircraft on one site, as a cautious example. When two aircraft share a dock, the cost per aircraft falls, so the saving is even bigger.

**23. What is your biggest weakness?** (CEO)

No brand, references or certificates yet, little capital against big rivals, and few suppliers for key parts. So we also sell to private operators, who decide faster, and partner with Indra, Airbus or Thales instead of fighting them.

**24. What if certification is late?** (CTO)

We hire a compliance engineer in Year 1, we stay under twenty-five kilos so civil flights need no type certificate, and we sell first in Spain. The pessimistic scenario already assumes slower sales.

**25. What do SORA and BVLOS mean?** (CTO)

BVLOS is flying beyond the pilot's visual line of sight. SORA, the Specific Operations Risk Assessment, is the safety case AESA approves before it allows those flights.

**26. Why Getafe if you cannot fly there?** (COO)

Getafe gives us the aerospace cluster, suppliers and engineers, at about five euros per square metre a month. We build there and fly at ATLAS, in Jaén, and CIAR, in Lugo.

**27. Why assume 60 days to get paid if the public sector pays in 30?** (CFO)

It's cautious: sixty days is the legal limit between companies under Ley 15/2010. Assuming sixty means we finance more receivables, so the plan holds less cash, not more.

**28. Your payback is 4.5 years. Is that discounted?** (CFO)

No, it is simple payback. Discounted at fourteen point nine percent, the five cash flows alone do not pay back within five years. That is why we show NPV with residual value: in Year 5 the company still owns four point nine five million of assets and working capital.

**29. In the pessimistic case NPV is minus 1.85 M€. Why invest?** (CMO)

That case cuts units by twenty-five percent and price by five at the same time. Even then we make a profit from Year 4, and the credit line covers the cash. With twenty percent more units, NPV is four point three seven million. The risk is sales speed, and we test it with paid thirty-day trials.

**30. Why is your team the right one?** (CEO)

We are six aerospace engineers, and VRIO rates that as parity, so we don't sell the team as our advantage. What we add is focus: one product family, and partners like Indra for the big programmes. Vesting keeps the founders here for four years.

**31. 0.7% of which market?** (CMO)

Of the European small-drone market forecast for 2030, three point seven billion euros. Today's market was two point one billion in 2025, so in Year 5 we are about one point three percent of the 2025 market.

**32. Do you have any proof that customers want this?** (CMO)

No signed orders yet, and we say so. What we have is public demand: Spain's eighteen-million Skydio tender, Romania's order of twenty-two Vectors and the EU Defence Readiness Roadmap. We test it with paid thirty-day trials, and we only hire against signed orders.

**33. How do your investors get their money back?** (CFO)

The plan doesn't model an exit. Investors own forty percent of a company that earns five point three six million in Year 5, with six point eight one million in cash and no debt, and they hold two of the five board seats.

**34. Why a fixed-wing VTOL and not a quadcopter?** (CTO)

A quadcopter like Skydio's flies under an hour. Wings give us three hours, so one aircraft covers a whole perimeter, and vertical take-off still lets it land on the dock with nobody around.

**35. 24/7 or 300 hours a year?** (CMO)

Both: the dock keeps the aircraft ready day and night, and the TCO counts three hundred flight hours a year, the same hours as the helicopter patrol we compare with.

**36. Skydio is famous for autonomy. Why is yours different?** (Head of AI)

Skydio's autonomy flies short quadcopter missions, under an hour. Ours flies three-hour fixed-wing patrols and lands on its dock alone. The part our VRIO rates as sustained is the on-board perception and GPS-free navigation; the dock and the ITAR-free supply chain give us a two-to-three-year lead.

**37. Who buys your six systems in Year 1?** (CMO)

Six systems is one or two small contracts, all in Spain. We target launch pilots with public buyers and private operators after a paid thirty-day trial, and we hire only against signed orders.

**38. Which founders sit on the board?** (CEO)

The CEO, the CTO and the CFO: the CEO, the CTO and the CFO, with the two seed investors. The other founders lead their departments and report to the CEO.

**39. Your leverage index is above one in Year 1. Is debt helping you?** (CEO)

Not in Years 1 and 2: with losses, an index above one means the debt makes the loss bigger. From Year 3, return on assets of about sixteen percent is above the six percent cost of debt, so leverage helps.

**40. Why constant 2027 prices?** (CEO)

The plan states every figure at constant 2027 prices and without VAT, so the five years compare like with like, and the margins do not depend on an inflation guess.

**41. Is the payroll on the slide the full cost?** (COO)

No, it is gross pay. We add thirty-two percent employer social security, and the model includes it in personnel costs.

**42. Year 5 runs at 120 of 126 systems of capacity. What if demand peaks?** (COO)

Each cell works one shift, so a second shift is available for delivery peaks, and we open a new cell only with signed orders.

**43. How do you handle the dual-use and reputation risk?** (CEO)

We build no armed drones, and that is written policy. Every export has end-user control under the EU dual-use regulation, and our first sales are in Spain.
