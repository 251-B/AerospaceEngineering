Problem 17 · Slide 6 Benchmark

Linear Central Force under Gravity

Slide 6 / Problems.pdf P10

A force attracts a heavy particle $M$ of mass $m$ to a point $O$. The attractive force is proportional to the mass of the particle and to the distance $OM$, with proportionality constant $k^2$ ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{attract}} = -m k^2\mathbf{r}$). The coordinate system $Oxyz$ has origin at the center of attraction $O$ and axis $Oz$ is vertically oriented with gravity $\mathbf{g} = -g\mathbf{k}$.

Initially at $t = 0$: $(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}, 0, 0\right)$ and $(\dot{x}_0, \dot{y}_0, \dot{z}_0) = \left(0, \frac{2g}{k}, 0\right)$.

(a) Describe the motion of $M$, specifying clearly the shape of the path.
(b) What is the velocity of the particle as a function of $t$?

✅ Full Analytical Solution (4-Phase Methodology)
Phase 1: Statement & Hypotheses

Free particle in 3D ($\text{CDOF} = 3$). Inertial Cartesian frame $S_0$. Total force $\mathbf{F} = -m k^2 \mathbf{r} - mg\mathbf{k}$.

Phase 2: Vector Equations of Motion

By Newton's Second Law, the Cartesian equations decouple completely:

$$ \ddot{x} + k^2 x = 0, \qquad \ddot{y} + k^2 y = 0, \qquad \ddot{z} + k^2 z = -g $$
Phase 3: Mathematical Deduction
  1. Integrating $x(t)$: $x(t) = \frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}\cos(kt)$.
  2. Integrating $y(t)$: $y(t) = \frac{2g}{k^2}\sin(kt)$.
  3. Integrating $z(t)$: Equilibrium is $z_{\text{eq}} = -g/k^2$. With $z(0) = 0, \dot{z}(0) = 0$, $z(t) = \frac{g}{k^2}(\cos(kt) - 1)$.
  4. Planar Confinement: Eliminating $\cos(kt)$ between $x$ and $z$: $$ z(t) = \frac{x(t)}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{g}{k^2} \implies x - \sqrt{3}z - \frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2} = 0 $$ The motion is strictly confined to an inclined plane forming $30^\circ$ with the vertical.
  5. Elliptic Trajectory: Eliminating time $t$ between $x$ and $y$: $$ \left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{3}g/k^2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{y}{2g/k^2}\right)^2 = \cos^2(kt) + \sin^2(kt) = 1 $$ The path is an ellipse centered at $(0, 0, -g/k^2)$ with semi-axes $a_x = \frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}$ and $b_y = \frac{2g}{k^2}$.
  6. Velocity Vector and Constant Speed: $$ \mathbf{v}(t) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k}\sin(kt)\,\mathbf{i} + \frac{2g}{k}\cos(kt)\,\mathbf{j} - \frac{g}{k}\sin(kt)\,\mathbf{k} $$ $$ \|\mathbf{v}(t)\|^2 = \frac{g^2}{k^2}(3\sin^2(kt) + 4\cos^2(kt) + \sin^2(kt)) = \frac{4g^2}{k^2} \implies \|\mathbf{v}(t)\| = \frac{2g}{k} = \text{const} $$
Phase 4: Physical Interpretation

The speed is strictly constant despite the elliptical trajectory due to the circular symmetry of the total potential energy about the displaced center $(0, 0, -g/k^2)$. Dimensional verification: $[v] = [g/k] = \text{m/s}$.

Problem 42 · Advanced Power-Law Field

Particle Attracted to the Origin ($F = m\mu / r^\alpha$)

Problems.pdf P29

A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is attracted by the origin $O$ of inertial frame $S_0$ by a force with magnitude $F = \frac{m\mu}{r^\alpha}$, where $\mu, \alpha > 0$.

(a) Show that angular momentum $\mathbf{H}_O$ and mechanical energy $E$ are conserved, and that motion is planar.
(b) Using polar coordinates, derive a 2nd-order ODE for $u \equiv 1/r$ as a function of $\theta$.
(c) Find a first integral and determine for $\alpha = 1, 2, 3, 4$ under which conditions: (i) particle escapes to infinity ($u \to 0$); (ii) particle reaches origin ($u \to \infty$); (iii) particle oscillates between two radii.

✅ Full Analytical Solution (4-Phase Methodology)
Phase 1 & 2: Planar Central Motion

Torque $\mathbf{M}_O = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} \equiv \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{H}_O = \text{const}$. Since $\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{H}_O = 0$ and $\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{H}_O = 0$, motion is planar. Because $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$, energy $\xi = \frac{1}{2}v^2 + V_0(r) = \text{const}$.

Phase 3: Generalized Binet Equation

Using specific angular momentum $h = r^2\dot{\theta}$ and $u = 1/r$:

$$ \dot{r} = -h\frac{du}{d\theta}, \qquad \ddot{r} = -h^2 u^2 \frac{d^2u}{d\theta^2} $$ $$ \ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2 = -\mu u^\alpha \implies \frac{d^2u}{d\theta^2} + u = \frac{\mu}{h^2} u^{\alpha - 2} $$

First Integral & Effective Potential: Multiplying by $u'$ and integrating:

$$ (u')^2 + W_{\text{eff}}(u) = \frac{2\xi}{h^2} \equiv E^*, \qquad W_{\text{eff}}(u) = u^2 + \frac{2V_0(1/u)}{h^2} $$

Regime Analysis for $\alpha \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$:

  • $\alpha = 2$ (Kepler): $u'' + u = \mu/h^2$, $W(u) = u^2 - \frac{2\mu}{h^2}u$. Parabola with minimum at $u = \mu/h^2$. Escapes to infinity if $E^* \ge 0$. Collapsing to $r = 0$ is impossible due to the centrifugal barrier. Bounded oscillation (ellipses) occurs for $-(\mu/h^2)^2 < E^* < 0$.
  • $\alpha = 1$ ($F \propto 1/r$): $W(u) = u^2 - \frac{2\mu}{h^2}\ln(u)$. Since $W(u) \to +\infty$ both as $u \to 0$ and $u \to \infty$, escape to infinity and collision with origin are both impossible. All orbits are bound and oscillate.
  • $\alpha = 3$ ($F \propto 1/r^3$): $u'' + (1 - \mu/h^2)u = 0$, $W(u) = (1 - \mu/h^2)u^2$. If $h^2 < \mu$, $u(\theta)$ grows exponentially and the particle collapses into the center $r = 0$!
  • $\alpha = 4$ ($F \propto 1/r^4$): $W(u) = u^2 - \frac{2\mu}{3h^2}u^3$. A cubic barrier with maximum at $u_{\text{barrier}} = h^2/\mu$. If $E^*$ exceeds the barrier, the particle overcomes the centrifugal barrier and plunges into $r = 0$.
Phase 4: Physical Interpretation

For $\alpha \ge 3$, the attractive central force overcomes the repulsive centrifugal potential ($1/r^2$) at short distances, enabling orbital plunge.

Problem 43 · Holonomic Multi-Particle System

Two Connected Particles, Plane with Hole

Problems.pdf P30

A smooth horizontal plane has a small hole at $O$. A massless cord of length $2a$ passes through the hole, connecting two particles $P_1$ and $P_2$ of identical mass $m$. $P_1$ moves on the plane and $P_2$ moves vertically below the hole. Initially $r(0) = a$, and $P_1$ is pushed with transverse speed $v_0$.

(a) Find $v_0$ for circular motion.
(b) Find the cord tension in circular motion.
(c) If $v_0 = \sqrt{8ag/3}$, reduce the problem to quadratures.
(d) Show that $P_1$ oscillates between $r = a$ and $r = 2a$.
(e) Show that cord tension is $T = mg\left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{4a^3}{3r^3}\right)$.
(f) Write new equations if the plane has friction $\mu$.

✅ Full Analytical Solution (4-Phase Methodology)
Phase 1 & 2: Holonomic System

Constraint $r - z_2 = 2a \implies z_2 = r - 2a$. Energy $E = m\dot{r}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m\frac{h^2}{r^2} + mgr - 2mga = \text{const}$. Specific angular momentum $h = a v_0 = \text{const}$.

Phase 3: Mathematical Deduction
  1. (a) Circular motion ($r \equiv a$): $$ T = m\frac{v_0^2}{a} = mg \implies v_0 = \sqrt{ag} $$
  2. (b) Tension in circular orbit: $T = mg$.
  3. (c) Quadratures for $v_0 = \sqrt{8ag/3}$ ($h^2 = \frac{8}{3}a^3 g$): Initial energy: $E = \frac{1}{2}m(8ag/3) - mga = \frac{1}{3}mga$. $$ \dot{r}^2 = \frac{g}{3r^2}\left[ -3r^3 + 7a r^2 - 4a^3 \right] \implies \int_a^r \frac{r'\,dr'}{\sqrt{-3r'^3 + 7a r'^2 - 4a^3}} = \pm \sqrt{\frac{g}{3}}\,t $$
  4. (d) Oscillation Limits: Factoring with $x = r/a$: $-3x^3 + 7x^2 - 4 = -(x - 1)(x - 2)(3x + 2) \ge 0$. Because $r > 0$, $(3x+2) > 0$. Thus $-(x-1)(x-2) \ge 0 \implies 1 \le x \le 2 \implies a \le r(t) \le 2a$. The turning points are $r = a$ and $r = 2a$.
  5. (e) Cord Tension: For $P_2$: $m\ddot{r} = T - mg \implies T = m(g + \ddot{r})$. Differentiating $\dot{r}^2 = \frac{7}{3}ga - gr - \frac{4a^3 g}{3r^2}$: $$ 2\dot{r}\ddot{r} = \left(-g + \frac{8a^3 g}{3r^3}\right)\dot{r} \implies \ddot{r} = -\frac{g}{2} + \frac{4a^3 g}{3r^3} $$ $$ T = m\left(g - \frac{g}{2} + \frac{4a^3 g}{3r^3}\right) = mg\left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{4a^3}{3r^3}\right) \quad \text{Q.E.D.} $$
  6. (f) Equations with Friction $\mu$: $$ 2m\ddot{r} - mr\dot{\theta}^2 = -mg - \mu mg \frac{\dot{r}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}}, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\left(r^2\dot{\theta}\right) = -\mu g \frac{r^2\dot{\theta}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}} $$
Phase 4: Physical Checks

At $r = a$, $T = \frac{11}{6}mg > 0$; at $r = 2a$, $T = \frac{2}{3}mg > 0$. Cord remains strictly under tension at all times.

Problem 44 · Orbital Mechanics

The Little Prince Throwing Seeds

Problems.pdf P31

The little Prince sits on his spherical, non-rotating asteroid of radius $R$ and mass $M$. He launches a baobab seed with initial velocity of magnitude $v_0$ and angle $\beta = 60^\circ$ with respect to the local horizontal. Knowing that it reaches an apocenter of $r_a = 3R$ before falling back, determine $v_0$ as a function of $G, M, R$.

✅ Full Analytical Solution (4-Phase Methodology)
Phase 1 & 2: Launch Kinematics

At surface $r = R$, launch velocity has components $v_{r0} = v_0\sin(60^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}v_0$ and $v_{\theta 0} = v_0\cos(60^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}v_0$. Central gravity $\mathbf{F}_g = -\frac{GMm}{r^2}\mathbf{e}_r$.

Phase 3: Mathematical Deduction
  1. Specific Angular Momentum Conservation: $$ h = R v_{\theta 0} = \frac{1}{2} R v_0 $$ At apocenter $r_a = 3R$, velocity is purely transverse: $$ h = r_a v_a = 3R v_a \implies v_a = \frac{h}{3R} = \frac{1}{6}v_0 $$
  2. Specific Mechanical Energy Conservation: $$ \xi = \frac{1}{2}v_0^2 - \frac{GM}{R} = \frac{1}{2}v_a^2 - \frac{GM}{r_a} $$ Substitute $v_a = v_0/6$ and $r_a = 3R$: $$ \frac{1}{2}v_0^2 - \frac{GM}{R} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{v_0}{6}\right)^2 - \frac{GM}{3R} = \frac{v_0^2}{72} - \frac{GM}{3R} $$
  3. Algebraic Isolation of $v_0$: $$ \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{72}\right)v_0^2 = \left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right)\frac{GM}{R} $$ $$ \frac{35}{72}v_0^2 = \frac{2}{3}\frac{GM}{R} \implies v_0^2 = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{72}{35}\frac{GM}{R} = \frac{48}{35}\frac{GM}{R} $$ $$ v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{48}{35}\frac{GM}{R}} $$
Phase 4: Physical Interpretation

Escape velocity is $v_e = \sqrt{2GM/R} = \sqrt{\frac{70}{35}\frac{GM}{R}}$. Since $\frac{48}{35} \approx 1.371 < 2$, $v_0 < v_e$, confirming the seed is gravitationally bound and returns. Dimensional verification: $[v_0] = \sqrt{[G][M]/[R]} = \text{m/s}$.

Problem 39 · Constrained Hanging Cable

Particle on a String with a Plate

Problems.pdf P27

A heavy particle $P$ of mass $m$ is connected to origin $O$ on a horizontal thin square plate by a massless inextensible string of length $\ell$. Distance to plate edge is $d$. String hangs over the edge at $Q$. Initially $\theta(0) = \phi(0) = \pi/3$, $v_0^P(0) = \sqrt{\ell g}$.

(a) How many degrees of freedom does the system have?
(b) Obtain unit vector $\mathbf{u}_{QP}$ as function of $\theta$ and $\phi$.
(c) Obtain position, velocity, and acceleration vectors of $Q$ and $P$.
(d) Formulate equations of motion and discuss conservation laws.

✅ Full Analytical Solution (4-Phase Methodology)
Phase 1 & 2: Geometry & Kinematics

Total $\text{CDOF} = 2$ (angles $\theta$ and $\phi$). Inextensible string constraint $L_{OQ} + L_{QP} = \ell$ with $L_{OQ} = \frac{d}{\sin\theta}$. Contact point $\mathbf{r}_0^Q = d\cot\theta\,\mathbf{i}_0 + d\,\mathbf{j}_0$.

Phase 3: Mathematical Deduction
  1. (a) CDOF: 2 degrees of freedom ($\theta, \phi$).
  2. (b) Unit vector: $\mathbf{u}_{QP} = \sin\phi\cos\theta\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \sin\phi\sin\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0 - \cos\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0$.
  3. (c) Kinematic vectors: $$ \mathbf{r}_0^P = \mathbf{r}_0^Q + \left(\ell - \frac{d}{\sin\theta}\right)\mathbf{u}_{QP} $$ $$ \mathbf{v}_0^Q = -d\frac{\dot{\theta}}{\sin^2\theta}\,\mathbf{i}_0, \qquad \mathbf{v}_0^P = \mathbf{v}_0^Q + \frac{d\dot{\theta}\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\mathbf{u}_{QP} + \left(\ell - \frac{d}{\sin\theta}\right)\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{QP}}{dt} $$
  4. (d) Energy Conservation: Because string slides without friction over the edge, constraint reaction does no work. Mechanical energy $E = \frac{1}{2}m(v_0^P)^2 - mg z_P = \text{const}$.
Phase 4: Physical Interpretation

Hanging length $\ell - d/\sin\theta$ varies smoothly with angle $\theta$. Energy is strictly conserved.
