Problem 17

Linear Central Force under Gravity

Central Force + Gravity

A force attracts a heavy particle $M$ of mass $m$ to a point $O$. The attractive force is proportional to the mass of the particle and to the distance $OM$. The proportionality coefficient is $k^2$. The coordinate system $Oxyz$ has the origin at the centre of attraction $O$ and the axis $Oz$ is vertically oriented. Initially, the coordinates of $M$ are

$$ (x_0, y_0, z_0) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}, 0, 0\right) $$

and its initial velocity is

$$ (\dot{x}_0, \dot{y}_0, \dot{z}_0) = \left(0, \frac{2g}{k}, 0\right) $$
  • (a)Describe the motion of $M$, specifying clearly the shape of the path.
  • (b)What is the velocity of the particle as a function of $t$?
Worked Solution (4-Phase Methodology) [ Click to Expand ]
Phase 1

Physical Statement, Hypotheses & Parameters

  • System: a heavy particle $M$ of mass $m$ under (i) a linear central attraction $\mathbf{F}_a = -mk^2\mathbf{r}$ towards $O$ and (ii) its weight $\mathbf{F}_g = -mg\mathbf{k}$ ($Oz$ points up).
  • Hypotheses: $S_0 = \{O; \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\}$ is inertial; no drag or friction; $m$, $g$, $k$ are positive constants.
  • Degrees of freedom: free particle in 3D space, $\text{CDOF} = 3$ with coordinates $(x, y, z)$.
  • Initial conditions ($t = 0$): $\mathbf{r}(0) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{v}(0) = \dfrac{2g}{k}\mathbf{j}$.

Phase 2

Reference Frames, Vector Bases & Coordinate Geometry

  • Inertial frame: $S_0 = \{O; \mathcal{B}_0\}$, $\mathcal{B}_0 = \{\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\}$; $\mathbf{r} = x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{v} = \dot{x}\mathbf{i} + \dot{y}\mathbf{j} + \dot{z}\mathbf{k}$.
  • Orbit-plane basis (introduced after the planar result of Phase 3): $\mathcal{B}_1 = \{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \mathbf{e}_3\}$ with
$$ \mathbf{e}_1 = \left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, 0, \tfrac{1}{2}\right), \qquad \mathbf{e}_2 = \mathbf{j}, \qquad \mathbf{e}_3 = \mathbf{e}_1 \times \mathbf{e}_2 = \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}, 0, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) $$

Change-of-basis matrix (columns are the components of $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \mathbf{e}_3$ in $\mathcal{B}_0$):

$$ [{}_0 R_1] = \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{3}/2 & 0 & -1/2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1/2 & 0 & \sqrt{3}/2 \end{pmatrix} $$

Checks. $\mathbf{e}_1\cdot\mathbf{e}_1 = \tfrac{3}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4} = 1$, $\mathbf{e}_3\cdot\mathbf{e}_3 = \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{3}{4} = 1$, $\mathbf{e}_1\cdot\mathbf{e}_3 = -\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} = 0$, hence $[{}_0R_1]^T[{}_0R_1] = I$; and $\det[{}_0R_1] = \tfrac{3}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4} = 1$.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Mathematical Deduction

1. Equations of motion

Pedagogical Justification: $m\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{F}_a + \mathbf{F}_g$ in the inertial frame. Because the restoring force is linear in $\mathbf{r}$, each Cartesian component decouples.
$$ m\left(\ddot{x}\mathbf{i} + \ddot{y}\mathbf{j} + \ddot{z}\mathbf{k}\right) = -mk^2\left(x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}\right) - mg\mathbf{k} \implies \ddot{x} + k^2x = 0, \quad \ddot{y} + k^2y = 0, \quad \ddot{z} + k^2z = -g $$

2. Integration of $x(t)$ and $y(t)$

Pedagogical Justification: $\ddot{q} + k^2q = 0$ is linear with constant coefficients; its characteristic roots are $\lambda = \pm ik$.
$$ x(t) = A_1\cos kt + B_1\sin kt, \qquad \dot{x}(t) = -kA_1\sin kt + kB_1\cos kt $$

Initial data $x(0) = \sqrt{3}g/k^2$, $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ give $A_1 = \sqrt{3}g/k^2$ and $kB_1 = 0$. Likewise $y(0) = 0 \Rightarrow A_2 = 0$ and $\dot{y}(0) = 2g/k \Rightarrow kB_2 = 2g/k \Rightarrow B_2 = 2g/k^2$:

$$ x(t) = \frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}\cos kt, \qquad y(t) = \frac{2g}{k^2}\sin kt $$

3. Integration of $z(t)$

Pedagogical Justification: The equation $\ddot{z} + k^2z = -g$ is forced by a constant. A constant particular solution $z_p$ satisfies $k^2z_p = -g$, i.e. $z_p = -g/k^2$ (the equilibrium height), and the homogeneous part is as before.
$$ z(t) = -\frac{g}{k^2} + A_3\cos kt + B_3\sin kt, \qquad \dot{z}(t) = -kA_3\sin kt + kB_3\cos kt $$

$z(0) = 0 \Rightarrow A_3 = g/k^2$ and $\dot{z}(0) = 0 \Rightarrow B_3 = 0$:

$$ z(t) = \frac{g}{k^2}\left(\cos kt - 1\right) $$

4. Shape of the path

Planar confinement. $x$ and $z$ share the phase function $\cos kt$: from $\cos kt = k^2x/(\sqrt{3}g)$,

$$ z = \frac{g}{k^2}\left(\frac{k^2x}{\sqrt{3}g} - 1\right) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{g}{k^2} \implies x - \sqrt{3}\,z - \frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2} = 0 $$

The plane has normal $\mathbf{n} = (1, 0, -\sqrt{3})/2$ and contains the direction $(\sqrt{3}, 0, 1)$, which makes $\arctan(1/\sqrt{3}) = 30^\circ$ with the horizontal: the plane is inclined $30^\circ$ to the horizontal.

Circle. With $\mathbf{C} = (0, 0, -g/k^2)$, the distance to $\mathbf{C}$ is constant:

$$ x^2 + y^2 + \left(z + \frac{g}{k^2}\right)^2 = \frac{g^2}{k^4}\left[3\cos^2 kt + 4\sin^2 kt + \cos^2 kt\right] = \frac{4g^2}{k^4} $$

The distance from $\mathbf{C}$ to the plane is $\left|0 - \sqrt{3}(-g/k^2) - \sqrt{3}g/k^2\right|/\sqrt{4} = 0$, so the plane passes through the centre of the sphere and the path is a great circle of radius $R_c = 2g/k^2$, covered at angular rate $k$. In the plane basis of Phase 2 the three solutions combine into one vector identity:

$$ \mathbf{r}(t) - \mathbf{C} = R_c\left[\cos kt\,\mathbf{e}_1 + \sin kt\,\mathbf{e}_2\right] $$

since $R_c\cos kt\,\mathbf{e}_1 = \left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}g}{k^2}\cos kt,\, 0,\, \tfrac{g}{k^2}\cos kt\right)$ reproduces $x(t)$ and $z(t) + g/k^2$, and $R_c\sin kt\,\mathbf{e}_2$ reproduces $y(t)$. The $\mathbf{e}_3$-component vanishes (planar motion) and $\mathbf{e}_3\cdot\mathbf{k} = \sqrt{3}/2$ confirms the $30^\circ$ inclination of the normal to the vertical. Only the projection on $Oxy$, $\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{3}g/k^2}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{y}{2g/k^2}\right)^2 = 1$, is an ellipse; the circle is seen obliquely.

5. Velocity (part b)

Pedagogical Justification: Differentiate each component with respect to time (chain rule $\frac{d}{dt}\cos kt = -k\sin kt$).
$$ \mathbf{v}(t) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}g}{k}\sin kt\,\mathbf{i} + \frac{2g}{k}\cos kt\,\mathbf{j} - \frac{g}{k}\sin kt\,\mathbf{k} $$
$$ v^2 = \frac{g^2}{k^2}\left[3\sin^2 kt + 4\cos^2 kt + \sin^2 kt\right] = \frac{4g^2}{k^2}\left(\sin^2 kt + \cos^2 kt\right) = \frac{4g^2}{k^2} \implies v = \frac{2g}{k} = \text{const} $$
Phase 4

Physical Interpretation & Dimensional Verification

  • Why the speed is constant. The force is conservative with potential $V = \tfrac{1}{2}mk^2(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) + mgz = \tfrac{1}{2}mk^2\left[x^2 + y^2 + (z + g/k^2)^2\right] - \dfrac{mg^2}{2k^2}$, which depends only on the distance to $\mathbf{C}$. That distance is constant on the circle, so $V$ and therefore the kinetic energy are constant; also $v = kR_c = 2g/k$.
  • Initial-condition check: $\mathbf{r}(0) = (\sqrt{3}g/k^2, 0, 0)$ and $\mathbf{v}(0) = (0, 2g/k, 0)$ are reproduced by the formulas above.
  • Limit $g \to 0$: the centre $\mathbf{C} \to O$ and the path is the usual elliptic orbit of the isotropic oscillator, here in a plane through $O$.
  • Dimensions: $[g/k^2] = \dfrac{\text{m/s}^2}{\text{s}^{-2}} = \text{m}$, $[g/k] = \text{m/s}$, $[kt] = 1$.
  • Numerical check (Python, $g = 9.81$, $k = 2$): over $20$ s, $\max\left|\|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{C}\| - R_c\right| = 9\times 10^{-16}$ m, $\max|v - 2g/k| = 2\times 10^{-15}$ m/s, the plane residual $x - \sqrt{3}z - \sqrt{3}g/k^2$ stays below $3\times 10^{-15}$ m, and the finite-difference residual of $\ddot{x} + k^2x = 0$ and $\ddot{z} + k^2z + g = 0$ is below $4\times 10^{-7}$.
  • Official key (Problems.pdf, PDF page 73): circle given by the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + (z + g/k^2)^2 = 4g^2/k^4$ and the plane $x - \sqrt{3}z = \sqrt{3}g/k^2$, constant speed $2g/k$.

Problem 39

Particle on a String over a Plate Edge

String + Edge Constraint

A heavy point particle $P$ with mass $m$ is connected with point $O$ on the upper surface of a thin square plate by a massless, inextensible string of length $\ell$. The distance between $O$ and the edge of the plate is $d$. The string hangs over the edge of the plate and can slide without friction. You can assume that the string remains tense and in contact with the plate at all times.

We denote the geometric point where the string is in contact with the plate edge as $Q$. We define the inertial reference frame $S_0 : \{O, \mathcal{B}_0\}$, with $\mathcal{B}_0 = \{\mathbf{i}_0, \mathbf{j}_0, \mathbf{k}_0\}$ as shown in the figure (Problems.pdf, PDF page 27). We define the angle $\theta$ between the $Ox_0$ axis and the string segment $OQ$, and the angle $\phi$ between a vertical plane containing the plate edge and the line perpendicular to the plate edge that passes through $P$. Initially at time $t = 0$, $\theta(0) = \phi(0) = \pi/3$ rad, and the modulus of the velocity of $P$ is $v_0^P(0) = \sqrt{\ell g}$, where $g$ is the magnitude of the acceleration due to gravity.

Hint: Note that under the conditions of the problem, the two angles marked with dashed lines are equal.

  • (a)How many degrees of freedom does the system have?
  • (b)Obtain the general expression of a unit vector in the direction from $Q$ to $P$ as a function of $\theta$ and $\phi$.
  • (c)Obtain the general expression for the position, velocity, and acceleration vectors of $Q$ and $P$ in $S_0$ as a function of $\theta$, $\phi$, their derivatives, and the parameters of the problem.
  • (d)Identify and express mathematically all the forces acting on $P$.
  • (e)Find a set of second order differential equations to determine the evolution of $\theta$ and $\phi$ in time, free of any other unknowns.
  • (f)Does the tension force exert work on $P$? Provide proof of your answer.
  • (g)Derive the general expression of the mechanical energy of $P$ in $S_0$ and compute its value initially. Is this a conserved quantity of motion? Justify your answer.
Worked Solution (4-Phase Methodology) [ Click to Expand ]
Phase 1

Physical Statement, Hypotheses & Parameters

  • System: heavy particle $P$ on a massless string of length $\ell$ that slides without friction over the straight edge of a plate; $Q$ is the contact point.
  • Hypotheses: $S_0$ is inertial and $\mathbf{g} = -g\mathbf{k}_0$ ($Oz_0$ up). The edge is the line $x_0 = d$, $z_0 = 0$, parallel to $\mathbf{j}_0$ (read from the figure). The edge is smooth and the string is massless, so the tension $T \ge 0$ is the same on both sides of $Q$ and the edge force on the string is normal to the edge. $P$ does not touch the plate. No drag.
  • Degrees of freedom: $P$ has $3$ coordinates and one holonomic constraint $\|\mathbf{OQ}\| + \|\mathbf{QP}\| = \ell$, so $\text{CDOF} = 3 - 1 = 2$ with generalized coordinates $(\theta, \phi)$.
  • Initial state: $\theta(0) = \phi(0) = \pi/3$ and $\|\mathbf{v}_0^P(0)\| = \sqrt{\ell g}$. The statement gives only the speed, not its direction; for numerical checks $\dot{\phi}(0) = 0$ is assumed (the initial energy does not depend on it).

Parts (a)-(e) and (g) are derived here from the statement and the figure on PDF page 27, with two explicit assumptions: the edge is the line $x_0 = d$ parallel to $\mathbf{j}_0$, and $\phi$ is measured as described in the statement. Phase 2

Reference Frames, Vector Bases & Coordinate Geometry

  • Contact point $Q$. It lies on the edge with $OQ$ at angle $\theta$ from $Ox_0$:
$$ \mathbf{r}_0^Q = d\,\mathbf{i}_0 + d\tan\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0, \qquad \|\mathbf{OQ}\| = \frac{d}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{OQ} = \cos\theta\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0 $$

Hanging length: $\rho \equiv \|\mathbf{QP}\| = \ell - d/\cos\theta$. Define $w \equiv \rho\cos\theta = \ell\cos\theta - d \gt 0$.

Why the dashed angles are equal. The string element at $Q$ is massless, so the forces on it add to zero: $-T\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{OQ}$ (pull towards $O$), $+T\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{QP}$ (pull towards $P$) and the edge force $\mathbf{N}$. A smooth edge pushes only normally to itself, so the $\mathbf{j}_0$-component gives $-T\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{OQ}\cdot\mathbf{j}_0 + T\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{QP}\cdot\mathbf{j}_0 = 0$, that is $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{QP}\cdot\mathbf{j}_0 = \sin\theta$, the statement of the hint.

Part (b). The $\mathbf{j}_0$-component of $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{QP}$ is $\sin\theta$; the remainder has modulus $\sqrt{1 - \sin^2\theta} = \cos\theta$ and lies in the plane normal to the edge, where a downward line making the angle $\phi$ with the vertical plane $x_0 = d$ has direction $\sin\phi\,\mathbf{i}_0 - \cos\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0$. Hence

$$ \hat{\mathbf{u}} \equiv \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{QP} = \cos\theta\sin\phi\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0 - \cos\theta\cos\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0, \qquad \|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|^2 = \cos^2\theta\left(\sin^2\phi + \cos^2\phi\right) + \sin^2\theta = 1 $$

Moving basis $\mathcal{B}_1 = \{\hat{\mathbf{u}}, \mathbf{e}_\theta, \mathbf{e}_\phi\}$. Differentiating $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ with respect to $\theta$ and $\phi$ gives $\partial\hat{\mathbf{u}}/\partial\theta = \mathbf{e}_\theta$ and $\partial\hat{\mathbf{u}}/\partial\phi = \cos\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\phi$ with the unit vectors

$$ \mathbf{e}_\theta = -\sin\theta\sin\phi\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \cos\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0 + \sin\theta\cos\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0, \qquad \mathbf{e}_\phi = \cos\phi\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \sin\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0 $$

Change-of-basis matrix (columns are the components of $\hat{\mathbf{u}}, \mathbf{e}_\theta, \mathbf{e}_\phi$ in $\mathcal{B}_0$):

$$ [{}_0 R_1] = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta\sin\phi & -\sin\theta\sin\phi & \cos\phi \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ -\cos\theta\cos\phi & \sin\theta\cos\phi & \sin\phi \end{pmatrix} $$
  • Orthonormality ($R^TR = I$): $\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}} = 1$; $\mathbf{e}_\theta\cdot\mathbf{e}_\theta = \sin^2\theta\sin^2\phi + \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta\cos^2\phi = 1$; $\mathbf{e}_\phi\cdot\mathbf{e}_\phi = 1$; $\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_\theta = \sin\theta\cos\theta\left(1 - \sin^2\phi - \cos^2\phi\right) = 0$; $\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_\phi = \cos\theta\sin\phi\cos\phi - \cos\theta\cos\phi\sin\phi = 0$; $\mathbf{e}_\theta\cdot\mathbf{e}_\phi = -\sin\theta\sin\phi\cos\phi + \sin\theta\cos\phi\sin\phi = 0$.
  • Determinant: component by component, $\hat{\mathbf{u}}\times\mathbf{e}_\theta = \cos\phi\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \sin\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0 = \mathbf{e}_\phi$ (using $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$), so the triad is right-handed and $\det[{}_0R_1] = +1$.

Derivatives of the moving basis (from the partial derivatives, $\dot{\mathbf{e}}_i = \dot{\theta}\,\partial\mathbf{e}_i/\partial\theta + \dot{\phi}\,\partial\mathbf{e}_i/\partial\phi$):

$$ \dot{\hat{\mathbf{u}}} = \dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta + \dot{\phi}\cos\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\phi, \qquad \dot{\mathbf{e}}_\theta = -\dot{\theta}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} - \dot{\phi}\sin\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\phi, \qquad \dot{\mathbf{e}}_\phi = \dot{\phi}\left(-\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\theta\right) $$

For instance $\partial\mathbf{e}_\theta/\partial\theta = (-\cos\theta\sin\phi, -\sin\theta, \cos\theta\cos\phi) = -\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ and $\partial\mathbf{e}_\phi/\partial\phi = (-\sin\phi, 0, \cos\phi) = -\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\theta$. The matrix $[{}_0R_1]^T[{}_0\dot R_1]$ built from these relations is skew-symmetric, as required for a rotating orthonormal basis.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Mathematical Deduction

(a) Degrees of freedom

Two: $(\theta, \phi)$, as counted in Phase 1.

(b) Unit vector from $Q$ to $P$

Obtained in Phase 2: $\hat{\mathbf{u}} = \cos\theta\sin\phi\,\mathbf{i}_0 + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0 - \cos\theta\cos\phi\,\mathbf{k}_0$.

(c) Position, velocity and acceleration of $Q$ and $P$

Pedagogical Justification: $Q$ slides along a straight edge, so only the chain rule on $\tan\theta$ is needed. $P$ is $Q$ plus a vector of variable length along a variable direction, so the product rule is needed.

Point $Q$ (chain rule $\frac{d}{dt}\tan\theta = \dot{\theta}/\cos^2\theta$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\cos^{-2}\theta = 2\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}/\cos^3\theta$):

$$ \mathbf{r}_0^Q = d\,\mathbf{i}_0 + d\tan\theta\,\mathbf{j}_0, \qquad \mathbf{v}_0^Q = \frac{d\,\dot{\theta}}{\cos^2\theta}\,\mathbf{j}_0, \qquad \mathbf{a}_0^Q = \frac{d}{\cos^2\theta}\left(\ddot{\theta} + 2\tan\theta\,\dot{\theta}^2\right)\mathbf{j}_0 $$

Position of $P$:

$$ \mathbf{r}_0^P = \mathbf{r}_0^Q + \rho\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}, \qquad \rho = \ell - \frac{d}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \dot{\rho} = -\frac{d\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta}\,\dot{\theta} $$

Velocity of $P$ (product rule): $\mathbf{v}_0^P = \mathbf{v}_0^Q + \dot{\rho}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} + \rho\,\dot{\hat{\mathbf{u}}}$. Project on $\mathcal{B}_1$ with $\mathbf{j}_0\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}} = \sin\theta$, $\mathbf{j}_0\cdot\mathbf{e}_\theta = \cos\theta$, $\mathbf{j}_0\cdot\mathbf{e}_\phi = 0$:

  • $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$-component: $\dfrac{d\dot{\theta}\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta} + \dot{\rho} = \dfrac{d\dot{\theta}\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta} - \dfrac{d\dot{\theta}\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta} = 0$.
  • $\mathbf{e}_\theta$-component: $\dfrac{d\dot{\theta}\cos\theta}{\cos^2\theta} + \rho\dot{\theta} = \dot{\theta}\left(\dfrac{d}{\cos\theta} + \ell - \dfrac{d}{\cos\theta}\right) = \ell\dot{\theta}$.
  • $\mathbf{e}_\phi$-component: $0 + \rho\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi} = w\dot{\phi}$.
$$ \boxed{\mathbf{v}_0^P = \ell\dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta + w\dot{\phi}\,\mathbf{e}_\phi}, \qquad \|\mathbf{v}_0^P\|^2 = \ell^2\dot{\theta}^2 + w^2\dot{\phi}^2, \qquad w = \ell\cos\theta - d $$

Acceleration of $P$. Differentiate with the product rule, using $\dot{w} = -\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}$ and the basis derivatives of Phase 2:

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\left(\ell\dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta\right) = \ell\ddot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta + \ell\dot{\theta}\left(-\dot{\theta}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} - \dot{\phi}\sin\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\phi\right) $$
$$ \frac{d}{dt}\left(w\dot{\phi}\,\mathbf{e}_\phi\right) = \left(\dot{w}\dot{\phi} + w\ddot{\phi}\right)\mathbf{e}_\phi + w\dot{\phi}^2\left(-\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{e}_\theta\right) $$

Adding both and collecting terms (the $\mathbf{e}_\phi$ coefficient gathers $-\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi}$ from the first line and $\dot{w}\dot{\phi} = -\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi}$ from the second):

$$ \boxed{\mathbf{a}_0^P = \left(-\ell\dot{\theta}^2 - w\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2\right)\hat{\mathbf{u}} + \left(\ell\ddot{\theta} + w\sin\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2\right)\mathbf{e}_\theta + \left(w\ddot{\phi} - 2\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi}\right)\mathbf{e}_\phi} $$

Components in $\mathcal{B}_0$ follow by multiplying the column of $\mathcal{B}_1$-components by $[{}_0R_1]$.

(d) Forces on $P$

Pedagogical Justification: $P$ is a free particle in contact only with the string, so the forces are its weight and the string tension. The edge force acts on the (massless) string at $Q$, not on $P$.
  • Weight: $\mathbf{W} = -mg\,\mathbf{k}_0$. With $\mathbf{k}_0\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}} = -\cos\theta\cos\phi$, $\mathbf{k}_0\cdot\mathbf{e}_\theta = \sin\theta\cos\phi$, $\mathbf{k}_0\cdot\mathbf{e}_\phi = \sin\phi$: $\;\mathbf{W} = mg\cos\theta\cos\phi\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} - mg\sin\theta\cos\phi\,\mathbf{e}_\theta - mg\sin\phi\,\mathbf{e}_\phi$.
  • Tension: $\mathbf{T} = -T\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}$, directed towards $Q$, with $T \ge 0$ unknown.

(e) Equations of motion free of $T$

Pedagogical Justification: Newton’s second law $m\mathbf{a}_0^P = \mathbf{W} + \mathbf{T}$ has the three unknowns $\ddot{\theta}, \ddot{\phi}, T$. Projecting on $\mathcal{B}_1$ isolates $T$ in the $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ equation, because $\mathbf{T}$ has no component along $\mathbf{e}_\theta$ or $\mathbf{e}_\phi$.
$$ \hat{\mathbf{u}}: \; m\left(-\ell\dot{\theta}^2 - w\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2\right) = mg\cos\theta\cos\phi - T $$
$$ \mathbf{e}_\theta: \; m\left(\ell\ddot{\theta} + w\sin\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2\right) = -mg\sin\theta\cos\phi $$
$$ \mathbf{e}_\phi: \; m\left(w\ddot{\phi} - 2\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi}\right) = -mg\sin\phi $$

The last two contain only $\theta, \phi$ and their derivatives; they are the requested pair (with $w = \ell\cos\theta - d$):

$$ \boxed{\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{\sin\theta}{\ell}\left[\left(\ell\cos\theta - d\right)\dot{\phi}^2 + g\cos\phi\right]}, \qquad \boxed{\ddot{\phi} = \frac{2\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi} - g\sin\phi}{\ell\cos\theta - d}} $$

The $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ equation then gives the tension once the motion is known:

$$ T = m\left[g\cos\theta\cos\phi + \ell\dot{\theta}^2 + \left(\ell\cos\theta - d\right)\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2\right] $$

which is positive whenever $\cos\phi \ge 0$ and $w \gt 0$, consistent with a taut string.

Cross-check by Lagrange’s equations. With kinetic energy $\tfrac{1}{2}m(\ell^2\dot{\theta}^2 + w^2\dot{\phi}^2)$ and $V = mgz_P$, $z_P = \mathbf{r}_0^P\cdot\mathbf{k}_0 = -\rho\cos\theta\cos\phi = -w\cos\phi$, the Lagrangian is $L = \tfrac{1}{2}m(\ell^2\dot{\theta}^2 + w^2\dot{\phi}^2) + mgw\cos\phi$. With $\partial w/\partial\theta = -\ell\sin\theta$:

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\theta}} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta} = m\ell^2\ddot{\theta} + m\ell\sin\theta\left(w\dot{\phi}^2 + g\cos\phi\right) = 0 $$
$$ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi} = m\left(w^2\ddot{\phi} + 2w\dot{w}\dot{\phi}\right) + mgw\sin\phi = 0 $$

Dividing the first by $m\ell$ and the second by $mw$ reproduces the two boxed equations. A symbolic comparison at three random states confirmed that the Lagrange expressions equal the $\mathbf{e}_\theta$ and $\mathbf{e}_\phi$ Newton equations multiplied by $\ell$ and $w$ respectively (agreement to machine precision).

(f) Work of the tension

Pedagogical Justification: The power of the tension on $P$ is $\mathbf{T}\cdot\mathbf{v}_0^P$; it vanishes if $\mathbf{v}_0^P \perp \hat{\mathbf{u}}$.
$$ P_T = \mathbf{T}\cdot\mathbf{v}_0^P = -T\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\left(\ell\dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta + w\dot{\phi}\,\mathbf{e}_\phi\right) = 0 $$

because $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{e}_\theta$ and $\mathbf{e}_\phi$. Equivalently, the $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$-component of $\mathbf{v}_0^P$ in (c) cancelled: the string slides over the edge so that the hanging length grows at the rate at which $Q$ moves along $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$. No: the tension does no work on $P$ (official key, PDF page 76: $\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\mathbf{v}_0^P = 0$).

(g) Mechanical energy

General expression (kinetic energy from (c); gravitational potential energy with $Oz_0$ up and $V = 0$ on the plate plane):

$$ E = \tfrac{1}{2}m\|\mathbf{v}_0^P\|^2 + mgz_P = \tfrac{1}{2}m\left[\ell^2\dot{\theta}^2 + \left(\ell\cos\theta - d\right)^2\dot{\phi}^2\right] - mg\left(\ell\cos\theta - d\right)\cos\phi $$

Initial value. At $t = 0$: $\tfrac{1}{2}m\|\mathbf{v}_0^P\|^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}m\ell g$, $w(0) = \ell\cos(\pi/3) - d = \ell/2 - d$ and $\cos\phi(0) = 1/2$:

$$ E_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}m\ell g - mg\left(\frac{\ell}{2} - d\right)\frac{1}{2} = mg\left(\frac{\ell}{2} - \frac{\ell}{4} + \frac{d}{2}\right) = \frac{mg\,(\ell + 2d)}{4} $$

Conservation. By the work-energy theorem, $\frac{d}{dt}\tfrac{1}{2}m\|\mathbf{v}\|^2 = \mathbf{W}\cdot\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{T}\cdot\mathbf{v}$. The tension term is zero by (f), and $\mathbf{W}\cdot\mathbf{v} = -\frac{d}{dt}(mgz_P)$ because gravity is conservative. Hence $E$ is conserved. Direct check, differentiating $E$ and inserting the equations of (e):

$$ \frac{\dot{E}}{m} = \ell^2\dot{\theta}\ddot{\theta} + w^2\dot{\phi}\ddot{\phi} + w\dot{w}\dot{\phi}^2 - g\dot{w}\cos\phi + gw\sin\phi\,\dot{\phi} $$

with $\ell^2\dot{\theta}\ddot{\theta} = -\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\left(w\dot{\phi}^2 + g\cos\phi\right)$, $w^2\dot{\phi}\ddot{\phi} = w\dot{\phi}\left(2\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi} - g\sin\phi\right)$ and $\dot{w} = -\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}$. The terms in $\ell w\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\phi}^2$ add to $-1 + 2 - 1 = 0$, the terms in $g\ell\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}\cos\phi$ add to $-1 + 1 = 0$, and the terms in $gw\dot{\phi}\sin\phi$ add to $-1 + 1 = 0$, so $\dot{E} = 0$.

Angular momentum about $O$ is not conserved. The torque of the tension about $O$ has the $\mathbf{k}_0$-component $-T\,(\mathbf{r}_0^Q\times\hat{\mathbf{u}})\cdot\mathbf{k}_0 = -Td\sin\theta\,(1 - \sin\phi)$, which is non-zero at $t = 0$, and the weight has a non-zero horizontal torque. No component of $\mathbf{H}_O$ is a first integral in general, so the energy is the conserved quantity asked about.

Problem 42

(Advanced problem.) A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is attracted by the origin of coordinates $O$ of the inertial reference frame $S_0$ by a force with magnitude

$$ F = \frac{m\mu}{r^\alpha} $$

where $r$ is the distance between $O$ and $P$, and $\mu, \alpha$ are known positive constants.

Notation. The official text writes $F = m\alpha/r^\beta$ (strength $\alpha$, exponent $\beta$) and asks for $\beta = 1, 2, 3, 4$. Here the strength is called $\mu$ and the exponent $\alpha$, so the cases "$\alpha = 1, 2, 3, 4$" below are the official $\beta = 1, 2, 3, 4$, and $\mu/h^2$ is the official $\alpha/h^2$.
  • (a)Show that the angular momentum vector and the mechanical energy of $P$ are conserved quantities of motion, and that the motion of $P$ occurs in a plane.
  • (b)Using polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ in the plane of motion, find a second-order differential equation to solve for $u \equiv 1/r$ as a function of $\theta$ (i.e., $u = u(\theta)$).
  • (c)Find a first integral of this equation and discuss the limits of motion of $u$ as a function of $\mu/h^2$, $\alpha$, and the values of $u$ and $u' \equiv du/d\theta$ at $t = 0$, where $h$ is the magnitude of the angular momentum vector about the origin per unit mass. Phase 3

Step-by-Step Mathematical Deduction

Part (a): conservation laws and planar motion

Pedagogical Justification: The force line of action passes through the fixed point $O$, so its torque about $O$ vanishes, and the angular momentum theorem about a fixed point (Notes Eq. 4.53) gives a constant $\mathbf{H}_O$.
$$ \mathbf{M}_O = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F} = r\mathbf{e}_r\times\left(-\frac{m\mu}{r^\alpha}\mathbf{e}_r\right) = \mathbf{0} \implies \left.\frac{d\mathbf{H}_O}{dt}\right|_0 = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{H}_O = m\,\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{v} = \text{const} $$

Since $\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{H}_O \equiv 0$ and $\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{H}_O \equiv 0$, $P$ stays in the plane through $O$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{H}_O$.

Pedagogical Justification: The force is purely radial and depends only on $r$, so its elementary work $\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = F(r)\,dr$ is the exact differential of a function of $r$ (equivalently $\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$). The force is conservative, so the energy is conserved. The potential energy per unit mass follows from Barrow's rule with the reference at infinity (for $\alpha \gt 1$; for $\alpha \le 1$ the reference point is arbitrary):
$$ V_0(r) = -\int_\infty^r\left(-\frac{\mu}{s^\alpha}\right)ds = \mu\left[\frac{s^{1-\alpha}}{1 - \alpha}\right]_\infty^r = -\frac{\mu}{(\alpha - 1)\,r^{\alpha - 1}} \quad (\alpha \gt 1), \qquad V_0(r) = \mu\ln r \quad (\alpha = 1) $$
$$ \xi = \tfrac{1}{2}v^2 + V_0(r) = \text{const} $$

Part (b): Binet equation for a power law

Pedagogical Justification: Eliminate time with $h = r^2\dot{\theta} = \text{const}$, i.e. $\dot{\theta} = hu^2$. The chain rule for $r = u^{-1}$ gives $dr/d\theta = -u'/u^2$.
$$ \dot{r} = \frac{dr}{d\theta}\dot{\theta} = \left(-\frac{u'}{u^2}\right)\left(hu^2\right) = -hu', \qquad \ddot{r} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(-hu'\right) = -hu''\,\dot{\theta} = -h^2u^2u'' $$

The radial equation $\ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2 = -\mu/r^\alpha = -\mu u^\alpha$ with $r\dot{\theta}^2 = \frac{1}{u}(hu^2)^2 = h^2u^3$ becomes

$$ -h^2u^2u'' - h^2u^3 = -\mu u^\alpha \quad\overset{\div\,(-h^2u^2)}{\Longrightarrow}\quad \boxed{u'' + u = \frac{\mu}{h^2}\,u^{\alpha - 2}} $$

Part (c): first integral

Pedagogical Justification: Write the specific energy with $\dot{r} = -hu'$ and $r\dot{\theta} = hu$. Since $\xi$ is constant, this is a first integral; it can be checked by multiplying the Binet equation by $u'$ and integrating in $\theta$.
$$ \xi = \tfrac{1}{2}h^2\left[(u')^2 + u^2\right] + V_0\!\left(\frac{1}{u}\right) \implies \boxed{(u')^2 + W_{\text{eff}}(u) = E^*}, \quad E^* \equiv \frac{2\xi}{h^2}, \quad W_{\text{eff}}(u) = u^2 + \frac{2V_0(1/u)}{h^2} $$

Check: $\frac{d}{d\theta}\left[(u')^2 + W_{\text{eff}}\right] = 2u'u'' + W_{\text{eff}}'u' = 0$ gives $u'' = -\tfrac{1}{2}W_{\text{eff}}'$, which is the Binet equation. Motion exists only where $W_{\text{eff}}(u) \le E^*$; turning points ($u' = 0$) are the roots of $W_{\text{eff}}(u) = E^*$, and a minimum or a maximum of $W_{\text{eff}}$ is a circular orbit.

Case $\alpha = 2$: inverse square (Kepler)

  • Binet: $u'' + u = \mu/h^2$. Potential: $V_0 = -\mu u$, so $W_{\text{eff}} = u^2 - \dfrac{2\mu}{h^2}u = \left(u - \dfrac{\mu}{h^2}\right)^2 - \left(\dfrac{\mu}{h^2}\right)^2$, a parabola with minimum $-(\mu/h^2)^2$ at $u = \mu/h^2$.
  • (i) Escape ($u \to 0$): possible if $E^* \ge W_{\text{eff}}(0) = 0$, i.e. $\xi \ge 0$ (parabola or hyperbola).
  • (ii) Reaching $r = 0$: impossible for $h \ne 0$, because $W_{\text{eff}} \to +\infty$ as $u \to \infty$.
  • (iii) Oscillation: for $-(\mu/h^2)^2 \lt E^* \lt 0$, i.e. $-\mu^2/(2h^2) \lt \xi \lt 0$: $u$ oscillates between the two roots (periapsis and apoapsis of an ellipse); $E^* = -(\mu/h^2)^2$ is the circular orbit.

Case $\alpha = 1$: $F \propto 1/r$

  • Binet: $u'' + u = \dfrac{\mu}{h^2u}$. Potential $V_0(1/u) = -\mu\ln u$, so $W_{\text{eff}} = u^2 - \dfrac{2\mu}{h^2}\ln u$.
  • $W_{\text{eff}} \to +\infty$ both as $u \to 0$ (because $-\ln u \to +\infty$) and as $u \to \infty$. Its minimum is at $2u - \dfrac{2\mu}{h^2u} = 0$, i.e. $u_0 = \sqrt{\mu}/h$.
  • (i) Escape is impossible for any finite energy. (ii) Reaching $r = 0$ is impossible. (iii) Every orbit with $E^* \gt W_{\text{eff}}(u_0)$ oscillates between two finite turning points; $E^* = W_{\text{eff}}(u_0)$ is the circular orbit $r = h/\sqrt{\mu}$.

Case $\alpha = 3$: $F \propto 1/r^3$

  • $V_0 = -\dfrac{\mu}{2r^2} = -\dfrac{\mu}{2}u^2$. Binet: $u'' + \lambda u = 0$ with $\lambda \equiv 1 - \mu/h^2$. Then $W_{\text{eff}} = \lambda u^2$ and $(u')^2 + \lambda u^2 = E^*$.
  • $h^2 \gt \mu$ ($\lambda = \kappa^2 \gt 0$, $\kappa = \sqrt{1 - \mu/h^2}$): $u = A\cos(\kappa\theta + \delta)$ with $A = \sqrt{u_0^2 + (u_0'/\kappa)^2}$. The cosine vanishes at a finite angle, so $u \to 0$ in every case: (i) the particle always escapes; a particle moving inward ($u_0' \gt 0$) is first reflected at the periapsis $u = A$. (ii) never, since $u \le A$. (iii) no oscillation between two positive values.
  • $h^2 \lt \mu$ ($\lambda = -\kappa^2 \lt 0$, $\kappa = \sqrt{\mu/h^2 - 1}$): $u = C_1e^{\kappa\theta} + C_2e^{-\kappa\theta}$ with $C_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}(u_0 + u_0'/\kappa)$, $C_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(u_0 - u_0'/\kappa)$. If $C_1 \gt 0$ (i.e. $u_0' \gt -\kappa u_0$), $u \to \infty$: (ii) the particle reaches the origin. If $C_1 \lt 0$ ($u_0' \lt -\kappa u_0$), $u$ reaches $0$ at a finite angle: (i) escape. If $C_1 = 0$, $u = u_0e^{-\kappa\theta} \to 0$ only asymptotically (spiral $r = r_0e^{\kappa\theta}$), a limiting case of escape. (iii) no oscillation.
  • $h^2 = \mu$ ($\lambda = 0$): $u'' = 0 \Rightarrow u = u_0 + u_0'\theta$ (reciprocal spiral). $u_0' \gt 0$: reaches the origin; $u_0' \lt 0$: escapes at $\theta = u_0/|u_0'|$; $u_0' = 0$: circle $r = 1/u_0$.

Case $\alpha = 4$: $F \propto 1/r^4$

  • Binet: $u'' + u = \dfrac{\mu}{h^2}u^2$. $V_0 = -\dfrac{\mu}{3}u^3$, so $W_{\text{eff}} = u^2 - \dfrac{2\mu}{3h^2}u^3 = u^2\left(1 - \dfrac{2\mu}{3h^2}u\right)$.
  • $W_{\text{eff}}'(u) = 2u - \dfrac{2\mu}{h^2}u^2 = 2u\left(1 - \dfrac{\mu}{h^2}u\right)$: stationary points $u = 0$ (minimum, $W = 0$) and $u_b = h^2/\mu$ (maximum, barrier $W_{\max} = u_b^2 - \dfrac{2\mu}{3h^2}u_b^3 = \dfrac{h^4}{3\mu^2}$). For $u \gt u_b$, $W_{\text{eff}}$ decreases without bound and crosses zero at $u_0 = 3h^2/(2\mu) \gt u_b$.
  • There is no interior minimum, so the allowed set $\{W_{\text{eff}} \le E^*\}$ either contains $u = 0$ or extends to $u \to \infty$. Because $\dot{\theta} = hu^2 \gt 0$, $u'$ has the sign of $\dot{u}$.
  • $0 \le E^* \lt W_{\max}$: two roots $u_1 \in [0, u_b)$ and $u_2 \gt u_b$; allowed $u \le u_1$ or $u \ge u_2$. If $u(0) \le u_1$ the particle is reflected at $u_1$ and escapes; if $u(0) \ge u_2$ it is reflected at $u_2$ and falls to the origin.
  • $E^* \lt 0$: only $u \ge u_2 \gt u_0$ is allowed; the particle reaches $r = 0$. $E^* \gt W_{\max}$: no turning points; $u'(0) \lt 0$ (moving outward) escapes and $u'(0) \gt 0$ (moving inward) falls to the origin. $E^* = W_{\max}$: the unstable circular orbit $u = u_b$ and asymptotic approaches to it.
  • (i) escape: $0 \le E^* \lt W_{\max}$ with $u(0) \lt u_b$, or $E^* \gt W_{\max}$ with $u'(0) \lt 0$. (ii) reaching $r = 0$: $E^* \lt 0$, or $0 \le E^* \lt W_{\max}$ with $u(0) \gt u_b$, or $E^* \gt W_{\max}$ with $u'(0) \gt 0$. (iii) bounded oscillation between two values of $u$: does not occur; it would need an interior minimum of $W_{\text{eff}}$, and the only bounded motion is the unstable circle $u = u_b$.

Reference Frames, Vector Bases & Coordinate Geometry

  • Inertial frame: $S_0 = \{O; \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\}$, horizontal plane $z = 0$, $\mathbf{k}$ up.
  • Change of basis (polar basis of $P_1$). $\mathcal{B}_1 = \{\mathbf{e}_r, \mathbf{e}_\theta, \mathbf{k}\}$ is the inertial basis rotated by $\theta(t)$ about $\mathbf{k}$:
$$ [{}_0 R_1] = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det[{}_0R_1] = 1, \qquad [{}_0R_1]^T[{}_0R_1] = I $$

with $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{10} = \dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{k}$, $\dot{\mathbf{e}}_r = \dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta$ and $\dot{\mathbf{e}}_\theta = -\dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_r$ (Poisson).

  • Kinematics: $\mathbf{r}_1 = r\mathbf{e}_r$, $\mathbf{v}_1 = \dot{r}\mathbf{e}_r + r\dot{\theta}\mathbf{e}_\theta$; $\mathbf{r}_2 = (r - 2a)\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{v}_2 = \dot{r}\mathbf{k}$.
$$ T_{\text{kin}} = \tfrac{1}{2}m\left(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right) + \tfrac{1}{2}m\dot{r}^2 = m\dot{r}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}mr^2\dot{\theta}^2, \qquad V = mgz_2 = mg(r - 2a) $$
Phase 3

Step-by-Step Mathematical Deduction

(a) Circular motion, $r \equiv a$

Pedagogical Justification: On the plane the only horizontal force on $P_1$ is the tension $-T\mathbf{e}_r$, which is central. For $r = a$ the radial acceleration is purely centripetal; $P_2$ must stay at rest.
$$ P_1: \; m\left(\ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2\right) = -T \;\xrightarrow{\;r = a,\ \ddot{r} = 0\;}\; T = ma\dot{\theta}^2 = \frac{mv_0^2}{a}, \qquad P_2: \; m\ddot{z}_2 = T - mg = 0 \implies T = mg $$
$$ \frac{mv_0^2}{a} = mg \implies v_0 = \sqrt{ag} $$

(b) Tension in the circular case

$$ T = mg $$

(c) Quadratures for $v_0 = \sqrt{8ag/3}$

Pedagogical Justification: The tension on $P_1$ is central, so $h = r^2\dot{\theta}$ is constant; the plane and hole are smooth and the cord inextensible, so $E = T_{\text{kin}} + V$ is constant. The two first integrals leave one equation for $r(t)$.
$$ h = r^2\dot{\theta} = a v_0 = a\sqrt{\frac{8ag}{3}} \implies h^2 = \frac{8}{3}a^3g $$
$$ E = m\dot{r}^2 + \frac{mh^2}{2r^2} + mg(r - 2a), \qquad E(0) = \tfrac{1}{2}m\cdot\frac{8ag}{3} - mga = \frac{1}{3}mga $$

Equating, dividing by $m$ and isolating $\dot{r}^2$:

$$ \dot{r}^2 + \frac{4a^3g}{3r^2} + gr - 2ga = \frac{1}{3}ga \implies \dot{r}^2 = \frac{7}{3}ga - gr - \frac{4a^3g}{3r^2} = \frac{g}{3r^2}\left(7ar^2 - 3r^3 - 4a^3\right) $$

Separating variables and applying Barrow's rule from $r(0) = a$:

$$ \frac{dr}{dt} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{g}{3}}\,\frac{\sqrt{-3r^3 + 7ar^2 - 4a^3}}{r} \implies \int_a^r\frac{r'\,dr'}{\sqrt{-3r'^3 + 7ar'^2 - 4a^3}} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{g}{3}}\,t $$

(d) Oscillation between $r = a$ and $r = 2a$

Pedagogical Justification: Motion requires $\dot{r}^2 \ge 0$, i.e. $P(r) = -3r^3 + 7ar^2 - 4a^3 \ge 0$. With $x = r/a$ the polynomial is $a^3(-3x^3 + 7x^2 - 4)$, and the hint gives its roots.
$$ -3x^3 + 7x^2 - 4 = -(x - 1)(x - 2)(3x + 2) $$

Check of the roots: $x = 1$: $-3 + 7 - 4 = 0$; $x = 2$: $-24 + 28 - 4 = 0$; $x = -2/3$: $\frac{8}{9} + \frac{28}{9} - 4 = 0$. Since $r \gt 0$, $3x + 2 \gt 0$ and $P \ge 0$ requires $(x - 1)(x - 2) \le 0$:

$$ 1 \le x \le 2 \iff a \le r(t) \le 2a $$

The turning points ($\dot{r} = 0$) are $r_{\min} = a$ and $r_{\max} = 2a$, and the motion oscillates between them.

(e) Tension of the cord

Pedagogical Justification: Apply Newton's second law to $P_2$ (weight and tension) with $\ddot{z}_2 = \ddot{r}$, because $z_2 = r - 2a$.
$$ m\ddot{r} = T - mg \implies T = m\left(g + \ddot{r}\right) $$

Differentiate $\dot{r}^2 = \frac{7}{3}ga - gr - \frac{4a^3g}{3r^2}$ with respect to time (chain rule, $\frac{d}{dt}r^{-2} = -2r^{-3}\dot{r}$):

$$ 2\dot{r}\ddot{r} = \left(-g + \frac{8a^3g}{3r^3}\right)\dot{r} \implies \ddot{r} = -\frac{g}{2} + \frac{4a^3g}{3r^3} $$
$$ \boxed{T = mg\left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{4a^3}{3r^3}\right)} $$

$T \gt 0$ for all $r \in [a, 2a]$, so the cord stays taut.

(f) Rough plane

Pedagogical Justification: Coulomb friction acts on $P_1$ opposite to its velocity, with magnitude $\mu N_1$. The normal force is $N_1 = mg$, because the cord is horizontal on the plane and only the weight acts vertically.
$$ \mathbf{F}_f = -\mu mg\,\frac{\mathbf{v}_1}{\|\mathbf{v}_1\|} = -\mu mg\,\frac{\dot{r}\,\mathbf{e}_r + r\dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}} $$
$$ \mathbf{e}_r: \; m\left(\ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2\right) = -T - \mu mg\frac{\dot{r}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}} $$
$$ \mathbf{e}_\theta: \; m\left(r\ddot{\theta} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}\right) = -\mu mg\frac{r\dot{\theta}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}}, \qquad P_2: \; m\ddot{r} = T - mg $$

Adding the $\mathbf{e}_r$ equation and the $P_2$ equation eliminates $T$; multiplying the $\mathbf{e}_\theta$ equation by $r$ gives the evolution of $h$:

$$ 2\ddot{r} - r\dot{\theta}^2 = -g - \mu g\frac{\dot{r}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}}, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\left(r^2\dot{\theta}\right) = -\mu g\,\frac{r^2\dot{\theta}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2}} $$

Angular momentum and energy are no longer conserved (friction dissipates energy), so the problem cannot be reduced to the quadratures of (c).

Phase 4

Physical Interpretation & Dimensional Verification

  • Tension at the turning points: $T(a) = mg\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{4}{3}\right) = \tfrac{11}{6}mg$ and $T(2a) = mg\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{4}{24}\right) = \tfrac{2}{3}mg$, both positive.
  • Consistency with (a): for $v_0^2 = ag$, $h^2 = a^3g$ and $\ddot{r}(a) = -\tfrac{g}{2} + \tfrac{h^2}{2a^3} = 0$, so $r = a$ is an equilibrium of the radial equation, as expected for the circular case.
  • Radial period (consequence of (c) and (d)): with $x = r/a$ and $x = \tfrac{3}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2}\cos s$, $(x - 1)(2 - x) = \tfrac{1}{4}\sin^2 s$ and $dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin s\,ds$, so $\tau_r = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{3a}{g}}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\dfrac{x(s)\,ds}{\sqrt{3x(s) + 2}} \approx 6.342\sqrt{\dfrac{a}{g}}$.
  • Numerical check (Python, RK4 on $\ddot{r} = -g/2 + 4a^3g/(3r^3)$ with $a = g = 1$, $r(0) = 1$, $\dot{r}(0) = 0$): maximum radius $2.0000$, period $6.3425$ s (the quadrature gives $6.3424$), and the energy-integral residual stays below $4\times 10^{-14}$.
  • Dimensions: $[T] = [m][g] = \text{N}$ and $[v_0] = \sqrt{[a][g]} = \text{m/s}$.

Problem 44

The Little Prince Throwing Seeds

Angular Momentum + Vis-Viva

The little Prince is on his spherical asteroid, which has radius $R$ and mass $M$, and does not rotate. While he sits at the equator, he throws a baobab seed with an initial velocity of magnitude $v_0$ and angle $\beta = 60^\circ$ with respect to the local horizontal. Knowing that it reaches an apocenter of $r_a = 3R$ before falling back, determine $v_0$ as a function of the parameters of the problem and the universal gravitational constant $G$.

Worked Solution (4-Phase Methodology) [ Click to Expand ]
Phase 1

Physical Statement, Hypotheses & Parameters

  • System: a seed of mass $m \ll M$ launched from the surface ($r_0 = R$) with speed $v_0$ at $60^\circ$ above the local horizontal; it reaches the apocenter $r_a = 3R$.
  • Hypotheses: spherically symmetric, non-rotating asteroid, so the gravitational force $\mathbf{F} = -\dfrac{GMm}{r^2}\mathbf{e}_r$ is central and conservative with $\mu = GM$; vacuum (no drag); inertial frame $S_0$ centred on the asteroid.
  • Degrees of freedom: free particle, planar motion by central-force conservation of $\mathbf{H}_O$; coordinates $(r, \theta)$.
  • Initial state: $r = R$, radial component $v_{r0} = v_0\sin 60^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}v_0$ and transverse component $v_{\theta 0} = v_0\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac{1}{2}v_0$.

$M$, $R$mass and radius of the asteroidkg, m
$G$universal gravitational constant, $6.6743\times 10^{-11}$m$^3$/(kg s$^2$)
$\mu = GM$gravitational parameterm$^3$/s$^2$

Phase 2

Reference Frames, Vector Bases & Coordinate Geometry

  • Change of basis (polar basis in the orbital plane). $\mathcal{B}_1 = \{\mathbf{e}_r, \mathbf{e}_\theta, \mathbf{k}\}$ is the inertial basis rotated by the true anomaly $\theta$ about $\mathbf{k}$ (along $\mathbf{H}_O$):
$$ [{}_0 R_1] = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det[{}_0R_1] = 1, \qquad [{}_0R_1]^T[{}_0R_1] = I $$
  • Velocity in $\mathcal{B}_1$: $\mathbf{v} = \dot{r}\,\mathbf{e}_r + r\dot{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_\theta$; at launch $\dot{r} = v_0\sin 60^\circ$ and $r\dot{\theta} = v_0\cos 60^\circ$. At the apocenter $\dot{r} = 0$, so the velocity is purely transverse.
Phase 3

Step-by-Step Mathematical Deduction

1. Conservation of specific angular momentum

Pedagogical Justification: The gravitational force passes through $O$, so $\mathbf{M}_O = \mathbf{0}$ and $h = \|\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{v}\| = r^2\dot{\theta}$ is constant. Evaluate it at launch and at the apocenter, where $v_a = r_a\dot{\theta}_a$.
$$ h = R\left(v_0\cos 60^\circ\right) = \frac{1}{2}Rv_0, \qquad h = r_av_a = 3Rv_a \implies v_a = \frac{v_0}{6} $$

2. Conservation of specific energy

Pedagogical Justification: Gravity is conservative with $V_0 = -\mu/r$ (derived with Barrow's rule, reference at infinity), so $\xi = \tfrac{1}{2}v^2 - GM/r$ takes the same value at launch and at the apocenter.
$$ \frac{1}{2}v_0^2 - \frac{GM}{R} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{v_0}{6}\right)^2 - \frac{GM}{3R} = \frac{v_0^2}{72} - \frac{GM}{3R} $$

3. Solving for $v_0$

$$ \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{72}\right)v_0^2 = \left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right)\frac{GM}{R} \implies \frac{35}{72}v_0^2 = \frac{2}{3}\frac{GM}{R} \implies v_0^2 = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{72}{35}\frac{GM}{R} = \frac{48}{35}\frac{GM}{R} $$
$$ \boxed{v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{48}{35}\,\frac{GM}{R}}} $$
Phase 4

Physical Interpretation & Dimensional Verification

  • Bound orbit: the escape speed at the surface is $v_e = \sqrt{2GM/R}$, i.e. $v_e^2 = \tfrac{70}{35}GM/R$. Since $\tfrac{48}{35} \lt 2$, $v_0 \lt v_e$ and the orbit is an ellipse: the seed falls back.
  • Orbital parameters: $h^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}R^2v_0^2 = \tfrac{12}{35}GMR$, $p = h^2/\mu = \tfrac{12}{35}R$. From $r_a = p/(1 - e) = 3R$: $1 - e = \tfrac{4}{35}$ and $e = \tfrac{31}{35} \approx 0.8857$. The periapsis of the full conic is $r_p = p/(1 + e) = \tfrac{2}{11}R \lt R$, inside the asteroid; only the arc from the launch point to the apocenter is physical, and the seed lands before reaching the periapsis.
  • Independent check with the Binet solution. With $u = \tfrac{\mu}{h^2}(1 + e\cos\theta)$ and the launch values $u(\theta_0) = 1/R$, $u'(\theta_0) = -\dot{r}/h$ (here $\dot{r} = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}v_0$), the eccentricity formula of the theory page gives
$$ e = \frac{h^2}{\mu}\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{R} - \frac{\mu}{h^2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\dot{r}}{h}\right)^2} = \frac{12R}{35}\sqrt{\frac{529}{144R^2} + \frac{3}{R^2}} = \frac{12R}{35}\cdot\frac{31}{12R} = \frac{31}{35} $$

and the launch angle follows from $\tan\beta = \dot{r}/(r\dot{\theta}) = \dfrac{R}{p}\,e\sin\theta_0$ with $\cos\theta_0 = \dfrac{p/R - 1}{e} = -\dfrac{23}{31}$, $\sin\theta_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt{432}}{31}$:

$$ \tan\beta = \frac{35}{12}\cdot\frac{31}{35}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{432}}{31} = \frac{\sqrt{432}}{12} = \sqrt{3} \implies \beta = 60^\circ $$
  • Numerical check (Python, velocity-Verlet integration of $\ddot{\mathbf{r}} = -\mu\mathbf{r}/r^3$ with $\mu = R = 1$ and $v_0 = \sqrt{48/35}$): the maximum radius reached is $2.99999999981R$, in agreement with $r_a = 3R$.
  • Dimensions: $[v_0] = \sqrt{[G][M]/[R]} = \sqrt{\dfrac{\text{m}^3}{\text{kg}\,\text{s}^2}\cdot\dfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}}} = \text{m/s}$.
