Weight Fractions, Density and Modulus of a Carbon-Epoxy Composite

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf
  1. A composite material has an epoxy matrix reinforced with 68% in volume of unidirectional carbon fiber. The density of carbon fiber is 1.79 g/cm³ and of the epoxy resin is 1.20 g/cm³.

a) What's the percentage in weight of the carbon fibers and the epoxy resin? b) What's the average density of the composite? c) If the average density of the composite is 1.65 g/cm³, what's the percentage in volume of the carbon fibers inside the material? d) Which of the two materials has a higher Elastic modulus? Calculate its value.

Data: E_FC = 400 GPa and E_epoxy = 3 GPa. Assume isostrain conditions.

"The two materials" of part d) are the two composites of the statement: the one with 68 vol% fibres (parts a and b) and the one whose density is 1.65 g/cm³ (part c).

Official Solution: a) 76%; b) 1.6 g/cm³; c) 76%; d) 304.7 GPa
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Two phases, no porosity. The composite is fibre plus matrix only, so $V_f + V_m = 1$.
  2. Rule of mixtures. A property of the composite is the volume-weighted sum of the properties of its phases, $P_{CM} = \sum_i f_i p_i$ with $\sum_i f_i = 1$ [Session 22 Slide 27]; for the density, $\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + V_m\rho_m$.
  3. Mass of a phase. By the definition of density, the mass of each phase in a volume $V_c$ of composite is $m_i = \rho_i V_i = \rho_i f_i V_c$, so the weight fraction is $w_f = V_f\rho_f/\rho_c$.
  4. Isostrain. Load along the continuous, aligned fibres with a perfect bond: $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 22, Eq. 1].

Data

QuantityValue
$V_f$ (parts a, b)0.68
$\rho_f$ (carbon fibre)1.79 g/cm³
$\rho_m$ (epoxy)1.20 g/cm³
$\rho_c$ (part c)1.65 g/cm³
$E_f$, $E_m$400 GPa, 3 GPa

Unknowns: (a) $w_f$, $w_m$; (b) $\rho_c$; (c) $V_f$ when $\rho_c = 1.65$ g/cm³; (d) which composite is stiffer and its $E_{1C}$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 22, 27]:

$$\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + (1 - V_f)\rho_m \qquad w_f = \frac{V_f\rho_f}{\rho_c} \qquad E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$$

Strategy.

  1. Work with $1\ \text{cm}^3$ of composite: the mass of each phase is $\rho_i V_i$; their sum is $\rho_c$ (part b) and their ratio gives the weight fractions (part a).
  2. Invert the density rule of mixtures for $V_f$ (part c).
  3. Apply the isostrain rule to both composites and compare (part d).
Step-by-Step Calculation

1. a) and b) Masses in 1 cm³ of composite [Session 22 Slide 27]

In $1\ \text{cm}^3$ there are $0.68\ \text{cm}^3$ of fibre and $0.32\ \text{cm}^3$ of epoxy:

$$m_f = 0.68\ \text{cm}^3 \times 1.79\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 1.2172\ \text{g} \qquad m_m = 0.32\ \text{cm}^3 \times 1.20\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 0.3840\ \text{g}$$

Their sum is the mass of $1\ \text{cm}^3$ of composite, which is the density of part b):

$$\rho_c = 1.2172\ \text{g} + 0.3840\ \text{g} = 1.6012\ \text{g in } 1\ \text{cm}^3 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{\rho_c = 1.60\ g/cm^3}$$

Weight fractions:

$$w_f = \frac{1.2172\ \text{g}}{1.6012\ \text{g}} = 0.760 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{76.0\ \%\ \text{carbon fibre}} \qquad w_m = \frac{0.3840\ \text{g}}{1.6012\ \text{g}} = 0.240 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{24.0\ \%\ \text{epoxy}}$$

2. c) Volume fraction from the density [Session 22 Slide 27]

Writing $V_m = 1 - V_f$ in the density rule and solving for $V_f$:

$$\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + (1 - V_f)\rho_m = \rho_m + V_f(\rho_f - \rho_m) \ \Rightarrow\ V_f = \frac{\rho_c - \rho_m}{\rho_f - \rho_m}$$
$$V_f = \frac{1.65 - 1.20}{1.79 - 1.20} = \frac{0.45\ \text{g/cm}^3}{0.59\ \text{g/cm}^3} = 0.763 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{76.3\ vol\%\ \text{carbon fibre}}$$

3. d) Isostrain modulus of the two composites [Session 22 Slide 22]

Composite of parts a) and b), $V_f = 0.68$:

$$E_{1C} = 0.68 \times 400\ \text{GPa} + 0.32 \times 3\ \text{GPa} = 272.0 + 0.96 = 272.96\ \text{GPa}$$

Composite of part c), $V_f = 0.7627$:

$$E_{1C} = 0.7627 \times 400\ \text{GPa} + 0.2373 \times 3\ \text{GPa} = 305.08 + 0.71 = 305.8\ \text{GPa}$$

Answer: the composite of part c), with more fibre, is the stiffer one: $\mathbf{E_{1C} \approx 306\ GPa}$ (against 273 GPa for the 68 vol% composite).

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. $V_f$ and $w_f$ are dimensionless; $\rho_c$ is a volume-weighted sum of densities, in g/cm³; $E_{1C}$ is a sum of moduli, in GPa.
  • Bounds. $1.20 < 1.60 < 1.79$ g/cm³ and $3 < 273 < 400$ GPa: each mixture value lies between those of its phases.
  • Weight against volume. The fibre is the denser phase, so its weight fraction (76.0 %) is larger than its volume fraction (68 %).
  • Back-substitution. $0.7627 \times 1.79 + 0.2373 \times 1.20 = 1.650$ g/cm³, the density given in c).
  • Limit. For $V_f \to 1$ the modulus tends to $E_f = 400$ GPa; with $E_f \gg E_m$ the matrix adds less than 1 GPa, which is why $E_{1C} \approx V_f E_f$ [Session 22 Slide 22].

Interpretation

Because the modulus along the fibres is almost proportional to $V_f$, the composite of part c), with 8 more volume percent of fibre, is 12 % stiffer and only 3 % denser. This is the reason aerospace laminates are made with as high a fibre fraction as processing allows.

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf

The composite must contain a maximum fiber volume fraction of 40%,, and the minimum values for the elastic modulus and longitudinal tensile strength are set at 5.5·10⁴ and 1.2·10³ MPa respectively. he available candidate reinforcement materials include glass fibers, carbon fibers, and aramid fibers. Determine which reinforcement material is best suited to meet the specified requirements.

Official Solution: carbon fiber reinforced composite
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Isostrain. Continuous, aligned fibres loaded along their length with a perfect bond: $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 22, Eq. 1].
  2. Longitudinal strength. The strength follows the same rule of mixtures, $\sigma_C = \sigma_f V_f + \sigma_{y,m}(1 - V_f)$ [Session 22 Slide 26]. The table gives only one matrix stress (69 MPa), so it is used as the matrix term, as the key of the sheet does; the term is small next to the fibre term.
  3. Worst case. Both properties grow with $V_f$, so if a fibre fails at the largest allowed fraction, $V_f = 0.40$, it fails at any lower fraction.

  1. Evaluate both properties for each fibre at $V_f = 0.40$ and compare them with the limits.
  2. As a second view, solve each rule for the smallest $V_f$ that reaches the limit and compare it with 0.40.

$$E_{1C} = 0.40 \times 72 + 1.86 = 30.66\ \text{GPa} \qquad \sigma_{1C} = 0.40 \times 2000 + 41.4 = 841.4\ \text{MPa}$$

Carbon:

$$E_{1C} = 0.40 \times 350 + 1.86 = 141.86\ \text{GPa} \qquad \sigma_{1C} = 0.40 \times 3500 + 41.4 = 1441.4\ \text{MPa}$$

$$E_{1C} = 0.40 \times 124 + 1.86 = 51.46\ \text{GPa} \qquad \sigma_{1C} = 0.40 \times 3500 + 41.4 = 1441.4\ \text{MPa}$$

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. Each term of $E_{1C}$ is a fraction times a modulus (GPa) and each term of $\sigma_{1C}$ a fraction times a stress (MPa); the limits were converted to the same units (55 GPa, 1200 MPa).
  • Consistency. The two methods agree: a fibre passes at $V_f = 0.40$ exactly when its needed $V_f$ is below 0.40.
  • Limit. For $V_f \to 0$ both rules return the epoxy (3.1 GPa, 69 MPa), far below the limits: the matrix alone cannot do the job.

Interpretation

Strength and stiffness select differently: aramid and carbon have the same tensile strength, but aramid is about three times less stiff, so the modulus limit is the one that rules it out. This matches the slides: glass fibres have a low modulus and aramid a lower modulus than carbon [Session 21 Slides 17, 30], and carbon fibres give the best combination of strength, stiffness and low weight [Session 21 Slide 21].

Graphite-Epoxy and Graphite-Kevlar Hybrid Composite

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf
  1. A composite material has a 69.5% in weight of unidirectional graphite-HM fibers in an epoxy matrix.

a) Estimate the density and elastic modulus of the composite. b) If a 50% of the graphite fibers are replaced with Kevlar fibers, estimate the specific elastic modulus and the specific tensile strength in the longitudinal direction in this new hybrid composite. c) Calculate the minimum volumetric fraction of the graphite-HM fibers so that the initial composite has more strength than the hybrid composite of section b). d) Do the same calculations as in (c) considering Kevlar fibers.

Data:

MaterialDensity g/cm³Elastic modulus GPaTensile strength MPa
Epoxy resin1.233.570
Graphite-HM fiber1.863902400
Kevlar-49 fiber1.441313700
Official Solution: a) 1.61 g/cm³, 235.4 GPa; b) 106.5×10⁶ m²/s², 1.24×10⁶ m²/s²; c) 0.77; d) 0.49
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Weight to volume. The composition is given by weight. In 1 g of composite each phase occupies $w_i/\rho_i$; the sum of these volumes is $1/\rho_c$, and $V_i = (w_i/\rho_i)/\sum_j (w_j/\rho_j)$ (definition of density).
  2. Rule of mixtures along the fibres, for the modulus, the strength and the density, extended term by term to three phases: $P_{CM} = \sum_i f_i p_i$ [Session 22 Slide 27]; isostrain modulus [Session 22 Slide 22]; strength $\sigma_C = \sigma_f V_f + \sigma_m(1 - V_f)$ [Session 22 Slide 26], with the tabulated matrix strength as the matrix term.
  3. Hybrid of part b). Half of the graphite volume is replaced by the same volume of Kevlar, so $V_{gr} = V_K = V_f/2$ and $V_m$ is unchanged.
  4. Specific property = property / density [Session 21 Slide 43]; in SI, Pa/(kg/m³) = m²/s².
  5. Parts c) and d). "Initial composite" means the two-phase composite fibre + epoxy with the fibre of each part; its strength must exceed that of the hybrid.

Data

Constituent$\rho$ (g/cm³)$E$ (GPa)$\sigma$ (MPa)
Epoxy (m)1.233.570
Graphite-HM (gr)1.863902400
Kevlar 49 (K)1.441313700

$w_{gr} = 0.695$, $w_m = 0.305$.

Unknowns: (a) $\rho_c$, $E_{1C}$; (b) $E/\rho$ and $\sigma/\rho$ of the hybrid; (c) minimum $V_{gr}$; (d) minimum $V_K$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 22, 26, 27]:

$$V_i = \frac{w_i/\rho_i}{\sum_j w_j/\rho_j} \qquad \rho_c = \sum_i V_i\rho_i \qquad E_{1C} = \sum_i V_i E_i \qquad \sigma_{1C} = \sum_i V_i\sigma_i$$

Strategy.

  1. Convert the weight fractions to volume fractions; then density and modulus (a).
  2. Split the fibre volume in two halves; density, modulus and strength of the hybrid; divide by the density (b).
  3. Set the strength of a two-phase composite equal to the hybrid strength and solve for $V_f$ (c, d).
Step-by-Step Calculation

1. a) Volume fractions, density and modulus [Session 22 Slides 22, 27]

Volumes of the phases in 1 g of composite:

$$\frac{w_{gr}}{\rho_{gr}} = \frac{0.695\ \text{g}}{1.86\ \text{g/cm}^3} = 0.37366\ \text{cm}^3 \qquad \frac{w_m}{\rho_m} = \frac{0.305\ \text{g}}{1.23\ \text{g/cm}^3} = 0.24797\ \text{cm}^3$$

Total volume $0.62163\ \text{cm}^3$, so

$$V_{gr} = \frac{0.37366}{0.62163} = 0.6011 \qquad V_m = 0.3989 \qquad \rho_c = \frac{1\ \text{g}}{0.62163\ \text{cm}^3} = \mathbf{1.609\ g/cm^3}$$

Isostrain modulus:

$$E_{1C} = 0.6011 \times 390\ \text{GPa} + 0.3989 \times 3.5\ \text{GPa} = 234.43 + 1.40 = \mathbf{235.8\ GPa}$$

2. b) The hybrid composite [Session 22 Slides 22, 26, 27]

$V_{gr} = V_K = 0.6011/2 = 0.30055$, $V_m = 0.3989$.

$$\rho_h = 0.30055 \times 1.86 + 0.30055 \times 1.44 + 0.3989 \times 1.23 = 0.5590 + 0.4328 + 0.4906 = 1.4825\ \text{g/cm}^3$$
$$E_h = 0.30055 \times 390 + 0.30055 \times 131 + 0.3989 \times 3.5 = 117.21 + 39.37 + 1.40 = 157.98\ \text{GPa}$$
$$\sigma_h = 0.30055 \times 2400 + 0.30055 \times 3700 + 0.3989 \times 70 = 721.3 + 1112.0 + 27.9 = 1861.3\ \text{MPa}$$

Specific values, with $\rho_h = 1482.5\ \text{kg/m}^3$:

$$\frac{E_h}{\rho_h} = \frac{157.98\times10^9\ \text{Pa}}{1482.5\ \text{kg/m}^3} = \mathbf{106.6\times10^6\ m^2/s^2} \qquad \frac{\sigma_h}{\rho_h} = \frac{1861.3\times10^6\ \text{Pa}}{1482.5\ \text{kg/m}^3} = \mathbf{1.26\times10^6\ m^2/s^2}$$

3. c) Minimum graphite fraction [Session 22 Slide 26]

Strength of a graphite-epoxy composite with fraction $V$, set greater than the hybrid strength:

$$2400\,V + 70\,(1 - V) > 1861.3 \ \Rightarrow\ 2330\,V > 1791.3 \ \Rightarrow\ V > \frac{1791.3}{2330} = 0.769$$

$V_{gr,min} = 0.77$. The initial composite of part a), with $V_{gr} = 0.60$, has $2400 \times 0.6011 + 70 \times 0.3989 = 1470.6$ MPa, weaker than the hybrid.

4. d) Minimum Kevlar fraction [Session 22 Slide 26]

$$3700\,V + 70\,(1 - V) > 1861.3 \ \Rightarrow\ 3630\,V > 1791.3 \ \Rightarrow\ V > \frac{1791.3}{3630} = 0.493$$

$V_{K,min} = 0.49$.

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. $\text{Pa}/(\text{kg/m}^3) = (\text{kg}\,\text{m}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2})/(\text{kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}) = \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$, the unit of the key.
  • Fractions. $0.30055 + 0.30055 + 0.3989 = 1.0000$.
  • Trends. Kevlar is lighter, stronger and less stiff than graphite, so the hybrid is lighter (1.48 against 1.61 g/cm³), less stiff (158 against 236 GPa) and stronger (1861 against 1471 MPa) than the initial composite.
  • c) against d). Kevlar is the stronger fibre, so it needs a smaller fraction (0.49) than graphite (0.77) to beat the hybrid; both are below 1, so both are possible in principle.

Interpretation

A hybrid trades stiffness for strength and lower density: replacing half the graphite by Kevlar lowers the specific modulus from $235.8\times10^9/1609 = 146.6\times10^6$ to $106.6\times10^6$ m²/s² but raises the strength by 27 %. To match the hybrid strength with graphite alone, more than 77 vol% of fibre would be needed, above the 50 to 60 % of typical fibre composites [Session 22 Slide 22].

Discrepancy with the official solution

The key gives $1.24\times10^6$ m²/s² for the specific strength of part b); the rule of mixtures gives $1.861\times10^9/1482.5 = 1.26\times10^6$ m²/s² (with $V_f$ rounded to 0.60: $1858/1.482 = 1.25\times10^6$). The key value is reproduced only if the matrix term is dropped, $(0.30055 \times 6100)/1482.5 = 1.24\times10^6$ m²/s², but parts c) and d) of the same key (0.77, 0.49) need the full hybrid strength of about 1860 MPa, so the computed $1.26\times10^6$ m²/s² is kept. The other key values agree after rounding ($E_{1C} = 235.4$ GPa is the result for $V_f = 0.60$).

Modulus, Strength and Load Fraction of a Kevlar 49-Epoxy Composite

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 2
  1. Calculate the elastic modulus of a unidirectional composite material with epoxy matrix and Kevlar 49 fibers, where 63% of its volume is fibers and is under isostrain conditions. If the tensile strength of Kevlar 49 fibers is 3700 MPa and the tensile strength of the epoxy resin is 70 MPa, calculate the tensile strength of the composite material. What fraction of load is carried by the Kevlar fibers?

Data: E_kevlar = 131 GPa; E_epoxy = 3.5 GPa.

Official Solution: a) 83.82 GPa; b) 2.36 GPa; c) 0.984
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Isostrain. Load along the aligned fibres, perfect bond: $\varepsilon_C = \varepsilon_f = \varepsilon_m$ and $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slides 21-22].
  2. Strength. $\sigma_C = \sigma_f V_f + \sigma_{y,m}(1 - V_f)$ [Session 22 Slide 26], with the given resin strength as the matrix term.
  3. Load sharing. The total force is the sum of the forces in the phases, $F_C = F_f + F_m$, and $F_i = \sigma_i A_i$ [Session 22 Slide 21]. With a common strain, $\sigma_f = E_f\varepsilon$ and $\sigma_C = E_{1C}\varepsilon$, and $A_f/A_C = V_f$ for continuous fibres, so
$$\frac{F_f}{F_C} = \frac{\sigma_f A_f}{\sigma_C A_C} = \frac{E_f\varepsilon\,V_f}{E_{1C}\varepsilon} = \frac{V_f E_f}{E_{1C}}$$

Data

QuantityValue
$V_f$0.63
$E_f$, $E_m$131 GPa, 3.5 GPa
$\sigma_f$, $\sigma_m$3700 MPa, 70 MPa

Unknowns: $E_{1C}$, $\sigma_C$, $F_f/F_C$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 21, 22, 26]:

$$E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m \qquad \sigma_C = V_f\sigma_f + (1 - V_f)\sigma_m \qquad \frac{F_f}{F_C} = \frac{V_f E_f}{E_{1C}}$$

Strategy. Apply the three relations in order; the load fraction needs the modulus of the first step.

Step-by-Step Calculation

1. Elastic modulus [Session 22 Slide 22]

$$E_{1C} = 0.63 \times 131\ \text{GPa} + 0.37 \times 3.5\ \text{GPa} = 82.53 + 1.295 = \mathbf{83.83\ GPa}$$

2. Tensile strength [Session 22 Slide 26]

$$\sigma_C = 0.63 \times 3700\ \text{MPa} + 0.37 \times 70\ \text{MPa} = 2331 + 25.9 = 2356.9\ \text{MPa} = \mathbf{2.36\ GPa}$$

3. Fraction of the load carried by the fibres [Session 22 Slide 21]

$$\frac{F_f}{F_C} = \frac{0.63 \times 131\ \text{GPa}}{83.825\ \text{GPa}} = \frac{82.53}{83.825} = \mathbf{0.985}$$

The fibres carry 98.5 % of the load and the epoxy the remaining 1.5 %.

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. GPa in the modulus, MPa in the strength; the load fraction is GPa/GPa, dimensionless.
  • Bounds. $3.5 < 83.8 < 131$ GPa and $70 < 2357 < 3700$ MPa.
  • Approximation of the slides. With $E_f \gg E_m$, $E_{1C} \approx V_f E_f = 82.5$ GPa, within 1.5 % of the full result [Session 22 Slide 22].
  • Limits. $F_f/F_C \to 1$ when $E_m \to 0$, and $F_f/F_C = V_f$ when $E_f = E_m$ (a homogeneous material shares the load by area).

Interpretation

Under isostrain the stress in each phase is proportional to its modulus, so the fibre, 37 times stiffer than the epoxy, works at 37 times the stress. That is the "redistribution of the load" of Slide 22: the matrix holds the fibres in place and transfers the load, but almost all of it is carried by the fibres [Session 21 Slide 16].

E-Glass Polyester Rod: Modulus, Fibre and Matrix Forces and Extension

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 2
  1. For an E-glass fiber reinforced polyester composite with continuous aligned fibers, with 40% vol fraction fibers:

a) Determine the Modulus of elasticity in the longitudinal direction b) If a cylindrical rod 19 mm in diameter of this composite is stressed in the longitudinal direction to 50 MPa, determine the forces in the fiber and matrix phases, respectively. c) Estimate the specific tensile strength of the composite. d) If the rod was originally 1.5 m long, estimate its extension under the applied stress.

Data: E_m = 3400 MPa; σ_m = 69 MPa; ρ_m = 1.3 g/cm³; E_f = 72000 MPa, σ_f = 3500 MPa; ρ_f = 2.5 g/cm³.

Official Solution: a) 30.8 GPa; b) 13212N; 964N; c ) 809.8 kN m/kg; d) 2.44 mm
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Isostrain along the fibres with a perfect bond, $\varepsilon_C = \varepsilon_f = \varepsilon_m$ [Session 22 Slide 21]; linear elastic phases, so $\sigma_i = E_i\varepsilon$ [Session 22 Slide 20].
  2. Forces. $F_C = F_f + F_m$ with $F_i = \sigma_i A_i$ and $A_f = V_f A_C$, $A_m = V_m A_C$ [Session 22 Slide 21].
  3. Strength and density by the rule of mixtures [Session 22 Slides 26-27]; specific strength = strength/density [Session 21 Slide 43].
  4. Extension. Uniform strain over the length, $\Delta L = \varepsilon L_0$.

Data

QuantityFibre (E-glass)Matrix (polyester)
$V$0.400.60
$E$ (MPa)72 0003 400
$\sigma$ (MPa)3 50069
$\rho$ (g/cm³)2.51.3

Rod: $d = 19$ mm, $\sigma_C = 50$ MPa, $L_0 = 1.5$ m.

Unknowns: (a) $E_{1C}$; (b) $F_f$, $F_m$; (c) $\sigma_C/\rho_c$ at failure; (d) $\Delta L$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 21-22, 26-27]:

$$E_{1C} = V_f E_f + V_m E_m \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\sigma_C}{E_{1C}} \qquad F_i = E_i\,\varepsilon\,V_i A_C \qquad \Delta L = \varepsilon L_0$$

Strategy.

  1. Modulus by the isostrain rule (a).
  2. Common strain from the applied stress; stress in each phase from Hooke's law; force = stress × phase area (b).
  3. Strength and density by the rule of mixtures, then their ratio (c).
  4. Extension from the same strain (d).
Step-by-Step Calculation

1. a) Longitudinal modulus [Session 22 Slide 22]

$$E_{1C} = 0.40 \times 72\,000\ \text{MPa} + 0.60 \times 3\,400\ \text{MPa} = 28\,800 + 2\,040 = 30\,840\ \text{MPa} = \mathbf{30.8\ GPa}$$

2. b) Forces in the fibre and matrix [Session 22 Slide 21]

Cross-section and total force:

$$A_C = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} = \frac{\pi\,(19\ \text{mm})^2}{4} = 283.53\ \text{mm}^2 \qquad F_C = \sigma_C A_C = 50\ \text{N/mm}^2 \times 283.53\ \text{mm}^2 = 14\,176\ \text{N}$$

Common strain and phase stresses:

$$\varepsilon = \frac{50\ \text{MPa}}{30\,840\ \text{MPa}} = 1.6213\times10^{-3} \qquad \sigma_f = 72\,000 \times 1.6213\times10^{-3} = 116.73\ \text{MPa} \qquad \sigma_m = 3\,400 \times 1.6213\times10^{-3} = 5.512\ \text{MPa}$$

Forces:

$$F_f = \sigma_f V_f A_C = 116.73\ \text{N/mm}^2 \times 0.40 \times 283.53\ \text{mm}^2 = \mathbf{13\,239\ N}$$
$$F_m = \sigma_m V_m A_C = 5.512\ \text{N/mm}^2 \times 0.60 \times 283.53\ \text{mm}^2 = \mathbf{938\ N}$$

3. c) Specific tensile strength [Session 22 Slides 26-27]

$$\sigma_C = 0.40 \times 3\,500 + 0.60 \times 69 = 1\,400 + 41.4 = 1\,441.4\ \text{MPa} \qquad \rho_c = 0.40 \times 2.5 + 0.60 \times 1.3 = 1.78\ \text{g/cm}^3$$
$$\frac{\sigma_C}{\rho_c} = \frac{1\,441.4\times10^6\ \text{N/m}^2}{1\,780\ \text{kg/m}^3} = 809.8\times10^3\ \text{N m/kg} = \mathbf{809.8\ kN\,m/kg}$$

4. d) Extension of the rod

$$\Delta L = \varepsilon L_0 = 1.6213\times10^{-3} \times 1\,500\ \text{mm} = \mathbf{2.43\ mm}$$
Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. MPa = N/mm², so N/mm² × mm² = N. $(\text{N/m}^2)/(\text{kg/m}^3) = \text{N m/kg}$ (J/kg, the same as m²/s²).
  • Force balance. $13\,239 + 938 = 14\,177$ N $= F_C$ (rounding).
  • Ratio of forces. $F_f/F_m = V_f E_f/(V_m E_m) = 28\,800/2\,040 = 14.12$; also $13\,239/938 = 14.1$.
  • Stress balance. $0.40 \times 116.73 + 0.60 \times 5.512 = 46.69 + 3.31 = 50.0$ MPa, the applied stress [Session 22 Slide 21].
  • Strain. $1.6\times10^{-3}$ is far below the failure strain of the glass fibre, about $3500/72\,000 = 4.9\times10^{-3}$, so the rod is elastic.

Interpretation

At 40 vol% the glass carries 93.4 % of the load. The specific strength, about 810 kN m/kg, is the property where glass-fibre composites stand well above steel and aluminium in the comparisons of the slides [Session 21 Slide 43; Session 22 Slide 29], while their modulus (30.8 GPa) stays below that of aluminium: glass is chosen for strength and cost, not stiffness [Session 21 Slide 17].

Discrepancy with the official solution

The key splits the 14 176 N as 13 212 N (fibre) and 964 N (matrix). Under isostrain the ratio of the two forces is fixed by the data: $F_f/F_m = V_f E_f/(V_m E_m) = (0.40 \times 72\,000)/(0.60 \times 3\,400) = 14.12$, while the key gives $13\,212/964 = 13.71$, and its fibre share $13\,212/14\,176 = 0.932$ differs from $V_f E_f/E_{1C} = 28\,800/30\,840 = 0.934$. The computed forces, 13 239 N and 938 N, satisfy both the force balance and the isostrain ratio, so they are kept. Part d): $\Delta L = 2.43$ mm; the key's 2.44 mm follows from rounding $E_{1C}$ to 30.8 GPa ($50/30\,800 \times 1500 = 2.435$ mm).

Carbon-Epoxy Composite in psi: Modulus, Strength and Load Fraction

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 2
  1. Calculate the tensile modulus of elasticity of a unidirectional carbon-fiber-reinforced plastic composite material that contains 64 percent by volume of carbon fibers and is stressed under isostrain conditions. If the tensile strength of the carbon fibers of the 64 percent carbon-fiber-epoxy composite material is 0.31×10⁶ psi and that of the epoxy resin 9.20×10³ psi, calculate the strength of the composite material in psi. What fraction of the load is carried by the carbon fibers? The carbon fibers have a tensile modulus of elasticity of 54.0×10⁶ psi and the epoxy matrix a tensile modulus of elasticity of 0.530×10⁶ psi.
Official Solution: a) 1.39 GPa; b) 0.995
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Isostrain, perfect bond: $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 22, Eq. 1].
  2. Strength by the rule of mixtures, $\sigma_C = \sigma_f V_f + \sigma_{y,m}(1 - V_f)$ [Session 22 Slide 26], with the resin strength as the matrix term.
  3. Load fraction $F_f/F_C = V_f E_f/E_{1C}$, from $F_C = F_f + F_m$ under a common strain [Session 22 Slide 21].
  4. Units. The rules are linear, so they are applied in psi and converted at the end with 1 psi = 6894.757 Pa.

Data

QuantityFibre (carbon)Matrix (epoxy)
$V$0.640.36
$E$ (psi)$54.0\times10^6$$0.530\times10^6$
$\sigma$ (psi)$0.31\times10^6$$9.20\times10^3$

Unknowns: $E_{1C}$, $\sigma_C$ (psi), $F_f/F_C$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 21, 22, 26]:

$$E_{1C} = V_f E_f + V_m E_m \qquad \sigma_C = V_f\sigma_f + V_m\sigma_m \qquad \frac{F_f}{F_C} = \frac{V_f E_f}{E_{1C}}$$

Strategy. Apply the three relations in psi; convert the modulus and the strength to SI.

Step-by-Step Calculation

1. Tensile modulus [Session 22 Slide 22]

$$E_{1C} = 0.64 \times 54.0\times10^6 + 0.36 \times 0.530\times10^6 = 34.56\times10^6 + 0.191\times10^6 = \mathbf{34.75\times10^6\ psi}$$

In SI:

$$E_{1C} = 34.75\times10^6\ \text{psi} \times 6894.757\ \text{Pa/psi} = 239.6\times10^9\ \text{Pa} = \mathbf{239.6\ GPa}$$

2. Tensile strength [Session 22 Slide 26]

$$\sigma_C = 0.64 \times 0.31\times10^6 + 0.36 \times 9.20\times10^3 = 198\,400 + 3\,312 = \mathbf{201\,712\ psi}$$
$$\sigma_C = 201\,712\ \text{psi} \times 6894.757\ \text{Pa/psi} = 1.391\times10^9\ \text{Pa} = 1.39\ \text{GPa}$$

3. Fraction of the load carried by the fibres [Session 22 Slide 21]

$$\frac{F_f}{F_C} = \frac{0.64 \times 54.0\times10^6}{34.75\times10^6} = \frac{34.56}{34.75} = \mathbf{0.995}$$
Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. All moduli and strengths in psi, so the sums are in psi and the load fraction is dimensionless; the conversion factor has units Pa/psi.
  • Bounds. $0.53\times10^6 < 34.75\times10^6 < 54.0\times10^6$ psi.
  • Approximation of the slides. $E_{1C} \approx V_f E_f = 34.56\times10^6$ psi, 0.5 % below the full result, because $E_f/E_m \approx 100$ [Session 22 Slide 22].
  • Order of magnitude. 240 GPa lies in the range of the unidirectional carbon-epoxy laminates of the slides (UD carbon-epoxy, 57 vol%: 134 GPa with a lower-modulus fibre, [Session 21 Slide 41]); the fibre modulus here, 372 GPa, is that of a high-modulus fibre [Session 21 Slide 22].

Interpretation

With a fibre about 100 times stiffer than the matrix, 99.5 % of the load goes through the carbon. The matrix adds almost nothing to the stiffness or the strength; its role is to bond, protect and align the fibres and to transfer the load into them [Session 21 Slide 16].

Discrepancy with the official solution

The key gives "a) 1.39 GPa" for the first question, the tensile modulus. The value 1.39 GPa is the strength of the composite: $201\,712\ \text{psi} \times 6894.757\ \text{Pa/psi} = 1.39$ GPa. The isostrain modulus from the data of the statement is $34.75\times10^6$ psi = 239.6 GPa, and the statement asks for the strength in psi (201 712 psi). The key's b) = 0.995 is the load fraction and agrees.

a) The average density of the composite material. b) The weight percentage of the carbon fibers and the epoxy resin in the composite. Official Solution: a) 1.601 g/cm³; b) 76% fiber-24% resin; c) 105.1%; d) 67.9 g lighter, -29.8%
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Rule of mixtures for the density, $\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + V_m\rho_m$ [Session 22 Slide 27], and the mass of a phase $m_i = \rho_i V_i$ (definition of density).
  2. Isostrain modulus along the fibres, $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 22].
  3. Same bar. Composite and alloy bars have the same volume, $V = 1\ \text{cm}^2 \times 100\ \text{cm} = 100\ \text{cm}^3$, so their masses differ only through the density.
  4. The density of Al 2024 is used as printed on the sheet (2.28 g/cm³).

$$\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + V_m\rho_m \qquad w_f = \frac{V_f\rho_f}{\rho_c} \qquad E_{1C} = V_f E_f + V_m E_m \qquad \Delta = \frac{X_c - X_{Al}}{X_{Al}}$$

1. a) Density [Session 22 Slide 27]

$$\rho_c = 0.68 \times 1.79 + 0.32 \times 1.2 = 1.2172 + 0.3840 = \mathbf{1.601\ g/cm^3}$$

2. b) Weight fractions

The two terms of the density are the masses of fibre and epoxy in 1 cm³:

$$w_f = \frac{1.2172}{1.6012} = 0.760 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{76\ \%\ fibre} \qquad w_m = \frac{0.3840}{1.6012} = 0.240 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{24\ \%\ resin}$$

3. c) Modulus and change with respect to Al 2024 [Session 22 Slide 22]

$$E_{1C} = 0.68 \times 220\ \text{GPa} + 0.32 \times 3.0\ \text{GPa} = 149.6 + 0.96 = \mathbf{150.6\ GPa}$$
$$\Delta E = \frac{150.56 - 73.4}{73.4} = \frac{77.16}{73.4} = 1.051 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{+105.1\ \%}$$

The composite is about twice as stiff as the alloy along the fibres.

4. d) Mass change of the bar

Volume $V = 1\ \text{cm}^2 \times 100\ \text{cm} = 100\ \text{cm}^3$:

$$m_c = 1.6012\ \text{g/cm}^3 \times 100\ \text{cm}^3 = 160.12\ \text{g} \qquad m_{Al} = 2.28\ \text{g/cm}^3 \times 100\ \text{cm}^3 = 228.0\ \text{g}$$
$$\Delta m = 160.12 - 228.0 = \mathbf{-67.9\ g} \qquad \frac{\Delta m}{m_{Al}} = \frac{-67.88}{228.0} = \mathbf{-29.8\ \%}$$

The composite bar is 67.9 g lighter.

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. g/cm³ × cm³ = g; percentages are ratios of equal units.
  • Bounds. $1.2 < 1.601 < 1.79$ g/cm³; $3 < 150.6 < 220$ GPa.
  • Specific modulus. Composite $150.56/1.601 = 94.0$ GPa cm³/g against the alloy $73.4/2.28 = 32.2$ GPa cm³/g: 2.9 times higher along the fibres.
  • Same composite as T10-01. Parts a) and b) repeat T10-01 (same fractions and densities), and give the same values.

Interpretation

Replacing the alloy by the unidirectional composite doubles the stiffness and saves about 30 % of the mass (with the printed density of the alloy). The gain holds only along the fibres: across them the modulus falls towards the matrix value (isostress, [Session 22 Slide 24]), which is why real parts are laminated in several orientations [Session 21 Slide 37].

Density of Al 2024 on the sheet

The sheet prints 2.28 g/cm³ for Al 2024, and the key uses it. Handbook values for aluminium alloys of the 2xxx series are close to 2.78 g/cm³ (extension, not on the slides); with that value the saving would be $160.12 - 278.0 = -117.9$ g, that is $-42.4\ \%$. The solution above keeps the printed datum.

Boron-Aluminium Composite: Density and Longitudinal and Transverse Moduli

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 3
  1. Five kilograms of continuous boron fibers are introduced in a unidirectional orientation into 8 kg of an aluminum matrix. Calculate

a) The density of the composite. b) The modulus of elasticity parallel to the fibers. c) The modulus of elasticity perpendicular to the fibers.

Data: E_Al = 70 GPa; ρ_Al = 2.7 g/cm³; E_Boron = 380 GPa; ρ_Boron = 2.3 g/cm³.

Official Solution: a) 2.530 g/cm³; b) 201.1×10³ MPa; c) 106.4×10³ MPa
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Volumes from masses. $V_i = m_i/\rho_i$; the composite has no porosity, so its volume is the sum of the two.
  2. Parallel to the fibres: isostrain, $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 22, Eq. 1].
  3. Perpendicular to the fibres: isostress, the same stress in both phases and strains that add, $1/E_{2C} = V_f/E_f + V_m/E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 24, Eq. 2]. The boron fibre is taken as isotropic, so its transverse modulus $E'_f$ equals 380 GPa.

Data

ConstituentMass (kg)$\rho$ (g/cm³)$E$ (GPa)
Boron fibre (f)52.3380
Aluminium (m)82.770

Unknowns: $\rho_c$, $E_{1C}$, $E_{2C}$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 22, 24, 27]:

$$\rho_c = \frac{m_f + m_m}{V_f + V_m} \qquad E_{1C} = V_f E_f + V_m E_m \qquad \frac{1}{E_{2C}} = \frac{V_f}{E_f} + \frac{V_m}{E_m}$$

Strategy. Volumes of the two phases → volume fractions → density; then the upper (isostrain) and lower (isostress) rules.

Step-by-Step Calculation

1. Volumes and volume fractions

With 5 kg = 5000 g and 8 kg = 8000 g:

$$V_B = \frac{5000\ \text{g}}{2.3\ \text{g/cm}^3} = 2173.9\ \text{cm}^3 \qquad V_{Al} = \frac{8000\ \text{g}}{2.7\ \text{g/cm}^3} = 2963.0\ \text{cm}^3 \qquad V_c = 5136.9\ \text{cm}^3$$
$$V_f = \frac{2173.9}{5136.9} = 0.4232 \qquad V_m = \frac{2963.0}{5136.9} = 0.5768$$

2. a) Density [Session 22 Slide 27]

$$\rho_c = \frac{13\,000\ \text{g}}{5136.9\ \text{cm}^3} = \mathbf{2.531\ g/cm^3}$$

The rule of mixtures gives the same: $0.4232 \times 2.3 + 0.5768 \times 2.7 = 0.9734 + 1.5574 = 2.531$ g/cm³.

3. b) Modulus parallel to the fibres [Session 22 Slide 22]

$$E_{1C} = 0.4232 \times 380\ \text{GPa} + 0.5768 \times 70\ \text{GPa} = 160.82 + 40.38 = \mathbf{201.2\ GPa} = 201.2\times10^3\ \text{MPa}$$

4. c) Modulus perpendicular to the fibres [Session 22 Slide 24]

$$\frac{1}{E_{2C}} = \frac{0.4232}{380\ \text{GPa}} + \frac{0.5768}{70\ \text{GPa}} = 0.0011137 + 0.0082400 = 0.0093537\ \text{GPa}^{-1}$$
$$E_{2C} = \frac{1}{0.0093537\ \text{GPa}^{-1}} = \mathbf{106.9\ GPa} = 106.9\times10^3\ \text{MPa}$$

The closed form of the slide gives the same: $E_{2C} = E_f E_m/(V_f E_m + V_m E_f) = (380 \times 70)/(0.4232 \times 70 + 0.5768 \times 380) = 26\,600/248.81 = 106.9$ GPa.

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. g/cm³ from g over cm³; in the isostress rule each term is dimensionless over GPa, so $1/E_{2C}$ is in GPa⁻¹.
  • Bounds. $E_m = 70 < E_{2C} = 106.9 < E_{1C} = 201.2 < E_f = 380$ GPa: the isostress value is the lower bound and the isostrain value the upper bound [Session 22 Slides 25, 28].
  • Density. Boron is lighter than aluminium here, so adding fibre lowers the density (2.531 < 2.7 g/cm³) while it nearly triples the stiffness along the fibres.

Interpretation

Across the fibres the compliant aluminium layers deform the most and control the response, so $E_{2C}$ is only half of $E_{1C}$. The anisotropy is milder than in polymer composites ($E_f/E_m = 5.4$ here against about 100 for carbon-epoxy), because the metal matrix is itself stiff: one of the reasons boron-aluminium is used in structural MMC parts such as the DC-10 aft pylon skin [Session 23 Slide 47].

Discrepancy with the official solution

The key gives $106.4\times10^3$ MPa for part c). With the volume fraction of part a), $V_f = 0.4232$, the isostress rule gives $106.9\times10^3$ MPa (the bracket is written out above). The key value corresponds to $V_f = (1/70 - 1/106.4)/(1/70 - 1/380) = 0.419$, which does not follow from the data (5 kg of boron at 2.3 g/cm³ in 8 kg of aluminium at 2.7 g/cm³), so the computed $106.9\times10^3$ MPa is kept. The difference is 0.5 %.

Mass of Carbon Fibres for a Carbon-Polyimide Composite

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 3
  1. We want to produce 5 kg of a continuous unidirectional fiber-reinforced composite of HS (High Strength) carbon in a polyimide matrix that has a modulus of elasticity of at least 172×10³ MPa parallel to the fibers. How many kilograms of fibers are required?

Data: E_carbon fiber = 276×10³ MPa; E_PI = 2070 MPa; ρ_carbon fiber = 1.75 g/cm³; ρ_PI = 1.39 g/cm³.

Official Solution: 3.37 Kg
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Isostrain parallel to the continuous fibres: $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m$ [Session 22 Slide 22, Eq. 1].
  2. Minimum fibre content. $E_{1C}$ grows with $V_f$, so the least fibre that meets the requirement is the one that gives exactly $E_{1C} = 172\times10^3$ MPa.
  3. Mass fraction $w_f = V_f\rho_f/\rho_c$ with $\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + V_m\rho_m$ [Session 22 Slide 27]; no porosity.

QuantityValue
$E_f$ (HS carbon)$276\times10^3$ MPa

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 22, 27]:

$$V_f = \frac{E_{1C} - E_m}{E_f - E_m} \qquad w_f = \frac{V_f\rho_f}{V_f\rho_f + V_m\rho_m} \qquad m_f = w_f\,m_c$$

$$E_{1C} = E_m + V_f(E_f - E_m) \ \Rightarrow\ V_f = \frac{172\,000 - 2\,070}{276\,000 - 2\,070} = \frac{169\,930\ \text{MPa}}{273\,930\ \text{MPa}} = 0.6203$$

2. Mass fraction of fibre [Session 22 Slide 27]

Masses of fibre and matrix in 1 cm³ of composite:

$$V_f\rho_f = 0.6203 \times 1.75 = 1.0856\ \text{g} \qquad V_m\rho_m = 0.3797 \times 1.39 = 0.5278\ \text{g} \qquad \rho_c = 1.6134\ \text{g/cm}^3$$
$$w_f = \frac{1.0856}{1.6134} = 0.6729$$

3. Mass of fibres

$$m_f = 0.6729 \times 5\ \text{kg} = \mathbf{3.36\ kg}$$

and $5 - 3.36 = 1.64$ kg of polyimide.

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. $V_f$ is MPa/MPa and $w_f$ is (g/cm³)/(g/cm³), both dimensionless, so $m_f$ is in kg.
  • Back-substitution. $0.6203 \times 276\,000 + 0.3797 \times 2\,070 = 171\,203 + 786 = 171\,989 \approx 172\,000$ MPa.
  • Weight against volume. The fibre is the denser phase, so $w_f = 0.673 > V_f = 0.620$.
  • Feasibility. $V_f = 62$ % is slightly above the 50 to 60 % of typical fibre composites [Session 22 Slide 22]: the requirement asks for a well-packed unidirectional composite.

Interpretation

Because the matrix contributes less than 1 % of the modulus, the requirement is essentially $V_f \approx E_{1C}/E_f = 172/276 = 0.623$. A stiffer (HM) fibre would meet the same target with less fibre and a lighter part.

Stress on the Fibres of a Glass-Epoxy Rod

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 3
  1. An epoxy matrix is reinforced with 40 vol% E-glass fibers to produce a 2-cm-diameter composite that is to withstand a load of 25000 N that is applied parallel to the fiber length. Calculate the stress acting on each fiber.

Data: E_glass fiber = 72.4 x10³ MPa MPa; E_matrix = 2.8 x10³ MPa MPa.

The sheet prints the unit twice ("MPa MPa").

Official Solution: 188 MPa
Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Isostrain. The load is parallel to the fibres and the bond is perfect, so fibre, matrix and composite share the strain [Session 22 Slide 21]; each phase is linear elastic, $\sigma_i = E_i\varepsilon$ [Session 22 Slide 20].
  2. Force balance. $F_C = F_f + F_m$, with $F_i = \sigma_i A_i$ and $A_i = V_i A_C$ [Session 22 Slide 21].
  3. "Each fiber" carries the same stress, the fibre stress $\sigma_f$, because all fibres have the same strain and the same modulus.

Data

QuantityValue
$V_f$, $V_m$0.40, 0.60
$E_f$, $E_m$$72.4\times10^3$, $2.8\times10^3$ MPa
$d$2 cm = 20 mm
$F_C$25 000 N

Unknown: $\sigma_f$.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slides 21-22]:

$$\sigma_C = \frac{F_C}{A_C} \qquad E_{1C} = V_f E_f + V_m E_m \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\sigma_C}{E_{1C}} \qquad \sigma_f = E_f\,\varepsilon$$

Combining them, $\sigma_f = \sigma_C E_f/E_{1C}$: the fibre stress is the mean stress amplified by the ratio of the fibre modulus to the composite modulus.

Step-by-Step Calculation

1. Mean stress on the rod

$$A_C = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} = \frac{\pi\,(20\ \text{mm})^2}{4} = 314.16\ \text{mm}^2 \qquad \sigma_C = \frac{25\,000\ \text{N}}{314.16\ \text{mm}^2} = 79.58\ \text{MPa}$$

2. Modulus and common strain [Session 22 Slide 22]

$$E_{1C} = 0.40 \times 72.4\times10^3 + 0.60 \times 2.8\times10^3 = 28\,960 + 1\,680 = 30\,640\ \text{MPa}$$
$$\varepsilon = \frac{79.58\ \text{MPa}}{30\,640\ \text{MPa}} = 2.597\times10^{-3}$$

3. Stress on the fibres

$$\sigma_f = E_f\,\varepsilon = 72\,400\ \text{MPa} \times 2.597\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{188.0\ MPa}$$

The matrix, at the same strain, carries $\sigma_m = 2\,800 \times 2.597\times10^{-3} = 7.27$ MPa.

Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. N/mm² = MPa; the strain is MPa/MPa, dimensionless; MPa × dimensionless = MPa.
  • Force balance. $F_f + F_m = (188.04 \times 0.40 + 7.272 \times 0.60) \times 314.16 = (75.22 + 4.36) \times 314.16 = 25\,000$ N.
  • Stress ratio. $\sigma_f/\sigma_m = E_f/E_m = 25.9$, as isostrain requires [Session 22 Slide 22].
  • Limit. If the matrix were as stiff as the fibre, $\sigma_f$ would equal the mean stress 79.6 MPa; the soft matrix pushes the fibre stress up to 2.4 times the mean.

Interpretation

The fibres work at 188 MPa, far below the strength of E-glass (3450 MPa [Session 21 Slide 18]), while the epoxy works at only 7 MPa: the rod has a large margin, and the margin is set by the fibres, which carry $75.2/79.6 = 94.5$ % of the load.

Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Same part, same volume. The new part has the shape and size of the titanium part, so its volume is $V = m_{Ti}/\rho_{Ti}$ and its mass is $m = \rho_c V$.
  2. Same modulus parallel to the fibres. Isostrain, $E_{1C} = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f)E_m = 110$ GPa [Session 22 Slide 22, Eq. 1]; the least fibre that reaches it is used.
  3. Density by the rule of mixtures, $\rho_c = V_f\rho_f + V_m\rho_m$ [Session 22 Slide 27].
  4. Specific modulus $E/\rho$ [Session 21 Slide 43], in N m/kg (= m²/s²).

Density of a Porous Tungsten Matrix Infiltrated with Silver

 Original statement · Problems T10_CompositeMaterials.pdf, p. 4
  1. A tungsten matrix with 20% porosity is infiltrated with silver. Assuming that the pores are interconnected, what is the density of the composite before and after infiltration with silver? The density of pure tungsten is 19.25 g/cm³ and that of pure silver is 10.49 g/cm³.

(Solution 17.5g/cm³)

Hypotheses & Parameters

Hypotheses

  1. Porosity by volume. 20 % of the volume of the tungsten body is pores, 80 % is solid tungsten.
  2. Empty pores before infiltration. The air in the pores has negligible mass, so the pores add volume but no mass.
  3. Complete infiltration. The pores are interconnected, so the liquid silver reaches all of them and fills the whole 20 % (the principle of liquid infiltration of a porous preform [Session 23 Slides 33-35]).
  4. Rule of mixtures for the density, $\rho_c = \sum_i f_i\rho_i$ [Session 22 Slide 27], with a pore phase of zero density before infiltration.

Data

ConstituentVolume fraction$\rho$ (g/cm³)
Tungsten0.8019.25
Pores / silver0.200 / 10.49

Unknowns: $\rho$ before and after infiltration.

Model & Strategy

[Session 22 Slide 27]:

$$\rho_{before} = f_W\rho_W + f_p \cdot 0 \qquad \rho_{after} = f_W\rho_W + f_p\rho_{Ag}$$

Strategy. Work with 1 cm³ of the body: tungsten mass plus the mass that fills the pores (none before, silver after).

Step-by-Step Calculation

1. Before infiltration [Session 22 Slide 27]

$$\rho_{before} = 0.80 \times 19.25\ \text{g/cm}^3 = \mathbf{15.40\ g/cm^3}$$

2. After infiltration [Session 22 Slide 27]

$$\rho_{after} = 0.80 \times 19.25 + 0.20 \times 10.49 = 15.40 + 2.098 = \mathbf{17.50\ g/cm^3}$$
Result & Checks

Checks

  • Units. Volume fraction × g/cm³ = g/cm³.
  • Bounds. $\rho_{Ag} = 10.49 < 17.50 < \rho_W = 19.25$ g/cm³: the infiltrated body is a two-phase mixture and lies between its phases.
  • Mass gained. Each cm³ gains $0.20 \times 10.49 = 2.10$ g of silver, 13.6 % of the mass of the porous tungsten.
  • Limits. Zero porosity gives pure tungsten (19.25 g/cm³); 100 % porosity filled with silver gives pure silver.

Interpretation

The porous tungsten acts as a preform and the silver as the infiltrated phase, the route of the liquid-phase processing of metal matrix composites [Session 23 Slides 33-35]. The infiltration closes the porosity and raises the density by 2.1 g/cm³; if the pores were closed (not interconnected), the silver could not enter and the density would stay at 15.4 g/cm³.
