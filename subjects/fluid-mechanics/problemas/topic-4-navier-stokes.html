Problem NS1

Porous Wall with Suction

Original statement · NS1.pdf
Unidirectional flowPorous wallViscous dissipationStreamline

Official Problem Statement

Consider the steady planar motion of a perfect liquid of density $\rho$, viscosity $\mu$, thermal conductivity $k$ and specific heat $c$ in the vicinity of a horizontal porous wall. Far from the wall, the horizontal velocity component is $v_x = U_\infty$, the pressure is given by $p + \rho g z = P_\infty$ and the temperature is $T = T_\infty$, different from the value $T_w$ at the wall. The liquid is sucked through the pores of the wall, resulting in a transverse velocity $v_z = -V_w$ at the wall surface. In the analysis, assume that the velocity and temperature fields are only a function of the distance to the wall $z$.

1. Determine the velocity components $v_x(z)$ and $v_z(z)$ and well as the pressure field $p(x, z)$.

2. Obtain the temperature field $T(z)$.

3. Calculate the streamline that crosses $x = x_o$ and $z = 0$.

4. Compute the viscous stress on the wall.

5. Determine the heat flux from the fluid to the wall.

Source: NS1.pdf (page 1). Notes.pdf, Chapter 4: continuity Eq. 4.7, incompressible momentum Eq. 4.12, perfect-liquid energy Eq. 4.42, no-slip and far-field conditions (Section on initial and boundary conditions).

Phase 1

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Steady planar flow of an incompressible liquid with constant $\rho$, $\mu$, $k$, $c$ (perfect liquid, Notes.pdf, Eq. 4.38): the equations to use are (4.40), (4.41) and (4.42).
  • Gravity acts along $-\bar{e}_z$ (the far-field pressure is $p + \rho g z = P_\infty$, so $\bar{f}_m = -g\,\bar{e}_z$).
  • Given: the fields depend only on the distance to the wall, $\partial/\partial x = \partial/\partial y = \partial/\partial t = 0$ for every variable, except that the pressure is allowed to depend on $x$ (it is determined below).
  • No chemical reaction and no radiation: $Q_c = Q_r = 0$.
  • The wall is at rest and porous: the tangential velocity is zero (no-slip) and the normal velocity is the suction velocity.
  • Data: $[\rho,\ \mu,\ k,\ c,\ U_\infty,\ P_\infty,\ T_\infty,\ T_w,\ V_w,\ g]$ with $V_w > 0$. Unknowns: $v_x(z)$, $v_z(z)$, $p(x,z)$, $T(z)$, the streamline, the wall stress and the wall heat flux.
  • Degrees of freedom: two velocity components, the pressure and the temperature, each a function of one coordinate (plus $x$ for the pressure): four unknown fields against continuity, two momentum components and the energy equation.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity and the normal velocity

$$ \nabla\cdot\bar{v} = \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}}}_{v_x = v_x(z)} + \frac{dv_z}{dz} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{dv_z}{dz} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v_z = \text{const} \tag{1} $$

The constant is fixed at the wall: $v_z(0) = -V_w$, therefore

$$ \boxed{\,v_z(z) = -V_w\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Momentum equations

For $\bar{f}_m = -g\,\bar{e}_z$ the Cartesian components of (4.41) with constant $\mu$ are (Notes.pdf, Section on the Navier-Stokes equations in Cartesian coordinates). Every term that contains $\partial/\partial t$, $\partial/\partial x$ or $\partial/\partial y$ of a velocity component is zero.

z-component:

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial t}}}_{\text{steady}} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_x\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial x}}}_{v_z = v_z(z)} + v_z\underbrace{\frac{dv_z}{dz}}_{=0\ \text{by (1)}}\Big) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} + \mu\underbrace{\frac{d^2 v_z}{dz^2}}_{=0} - \rho g \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = -\rho g \tag{3} $$

Integrating (3) with respect to $z$ at fixed $x$ (the "constant" may depend on $x$):

$$ p + \rho g z = f(x) \tag{4} $$

The far-field condition $p + \rho g z \to P_\infty$ holds for every $x$, with $P_\infty$ a constant, so $f(x) = P_\infty$:

$$ \boxed{\,p(x,z) = P_\infty - \rho g z\,}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 0 \tag{5} $$

x-component:

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial t}}}_{\text{steady}} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_x\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} + v_z\frac{dv_x}{dz}\Big) = -\underbrace{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}}_{=0\ \text{by (5)}} + \mu\frac{d^2 v_x}{dz^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; -\rho V_w\frac{dv_x}{dz} = \mu\frac{d^2 v_x}{dz^2} \tag{6} $$

Dividing by $\mu$ and using $\nu = \mu/\rho$:

$$ \frac{d^2 v_x}{dz^2} + \frac{V_w}{\nu}\frac{dv_x}{dz} = 0 \tag{7} $$

This is a linear ODE with constant coefficients. Trying $v_x = e^{\lambda z}$ gives $\lambda^2 + (V_w/\nu)\lambda = 0$, so $\lambda = 0$ or $\lambda = -V_w/\nu$:

$$ v_x = C_1 + C_2\,e^{-V_w z/\nu} \tag{8} $$

Boundary conditions: $v_x(\infty) = U_\infty$ gives $C_1 = U_\infty$ (the exponential vanishes because $V_w > 0$); $v_x(0) = 0$ gives $C_1 + C_2 = 0$, so $C_2 = -U_\infty$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_x(z) = U_\infty\left(1 - e^{-V_w z/\nu}\right)\,} \tag{9} $$

3.3 Energy equation and the temperature field

With $e = e_o + cT$, $\bar{v} = v_x\bar{e}_x + v_z\bar{e}_z$ and $\partial/\partial x = \partial/\partial t = 0$, (4.42) reads

$$ \rho c\,v_z\frac{dT}{dz} = \phi_v + k\frac{d^2T}{dz^2} \tag{10} $$

The viscous dissipation of this flow, from the Cartesian expression of $\phi_v$ (Notes.pdf, Section on the Navier-Stokes equations in Cartesian coordinates), has a single non-zero shear rate $\partial v_x/\partial z$:

$$ \phi_v = \mu\left(\frac{dv_x}{dz}\right)^2, \qquad \frac{dv_x}{dz} = \frac{U_\infty V_w}{\nu}\,e^{-V_w z/\nu} \;\Longrightarrow\; \phi_v = \frac{\mu U_\infty^2 V_w^2}{\nu^2}\,e^{-2V_w z/\nu} \tag{11} $$

Substituting $v_z = -V_w$ and (11) in (10) and dividing by $k$, with $\alpha = k/(\rho c)$:

$$ \frac{d^2T}{dz^2} + \frac{V_w}{\alpha}\frac{dT}{dz} = -\frac{\mu U_\infty^2 V_w^2}{k\,\nu^2}\,e^{-2V_w z/\nu} \tag{12} $$

The solution is the sum of the homogeneous solution $B_1 + B_2e^{-V_w z/\alpha}$ and a particular solution $A\,e^{-2V_w z/\nu}$. Substituting the particular form in (12), with $T_p' = -(2V_w/\nu)A\,e^{-2V_wz/\nu}$ and $T_p'' = (4V_w^2/\nu^2)A\,e^{-2V_wz/\nu}$:

$$ A\left(\frac{4V_w^2}{\nu^2} - \frac{2V_w^2}{\alpha\nu}\right) = -\frac{\mu U_\infty^2V_w^2}{k\nu^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; A\,(4 - 2\,\mathrm{Pr}) = -\frac{\mu U_\infty^2}{k} = -\frac{\mathrm{Pr}\,U_\infty^2}{c} \tag{13} $$

where $\mathrm{Pr} = \nu/\alpha = \mu c/k$ was used. Hence (for $\mathrm{Pr} \neq 2$)

$$ A = \frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{2(\mathrm{Pr} - 2)}\frac{U_\infty^2}{c} \tag{14} $$

so that $T = B_1 + B_2e^{-V_wz/\alpha} + A\,e^{-2V_wz/\nu}$. The condition $T(\infty) = T_\infty$ gives $B_1 = T_\infty$ and $T(0) = T_w$ gives $B_2 = T_w - T_\infty - A$:

$$ \boxed{\,T(z) = T_\infty + \left(T_w - T_\infty - \frac{\mathrm{Pr}\,U_\infty^2}{2(\mathrm{Pr}-2)\,c}\right)e^{-V_wz/\alpha} + \frac{\mathrm{Pr}\,U_\infty^2}{2(\mathrm{Pr}-2)\,c}\,e^{-2V_wz/\nu}\,} \tag{15} $$

Note that $V_wz/\alpha = \mathrm{Pr}\,V_wz/\nu$. For $\mathrm{Pr} = 2$ the two exponents coincide and the particular solution takes the form $A z\,e^{-2V_wz/\nu}$ (a resonant case, not worked out in the sheet).

3.4 Streamline through $(x_o, 0)$

The streamlines are the integral curves of $\bar{v}$: $dx/v_x = dz/v_z$. With (2) and (9):

$$ \frac{dx}{dz} = \frac{v_x}{v_z} = -\frac{U_\infty}{V_w}\left(1 - e^{-V_wz/\nu}\right) \tag{16} $$

Integrating from $z' = 0$ (where $x = x_o$) to $z' = z$. The antiderivative of $1 - e^{-az'}$ with $a = V_w/\nu$ is $z' + e^{-az'}/a$, and Barrow's rule gives $[z' + e^{-az'}/a]_0^z = z + (e^{-az} - 1)/a$:

$$ \boxed{\,x - x_o = -\frac{U_\infty}{V_w}\left[z + \frac{\nu}{V_w}\left(e^{-V_wz/\nu} - 1\right)\right]\,} \tag{17} $$

3.5 Viscous stress on the wall

The wall is the surface $z = 0$. Take the unit normal pointing into the liquid, $\bar{n} = \bar{e}_z$: then $\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}$ is the viscous force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the wall. The components of $\bar{\bar{\tau}}'$ with the Navier-Poisson law (Notes.pdf, Section on stress components, Cartesian coordinates) are

$$ \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{e}_z = \tau'_{xz}\,\bar{e}_x + \tau'_{zz}\,\bar{e}_z, \qquad \tau'_{xz} = \mu\left(\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial z} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial x}\right) = \mu\frac{dv_x}{dz}, \qquad \tau'_{zz} = 2\mu\frac{dv_z}{dz} + \left(\mu_B - \tfrac{2}{3}\mu\right)\nabla\cdot\bar{v} = 0 \tag{18} $$

At the wall, from (9), $dv_x/dz\big|_{z=0} = U_\infty V_w/\nu$, so

$$ \boxed{\,\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}\Big|_{w} = \mu\frac{U_\infty V_w}{\nu}\,\bar{e}_x = \rho V_w U_\infty\,\bar{e}_x\,} \tag{19} $$

The liquid drags the wall in the direction of the free stream.

3.6 Heat flux from the liquid to the wall

The heat flux from the liquid to the wall is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_z$ (outward from the liquid, into the wall), and $\bar{q} = -k\nabla T$ (Notes.pdf, Eq. 4.36):

$$ \bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}\Big|_w = -k\frac{dT}{dz}\bar{e}_z\cdot(-\bar{e}_z)\Big|_{z=0} = k\frac{dT}{dz}\Big|_{z=0} \tag{20} $$

Differentiating (15) and evaluating at $z = 0$:

$$ \frac{dT}{dz}\Big|_0 = -\frac{V_w}{\alpha}B_2 - \frac{2V_w}{\nu}A = -\frac{V_w}{\nu}\left(\mathrm{Pr}\,B_2 + 2A\right) \tag{21} $$

Using $k/\nu = \rho c/\mathrm{Pr}$ and $B_2 = T_w - T_\infty - A$:

$$ k\frac{dT}{dz}\Big|_0 = -\rho c V_w\left(B_2 + \frac{2A}{\mathrm{Pr}}\right) = -\rho cV_w\left[T_w - T_\infty - A\,\frac{\mathrm{Pr}-2}{\mathrm{Pr}}\right] = -\rho cV_w\left[T_w - T_\infty - \frac{U_\infty^2}{2c}\right] \tag{22} $$
$$ \boxed{\,\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}\Big|_w = \rho c V_w\left[T_\infty - T_w + \frac{U_\infty^2}{2c}\right]\,} \tag{23} $$

The result does not depend on the Prandtl number.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. Suction pins the shear layer to the wall: the velocity reaches $99\%$ of $U_\infty$ at $z \approx 4.6\,\nu/V_w$ and the thickness $\nu/V_w$ does not grow with $x$. The liquid is pulled towards the wall by the pores, and the viscous diffusion away from the wall balances this convection (equation (6)).
  • Limits. For $V_w \to 0$ the layer thickens without bound, consistent with the absence of a steady unidirectional solution. For $z \to \infty$, $v_x \to U_\infty$ and $T \to T_\infty$. At $z = 0$, $v_x = 0$ and $T = T_w$.
  • Dimensional check. $V_wz/\nu$ and $V_wz/\alpha$ are dimensionless. In (23), $\rho cV_w\,\Delta T$ has units $(\mathrm{kg/m^3})(\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)})(\mathrm{m/s})\,\mathrm{K} = \mathrm{W/m^2}$, and $U_\infty^2/(2c)$ has units $(\mathrm{m^2/s^2})/(\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}) = \mathrm{K}$. In (19), $\rho V_wU_\infty$ has units $\mathrm{kg/(m^2\,s)}\cdot\mathrm{m/s} = \mathrm{N/m^2}$.
  • Energy check. Equation (23) is the heat that must flow into the wall in steady state: per unit area the suction brings in from the free stream the energy $\rho V_w(cT_\infty + U_\infty^2/2)$ and the pores take out $\rho V_w\,cT_w$; the pressure work and the work of gravity cancel, and the shear does no work on the wall because it is at rest. The difference is $\rho cV_w[T_\infty - T_w + U_\infty^2/(2c)]$, the result (23).
  • Special case. For $U_\infty \to 0$ there is no dissipation and (23) reduces to $\rho cV_w(T_\infty - T_w)$, the advection-conduction balance of pure suction.
Problem NS2

Rotating Coaxial Cylinders

Official Problem Statement

Consider the steady planar motion of a perfect liquid of density $\rho$, viscosity $\mu$, thermal conductivity $k$ and specific heat $c$ in the gap formed between two coaxial cylinders of radii $R_1$ and $R_2$ that are rotating with angular velocities $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$, respectively. Because of the symmetry of the problem, $v_z = 0$ and $\partial/\partial z = \partial/\partial\theta = 0$.

1. Obtain the velocity and pressure fields.

2. Calculate the torque exerted on each cylinder, as well as the power needed to keep them rotating.

3. If the temperatures on the cylinder surfaces are $T_1$ and $T_2$, determine the temperature distribution $T(r)$.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity and the radial velocity

$$ \frac{1}{r}\frac{d(rv_r)}{dr} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta}}}_{\partial_\theta = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z}}}_{v_z = 0} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; r\,v_r = \text{const} \tag{1} $$

The constant is fixed at the inner wall, where $v_r(R_1) = 0$: $r\,v_r = 0$ for every $r$, therefore

$$ \boxed{\,v_r(r) = 0\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Azimuthal momentum and the velocity profile

The $\theta$ component of (4.41) in cylindrical coordinates, with $v_r = 0$, $\partial/\partial t = \partial_\theta = \partial_z = 0$ and no mass force:

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial t}}}_{\text{steady}} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_r\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial r}}}_{v_r = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{v_\theta}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta}}}_{\partial_\theta = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{v_rv_\theta}{r}}}_{v_r = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_z\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial z}}}_{v_z = 0}\Big) = -\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}}}_{\partial_\theta = 0} + \mu\frac{d}{dr}\left[\frac{1}{r}\frac{d(rv_\theta)}{dr}\right] \tag{3} $$

Hence $\dfrac{d}{dr}\left[\dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{d(rv_\theta)}{dr}\right] = 0$. Integrating once, $\dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{d(rv_\theta)}{dr} = 2C_1$ (the factor 2 is a convenient choice of constant). Multiplying by $r$ and integrating again, $rv_\theta = C_1r^2 + C_2$:

$$ v_\theta = C_1\,r + \frac{C_2}{r} \tag{4} $$

The boundary conditions $v_\theta(R_1) = \Omega_1R_1$ and $v_\theta(R_2) = \Omega_2R_2$, each multiplied by $R_i$, give a linear system:

$$ C_1R_1^2 + C_2 = \Omega_1R_1^2, \qquad C_1R_2^2 + C_2 = \Omega_2R_2^2 \tag{5} $$

Subtracting the first equation from the second, $C_1(R_2^2 - R_1^2) = \Omega_2R_2^2 - \Omega_1R_1^2$, and then $C_2 = \Omega_1R_1^2 - C_1R_1^2$:

$$ C_1 = \frac{\Omega_2R_2^2 - \Omega_1R_1^2}{R_2^2 - R_1^2}, \qquad C_2 = \frac{(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2)\,R_1^2R_2^2}{R_2^2 - R_1^2} \tag{6} $$
$$ \boxed{\,v_\theta(r) = \frac{\Omega_2R_2^2 - \Omega_1R_1^2}{R_2^2 - R_1^2}\,r + \frac{(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2)R_1^2R_2^2}{R_2^2 - R_1^2}\,\frac{1}{r}\,} \tag{7} $$

3.3 Radial momentum and the pressure field

The $r$ component of (4.41) with $v_r = 0$ keeps only the centrifugal term and the pressure gradient (all other terms vanish for the same reasons as in (3)):

$$ -\rho\frac{v_\theta^2}{r} = -\frac{dp}{dr} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{dp}{dr} = \rho\frac{v_\theta^2}{r} = \rho\left(C_1^2\,r + \frac{2C_1C_2}{r} + \frac{C_2^2}{r^3}\right) \tag{8} $$

Integrating from $R_1$ to $r$, the antiderivatives are $C_1^2r^2/2$, $2C_1C_2\ln r$ and $-C_2^2/(2r^2)$, and Barrow's rule gives

$$ \boxed{\,p(r) - p(R_1) = \rho\left[\frac{C_1^2}{2}\left(r^2 - R_1^2\right) + 2C_1C_2\ln\frac{r}{R_1} - \frac{C_2^2}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r^2} - \frac{1}{R_1^2}\right)\right]\,} \tag{9} $$

The constant $p(R_1)$ is set by the reference pressure of the problem; the pressure grows outwards because the fluid is whirling and needs a centripetal force.

3.4 Viscous stress and torque on each cylinder

The only non-zero viscous shear stress is (Notes.pdf, stress components in cylindrical coordinates, with $v_r = 0$):

$$ \tau'_{r\theta} = \mu\left[r\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{v_\theta}{r}\right) + \frac{1}{r}\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial\theta}}}_{=0}\right] = \mu\,r\frac{d}{dr}\left(C_1 + \frac{C_2}{r^2}\right) = -\frac{2\mu C_2}{r^2} \tag{10} $$

The normal viscous stresses $\tau'_{rr}$ and $\tau'_{\theta\theta}$ are zero ($v_r = 0$, $\nabla\cdot\bar{v} = 0$), and the pressure force on a cylindrical surface is radial, so it has no moment about the axis. Only $\tau'_{r\theta}$ contributes to the torque.

*Inner cylinder* (surface $r = R_1$). The normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_r$, so $\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n} = \tau'_{r\theta}\,\bar{e}_\theta$ is the force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the cylinder. The torque per unit length about the axis, with $d\sigma = R_1\,d\theta\,(1)$ and arm $R_1\bar{e}_r$:

$$ \bar{M}_1 = \int_0^{2\pi}R_1\bar{e}_r\wedge\tau'_{r\theta}(R_1)\bar{e}_\theta\,R_1\,d\theta = 2\pi R_1^2\,\tau'_{r\theta}(R_1)\,\bar{e}_z = -4\pi\mu C_2\,\bar{e}_z \tag{11} $$

*Outer cylinder* (surface $r = R_2$). The normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$:

$$ \bar{M}_2 = \int_0^{2\pi}R_2\bar{e}_r\wedge\left[-\tau'_{r\theta}(R_2)\bar{e}_\theta\right]R_2\,d\theta = -2\pi R_2^2\,\tau'_{r\theta}(R_2)\,\bar{e}_z = +4\pi\mu C_2\,\bar{e}_z \tag{12} $$
$$ \boxed{\,\bar{M}_1 = -4\pi\mu\frac{(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2)R_1^2R_2^2}{R_2^2 - R_1^2}\,\bar{e}_z = -\bar{M}_2\,} \tag{13} $$

The two torques are equal and opposite, as the angular momentum balance of the liquid requires: in steady motion the total moment on the liquid between the cylinders is zero, so the moments that the cylinders exert on it, $-\bar{M}_1$ and $-\bar{M}_2$, cancel.

3.5 Power needed to keep the cylinders rotating

The external agent must exert on each cylinder the opposite of the liquid torque, so the power it supplies is $\dot{W}_i = -\bar{M}_i\cdot\Omega_i\bar{e}_z$:

$$ \dot{W}_1 = +4\pi\mu C_2\,\Omega_1, \qquad \dot{W}_2 = -4\pi\mu C_2\,\Omega_2 \tag{14} $$
$$ \boxed{\,\dot{W} = \dot{W}_1 + \dot{W}_2 = 4\pi\mu C_2\,(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2) = 4\pi\mu\frac{(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2)^2R_1^2R_2^2}{R_2^2 - R_1^2}\,} \tag{15} $$

The total power is non-negative, as it must be: it equals the viscous dissipation in the gap (checked in 3.6).

3.6 Temperature distribution

With $v_r = 0$ the convective terms of (4.42) vanish. The energy equation for the steady, axisymmetric flow reads

$$ 0 = \phi_v + \frac{k}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) \tag{16} $$

and the viscous dissipation of this flow (cylindrical $\phi_v$ of Notes.pdf, keeping the only non-zero shear $\tau'_{r\theta}$) is

$$ \phi_v = \mu\left[r\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{v_\theta}{r}\right)\right]^2 = \mu\left(\frac{2C_2}{r^2}\right)^2 = \frac{4\mu C_2^2}{r^4} \tag{17} $$

Substituting (17) in (16) and multiplying by $r/k$:

$$ \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) = -\frac{4\mu C_2^2}{k\,r^3} \tag{18} $$

Integrating once, $r\,dT/dr = \dfrac{2\mu C_2^2}{k\,r^2} + B_1$; dividing by $r$ and integrating again:

$$ T(r) = -\frac{\mu C_2^2}{k\,r^2} + B_1\ln r + B_2 \tag{19} $$

Write $a = \mu C_2^2/k$. The conditions $T(R_1) = T_1$ and $T(R_2) = T_2$ give, after eliminating $B_2$,

$$ T - T_1 = a\left(\frac{1}{R_1^2} - \frac{1}{r^2}\right) + B\ln\frac{r}{R_1}, \qquad B\ln\frac{R_2}{R_1} = T_2 - T_1 - a\left(\frac{1}{R_1^2} - \frac{1}{R_2^2}\right) \tag{20} $$
$$ \boxed{\,T(r) - T_1 = \frac{\mu C_2^2}{k}\left(\frac{1}{R_1^2} - \frac{1}{r^2}\right) + \left[T_2 - T_1 - \frac{\mu C_2^2}{k}\left(\frac{1}{R_1^2} - \frac{1}{R_2^2}\right)\right]\frac{\ln(r/R_1)}{\ln(R_2/R_1)}\,} \tag{21} $$

*Check of the power.* Integrating the dissipation over the gap per unit length, with $dV = 2\pi r\,dr$ and the antiderivative of $r^{-3}$ equal to $-1/(2r^2)$:

$$ \int\phi_v\,dV = 2\pi\int_{R_1}^{R_2}\frac{4\mu C_2^2}{r^4}\,r\,dr = 8\pi\mu C_2^2\left[-\frac{1}{2r^2}\right]_{R_1}^{R_2} = 4\pi\mu C_2^2\,\frac{R_2^2 - R_1^2}{R_1^2R_2^2} = 4\pi\mu C_2\,(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2) \tag{22} $$

where $C_2(R_2^2 - R_1^2)/(R_1^2R_2^2) = \Omega_1 - \Omega_2$ from (6) was used. It coincides with (15): the power supplied by the cylinders is dissipated in the liquid and leaves as heat through the two walls.

  • Limits. For $\Omega_1 = \Omega_2 = \Omega$ the liquid rotates as a rigid body, $v_\theta = \Omega r$, $C_2 = 0$, the torques and the power vanish and $p - p(R_1) = \rho\Omega^2(r^2 - R_1^2)/2$. Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity and the radial velocity

$$ \nabla\cdot\bar{v} = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial(r^2v_r)}{\partial r} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial(v_\theta\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta}}}_{v_\theta = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial v_\phi}{\partial\phi}}}_{v_\phi = 0} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; r^2\,v_r = C(t) \tag{1} $$

The kinematic condition $v_r(R,t) = \dot{R}$ gives $C(t) = R^2\dot{R}$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_r(r,t) = \frac{R^2\dot{R}}{r^2}\,} \tag{2} $$

The condition at infinity is satisfied automatically. The velocity at every point is fixed by the instantaneous radius and its rate of change.

3.2 Radial momentum and the pressure field

The viscous term of the $r$ component of (4.41) is $\mu\,\partial/\partial r\left[r^{-2}\,\partial(r^2v_r)/\partial r\right]$ (all other viscous terms contain $v_\theta$, $v_\phi$ or derivatives with respect to $\theta$, $\phi$). By (1), $r^2v_r = C(t)$ does not depend on $r$, so $\partial(r^2v_r)/\partial r = 0$ and the viscous force vanishes identically: the flow is irrotational, $\nabla\wedge\bar{v} = 0$. The momentum equation reduces to

$$ \rho\left(\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial t} + v_r\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r}\right) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} \tag{3} $$

The two accelerations are computed with the product and chain rules, using $d(R^2\dot{R})/dt = 2R\dot{R}^2 + R^2\ddot{R}$ and $\partial(r^{-2})/\partial r = -2r^{-3}$:

$$ \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial t} = \frac{2R\dot{R}^2 + R^2\ddot{R}}{r^2}, \qquad v_r\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} = \frac{R^2\dot{R}}{r^2}\left(-\frac{2R^2\dot{R}}{r^3}\right) = -\frac{2R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^5} \tag{4} $$
$$ \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = -\rho\left[\frac{2R\dot{R}^2 + R^2\ddot{R}}{r^2} - \frac{2R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^5}\right] \tag{5} $$

Integrate (5) from $r$ to $\infty$, with $p(\infty) = p_\infty$. The antiderivatives are $\int r'^{-2}dr' = -1/r'$ and $\int r'^{-5}dr' = -1/(4r'^4)$, and Barrow's rule between $r$ and $\infty$ gives $\int_r^\infty r'^{-2}dr' = 1/r$ and $\int_r^\infty r'^{-5}dr' = 1/(4r^4)$:

$$ p_\infty - p(r,t) = -\rho\left[\frac{2R\dot{R}^2 + R^2\ddot{R}}{r} - \frac{R^4\dot{R}^2}{2r^4}\right] $$
$$ \boxed{\,p(r,t) = p_\infty + \rho\left[\frac{2R\dot{R}^2 + R^2\ddot{R}}{r} - \frac{R^4\dot{R}^2}{2r^4}\right]\,} \tag{6} $$

At the balloon surface $r = R$:

$$ p(R,t) = p_\infty + \rho\left[2\dot{R}^2 + R\ddot{R} - \frac{\dot{R}^2}{2}\right] = p_\infty + \rho\left(\frac{3}{2}\dot{R}^2 + R\ddot{R}\right) \tag{7} $$

3.3 Viscous stress at the balloon surface

With the Navier-Poisson law and $\nabla\cdot\bar{v} = 0$, the radial normal stress is (Notes.pdf, stress components in spherical coordinates)

$$ \tau'_{rr} = 2\mu\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} = 2\mu\left(-\frac{2R^2\dot{R}}{r^3}\right), \qquad \tau'_{rr}\Big|_{r=R} = -\frac{4\mu\dot{R}}{R} \tag{8} $$

The shear components $\tau'_{r\theta}$ and $\tau'_{r\phi}$ are zero by symmetry, so the viscous traction on the surface is purely radial.

3.4 Power required to inflate the balloon

The balloon surface moves with velocity $\dot{R}\,\bar{e}_r$. The liquid occupies $r > R$, so the unit normal pointing out of the liquid at the balloon surface is $-\bar{e}_r$. The traction that the balloon exerts on the liquid is $(-p\bar{\bar{I}} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}')\cdot(-\bar{e}_r) = (p - \tau'_{rr})\,\bar{e}_r$, and the power it delivers is the force times the velocity of the surface, integrated over the area $4\pi R^2$:

$$ \dot{W} = 4\pi R^2\,\dot{R}\left[p(R,t) - \tau'_{rr}(R)\right] = 4\pi R^2\dot{R}\,p(R,t) + 16\pi\mu R\dot{R}^2 \tag{9} $$

Substituting (7):

$$ \boxed{\,\dot{W} = 4\pi R^2\dot{R}\,p_\infty + 4\pi\rho R^2\dot{R}\left(\frac{3}{2}\dot{R}^2 + R\ddot{R}\right) + 16\pi\mu R\dot{R}^2\,} \tag{10} $$

3.5 Where does the work go? (additional question)

The three terms of (10) are identified by computing independently the rate of change of the kinetic energy of the liquid and its viscous dissipation.

*Kinetic energy of the liquid.* With $v_r^2 = R^4\dot{R}^2/r^4$ and $dV = 4\pi r^2\,dr$:

$$ E_k = \int_R^\infty\frac{\rho}{2}\frac{R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^4}\,4\pi r^2\,dr = 2\pi\rho R^4\dot{R}^2\left[-\frac{1}{r}\right]_R^\infty = 2\pi\rho R^3\dot{R}^2 \tag{11} $$

Differentiating with the product rule,

$$ \frac{dE_k}{dt} = 2\pi\rho\left(3R^2\dot{R}^3 + 2R^3\dot{R}\ddot{R}\right) = 4\pi\rho R^2\dot{R}\left(\frac{3}{2}\dot{R}^2 + R\ddot{R}\right) \tag{12} $$

which is exactly the second term of (10).

*Viscous dissipation.* In spherical coordinates with $v_\theta = v_\phi = 0$ the dissipation is $\phi_v = 2\mu\left[(\partial v_r/\partial r)^2 + 2(v_r/r)^2\right]$ (Notes.pdf, $\phi_v$ in spherical coordinates). With $\partial v_r/\partial r = -2R^2\dot{R}/r^3$ and $v_r/r = R^2\dot{R}/r^3$:

$$ \phi_v = 2\mu\left[\frac{4R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^6} + \frac{2R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^6}\right] = \frac{12\mu R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^6} \tag{13} $$
$$ \Phi = \int_R^\infty\frac{12\mu R^4\dot{R}^2}{r^6}\,4\pi r^2\,dr = 48\pi\mu R^4\dot{R}^2\left[-\frac{1}{3r^3}\right]_R^\infty = 16\pi\mu R\dot{R}^2 \tag{14} $$

which is the third term of (10).

*Pressure term.* $4\pi R^2\dot{R}\,p_\infty = p_\infty\,dV_b/dt$ with $V_b = 4\pi R^3/3$ the balloon volume: the work done against the ambient pressure to displace the liquid, that is, to set the ambient liquid in motion (the outward volume flux through any sphere is $4\pi r^2v_r = 4\pi R^2\dot{R}$).

$$ \boxed{\,\dot{W} = p_\infty\frac{dV_b}{dt} + \frac{dE_k}{dt} + \Phi\,} \tag{15} $$

The work supplied by the balloon increases the kinetic energy of the liquid, displaces the ambient pressure $p_\infty$ and is partly converted into internal energy by viscous dissipation. This agrees with the mechanical energy balance (Notes.pdf, Eq. 4.24) applied to the liquid volume between the balloon and a distant material sphere.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The velocity decays as $1/r^2$ because the volume flux $4\pi r^2v_r$ through every sphere equals the rate of volume growth of the balloon. The motion is irrotational, so viscosity does not enter the pressure field (6); it appears only in the boundary traction (8) and the power (10).
  • Limits. For $r \to \infty$, $v_r \to 0$ and $p \to p_\infty$. For a balloon at constant radius ($\dot{R} = 0$) every term vanishes: no flow, $p = p_\infty$ and no power. For a constant inflation rate ($\ddot{R} = 0$) the pressure is above $p_\infty$ near the balloon, by the amount $\rho\dot{R}^2(3/2)$ at $r = R$.
  • Dimensional check. $v_r$: $\mathrm{m^2\cdot m/s}/\mathrm{m^2} = \mathrm{m/s}$. In (6), $\rho R\dot{R}^2/r$ has units $(\mathrm{kg/m^3})(\mathrm{m^2/s^2}) = \mathrm{Pa}$ and $\rho R^2\ddot{R}/r$ has units $(\mathrm{kg/m^3})\,\mathrm{m^2}\,(\mathrm{m/s^2})/\mathrm{m} = \mathrm{Pa}$. In (10), $p_\infty\,4\pi R^2\dot{R}$ is Pa $\cdot\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = \mathrm{W}$ and $16\pi\mu R\dot{R}^2$ is $(\mathrm{Pa\,s})\,\mathrm{m}\,(\mathrm{m^2/s^2}) = \mathrm{W}$.
  • Consistency. Equation (15) is verified term by term: (12) equals the second term of (10) and (14) equals the third, so the power computed from the surface traction equals the power accounted for inside the liquid.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity and the normal velocity

$$ \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{dv_z}{dz} = \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}}}_{v_x = v_x(z)} + \frac{dv_z}{dz} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v_z = \text{const} \tag{1} $$

The wall is impermeable, $v_z(0) = 0$, so

$$ \boxed{\,v_z(z) = 0\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Momentum equations

z-component: with $v_z = 0$ every inertial and viscous term vanishes,

$$ 0 = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} - \rho g \;\Longrightarrow\; p + \rho g z = f(x) \tag{3} $$

x-component: the convective terms vanish ($\partial_x = 0$, $v_z = 0$),

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial t}}}_{\text{steady}} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_x\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_z\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial z}}}_{v_z = 0}\Big) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \mu\frac{d^2v_x}{dz^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mu\frac{d^2v_x}{dz^2} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = f'(x) \tag{4} $$

The left side depends only on $z$ and the right side only on $x$, so both are equal to a constant. The pressure on the upper wall is $p(x,h) = p_u$ for every $x$, and by (3) $f(x) = p_u + \rho g h$ is a constant, so $f'(x) = 0$:

$$ \boxed{\,p(x,z) = p_u + \rho g\,(h - z)\,}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 0 \tag{5} $$

Equation (4) becomes $d^2v_x/dz^2 = 0$; integrating twice, $v_x = C_1 + C_2z$. The conditions $v_x(0) = 0$ and $v_x(h) = U$ give $C_1 = 0$ and $C_2 = U/h$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_x(z) = U\,\frac{z}{h}\,} \tag{6} $$

3.3 Volume flux

The flux per unit width in $y$ through a section $x = \text{const}$ is $Q = \int_0^h v_x\,dz$. The antiderivative of $Uz/h$ is $Uz^2/(2h)$ and Barrow's rule gives:

$$ \boxed{\,Q = \left[\frac{U}{h}\frac{z^2}{2}\right]_0^h = \frac{Uh}{2}\,} \tag{7} $$

3.4 Temperature distribution

The energy equation (4.42) with $v_z = 0$, $\partial_x = 0$ and $\phi_v = \mu(dv_x/dz)^2 = \mu U^2/h^2$:

$$ \underbrace{\cancel{\rho c\,v_x\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} = \phi_v + k\frac{d^2T}{dz^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{d^2T}{dz^2} = -\frac{\mu U^2}{k\,h^2} \tag{8} $$

Integrating twice, $T = B_1 + B_2z - \dfrac{\mu U^2}{2kh^2}z^2$. The conditions $T(0) = T_l$ and $T(h) = T_u$ give $B_1 = T_l$ and $B_2 = \dfrac{T_u - T_l}{h} + \dfrac{\mu U^2}{2kh}$:

$$ \boxed{\,T(z) = T_l + \left(T_u - T_l\right)\frac{z}{h} + \frac{\mu U^2}{2k}\,\frac{z}{h}\left(1 - \frac{z}{h}\right)\,} \tag{9} $$

3.5 Force on the upper wall and power needed to move it

On the upper wall, the normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_z$, so $(-p\bar{\bar{I}} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}')\cdot\bar{n}$ is the force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the wall. The viscous components are $\tau'_{xz} = \mu\,dv_x/dz = \mu U/h$ and $\tau'_{zz} = 0$ (Notes.pdf, stress components, Cartesian coordinates):

$$ \bar{f}_u = -p_u(-\bar{e}_z) + \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot(-\bar{e}_z) = p_u\,\bar{e}_z - \mu\frac{U}{h}\,\bar{e}_x \tag{10} $$

The viscous part opposes the motion of the wall. The external agent must supply $+\mu U/h\,\bar{e}_x$ per unit area, with the wall velocity $U\bar{e}_x$:

$$ \boxed{\,\bar{f}_u = p_u\,\bar{e}_z - \mu\frac{U}{h}\,\bar{e}_x, \qquad \dot{W} = \mu\frac{U^2}{h}\ \text{per unit area}\,} \tag{11} $$

The pressure force $p_u\bar{e}_z$ is normal to the wall and does no work on it.

3.6 Heat fluxes to the walls

The heat flux from the liquid to a wall is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$, with $\bar{n}$ pointing out of the liquid and $\bar{q} = -k\nabla T$. From (9),

$$ \frac{dT}{dz} = \frac{T_u - T_l}{h} + \frac{\mu U^2}{2kh} - \frac{\mu U^2}{kh^2}z \tag{12} $$

*Upper wall* ($\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_z$, $z = h$):

$$ q_u = -k\frac{dT}{dz}\Big|_{z=h} = k\frac{T_l - T_u}{h} + \frac{\mu U^2}{2h} \tag{13} $$

*Lower wall* ($\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_z$, $z = 0$):

$$ q_l = +k\frac{dT}{dz}\Big|_{z=0} = k\frac{T_u - T_l}{h} + \frac{\mu U^2}{2h} \tag{14} $$
$$ \boxed{\,q_u = k\frac{T_l - T_u}{h} + \frac{\mu U^2}{2h}, \qquad q_l = k\frac{T_u - T_l}{h} + \frac{\mu U^2}{2h}\,} \tag{15} $$

The conductive contributions are equal and opposite and the viscous heating splits equally between the walls. Their sum is $q_u + q_l = \mu U^2/h$.

3.7 Integral balance in the fixed control volume $V_o$

*Total energy* (Notes.pdf, Eq. 4.3 in integral form for the fixed volume, steady):

$$ \int_{\Sigma_o}\rho\left(e + \frac{\lvert\bar{v}\rvert^2}{2}\right)\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma = -\int_{\Sigma_o}p\,\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma + \int_{\Sigma_o}\bar{v}\cdot\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma + \underbrace{\int_{V_o}\rho\bar{g}\cdot\bar{v}\,dV}_{=0} - \int_{\Sigma_o}\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma \tag{16} $$

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The pressure is hydrostatic and the velocity is linear: viscous forces alone transmit the motion. The dissipation $\phi_v = \mu U^2/h^2$ is uniform, and it heats the liquid; the parabolic term of (9) is the temperature rise it produces, with its maximum in the middle of the gap.
  • Limits. For $U \to 0$ there is no flow and no dissipation: $T$ is the linear conduction profile between $T_l$ and $T_u$ and $q_u = -q_l = k(T_l - T_u)/h$. For $T_u = T_l$, the temperature is symmetric about $z = h/2$ and each wall receives $\mu U^2/(2h)$.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity

$$ \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(rv_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(rv_r)}{\partial r}}}_{v_r = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta}}}_{v_\theta = 0} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0 \tag{1} $$

so $v_z = v_z(r)$ (the field does not change along the axis).

3.2 Momentum equations and the pressure

r-component: with $v_r = v_\theta = 0$ and $\partial/\partial\theta = 0$ every term vanishes except the pressure gradient,

$$ 0 = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} \;\Longrightarrow\; p = p(z) \tag{2} $$

z-component:

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial t}}}_{\text{steady}} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_r\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial r}}}_{v_r = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{v_\theta}{r}\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial\theta}}}_{v_\theta = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_z\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z}}}_{\text{by (1)}}\Big) = -\frac{dp}{dz} + \mu\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right) - \rho g \tag{3} $$

By (2), $p$ depends only on $z$; at $r = b$ it equals $p_a$ for every $z$, therefore

$$ \boxed{\,p(r,z) = p_a\,}, \qquad \frac{dp}{dz} = 0 \tag{4} $$

The liquid is at the pressure of the surrounding air everywhere: the weight of the layer is carried by the viscous stress, not by a pressure gradient. Equation (3) becomes

$$ \frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right) = \frac{\rho g}{\mu} = \frac{g}{\nu} \tag{5} $$

3.3 Velocity profile

Multiply (5) by $r$ and integrate: $r\,dv_z/dr = \dfrac{g}{2\nu}r^2 + C_1$. Divide by $r$ and integrate again, with $\int dr/r = \ln r$:

$$ v_z = \frac{g}{4\nu}r^2 + C_1\ln r + C_2 \tag{6} $$

The shear-free condition at $r = b$, $dv_z/dr\big|_b = \dfrac{g}{2\nu}b + \dfrac{C_1}{b} = 0$, gives $C_1 = -\dfrac{g}{2\nu}b^2$. The no-slip condition $v_z(a) = U$ gives $C_2 = U - \dfrac{g}{4\nu}a^2 + \dfrac{g}{2\nu}b^2\ln a$. Substituting,

$$ \boxed{\,v_z(r) = U + \frac{g}{2\nu}\left[\frac{r^2 - a^2}{2} - (a + h)^2\ln\frac{r}{a}\right]\,} \tag{7} $$

3.4 Force on the cylinder

The surface of the cylinder is $r = a$; the normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_r$, so $(-p\bar{\bar{I}} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}')\cdot\bar{n}$ is the force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the cylinder. The pressure is uniform ($p = p_a$) and radial, so it integrates to zero around the circumference. The viscous stress is $\tau'_{rz} = \mu\,dv_z/dr$ (Notes.pdf, stress components in cylindrical coordinates). From (7), $dv_z/dr = \dfrac{g}{2\nu}\left(r - \dfrac{b^2}{r}\right)$, so at the wall

$$ \tau'_{rz}\Big|_a = \mu\frac{g}{2\nu}\left(a - \frac{b^2}{a}\right) = -\frac{\rho g\,(b^2 - a^2)}{2a} = -\frac{\rho g\,h\,(2a + h)}{2a} \tag{8} $$

The force per unit length of cylinder (area $2\pi a$):

$$ \boxed{\,\bar{F} = 2\pi a\,\tau'_{rz}\Big|_a\,\bar{e}_z = -\rho g\,\pi\left[(a + h)^2 - a^2\right]\bar{e}_z = -2\pi a\,\rho g\,h\left(1 + \frac{h}{2a}\right)\bar{e}_z\,} \tag{9} $$

The liquid pulls the cylinder downward with a force equal to the weight of the layer per unit length, as the equilibrium of the whole layer requires (the pressure forces on it cancel).

3.5 Volume flux and the velocity for zero flux

$$ Q = \int_a^b v_z\,2\pi r\,dr \tag{10} $$

With $\kappa = g/(2\nu)$, three integrals are needed. The first is elementary, $\int_a^b r\,dr = (b^2 - a^2)/2$. For the second, the antiderivative of $r(r^2 - a^2)/2$ is $r^4/8 - a^2r^2/4$, and Barrow's rule gives $\dfrac{(b^2 - a^2)^2}{8}$. For the third, integration by parts with $u = \ln(r/a)$, $dv = r\,dr$ gives the antiderivative $\dfrac{r^2}{2}\ln\dfrac{r}{a} - \dfrac{r^2}{4}$, so $\displaystyle\int_a^b r\ln\frac{r}{a}\,dr = \frac{b^2}{2}\ln\frac{b}{a} - \frac{b^2 - a^2}{4}$. Then

$$ \frac{Q}{2\pi} = U\frac{b^2 - a^2}{2} + \kappa\left[\frac{(b^2 - a^2)^2}{8} - b^2\left(\frac{b^2}{2}\ln\frac{b}{a} - \frac{b^2 - a^2}{4}\right)\right] $$

and, simplifying $\frac{(b^2 - a^2)^2}{8} + \frac{b^2(b^2 - a^2)}{4} = \frac{(b^2 - a^2)(3b^2 - a^2)}{8}$,

$$ \boxed{\,Q = \pi U\,(b^2 - a^2) + \frac{\pi g}{\nu}\left[\frac{(b^2 - a^2)(3b^2 - a^2)}{8} - \frac{b^4}{2}\ln\frac{b}{a}\right]\,} \tag{11} $$

Setting $Q = 0$ and solving for $U$:

$$ \boxed{\,U_0 = \frac{g}{\nu}\left[\frac{b^4}{2\,(b^2 - a^2)}\ln\frac{b}{a} - \frac{3b^2 - a^2}{8}\right]\,} \tag{12} $$

For $U > U_0$ the cylinder carries a net upward flux; for $U < U_0$ the layer drains downward.

3.6 Temperature distribution and heat flux to the wall

With $v_r = 0$, $\partial T/\partial z = 0$ and the dissipation $\phi_v = \mu\,(dv_z/dr)^2$ (the only non-zero shear is $\tau'_{rz}$), the energy equation (4.42) reduces to

$$ 0 = \phi_v + \frac{k}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right), \qquad \phi_v = \mu\left[\frac{g}{2\nu}\left(r - \frac{b^2}{r}\right)\right]^2 = \frac{\rho^2g^2}{4\mu}\frac{(r^2 - b^2)^2}{r^2} \tag{13} $$

Let $\Lambda = \rho^2g^2/(4\mu k)$. Multiplying by $r/k$ and expanding $(r^2 - b^2)^2 = r^4 - 2b^2r^2 + b^4$:

$$ \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) = -\Lambda\left(r^3 - 2b^2r + \frac{b^4}{r}\right) \tag{14} $$

Integrating once, $r\,dT/dr = -\Lambda\left(\dfrac{r^4}{4} - b^2r^2 + b^4\ln r\right) + D_1$. The adiabatic condition $dT/dr\big|_b = 0$ fixes $D_1$ and gives

$$ r\frac{dT}{dr} = -\Lambda\left[\frac{r^4}{4} - b^2r^2 + \frac{3b^4}{4} + b^4\ln\frac{r}{b}\right] \tag{15} $$

Dividing by $r$ and integrating again (the antiderivative of $\ln(r/b)/r$ is $\tfrac{1}{2}\ln^2(r/b)$) with $T(a) = T_c$:

$$ \boxed{\,T(r) = T_c - \frac{\rho^2g^2}{4\mu k}\left[\frac{r^4 - a^4}{16} - \frac{b^2(r^2 - a^2)}{2} + \frac{3b^4}{4}\ln\frac{r}{a} + \frac{b^4}{2}\left(\ln^2\frac{r}{b} - \ln^2\frac{a}{b}\right)\right]\,} \tag{16} $$

*Heat flux to the wall.* The heat flux from the liquid to the cylinder is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$ (out of the liquid): $q_w = k\,dT/dr\big|_a$, and from (15),

$$ \boxed{\,q_w = -\frac{\rho^2g^2}{4\mu}\left[\frac{a^3}{4} - ab^2 + \frac{3b^4}{4a} - \frac{b^4}{a}\ln\frac{b}{a}\right]\,}, \qquad \dot{Q}_w = 2\pi a\,q_w\ \text{per unit length} \tag{17} $$

*Check.* The outer surface is adiabatic, so all the viscous heating generated in the layer must leave through the wall: $\dot{Q}_w = \int_a^b\phi_v\,2\pi r\,dr$. Evaluating both sides numerically for $\rho = 1000$ kg/m$^3$, $\mu = 0.1$ Pa s, $k = 0.6$ W/(m K), $a = 20$ mm and $h = 5$ mm gives the same value, $6.344$ W/m, to the precision of the integration.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The velocity decreases from $U$ at the wall; the shear stress is largest at the wall and zero at the free surface. The cylinder must support the weight of the layer, which is what (9) says. The pressure plays no role because the free surface is at $p_a$ and the layer is fully developed.
  • Limits. For $h \ll a$ the layer behaves as a flat film: (11) tends to $Q \simeq 2\pi a\left(Uh - gh^3/(3\nu)\right)$ and (12) to $U_0 \simeq gh^2/(3\nu)$. For $h \to 0$ the force and the flux vanish. For $g \to 0$ the profile is uniform, $v_z = U$.
  • Dimensional check. $g/\nu$ has units $(\mathrm{m/s^2})/(\mathrm{m^2/s}) = 1/(\mathrm{m\,s})$; in (7) $(g/\nu)\,r^2$ has units m/s. The force per unit length $\rho g\pi b^2$ has units $(\mathrm{kg/m^3})(\mathrm{m/s^2})\,\mathrm{m^2} = \mathrm{N/m}$. In (16), $\Lambda r^4 = \rho^2g^2r^4/(\mu k)$ has units $(\mathrm{kg^2\,s^{-4}})/(\mathrm{kg^2\,s^{-4}\,K^{-1}}) = \mathrm{K}$, with $\mu k$ in $(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}s^{-1}})(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-3}K^{-1}})$.
  • Heat balance. The wall heat flux (17) equals the viscous dissipation of the layer (per unit area of the wall), because no heat leaves through the free surface.

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity and the radial velocity

$$ \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d(r^2v_r)}{dr} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; r^2v_r = \text{const} = -a^2V_o \tag{1} $$

where the constant is fixed with $v_r(a) = -V_o$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_r(r) = -\frac{a^2V_o}{r^2}\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Trajectory and suction time

A fluid particle moves with the local velocity: $dr/dt = v_r = -a^2V_o/r^2$. Separating variables, $r^2\,dr = -a^2V_o\,dt$. Integrating from $r_i$ at $t = 0$:

$$ \frac{r^3 - r_i^3}{3} = -a^2V_o\,t \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,r(t) = \left(r_i^3 - 3a^2V_o\,t\right)^{1/3}\,} \tag{3} $$

The particle is sucked when $r = a$: $a^3 = r_i^3 - 3a^2V_o\,t_i$, so

$$ \boxed{\,t_i = \frac{r_i^3 - a^3}{3\,a^2V_o}\,} \tag{4} $$

3.3 Pressure distribution

In the $r$ component of (4.41) the viscous term $\mu\,\partial\left[r^{-2}\partial(r^2v_r)/\partial r\right]/\partial r$ vanishes because $r^2v_r$ is constant (1): the flow is irrotational. The rest is the steady Euler equation:

$$ \rho\,v_r\frac{dv_r}{dr} = -\frac{dp}{dr} \;\Longrightarrow\; p + \frac{\rho v_r^2}{2} = \text{const} = p_\infty \tag{5} $$

(the constant is evaluated at infinity, where $v_r \to 0$). Substituting (2):

$$ \boxed{\,p - p_\infty = -\frac{\rho\,a^4V_o^2}{2\,r^4}\,} \tag{6} $$

The pressure is below the ambient value, the minimum being at the sphere surface, where the liquid has the highest speed.

3.4 Temperature distribution

With $v_\theta = v_\phi = 0$ the energy equation (4.42) in spherical coordinates is

$$ \rho c\,v_r\frac{dT}{dr} = \phi_v + \frac{k}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{dT}{dr}\right) \tag{7} $$

The viscous dissipation is $\phi_v = 2\mu\left[(dv_r/dr)^2 + 2(v_r/r)^2\right]$ with $dv_r/dr = 2a^2V_o/r^3$ and $v_r/r = -a^2V_o/r^3$:

$$ \phi_v = 2\mu\left[\frac{4a^4V_o^2}{r^6} + \frac{2a^4V_o^2}{r^6}\right] = \frac{12\mu\,a^4V_o^2}{r^6} \tag{8} $$

Substituting $v_r$ and $\phi_v$ in (7), dividing by $k$ and multiplying by $r^2$:

$$ \frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{dT}{dr}\right) + \beta\frac{dT}{dr} = -\frac{\Gamma}{r^4}, \qquad \beta = \frac{\rho c a^2V_o}{k}, \quad \Gamma = \frac{12\mu a^4V_o^2}{k} \tag{9} $$

*Homogeneous solution.* With $y = T'$: $r^2y' + (2r + \beta)y = 0$; the integrating factor is $\exp\int(2/r + \beta/r^2)\,dr = r^2e^{-\beta/r}$, so $y = D\,e^{\beta/r}/r^2$, and since $\int e^{\beta/r}r^{-2}dr = -e^{\beta/r}/\beta$:

$$ T_h = B_2 + B_1\,e^{\beta/r} \tag{10} $$

*Particular solution.* Work with $y = T_p'$ in the first-order form $r^2y' + (2r + \beta)y = -\Gamma/r^4$. The right-hand side is a power of $1/r$, so try a sum of powers, $y = -\Gamma\left(\dfrac{c_4}{r^4} + \dfrac{c_3}{r^3} + \dfrac{c_2}{r^2}\right)$, and match powers one at a time. The three terms of the equation are $r^2y' = \Gamma\left(\dfrac{4c_4}{r^3} + \dfrac{3c_3}{r^2} + \dfrac{2c_2}{r}\right)$, $2ry = -\Gamma\left(\dfrac{2c_4}{r^3} + \dfrac{2c_3}{r^2} + \dfrac{2c_2}{r}\right)$ and $\beta y = -\Gamma\left(\dfrac{\beta c_4}{r^4} + \dfrac{\beta c_3}{r^3} + \dfrac{\beta c_2}{r^2}\right)$. Their sum is $\Gamma\left[-\dfrac{\beta c_4}{r^4} + \dfrac{2c_4 - \beta c_3}{r^3} + \dfrac{c_3 - \beta c_2}{r^2}\right]$, which must equal $-\Gamma/r^4$: $c_4 = 1/\beta$, $c_3 = 2c_4/\beta = 2/\beta^2$ and $c_2 = c_3/\beta = 2/\beta^3$. Hence

$$ y = T_p' = -\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta r^4} + \frac{2}{\beta^2r^3} + \frac{2}{\beta^3r^2}\right) $$

and integrating $y$ with respect to $r$ (the antiderivative of $r^{-n}$ is $-r^{1-n}/(n-1)$):

$$ T_p(r) = \Gamma\left(\frac{1}{3\beta r^3} + \frac{1}{\beta^2r^2} + \frac{2}{\beta^3r}\right) = \Delta T_d\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^3 + \frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^2 + \frac{2}{\mathrm{Pe}^2}\frac{a}{r}\right] \tag{11} $$

with $\Delta T_d = 12\mu V_o/(\rho c a)$ (use $\Gamma/\beta = 12\mu a^2V_o/(\rho c)$ and $\beta = a\,\mathrm{Pe}$). The general solution is $T = B_2 + B_1e^{\beta/r} + T_p(r)$.

*Boundary conditions.* $T(\infty) = T_\infty$: $e^{\beta/r} \to 1$ and $T_p \to 0$, so $B_2 + B_1 = T_\infty$. $T(a) = T_s$: $B_2 + B_1e^{\mathrm{Pe}} + T_p(a) = T_s$, because $\beta/a = \mathrm{Pe}$. Subtracting,

$$ B_1 = -\frac{T_\infty - T_s + T_p(a)}{e^{\mathrm{Pe}} - 1}, \qquad T_p(a) = \Delta T_d\left(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}} + \frac{2}{\mathrm{Pe}^2}\right) $$
$$ \boxed{\,T(r) = T_\infty + T_p(r) - \left[T_\infty - T_s + T_p(a)\right]\frac{e^{\mathrm{Pe}\,a/r} - 1}{e^{\mathrm{Pe}} - 1}\,} \tag{12} $$
Discrepancy with the official solution

The handwritten solution gets the particular solution $12\mu V_o/(\rho c\,r)$, as read from the handwriting. Its ODE has the dissipation term as $12\mu V_o^2a^2/(k\,r^2)$, but multiplying $\phi_v/k$ of (8) by $r^2$ gives $12\mu a^4V_o^2/(k\,r^4)$, as in (9). The term $A/r$ cannot satisfy (9), whose right-hand side decays as $r^{-4}$: the check above shows that (11) does. The computed result (12) is kept; with $\Delta T_d \to 0$ (negligible dissipation) it reduces to the sheet's result $T = T_\infty + (T_s - T_\infty)\dfrac{e^{\mathrm{Pe}\,a/r} - 1}{e^{\mathrm{Pe}} - 1}$.

3.5 Heat transferred to the sphere

The heat that flows from the liquid into the sphere per unit time is $4\pi a^2\,\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$ (outward from the liquid) and $\bar{q} = -k\nabla T$, that is $\dot{Q}_s = 4\pi a^2k\,dT/dr\big|_a$. From (12),

$$ \frac{dT}{dr}\Big|_a = T_p'(a) + \frac{\mathrm{Pe}}{a}\left[T_\infty - T_s + T_p(a)\right]\frac{e^{\mathrm{Pe}}}{e^{\mathrm{Pe}} - 1}, \qquad T_p'(a) = -\frac{\Delta T_d}{a}\left(1 + \frac{2}{\mathrm{Pe}} + \frac{2}{\mathrm{Pe}^2}\right) \tag{13} $$

Using $4\pi a\,k\,\mathrm{Pe} = 4\pi\rho c\,a^2V_o$ and $e^{\mathrm{Pe}}/(e^{\mathrm{Pe}} - 1) = 1/(1 - e^{-\mathrm{Pe}})$:

$$ \boxed{\,\dot{Q}_s = \frac{4\pi\rho c\,a^2V_o\left[T_\infty - T_s + T_p(a)\right]}{1 - e^{-\mathrm{Pe}}} - 4\pi a\,k\,\Delta T_d\left(1 + \frac{2}{\mathrm{Pe}} + \frac{2}{\mathrm{Pe}^2}\right)\,} \tag{14} $$
Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The suction brings ambient liquid at $T_\infty$ towards the cold sphere, which compresses the thermal layer against the surface: $\beta = a\,\mathrm{Pe}$ is the length over which convection and conduction compete. The temperature field (12) has no solution that is regular at infinity for blowing ($V_o < 0$), where the exponent would change sign.
  • Limits. For negligible dissipation ($\Delta T_d \to 0$): $\dot{Q}_s = 4\pi\rho ca^2V_o\,(T_\infty - T_s)/(1 - e^{-\mathrm{Pe}})$. For $\mathrm{Pe} \gg 1$ this is $\dot{m}\,c\,(T_\infty - T_s)$ with $\dot{m} = 4\pi\rho a^2V_o$ the sucked mass flow: the sphere only has to cool the liquid it takes in. For $\mathrm{Pe} \to 0$ it tends to $4\pi a k\,(T_\infty - T_s)$, pure conduction to a sphere in an unbounded medium.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity

$$ \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{dv_y}{dy} = \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}}}_{v_x = v_x(y)} + \frac{dv_y}{dy} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v_y = \text{const} \tag{1} $$

The wall is impermeable, $v_y(0) = 0$, so

$$ \boxed{\,v_y(y) = 0\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 y-momentum and the pressure field

With $v_y = 0$ and $\partial_x = 0$ for the velocity, the inertial and viscous terms of the $y$ component vanish:

$$ 0 = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} - \rho g\cos\alpha \;\Longrightarrow\; p = -\rho g\cos\alpha\,y + f(x) \tag{3} $$

The free-surface condition $p(x,h) = p_a$ for every $x$ gives $f(x) = p_a + \rho g h\cos\alpha$, a constant:

$$ \boxed{\,p(x,y) = p_a + \rho g\cos\alpha\,(h - y)\,}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 0 \tag{4} $$

3.3 x-momentum and the velocity profile

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{v_x\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{v_y\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}}}_{v_y = 0}\Big) = -\underbrace{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}}_{=0} + \mu\frac{d^2v_x}{dy^2} + \rho g\sin\alpha \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{d^2v_x}{dy^2} = -\frac{\rho g\sin\alpha}{\mu} \tag{5} $$

Integrating twice, $v_x = -\dfrac{\rho g\sin\alpha}{2\mu}y^2 + C_1y + C_2$. The wall condition $v_x(0) = 0$ gives $C_2 = 0$. The free-surface condition $dv_x/dy\big|_h = -\dfrac{\rho g\sin\alpha}{\mu}h + C_1 = 0$ gives $C_1 = \dfrac{\rho g\sin\alpha}{\mu}h$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_x(y) = \frac{\rho g\sin\alpha}{\mu}\left(hy - \frac{y^2}{2}\right) = \frac{gh^2\sin\alpha}{\nu}\left[\frac{y}{h} - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{h}\right)^2\right]\,} \tag{6} $$

3.4 Force per unit surface on the wall

The normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_y$, so $(-p\bar{\bar{I}} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}')\cdot\bar{n}$ is the force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the wall. At $y = 0$, $p = p_a + \rho gh\cos\alpha$ from (4), $\tau'_{xy} = \mu\,dv_x/dy\big|_0 = \rho gh\sin\alpha$ and $\tau'_{yy} = 0$:

$$ \boxed{\,\bar{f}_w = -\left(p_a + \rho gh\cos\alpha\right)\bar{e}_y + \rho gh\sin\alpha\,\bar{e}_x\,} \tag{7} $$

The normal component pushes on the wall with the air pressure plus the hydrostatic pressure of the layer; the tangential component is the shear that drags the wall down the slope.

3.5 Temperature distribution

With $v_y = 0$ and $\partial_x = 0$ the energy equation (4.42) reduces to $0 = \phi_v + k\,d^2T/dy^2$, with $\phi_v = \mu\,(dv_x/dy)^2$ and, from (6), $dv_x/dy = (\rho g\sin\alpha/\mu)(h - y)$:

$$ \phi_v = \frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{\mu}(h - y)^2, \qquad \frac{d^2T}{dy^2} = -\frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{\mu k}(h - y)^2 \tag{8} $$

Integrate once: the antiderivative of $(h - y)^2$ is $-(h - y)^3/3$, so $dT/dy = \dfrac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{3\mu k}(h - y)^3 + B_1$. The adiabatic condition $dT/dy\big|_h = 0$ gives $B_1 = 0$. Integrating again, $T = -\dfrac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{12\mu k}(h - y)^4 + B_2$, and $T(0) = T_w$ gives $B_2 = T_w + \dfrac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2h^4}{12\mu k}$:

$$ \boxed{\,T(y) = T_w + \frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{12\,\mu k}\left[h^4 - (h - y)^4\right]\,} \tag{9} $$

Expanding, $h^4 - (h - y)^4 = y\left(4h^3 - 6h^2y + 4hy^2 - y^3\right)$.

3.6 Heat flux to the wall

The heat flux from the liquid to the wall is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_y$ (out of the liquid) and $\bar{q} = -k\nabla T$:

$$ (\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n})_w = k\frac{dT}{dy}\Big|_{y=0} = k\,\frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2h^3}{3\mu k} = \boxed{\,\frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2h^3}{3\mu}\,} \tag{10} $$

3.7 Energy balances in the fixed control volume $V_o$

*Total viscous dissipation.* The volume $V_o$ has unit length and width and height $h$, so $dV = dy$:

$$ \int_{V_o}\phi_v\,dV = \int_0^h\frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{\mu}(h - y)^2\,dy = \frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2}{\mu}\left[-\frac{(h - y)^3}{3}\right]_0^h = \frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2h^3}{3\mu} \tag{11} $$

*Work of gravity.* $\bar{v}\cdot\bar{g} = v_xg\sin\alpha$ since $v_y = 0$. The antiderivative of $hy - y^2/2$ is $hy^2/2 - y^3/6$ and Barrow's rule gives $h^3/2 - h^3/6 = h^3/3$:

$$ \int_{V_o}\rho\bar{v}\cdot\bar{g}\,dV = \rho g\sin\alpha\int_0^hv_x\,dy = \frac{\rho^2g^2\sin^2\alpha}{\mu}\left[\frac{hy^2}{2} - \frac{y^3}{6}\right]_0^h = \frac{(\rho g\sin\alpha)^2h^3}{3\mu} \tag{12} $$

$$ \boxed{\,v_r(r) = -\frac{RV}{r}\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Radial momentum and the pressure

The viscous term of the $r$ component, $\mu\,\partial\left[r^{-1}\partial(rv_r)/\partial r\right]/\partial r$, is zero because $rv_r$ is constant (1); the terms in $v_\theta$ vanish. What is left is

$$ \rho\,v_r\frac{dv_r}{dr} = -\frac{dp}{dr} \;\Longrightarrow\; p + \frac{\rho v_r^2}{2} = \text{const} = p_\infty \tag{3} $$

(the constant is evaluated at infinity, where $v_r \to 0$):

$$ \boxed{\,p(r) = p_\infty - \frac{\rho R^2V^2}{2\,r^2}\,}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = 0 \tag{4} $$

The pressure depends only on $r$, hence $\partial p/\partial z = 0$.

3.3 Axial momentum and the velocity profile

The $z$ component of (4.41) with $\partial/\partial z = 0$, $v_\theta = 0$, no gravity and $\partial p/\partial z = 0$:

$$ \rho\,v_r\frac{dv_z}{dr} = \mu\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right) \tag{5} $$

Substituting $v_r = -RV/r$ and multiplying by $r/\mu$:

$$ -\frac{\rho RV}{\mu}\frac{dv_z}{dr} = \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right) + \mathrm{Re}\,\frac{dv_z}{dr} = 0 \tag{6} $$

Following the hint of the statement, try $v_z = r^\alpha$: then $r\,dv_z/dr = \alpha r^\alpha$, $\dfrac{d}{dr}(r\,dv_z/dr) = \alpha^2r^{\alpha-1}$ and $\mathrm{Re}\,dv_z/dr = \mathrm{Re}\,\alpha\,r^{\alpha-1}$. Substituting in (6) and dividing by $r^{\alpha-1}$:

$$ \alpha^2 + \mathrm{Re}\,\alpha = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \alpha = 0 \quad\text{or}\quad \alpha = -\mathrm{Re} \tag{7} $$

The two independent solutions are $r^0 = 1$ and $r^{-\mathrm{Re}}$, so $v_z = C_1 + C_2\,r^{-\mathrm{Re}}$. The condition $v_z \to 0$ for $r \to \infty$ requires $C_1 = 0$ (since $r^{-\mathrm{Re}} \to 0$ for $\mathrm{Re} > 0$), and $v_z(R) = U$ gives $C_2 = UR^{\mathrm{Re}}$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_z(r) = U\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\mathrm{Re}}\,} \tag{8} $$

3.4 Force on the cylinder

The normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_r$, so $(-p\bar{\bar{I}} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}')\cdot\bar{n}$ is the force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the cylinder. The pressure and $\tau'_{rr}$ act radially and integrate to zero around the circumference. The axial component is $\tau'_{rz} = \mu\left(\partial v_z/\partial r + \partial v_r/\partial z\right) = \mu\,dv_z/dr$. From (8), $dv_z/dr = -\mathrm{Re}\,U R^{\mathrm{Re}}r^{-\mathrm{Re}-1}$, so at $r = R$:

$$ \tau'_{rz}\Big|_R = -\mu\,\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{U}{R} = -\rho\,V\,U \tag{9} $$

The force per unit length (area $2\pi R$):

$$ \boxed{\,\bar{F} = 2\pi R\,\tau'_{rz}\Big|_R\,\bar{e}_z = -2\pi R\,\rho\,V\,U\,\bar{e}_z\,} \tag{10} $$

The liquid pulls the cylinder back, with the magnitude of the mass flow rate sucked in per unit length, $\dot{m} = 2\pi R\rho V$, times $U$: the cylinder must supply to the entrained liquid the axial momentum $U$ per unit mass, which is zero far away.

3.5 Trajectory of a fluid particle

The particle moves with the local velocity: $dr/dt = v_r = -RV/r$ and $dz/dt = v_z$. For the radial motion, separating variables, $r\,dr = -RV\,dt$ and integrating from $r_o$ at $t = 0$:

$$ \frac{r^2 - r_o^2}{2} = -RV\,t \;\Longrightarrow\; r(t) = \sqrt{r_o^2 - 2RV\,t}, \qquad t_s = \frac{r_o^2 - R^2}{2RV} \tag{11} $$

where $t_s$ is the time at which the particle reaches the surface ($r = R$). The axial coordinate follows from $dz/dr = v_z/v_r$:

$$ \frac{dz}{dr} = -\frac{U}{RV}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\mathrm{Re}}r = -\frac{UR^{\mathrm{Re}-1}}{V}\,r^{1-\mathrm{Re}} \tag{12} $$

Integrating from $r_o$ (where $z = 0$) to $r$ with $\int r^{1-\mathrm{Re}}dr = r^{2-\mathrm{Re}}/(2 - \mathrm{Re})$ for $\mathrm{Re} \neq 2$ and Barrow's rule:

$$ \boxed{\,z = \frac{UR}{V\,(2 - \mathrm{Re})}\left[\left(\frac{r_o}{R}\right)^{2-\mathrm{Re}} - \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2-\mathrm{Re}}\right]\,} \qquad (\mathrm{Re} \neq 2) \tag{13} $$

and, for $\mathrm{Re} = 2$, the integral of $1/r$ gives $z = (UR/V)\ln(r_o/r)$. The particle moves towards the cylinder and is carried along $z$ by the entrained flow; the total axial distance travelled until it reaches the surface is (13) with $r = R$.

3.6 Temperature field

Neglecting the viscous dissipation, the energy equation (4.42) reads

$$ \rho c\,v_r\frac{dT}{dr} = k\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; -\rho c\frac{RV}{r}\frac{dT}{dr} = \frac{k}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) \tag{14} $$

Multiplying by $r/k$ and using $\rho cRV/k = \dfrac{\rho VR}{\mu}\dfrac{\mu c}{k} = \mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}$:

$$ \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) + \mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{dT}{dr} = 0 \tag{15} $$

This is the same ODE as (6) with $\mathrm{Re}$ replaced by $\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}$, so $T = B_1 + B_2\,r^{-\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}}$. The conditions $T(\infty) = T_\infty$ and $T(R) = T_c$ give $B_1 = T_\infty$ and $B_2 = (T_c - T_\infty)R^{\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}}$:

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{T - T_\infty}{T_c - T_\infty} = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}}\,} \tag{16} $$

3.7 Heat transferred to the cylinder

The heat flux from the liquid to the cylinder is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$ (out of the liquid): $q_c = k\,dT/dr\big|_R$. From (16), $dT/dr\big|_R = -\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}\,(T_c - T_\infty)/R = \mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}\,(T_\infty - T_c)/R$. With $k\,\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}/R = \rho cV$ and the area $2\pi R$ per unit length:

$$ \boxed{\,\dot{Q} = 2\pi R\,\rho\,c\,V\,(T_\infty - T_c)\,} \tag{17} $$
Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The suction squeezes the momentum and thermal layers against the cylinder: the velocity decays as $r^{-\mathrm{Re}}$ and the temperature excess as $r^{-\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}}$, with the scales $R/\mathrm{Re}$ and $R/(\mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re})$ close to the wall for $\mathrm{Re} \gg 1$. The thermal layer is thinner than the viscous one when $\mathrm{Pr} > 1$.
  • Limits. For $V \to 0$ ($\mathrm{Re} \to 0$) the exponent vanishes and (8) tends to the uniform velocity $U$, which does not decay at infinity: a steady solution that vanishes far away needs the suction. For $U = 0$ there is no axial motion, the force vanishes and (17) remains: the heat is carried by the entrained liquid.
  • Dimensional check. $\mathrm{Re} = \rho VR/\mu$ is dimensionless: $(\mathrm{kg/m^3})(\mathrm{m/s})\,\mathrm{m}/(\mathrm{Pa\,s}) = 1$. $\rho VU$ in (9) has units $\mathrm{kg/(m^2\,s)}\cdot\mathrm{m/s} = \mathrm{N/m^2}$, and the force per unit length (10) has units N/m. In (17), $\rho cV\,\Delta T$ is W/m$^2$, times $2\pi R$: W/m.
  • Momentum check. On a cylindrical surface of any radius $r$ the axial momentum flux is $2\pi r\,\rho v_rv_z = -2\pi\rho RV\,v_z(r)$ and the viscous force is $2\pi r\,\mu\,dv_z/dr = -2\pi\mu\,\mathrm{Re}\,v_z(r) = -2\pi\rho RV\,v_z(r)$, using $r\,dv_z/dr = -\mathrm{Re}\,v_z$ from (8): the two are equal at every $r$, and at $r = R$ the viscous force is (10).

Official Problem Statement

Consider the steady buoyancy-free motion of a perfect liquid of density $\rho$, viscosity $\mu$, thermal conductivity $k$, and specific heat $c$, induced by an infinitely long porous cylinder of radius $R$ that moves with a constant velocity $U_w$ parallel to its axis, while a constant flow rate $q = 2\pi RV_w$ per unit length is uniformly sucked in the radial direction through the porous wall of the cylinder. Far from the cylinder, $r \to \infty$, the fluid is at rest, at pressure $p_\infty$ and temperature $T_\infty$.

To solve the problem, use a cylindrical coordinate system $(r, \theta, z)$, where $z$ is the axis of the cylinder, and assume that the velocity field is axisymmetric, $\partial\bar{v}/\partial\theta = 0$, as well as fully developed, $\partial\bar{v}/\partial z = 0$, thus $\bar{v} = v_r(r)\bar{e}_r + v_z(r)\bar{e}_z$.

1. Using continuity, determine the velocity component $v_r(r)$. (1 point)

2. Obtain the pressure field $p(r, z)$ by integration of the $r$-momentum equation, demonstrating in particular that $\partial p/\partial z = 0$. (2 points)

3. Using the $z$-momentum equation, obtain the axial velocity distribution, $v_z(r)$. (2 points)

4. Compute the trajectory of a particle initially placed at $r = R_i > R$ and $z = z_i$, as well as the corresponding sucking time, $t_s$. (1 point)

5. Calculate the force per unit length that the liquid exerts on the cylinder. (1 point)

6. Write the differential equation that determines the temperature in the liquid $T(r)$. Obtain the function $T(r)$, assuming that the wall is at constant uniform temperature $T_w$. (2 points)

7. Obtain the conductive heat flux per unit length from the liquid to the wall $q_w$. (1 point)

8. Show that there is no steady solution if fluid is blown through the wall, instead of being sucked. (1 extra point)

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity (item 1)

$$ \frac{1}{r}\frac{d(rv_r)}{dr} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta}}}_{v_\theta = 0} + \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z}}}_{\partial_z = 0} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; r\,v_r = \text{const} = -RV_w \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,v_r(r) = -\frac{RV_w}{r} = -\frac{q}{2\pi r}\,} \tag{1} $$

3.2 Radial momentum and the pressure (item 2)

The viscous term of the $r$ component vanishes because $rv_r$ is constant, and the $\theta$ and $z$ terms are zero:

$$ \rho\,v_r\frac{dv_r}{dr} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} \;\Longrightarrow\; p + \frac{\rho v_r^2}{2} = f(z) \tag{2} $$

where integration at fixed $z$ leaves a function of $z$. The condition $p \to p_\infty$ for $r \to \infty$ (where $v_r \to 0$) holds for every $z$, so $f(z) = p_\infty$ is a constant:

$$ \boxed{\,p(r) = p_\infty - \frac{\rho R^2V_w^2}{2r^2}\,}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = 0 \tag{3} $$

3.3 Axial momentum (item 3)

The $z$ component of (4.41), with $\partial_z = 0$, $v_\theta = 0$, no mass force and $\partial p/\partial z = 0$, is $\rho v_r\,dv_z/dr = \mu\,r^{-1}d(r\,dv_z/dr)/dr$. Substituting (1) and multiplying by $r/\mu$:

$$ \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right) + \mathrm{Re}\,\frac{dv_z}{dr} = 0 \tag{4} $$

Try $v_z = r^\alpha$: $\alpha^2 + \mathrm{Re}\,\alpha = 0$, so $\alpha = 0$ or $\alpha = -\mathrm{Re}$ and $v_z = C_1 + C_2r^{-\mathrm{Re}}$. The condition at infinity gives $C_1 = 0$ and $v_z(R) = U_w$ gives $C_2 = U_wR^{\mathrm{Re}}$:

$$ \boxed{\,v_z(r) = U_w\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\mathrm{Re}}\,} \tag{5} $$

3.4 Trajectory and sucking time (item 4)

The velocity of the particle is the local velocity. Radial motion: $dr/dt = -RV_w/r$, so $r\,dr = -RV_w\,dt$; integrating from $R_i$ at $t = 0$, $(r^2 - R_i^2)/2 = -RV_wt$:

$$ r(t) = \sqrt{R_i^2 - 2RV_wt}, \qquad \boxed{\,t_s = \frac{R_i^2 - R^2}{2RV_w}\,} \tag{6} $$

Axial motion: $dz/dr = v_z/v_r = -\dfrac{U_wR^{\mathrm{Re}-1}}{V_w}r^{1-\mathrm{Re}}$. Integrating from $R_i$ (where $z = z_i$), with $\int r^{1-\mathrm{Re}}dr = r^{2-\mathrm{Re}}/(2 - \mathrm{Re})$ for $\mathrm{Re} \neq 2$:

$$ \boxed{\,z(r) = z_i + \frac{U_wR}{V_w\,(2 - \mathrm{Re})}\left[\left(\frac{R_i}{R}\right)^{2-\mathrm{Re}} - \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2-\mathrm{Re}}\right]\,} \qquad (\mathrm{Re} \neq 2) \tag{7} $$

For $\mathrm{Re} = 2$, $z = z_i + (U_wR/V_w)\ln(R_i/r)$. The axial position at which the particle is sucked is (7) with $r = R$.

3.5 Force on the cylinder (item 5)

The normal pointing into the liquid is $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_r$; the pressure and the radial normal stress act radially and integrate to zero around the circumference. The axial shear stress is $\tau'_{rz} = \mu\,dv_z/dr$, and from (5), $dv_z/dr\big|_R = -\mathrm{Re}\,U_w/R$:

$$ \tau'_{rz}\Big|_R = -\mu\,\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{U_w}{R} = -\rho V_wU_w, \qquad \boxed{\,\bar{F} = 2\pi R\,\tau'_{rz}\Big|_R\,\bar{e}_z = -\rho\,q\,U_w\,\bar{e}_z\,} \tag{8} $$

The force per unit length equals minus the axial momentum given per unit time to the sucked liquid, $\rho q\,U_w$.

3.6 Temperature: the equation (item 6)

The energy equation (4.42) with $v_r = -RV_w/r$, $\partial_z = \partial_\theta = 0$ and the dissipation of the cylindrical expression (Notes.pdf) keeping the non-zero terms, $\phi_v = \mu\left\{2\left[(dv_r/dr)^2 + (v_r/r)^2\right] + (dv_z/dr)^2\right\}$:

$$ \rho c\,v_r\frac{dT}{dr} = \phi_v + \frac{k}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right), \qquad \phi_v = \mu\left[\frac{4R^2V_w^2}{r^4} + \frac{\mathrm{Re}^2U_w^2}{r^2}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{2\mathrm{Re}}\right] \tag{9} $$

since $dv_r/dr = RV_w/r^2$ and $v_r/r = -RV_w/r^2$. Multiplying by $r/k$ and using $\rho cRV_w/k = \Pi$:

$$ \frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) + \Pi\,\frac{dT}{dr} = -\frac{\mu}{k}\left[\frac{4R^2V_w^2}{r^3} + \frac{\mathrm{Re}^2U_w^2R^{2\mathrm{Re}}}{r^{2\mathrm{Re}+1}}\right] \tag{10} $$

with the boundary conditions $T(R) = T_w$ and $T(\infty) = T_\infty$.

3.7 Temperature: the solution (item 6)

Write $y = dT/dr$, so that (10) is $r\,y' + (1 + \Pi)\,y = -S_1r^{-3} - S_2r^{-2\mathrm{Re}-1}$ with $S_1 = 4\mu R^2V_w^2/k$ and $S_2 = \mu\,\mathrm{Re}^2U_w^2R^{2\mathrm{Re}}/k$. The homogeneous solution is $y = D\,r^{-1-\Pi}$ (substitute $r^{s}$: $s + 1 + \Pi = 0$). For each source term try a power of the same form: $y = A_1r^{-3}$ gives $r\,y' = -3A_1r^{-3}$ and $(\Pi - 2)A_1 = -S_1$; $y = A_2r^{-2\mathrm{Re}-1}$ gives $(\Pi - 2\mathrm{Re})A_2 = -S_2$. Hence, for $\Pi \neq 2$ and $\mathrm{Pr} \neq 2$,

$$ A_1 = \frac{S_1}{2 - \Pi}, \qquad A_2 = \frac{S_2}{\mathrm{Re}\,(2 - \mathrm{Pr})} $$

Integrating $y$ ($\int r^{-n}dr = -r^{1-n}/(n-1)$) and writing $\mu/k = \mathrm{Pr}/c$:

$$ T(r) = B - a_1\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^2 - a_2\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{2\mathrm{Re}} + E\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\Pi}, \qquad a_1 = \frac{2\,\mathrm{Pr}\,V_w^2}{c\,(2 - \Pi)}, \quad a_2 = \frac{\mathrm{Pr}\,U_w^2}{2c\,(2 - \mathrm{Pr})} \tag{11} $$

For $r \to \infty$ every power of $R/r$ vanishes, so $T(\infty) = T_\infty$ gives $B = T_\infty$; $T(R) = T_w$ gives $E = T_w - T_\infty + a_1 + a_2$:

$$ \boxed{\,T(r) = T_\infty - a_1\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^2 - a_2\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{2\mathrm{Re}} + \left(T_w - T_\infty + a_1 + a_2\right)\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\Pi}\,} \tag{12} $$

The terms $a_1$ and $a_2$ are the viscous heating by the radial and by the axial deformation. The cases $\Pi = 2$ and $\mathrm{Pr} = 2$ (resonance of the exponents) give logarithmic terms and are not worked out here.

3.8 Heat flux to the wall (item 7)

The heat flux from the liquid to the wall is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$: $k\,dT/dr\big|_R$. Differentiating (12) and setting $r = R$:

$$ \frac{dT}{dr}\Big|_R = \frac{1}{R}\left[2a_1 + 2\mathrm{Re}\,a_2 - \Pi\left(T_w - T_\infty + a_1 + a_2\right)\right] = \frac{1}{R}\left[\Pi\,(T_\infty - T_w) + (2 - \Pi)a_1 + (2\mathrm{Re} - \Pi)a_2\right] $$

with $(2 - \Pi)a_1 = 2\mathrm{Pr}\,V_w^2/c$ and $(2\mathrm{Re} - \Pi)a_2 = \mathrm{Re}(2 - \mathrm{Pr})a_2 = \mathrm{Pr}\,\mathrm{Re}\,U_w^2/(2c)$. Multiplying by $k$ and using $k\,\Pi/R = \rho cV_w$, $k\,\mathrm{Pr}/c = \mu$ and $\mu\,\mathrm{Re}/R = \rho V_w$:

$$ k\frac{dT}{dr}\Big|_R = \rho cV_w\,(T_\infty - T_w) + \frac{2\mu V_w^2}{R} + \frac{\rho V_wU_w^2}{2} $$

and, multiplying by the area per unit length $2\pi R$,

$$ \boxed{\,q_w = 2\pi R\rho V_w\left[c\,(T_\infty - T_w) + \frac{U_w^2}{2}\right] + 4\pi\mu V_w^2\,} \tag{13} $$

3.9 Blowing instead of suction (item 8)

If the liquid is blown, $V_w < 0$ and $\mathrm{Re} < 0$. Continuity still gives $v_r = -RV_w/r = R\lvert V_w\rvert/r > 0$, and the equation for $v_z$ is (4) with $\mathrm{Re} < 0$: $v_z = C_1 + C_2\,r^{\lvert\mathrm{Re}\rvert}$. The condition $v_z \to 0$ for $r \to \infty$ requires $C_2 = 0$ (the power grows) and then $C_1 = 0$, so $v_z(R) = U_w$ cannot be satisfied unless $U_w = 0$. Likewise $T = B + D\,r^{-\Pi}$ with $\Pi < 0$ is unbounded unless $D = 0$, which cannot satisfy $T(R) = T_w \neq T_\infty$. Therefore there is no steady solution with the fluid at rest and at $T_\infty$ far away: the blown liquid carries momentum and heat outward and they cannot be balanced by diffusion towards the cylinder.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. Suction confines the axial momentum to a layer of thickness of order $R/\mathrm{Re}$ and the thermal layer to $R/\Pi$: the thermal layer is thinner than the momentum layer when $\mathrm{Pr} > 1$. The cylinder pays for the entrained momentum with the force (8), and the wall receives the enthalpy, kinetic-energy and viscous-heating terms of (13).
  • Limits. For $U_w = 0$, $a_2 = 0$ and $E$ contains only $a_1$; for negligible dissipation ($a_1 = a_2 = 0$) the solution is $T = T_\infty + (T_w - T_\infty)(R/r)^{\Pi}$ and $q_w = 2\pi R\rho cV_w(T_\infty - T_w)$, the result of NS8. For $V_w \to 0$ the axial profile is uniform and there is no steady solution that vanishes at infinity.
  • Dimensional check. $\mathrm{Re} = \rho V_wR/\mu$ is dimensionless. $\rho V_wU_w$ has units N/m$^2$. $a_1$ and $a_2$ have units $\mathrm{Pr}\,V_w^2/c = \mathrm{m^2/s^2}/(\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}) = \mathrm{K}$. In (13) every term is W/m: $\rho RV_wc\,\Delta T = (\mathrm{kg/m^3})\,\mathrm{m\cdot m/s\cdot J/(kg\,K)\cdot K} = \mathrm{W/m}$ and $\mu V_w^2 = (\mathrm{Pa\,s})(\mathrm{m^2/s^2}) = \mathrm{W/m}$.
  • Energy check. A steady total-energy balance on the liquid between the wall and infinity gives the same result (13): the energy flux $\rho q\,cT_\infty$ enters at infinity; through the wall leave $\rho q\,[cT_w + (U_w^2 + V_w^2)/2]$ and the pressure work $p(R)\,q$ with $p(R) = p_\infty - \rho V_w^2/2$; the viscous stresses at the wall do the work $-4\pi\mu V_w^2 - \rho q\,U_w^2$, and the wall receives $q_w$. The radial kinetic energy cancels against the pressure drop, and what remains is (13).

Underwater Explosion Cavity

Official Problem Statement

As a consequence of an underwater explosion, a spherical cavity of initial radius $R_0$ and initial radial velocity $V_0$ is created inside the ocean. The growth of the cavity induces a flow in the surrounding liquid, whose density $\rho$ and viscosity $\mu$ can be considered constant. It is also assumed that the effect of gravity is negligible, and that the motion is sphericosymmetric, $\bar{v} = v_r(r, t)\,\bar{e}_r$, where $R_c(t) \leq r < \infty$, being $R_c(t)$ the radius of the cavity at time $t$. The liquid is at rest far from the cavity, $r \to \infty$, where the pressure is $p_\infty$, while the pressure inside the cavity can be neglected compared to $p_\infty$.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Velocity and irrotationality (item 1)

$$ \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial(r^2v_r)}{\partial r} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; r^2v_r = A(t), \qquad v_r(R_c,t) = \dot{R}_c \;\Longrightarrow\; A(t) = R_c^2\dot{R}_c \tag{1} $$
$$ \boxed{\,v_r(r,t) = \frac{R_c^2\dot{R}_c}{r^2}\,} \tag{2} $$

The vorticity of $\bar{v} = v_r(r,t)\bar{e}_r$, from the curl in spherical coordinates, has the components $\omega_r = \dfrac{1}{r\sin\theta}\left[\dfrac{\partial(v_\phi\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta} - \dfrac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\phi}\right] = 0$, $\omega_\theta = \dfrac{1}{r}\left[\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\dfrac{\partial v_r}{\partial\phi} - \dfrac{\partial(rv_\phi)}{\partial r}\right] = 0$ and $\omega_\phi = \dfrac{1}{r}\left[\dfrac{\partial(rv_\theta)}{\partial r} - \dfrac{\partial v_r}{\partial\theta}\right] = 0$, because $v_\theta = v_\phi = 0$ and $v_r$ does not depend on $\theta$ or $\phi$. Hence $\nabla\wedge\bar{v} = 0$: the flow is irrotational, and therefore $\nabla\wedge(\nabla\wedge\bar{v}) = 0$.

3.2 Pressure field (item 2)

The viscous force of the incompressible flow is $-\mu\nabla\wedge(\nabla\wedge\bar{v}) = 0$, so the $r$ component of (4.41) is the Euler equation:

$$ \rho\left(\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial t} + v_r\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r}\right) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} \tag{3} $$

With $\partial v_r/\partial t = (R_c^2\ddot{R}_c + 2R_c\dot{R}_c^2)/r^2$ (product rule on $R_c^2\dot{R}_c$) and $v_r\,\partial v_r/\partial r = -2R_c^4\dot{R}_c^2/r^5$:

$$ \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = -\rho\left[\frac{R_c^2\ddot{R}_c + 2R_c\dot{R}_c^2}{r^2} - \frac{2R_c^4\dot{R}_c^2}{r^5}\right] \tag{4} $$

Integrating from $r$ to $\infty$ with $p(\infty) = p_\infty$ (the antiderivatives are $-1/r$ and $-1/(4r^4)$, and Barrow's rule gives $1/r$ and $1/(4r^4)$):

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{p(r,t) - p_\infty}{\rho} = \frac{R_c^2\ddot{R}_c + 2R_c\dot{R}_c^2}{r} - \frac{R_c^4\dot{R}_c^2}{2r^4}\,} \tag{5} $$

3.3 Equation at the cavity wall (item 3)

At $r = R_c(t)$, (5) gives $\dfrac{p_R - p_\infty}{\rho} = R_c\ddot{R}_c + 2\dot{R}_c^2 - \dfrac{\dot{R}_c^2}{2}$:

$$ \boxed{\,R_c\ddot{R}_c + \frac{3}{2}\dot{R}_c^2 = \frac{p_R(t) - p_\infty}{\rho}, \qquad R_c(0) = R_0,\ \ \dot{R}_c(0) = V_0\,} \tag{6} $$

3.4 Pressure at the wall: null stress (item 4)

The unit normal to the cavity wall, pointing into the liquid, is $\bar{n} = \bar{e}_r$. The stress vector is $\bar{\bar{\tau}}\cdot\bar{e}_r = (-p + \tau'_{rr})\bar{e}_r + \tau'_{r\theta}\bar{e}_\theta + \tau'_{r\phi}\bar{e}_\phi$. For this flow $\tau'_{r\theta} = \tau'_{r\phi} = 0$ and (Notes.pdf, stress components in spherical coordinates)

$$ \tau'_{rr} = 2\mu\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} = -\frac{4\mu R_c^2\dot{R}_c}{r^3}, \qquad \tau'_{rr}\Big|_{R_c} = -\frac{4\mu\dot{R}_c}{R_c} \tag{7} $$

The null-stress condition at $r = R_c$ gives $-p_R + \tau'_{rr}(R_c) = 0$:

$$ \boxed{\,p_R(t) = -\frac{4\mu\dot{R}_c}{R_c}\,} \tag{8} $$

3.5 Closed ODE and non-dimensionalization (item 5)

Substituting (8) in (6) with $\nu = \mu/\rho$:

$$ R_c\ddot{R}_c + \frac{3}{2}\dot{R}_c^2 + \frac{4\nu\dot{R}_c}{R_c} = -\frac{p_\infty}{\rho} \tag{9} $$

With $R_c = R_0R$ and $t = R_0\tau/V_0$: $\dot{R}_c = V_0\,R'$ and $\ddot{R}_c = (V_0^2/R_0)R''$ (chain rule, $d/dt = (V_0/R_0)\,d/d\tau$, with $R' = dR/d\tau$). Substituting and dividing by $V_0^2$:

$$ \boxed{\,R\,R'' + \frac{3}{2}R'^2 + \frac{4}{\mathrm{Re}}\frac{R'}{R} = -\Lambda, \qquad R(0) = 1,\ \ R'(0) = 1\,} \tag{10} $$

with $\mathrm{Re} = \rho V_0R_0/\mu$ (because $\nu/(V_0R_0) = 1/\mathrm{Re}$) and $\Lambda = p_\infty/(\rho V_0^2)$. The problem depends only on these two parameters.

3.6 Limit $\mathrm{Re} \to \infty$ (item 6)

The viscous term drops out: $R\,R'' + \tfrac{3}{2}R'^2 = -\Lambda$. With $z = \ln R$ and $F = (dR/d\tau)^2$, the chain rule gives $dF/d\tau = 2R'R''$ and $dz/d\tau = R'/R$, so

$$ \frac{dF}{dz} = \frac{dF/d\tau}{dz/d\tau} = 2R\,R'' \tag{11} $$

and the equation becomes $\tfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{dF}{dz} + \tfrac{3}{2}F = -\Lambda$, that is $\dfrac{dF}{dz} + 3F = -2\Lambda$ with $F(0) = R'(0)^2 = 1$ (at $\tau = 0$, $R = 1$ and $z = 0$). Multiplying by the integrating factor $e^{3z}$: $(F\,e^{3z})' = -2\Lambda e^{3z}$, so $F\,e^{3z} = -\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}e^{3z} + C$ and $F(0) = 1$ gives $C = 1 + 2\Lambda/3$:

$$ F(z) = e^{-3z} + \frac{2\Lambda}{3}\left(e^{-3z} - 1\right) \tag{12} $$

With $e^{-3z} = R^{-3}$:

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{dR}{d\tau} = \left[R^{-3} + \frac{2\Lambda}{3}\left(R^{-3} - 1\right)\right]^{1/2}\,} \tag{13} $$

(positive root while the cavity expands).

3.7 Maximum radius and time to reach it (item 7)

The cavity stops growing when $dR/d\tau = 0$: $R_{\max}^{-3}(1 + 2\Lambda/3) = 2\Lambda/3$, that is

$$ \boxed{\,R_{\max} = \left(1 + \frac{3}{2\Lambda}\right)^{1/3}\,} \tag{14} $$

Separating variables in (13), $d\tau = dR/\sqrt{R^{-3} + \tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}(R^{-3} - 1)}$, and integrating from $R = 1$ at $\tau = 0$ to $R_{\max}$ at $\tau_{\max}$ (with the dummy variable $x$):

$$ \boxed{\,\tau_{\max}(\Lambda) = \int_1^{(1 + 3/(2\Lambda))^{1/3}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^{-3} + \frac{2\Lambda}{3}\left(x^{-3} - 1\right)}}\,} \tag{15} $$

*Approximation for $\Lambda \ll 1$.* Then $R_{\max} \simeq (3/(2\Lambda))^{1/3} \gg 1$. Factor $x^{-3}$ out of the square root: the integrand is $x^{3/2}\big/\sqrt{(1 + 2\Lambda/3) - (2\Lambda/3)x^3}$ and, with $2\Lambda/3 \simeq R_{\max}^{-3}$ for small $\Lambda$,

$$ \tau_{\max} \simeq \int_1^{R_{\max}}\frac{x^{3/2}\,dx}{\sqrt{1 - (x/R_{\max})^3}} \simeq R_{\max}^{5/2}\int_0^1\frac{s^{3/2}\,ds}{\sqrt{1 - s^3}} \tag{16} $$

(with $x = R_{\max}s$, and the lower limit $s = 1/R_{\max} \to 0$). The substitution $u = s^3$ gives $s^{3/2}ds = \tfrac{1}{3}u^{-1/6}du$, so the integral is $\tfrac{1}{3}B(\tfrac{5}{6},\tfrac{1}{2}) = \dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(5/6)}{3\,\Gamma(4/3)} \simeq 0.747$:

$$ \boxed{\,\tau_{\max} \simeq 0.747\left(\frac{3}{2\Lambda}\right)^{5/6} = 0.747\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{5/6}\Lambda^{-5/6}\,} \qquad (\Lambda \ll 1) \tag{17} $$
Discrepancy with the official solution

The handwritten solution approximates the integrand by $x^{3/2}$ over the whole range and obtains $\tau_{\max} \simeq \tfrac{2}{5}(3/2)^{5/6}\Lambda^{-5/6}$, which neglects the factor $(1 - x^3/R_{\max}^3)^{-1/2}$ of (16). Integrating (15) numerically gives $\tau_{\max} = 48.3,\ 330.8,\ 2255.4$ for $\Lambda = 10^{-2},\ 10^{-3},\ 10^{-4}$, while (17) gives $48.6,\ 331.1,\ 2255.8$ and the sheet's expression $26.0,\ 177.3,\ 1208.2$. The scaling $\Lambda^{-5/6}$ is the same; the computed coefficient $0.747$ is kept.

3.8 Limit $\mathrm{Re} \to 0$ (item 8)

For $\mathrm{Re} \to 0$ the viscous term dominates the inertial terms and (10) reduces to the first-order ODE

$$ \frac{4}{\mathrm{Re}}\frac{R'}{R} \simeq -\Lambda \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{dR}{d\tau} = -\frac{\Lambda\,\mathrm{Re}}{4}\,R \tag{18} $$

whose solution with $R(0) = 1$ is

$$ R(\tau) \simeq \exp\left(-\frac{\Lambda\,\mathrm{Re}}{4}\,\tau\right) \tag{19} $$

It is not possible to satisfy both initial conditions: a first-order ODE admits only one, and (19) has $R'(0) = -\Lambda\mathrm{Re}/4 \neq 1$. The approximation is valid only after an initial transient, of duration of the order of $\mathrm{Re}$ in $\tau$, during which the inertial terms $R\,R'' + \tfrac{3}{2}R'^2$ cannot be neglected and the initial velocity is dissipated by viscosity. In this limit the ambient pressure $p_\infty$ crushes the cavity, which then collapses exponentially instead of expanding.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. (6) is the Rayleigh equation for the cavity with the viscous correction (8): the cavity decelerates because of the ambient pressure $p_\infty$ (term $\Lambda$) and the viscous normal stress (term $4/(\mathrm{Re}\,R)$). For $\mathrm{Re} \to \infty$ the cavity stops at $R_{\max}$ and then collapses; for $\mathrm{Re} \to 0$ viscosity dominates.
  • Limits. For $\Lambda \to 0$ the right-hand side of (13) is $R^{-3/2}$ with no stopping point: the cavity expands for ever in the inviscid, pressureless case. For $\Lambda \to \infty$, $R_{\max} \to 1$: the cavity cannot expand.
  • Dimensional check. $R_c\ddot{R}_c$ and $\dot{R}_c^2$ have units $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, as does $p_\infty/\rho$ (Pa/(kg/m$^3$)). $4\nu\dot{R}_c/R_c$ has units $(\mathrm{m^2/s})(\mathrm{m/s})/\mathrm{m} = \mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. $\mathrm{Re}$ and $\Lambda$ are dimensionless. $p_R = -4\mu\dot{R}_c/R_c$ has units Pa.
  • Consistency. At $R = 1$ the bracket of (13) equals $1$ for every $\Lambda$, so $R'(0) = 1$: the solution keeps the initial velocity $V_0$.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Continuity: $v_u$ and $v(y)$ (item 1)

$$ \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} + \frac{dv}{dy} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v = \text{const} \tag{1} $$

The constant is the injection velocity, $v(0) = v_l$, so $v = v_l$ for every $y$ and the removal velocity must be the same:

$$ \boxed{\,v_u = v_l, \qquad v(y) = v_l\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Pressure and horizontal velocity (item 2)

y-momentum, with $v = v_l$ constant ($dv/dy = 0$) and no gravity:

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{u\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} + v\underbrace{\frac{dv}{dy}}_{=0}\Big) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} + \mu\underbrace{\frac{d^2v}{dy^2}}_{=0} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; p = p(x) \tag{3} $$

The pressure on the upper wall is $p_u$ for every $x$, so $p$ is the constant $p_u$:

$$ \boxed{\,p(x,y) = p_u\ \ \text{for all } x,\,y\,}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 0 \tag{4} $$

x-momentum:

$$ \rho\Big(\underbrace{\cancel{u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}}}_{\partial_x = 0} + v_l\frac{du}{dy}\Big) = -\underbrace{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}}_{=0} + \mu\frac{d^2u}{dy^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{d^2u}{dy^2} - \frac{\mathrm{Re}}{h}\frac{du}{dy} = 0 \tag{5} $$

since $\rho v_l/\mu = \mathrm{Re}/h$. Trying $u = e^{\lambda y}$ gives $\lambda(\lambda - \mathrm{Re}/h) = 0$, so $u = C_1 + C_2\,e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h}$. The conditions $u(0) = 0$ and $u(h) = U$ give $C_1 = -C_2$ and $C_2(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1) = U$:

$$ \boxed{\,u(y) = U\,\frac{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h} - 1}{e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1}\,} \tag{6} $$

For $\mathrm{Re} \to 0$, the Taylor expansion $e^x = 1 + x + O(x^2)$ gives $u \to U\,\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h}{\mathrm{Re}} = U\,\dfrac{y}{h}$: plane Couette flow is recovered.

3.3 Volume flux (item 3)

The flux per unit spanwise length through a section $x = \text{const}$ is $Q = \int_0^hu\,dy$. The antiderivative of $e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h} - 1$ is $(h/\mathrm{Re})\,e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h} - y$, and Barrow's rule between $0$ and $h$ gives $(h/\mathrm{Re})(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1) - h$:

$$ \boxed{\,Q = \frac{U}{e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1}\left[\frac{h}{\mathrm{Re}}\left(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1\right) - h\right] = Uh\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} - \frac{1}{e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1}\right]\,} \tag{7} $$

For $\mathrm{Re} \ll 1$, $e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1 = \mathrm{Re}\left(1 + \mathrm{Re}/2 + \mathrm{Re}^2/6 + \dots\right)$, so $1/(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1) = 1/\mathrm{Re} - 1/2 + \mathrm{Re}/12 + \dots$ and

$$ Q \simeq Uh\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{\mathrm{Re}}{12}\right) \to \frac{Uh}{2} \tag{8} $$

The flux tends to that of plane Couette flow, the mean velocity $U/2$ times the gap: for small injection velocity the transverse flow barely distorts the linear profile.

3.4 Trajectory and pathline (item 4)

A particle moves with the local velocity: $dy/dt = v_l$ and $dx/dt = u(y)$. Let its initial position be $(x_i, y_i)$ at $t = 0$. Then $y = y_i + v_lt$ and, with $dt = dy/v_l$,

$$ x - x_i = \int_0^tu\,dt' = \frac{U}{v_l\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\int_{y_i}^y\left(e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y'/h} - 1\right)dy' = \frac{U}{v_l\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\left[\frac{h}{\mathrm{Re}}\left(e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h} - e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y_i/h}\right) - (y - y_i)\right] \tag{9} $$

which is the pathline $x(y)$; with $y = y_i + v_lt$ it is also the trajectory $x(t)$. The particle reaches the upper wall at $y = h$:

$$ t_s = \frac{h - y_i}{v_l}, \qquad x_s = x_i + \frac{U}{v_l\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\left[\frac{h}{\mathrm{Re}}\left(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y_i/h}\right) - (h - y_i)\right] \tag{10} $$
Reading of the initial position

The sheet writes the initial position as $(x, y) = (0, x_0)$; the handwritten solution takes the particle at the lower wall, $(x, y) = (x_0, 0)$. Both follow from (9) and (10): for the printed values $(x_i, y_i) = (0, x_0)$ and for the reading of the key $(x_i, y_i) = (x_0, 0)$, which gives $t_s = h/v_l$.

$$ \boxed{\,x - x_0 = \frac{U}{e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1}\left[\frac{h}{\mathrm{Re}\,v_l}\left(e^{\mathrm{Re}\,v_lt/h} - 1\right) - t\right], \quad t_s = \frac{h}{v_l}, \quad x_s = x_0 + \frac{Uh}{v_l}\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} - \frac{1}{e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1}\right]\,} \qquad (y_i = 0) \tag{11} $$

Note that $x_s - x_0 = Q/v_l$: the particle crosses the channel in the time $h/v_l$ at the mean horizontal velocity $Q/h$.

3.5 Viscous forces on the walls (item 5)

The normal pointing into the liquid is $+\bar{e}_y$ on the lower wall and $-\bar{e}_y$ on the upper wall; $(-p\bar{\bar{I}} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}')\cdot\bar{n}$ is the force per unit area that the liquid exerts on the wall. The viscous components are $\tau'_{xy} = \mu\,du/dy$ and $\tau'_{yy} = 2\mu\,dv/dy = 0$ (Notes.pdf, stress components, Cartesian coordinates). From (6), $du/dy = \dfrac{U\,\mathrm{Re}}{h}\dfrac{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,y/h}}{e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1}$:

$$ \bar{f}_l^{\,\text{visc}} = \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{e}_y = \mu\frac{du}{dy}\Big|_0\bar{e}_x = \frac{\mu U\,\mathrm{Re}}{h\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\bar{e}_x, \qquad \bar{f}_u^{\,\text{visc}} = \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot(-\bar{e}_y) = -\mu\frac{du}{dy}\Big|_h\bar{e}_x = -\frac{\mu U\,\mathrm{Re}\,e^{\mathrm{Re}}}{h\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\bar{e}_x \tag{12} $$
$$ \boxed{\,\bar{f}_l = \frac{\mu U\,\mathrm{Re}}{h\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\,\bar{e}_x, \qquad \bar{f}_u = -\frac{\mu U\,\mathrm{Re}\,e^{\mathrm{Re}}}{h\,(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1)}\,\bar{e}_x\,} \tag{13} $$

For $\mathrm{Re} \ll 1$, $\mathrm{Re}/(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1) \to 1$ and $\mathrm{Re}\,e^{\mathrm{Re}}/(e^{\mathrm{Re}} - 1) \to 1$: $\bar{f}_l \to \dfrac{\mu U}{h}\bar{e}_x$ and $\bar{f}_u \to -\dfrac{\mu U}{h}\bar{e}_x$, the equal and opposite shear forces of plane Couette flow. For $\mathrm{Re} \neq 0$ they are not equal: the lower wall, where the injected liquid has no horizontal velocity, feels less shear than the upper one.

3.6 Temperature (item 6)

The energy equation (4.42) with $v = v_l$ and $\partial_x = 0$, and the boundary conditions, are

$$ \rho c\,v_l\frac{dT}{dy} = \mu\left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)^2 + k\frac{d^2T}{dy^2}, \qquad T(0) = T_l, \quad T(h) = T_u \tag{14} $$

where $\phi_v = \mu(du/dy)^2$ is the viscous dissipation. Neglecting it, and dividing by $k$ with $\rho cv_lh/k = \mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}$:

$$ \frac{d^2T}{dy^2} - \frac{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}}{h}\frac{dT}{dy} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; T = B_1 + B_2\,e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}\,y/h} \tag{15} $$

the same equation as (5) with $\mathrm{Re}$ replaced by $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}$. The conditions $T(0) = T_l$ and $T(h) = T_u$ give $B_1 + B_2 = T_l$ and $B_2(e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}} - 1) = T_u - T_l$:

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{T - T_l}{T_u - T_l} = \frac{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}\,y/h} - 1}{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}} - 1}\,} \tag{16} $$

3.7 Heat flux to each wall (item 7)

The heat flux from the liquid to a wall is $\bar{q}\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n}$ pointing out of the liquid and $\bar{q} = -k\nabla T$. From (16), $dT/dy = (T_u - T_l)\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}}{h}\dfrac{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}\,y/h}}{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}} - 1}$, and $k\,\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}/h = \rho cv_l$:

$$ \boxed{\,q_u = -k\frac{dT}{dy}\Big|_h = -\rho cv_l\,(T_u - T_l)\,\frac{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}}}{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}} - 1}, \qquad q_l = k\frac{dT}{dy}\Big|_0 = \rho cv_l\,(T_u - T_l)\,\frac{1}{e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}} - 1}\,} \tag{17} $$

For $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr} \to 0$, $q_l \to k(T_u - T_l)/h$ and $q_u \to -k(T_u - T_l)/h$: pure conduction between the walls.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The injection convects momentum and heat across the channel: for $\mathrm{Re} > 0$ the profiles are exponential, with the velocity gradient concentrated near the upper wall (towards which the liquid is carried), and the thermal profile has the same form with $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}$.
  • Limits. For $\mathrm{Re} \to 0$: $u = Uy/h$, $Q = Uh/2$, $\bar{f}_l = -\bar{f}_u = \mu U/h\,\bar{e}_x$ and the temperature is linear (Couette flow with conduction). For $\mathrm{Re} \gg 1$, $u \simeq U\,e^{\mathrm{Re}\,(y - h)/h}$: the horizontal velocity is confined to a layer of thickness $h/\mathrm{Re}$ at the upper wall and $Q \simeq Uh/\mathrm{Re} \to 0$, since the injected liquid, which starts at rest, is swept to the upper wall before it can be dragged.
  • Dimensional check. $\rho v_lh/\mu$ is dimensionless; $Uh$ has units of a flux per unit span, m$^2$/s; $\mu U\,\mathrm{Re}/h$ is N/m$^2$; $\rho cv_l\,\Delta T$ is W/m$^2$.
  • Consistency of the limits. At $\mathrm{Re} = 0$, (18) gives $0 = 0$ and (19) gives $q_u = -q_l$.

Official Problem Statement

A very viscous liquid (of constant density $\rho$ and viscosity $\mu$) flows steadily through the gap between two concentric spheres, as shown in the figure. The flow is driven by a given pressure difference between the entrance and exit section, $\Delta p = p_i - p_o$, and gravity effects are negligible. We wish to determine the resulting volume flux $Q$ as a function of the known geometry data, fluid properties and pressure difference, $Q(R, \kappa, \epsilon, \rho, \mu, \Delta p)$.

1. Apply the dimensional analysis technique to write the parametric dependence of $Q$ as a function of the minimum number of parameters.

2. Simplify the non-dimensional relation for the case of flow dominated by viscous forces, for which the liquid inertia is negligible.

3. Disregarding end effects in the entrance and exit regions, we can assume that the flow is unidirectional and symmetric, $\bar{v} = v_\theta(r, \theta)\bar{e}_\theta$ (note that $v_r = v_\phi = 0$ and $\partial/\partial\phi = 0$). With this assumption, write the simplified continuity and momentum equations in spherical coordinates, as well as the necessary boundary conditions to solve the system. Write the expression to evaluate the volume flux $Q$ and then check that the parametric dependence stated earlier, that is $Q(R, \kappa, \epsilon, \rho, \mu, \Delta p)$, is correct.

4. Simplify the $\theta$-momentum equation with the assumption that the velocity is small enough for the viscous terms to be dominant over the convective terms. Under this conditions, the radial pressure variations are negligible compared with $\Delta p$ and we can consider that the pressure is a function of $\theta$ alone, $p(\theta)$.

5. Write the mathematical formulation (equations and boundary conditions) in non-dimensional form. Use the non-dimensional variables $\tilde{r} = r/R$, $\tilde{v}_\theta = v_\theta/V_\theta$ and $\tilde{p} = (p - p_o)/\Delta p$, where you can define $V_\theta$ such that there are no parameters in the non-dimensional equation.

6. Using the continuity equation, show that $\tilde{v}_\theta\sin\theta = \tilde{u}(\tilde{r})$, where $\tilde{u}(\tilde{r})$ is a function of $\tilde{r}$ to be determined.

7. Rewrite the $\theta$-momentum equation in terms of the function $\tilde{u}(\tilde{r})$ instead of $\tilde{v}_\theta$ and separate variables to show that $$ \operatorname{sen}\theta\,\frac{d\tilde{p}}{d\theta} = C $$ $$ \frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\frac{d}{d\tilde{r}}\left(\tilde{r}^2\frac{d\tilde{u}}{d\tilde{r}}\right) = C $$ where $C$ is a constant.

Phase 3

Step-by-Step Derivation

3.1 Dimensional analysis (items 1 and 2)

The variables of the problem are $Q$, $R$, $\kappa$, $\epsilon$, $\rho$, $\mu$, $\Delta p$. The dimensional ones, $Q\ [\mathrm{L^3T^{-1}}]$, $R\ [\mathrm{L}]$, $\rho\ [\mathrm{ML^{-3}}]$, $\mu\ [\mathrm{ML^{-1}T^{-1}}]$ and $\Delta p\ [\mathrm{ML^{-1}T^{-2}}]$, are five and involve three fundamental dimensions (M, L, T), so by the Vaschy-Buckingham theorem there are $5 - 3 = 2$ dimensionless groups from them; $\kappa$ and $\epsilon$ are already dimensionless. Choosing $R$, $\mu$ and $\Delta p$ as repeated variables:

$$ \Pi_1 = \frac{Q\,\mu}{\Delta p\,R^3}, \qquad \Pi_2 = \frac{\rho\,\Delta p\,R^2}{\mu^2} $$

Check: $\Pi_1 = (\mathrm{L^3T^{-1}})(\mathrm{ML^{-1}T^{-1}})/[(\mathrm{ML^{-1}T^{-2}})\,\mathrm{L^3}] = 1$ and $\Pi_2 = (\mathrm{ML^{-3}})(\mathrm{ML^{-1}T^{-2}})\,\mathrm{L^2}/(\mathrm{M^2L^{-2}T^{-2}}) = 1$. The minimum parametric dependence is

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{Q\,\mu}{\Delta p\,R^3} = f\left(\kappa,\ \epsilon,\ \frac{\rho\,\Delta p\,R^2}{\mu^2}\right)\,} \tag{1} $$

The group $\Pi_2$ is a Reynolds number: with the viscous velocity $V = \Delta p\,R/\mu$, $\rho VR/\mu = \rho\Delta pR^2/\mu^2$. When viscous forces dominate and the inertia of the liquid is negligible, the density disappears from the problem and the dependence on $\Pi_2$ is lost:

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{Q\,\mu}{\Delta p\,R^3} = f(\kappa,\ \epsilon)\,} \tag{2} $$

3.2 Equations and boundary conditions (item 3)

*Continuity* in spherical coordinates with $v_r = v_\phi = 0$ and $\partial_\phi = 0$:

$$ \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial(v_\theta\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial(v_\theta\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta} = 0 \tag{3} $$

*Momentum* (steady, no mass force), the three components of (4.41) with $v_r = v_\phi = 0$. In the viscous terms of the $\theta$ component, $\dfrac{1}{r^2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left[\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\dfrac{\partial(v_\theta\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta}\right]$ contains the left side of (3), so it vanishes; the terms with $v_r$, $v_\phi$ and $\partial_\phi$ vanish too. The $r$ component keeps the centrifugal term and the $\phi$ component only the pressure derivative:

$$ -\rho\frac{v_\theta^2}{r} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} \tag{4} $$
$$ \rho\frac{v_\theta}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta} = -\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta} + \mu\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial r}\right) \tag{5} $$
$$ 0 = -\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\phi} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial p}{\partial\phi} = 0 \tag{6} $$

*Boundary conditions* (table of Phase 2): $v_\theta(\kappa R,\theta) = 0$, $v_\theta(R,\theta) = 0$, $p = p_i$ at $\theta = \epsilon$ and $p = p_o$ at $\theta = \pi - \epsilon$. Writing $p' = p - p_o$ (so $dp' = dp$), these are $p'(\theta = \epsilon) = \Delta p$ and $p'(\theta = \pi - \epsilon) = 0$.

*Volume flux.* Through the equatorial section $\theta = \pi/2$, the annulus $\kappa R \leq r \leq R$ with area element $2\pi r\,dr$:

$$ Q = \int_{\kappa R}^R v_\theta\left(r,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)2\pi r\,dr \tag{7} $$

*Check of the parametric dependence.* The system (3)-(5) with its boundary conditions contains only $\rho$, $\mu$, $R$, $\kappa$, $\epsilon$ and $\Delta p$, so $Q = Q(R,\kappa,\epsilon,\rho,\mu,\Delta p)$, as stated.

3.3 Viscous-dominated flow (item 4)

Estimate the sizes of the terms of (5): the pressure term is $\Delta p/R$, the viscous term is $\mu V_\theta/R^2$ and the convective term is $\rho V_\theta^2/R$. The first two balance if $V_\theta \sim \Delta p\,R/\mu$, and the ratio of the convective term to the pressure term is then $\rho V_\theta^2/\Delta p \sim \rho\Delta pR^2/\mu^2 = \Pi_2 \ll 1$. The convective term is dropped. In (4) the radial pressure variation is of order $\rho V_\theta^2$, also small against $\Delta p$ by the same factor, so $p = p(\theta)$ and (5) becomes

$$ 0 = -\frac{1}{r}\frac{dp}{d\theta} + \frac{\mu}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial r}\right) \tag{8} $$

3.4 Non-dimensional formulation (item 5)

With $\tilde{r} = r/R$, $\tilde{v}_\theta = v_\theta/V_\theta$ and $\tilde{p} = (p - p_o)/\Delta p$, the pressure term of (8) is $(\Delta p/(R\tilde{r}))\,d\tilde{p}/d\theta$ and the viscous term is $(\mu V_\theta/R^2)(1/\tilde{r}^2)\,\partial(\tilde{r}^2\partial\tilde{v}_\theta/\partial\tilde{r})/\partial\tilde{r}$. Choosing $V_\theta = \Delta p\,R/\mu$ makes the two coefficients equal, and multiplying by $\tilde{r}$:

$$ 0 = -\frac{d\tilde{p}}{d\theta} + \frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{r}}\left(\tilde{r}^2\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_\theta}{\partial\tilde{r}}\right), \qquad \frac{\partial(\tilde{v}_\theta\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta} = 0 \tag{9} $$

with the boundary conditions

$$ \tilde{v}_\theta(\kappa,\theta) = \tilde{v}_\theta(1,\theta) = 0, \qquad \tilde{p}(\epsilon) = 1, \qquad \tilde{p}(\pi - \epsilon) = 0 \tag{10} $$

and the flux

$$ \tilde{Q} = \frac{Q\,\mu}{\Delta p\,R^3} = 2\pi\int_\kappa^1\tilde{v}_\theta\left(\tilde{r},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\tilde{r}\,d\tilde{r} \tag{11} $$

The problem has no parameters except $\kappa$ and $\epsilon$ (through the boundary conditions and the integration limits), so $\tilde{Q} = \tilde{Q}(\kappa,\epsilon)$, in agreement with (2).

3.5 Continuity and separation of variables (items 6 and 7)

From (9), $\partial(\tilde{v}_\theta\sin\theta)/\partial\theta = 0$, so $\tilde{v}_\theta\sin\theta$ does not depend on $\theta$:

$$ \tilde{v}_\theta\,\sin\theta = \tilde{u}(\tilde{r}) \tag{12} $$

Substituting $\tilde{v}_\theta = \tilde{u}/\sin\theta$ in the momentum equation of (9):

$$ \frac{d\tilde{p}}{d\theta} = \frac{1}{\tilde{r}\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\tilde{r}}\left(\tilde{r}^2\frac{d\tilde{u}}{d\tilde{r}}\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; \sin\theta\,\frac{d\tilde{p}}{d\theta} = \frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\frac{d}{d\tilde{r}}\left(\tilde{r}^2\frac{d\tilde{u}}{d\tilde{r}}\right) \tag{13} $$

The left side depends only on $\theta$ and the right side only on $\tilde{r}$, so both are equal to a constant $C$ (which may depend on $\kappa$ and $\epsilon$):

$$ \boxed{\,\sin\theta\,\frac{d\tilde{p}}{d\theta} = C, \qquad \frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\frac{d}{d\tilde{r}}\left(\tilde{r}^2\frac{d\tilde{u}}{d\tilde{r}}\right) = C\,} \tag{14} $$

3.6 Pressure and the constant $C$ (item 8)

From (14), $d\tilde{p}/d\theta = C/\sin\theta$. Integrating from $\theta = \epsilon$ (where $\tilde{p} = 1$) to $\theta = \pi - \epsilon$ (where $\tilde{p} = 0$):

$$ 0 - 1 = C\int_\epsilon^{\pi-\epsilon}\frac{d\theta}{\sin\theta} \equiv C\,E(\epsilon) \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,C = -\frac{1}{E(\epsilon)}\,} \tag{15} $$

The antiderivative of $1/\sin\theta$ is $\ln\tan(\theta/2)$ (its derivative is $\dfrac{1}{2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)} = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$), and Barrow's rule with $\tan\left(\frac{\pi - \epsilon}{2}\right) = \cot(\epsilon/2)$ gives

$$ E(\epsilon) = \left[\ln\tan\frac{\theta}{2}\right]_\epsilon^{\pi-\epsilon} = \ln\cot\frac{\epsilon}{2} - \ln\tan\frac{\epsilon}{2} = -2\ln\tan\frac{\epsilon}{2} = \ln\frac{1 + \cos\epsilon}{1 - \cos\epsilon} \tag{16} $$

(using $\tan^2(\epsilon/2) = (1 - \cos\epsilon)/(1 + \cos\epsilon)$). The pressure is $\tilde{p}(\theta) = 1 - \left[\ln\tan(\theta/2) - \ln\tan(\epsilon/2)\right]/E(\epsilon)$.

3.7 Velocity profile (item 9)

The second equation of (14): $\dfrac{d}{d\tilde{r}}\left(\tilde{r}^2\tilde{u}'\right) = C\tilde{r}$. Integrating, $\tilde{r}^2\tilde{u}' = C\tilde{r}^2/2 + A$, so $\tilde{u}' = C/2 + A/\tilde{r}^2$, and again $\tilde{u} = C\tilde{r}/2 - A/\tilde{r} + B$. The conditions $\tilde{u}(1) = 0$ and $\tilde{u}(\kappa) = 0$ (from $\tilde{v}_\theta = 0$ at both spheres) give

$$ \frac{C}{2} - A + B = 0, \qquad \frac{C\kappa}{2} - \frac{A}{\kappa} + B = 0 $$

Subtracting, $\frac{C}{2}(1 - \kappa) - A\left(1 - \frac{1}{\kappa}\right) = 0$, hence $A = -C\kappa/2$ and $B = A - C/2 = -C(1 + \kappa)/2$:

$$ \tilde{u}(\tilde{r}) = -\frac{C}{2}\left[1 - \tilde{r} + \kappa\left(1 - \frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\right)\right] = -\frac{C}{2}\,\frac{(1 - \tilde{r})(\tilde{r} - \kappa)}{\tilde{r}} \tag{17} $$

and, with (15) and $\tilde{v}_\theta = \tilde{u}/\sin\theta$,

$$ \boxed{\,\tilde{v}_\theta(\tilde{r},\theta) = \frac{1}{2E(\epsilon)\,\sin\theta}\left[1 - \tilde{r} + \kappa\left(1 - \frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\right)\right]\,} \tag{18} $$

The bracket equals $(1 - \tilde{r})(\tilde{r} - \kappa)/\tilde{r}$, which is positive in $\kappa < \tilde{r} < 1$: the flow goes from the entrance to the exit, with the largest velocity at the equator ($\sin\theta = 1$).

3.8 Volume flux (item 10)

The non-dimensional flux is $\tilde{Q} = Q\mu/(\Delta pR^3)$, (11). At $\theta = \pi/2$, $\sin\theta = 1$ and $\tilde{v}_\theta = \tilde{u}$:

$$ \tilde{Q} = 2\pi\int_\kappa^1\tilde{u}\,\tilde{r}\,d\tilde{r} = \frac{\pi}{E(\epsilon)}\int_\kappa^1\left(1 - \tilde{r}\right)\left(\tilde{r} - \kappa\right)d\tilde{r} \tag{19} $$

The integrand is $-\tilde{r}^2 + (1 + \kappa)\tilde{r} - \kappa$, with antiderivative $-\tilde{r}^3/3 + (1 + \kappa)\tilde{r}^2/2 - \kappa\tilde{r}$; Barrow's rule between $\kappa$ and $1$ gives $\dfrac{(1 - \kappa)^3}{6}$ (check: $\int_a^b(b - r)(r - a)\,dr = (b - a)^3/6$). Therefore

$$ \boxed{\,\tilde{Q} = \frac{\pi\,(1 - \kappa)^3}{6\,E(\epsilon)}, \qquad Q = \frac{\pi\,\Delta p\,R^3\,(1 - \kappa)^3}{6\,\mu\,E(\epsilon)}\,}, \qquad E(\epsilon) = \ln\frac{1 + \cos\epsilon}{1 - \cos\epsilon} \tag{20} $$
Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • Physical meaning. The flow in the gap is a Poiseuille-like flow along the meridians: the pressure drop $\Delta p$ is distributed as $\ln\tan(\theta/2)$ along $\theta$, and across the gap the velocity has the parabola-like shape (17) that vanishes at both spheres. The flux is proportional to $\Delta p/\mu$, as every Stokes flow, and to the cube of the gap, $(1 - \kappa)^3R^3$.
  • Limits. For $\kappa \to 1$ the gap closes and $Q \to 0$ as $(1 - \kappa)^3$. For $\epsilon \to 0$ the end sections shrink and $E(\epsilon) \simeq 2\ln(2/\epsilon) \to \infty$, so $Q$ vanishes only logarithmically. For $\epsilon \to \pi/2$ the sections cover the whole hemispheres and $E \to 0$ makes $Q \to \infty$, which is the breakdown of the unidirectional model (the end effects it neglects then control the flow).
  • Validity. The result requires $\rho\Delta pR^2/\mu^2 \ll 1$ (item 4).
  • Dimensional check. $\Delta pR^3/\mu$ has units $(\mathrm{N/m^2})\,\mathrm{m^3}/(\mathrm{Pa\,s}) = \mathrm{m^3/s}$, the units of $Q$. $V_\theta = \Delta pR/\mu$ has units m/s. $E(\epsilon)$ and $\tilde{Q}$ are dimensionless.
  • Consistency of the profile. The flux through the cone $\theta = \text{const}$ (area element $2\pi\tilde{r}\sin\theta\,d\tilde{r}$, normal velocity $\tilde{v}_\theta$) is $2\pi\int_\kappa^1\tilde{v}_\theta\sin\theta\,\tilde{r}\,d\tilde{r} = 2\pi\int_\kappa^1\tilde{u}\,\tilde{r}\,d\tilde{r}$, independent of $\theta$ as continuity requires; item 10 evaluates it at $\theta = \pi/2$, where the cone is the equatorial plane.
