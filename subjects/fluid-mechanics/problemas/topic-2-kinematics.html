Problem K1

Flow over an Oscillating Porous Wall with Transverse Blowing

Stokes Porous Wall Unsteady Circulation

Official Problem Statement

A fluid is blown with velocity $V$ through a horizontal porous wall that is oscillating in its plane with velocity $U \cos(\Omega t)$. The resulting fluid velocity for $0 < y < \infty$ is given by (approximately):

$$ v_x = U e^{-y/\delta} \cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right), \qquad v_y = V $$

where the characteristic thickness $\delta = (2\nu/\Omega)^{1/2}$ depends on the kinematic viscosity of the fluid $\nu$.

  1. Determine the trajectory and path of a fluid particle initially located at $x=0$ and $y=0$.
  2. Obtain the streamline that intersects the point $x=0$ and $y=0$.
  3. Compute the vorticity $\nabla \wedge \vec{v}$. If $\nabla \wedge \vec{v} = 0$, obtain also the velocity potential.
  4. Calculate the rate of expansion $\nabla \cdot \vec{v}$. If $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$, obtain also the stream function.
  5. Equation of the fluid line formed by the fluid particles initially located along the vertical axis $x=0$.
  6. Determine the volume flux crossing the plane $x=0$ as a function of time $Q(t)$ along with its average value $Q_a = \frac{\Omega}{2\pi}\int_t^{t+2\pi/\Omega} Q(t) dt$.
  7. Obtain the volume flux leaving the sphere of radius $R$ centered at $x=0$ and $y=2R$.
  8. Compute the circulation around a square of side $L$ whose base lies parallel to the wall at a distance $y=L/2$. Redo the computation making use of Stokes theorem.

1 Trajectory and Pathline (Particle at $x_0 = 0, y_0 = 0$)

The Lagrangian motion in the vertical $y$-direction is given by:

$$ \frac{dy}{dt} = v_y = V \implies y(t) = V t + y_0 = V t \implies t = \frac{y}{V} $$

Substituting $y(t) = Vt$ into the horizontal velocity component:

$$ \frac{dx}{dt} = v_x = U e^{-Vt/\delta} \cos\left[ \left(\Omega - \frac{V}{\delta}\right) t \right] $$

Integrating using the standard primitive $\int e^{at}\cos(bt)dt = \frac{e^{at}}{a^2+b^2}[a\cos(bt) + b\sin(bt)]$ with $a = -V/\delta$ and $b = \Omega - V/\delta$:

Trajectory $x(t)$ $$ x(t) = \frac{U e^{-Vt/\delta}}{\left(\frac{V}{\delta}\right)^2 + \left(\Omega - \frac{V}{\delta}\right)^2} \left[ -\frac{V}{\delta}\cos\left(\left(\Omega - \frac{V}{\delta}\right)t\right) + \left(\Omega - \frac{V}{\delta}\right)\sin\left(\left(\Omega - \frac{V}{\delta}\right)t\right) \right] + \frac{\frac{UV}{\delta}}{\left(\frac{V}{\delta}\right)^2 + \left(\Omega - \frac{V}{\delta}\right)^2} $$

Substituting $t = y/V$ yields the Cartesian equation of the particle pathline $x(y)$.

2 Streamline through $(x=0, y=0)$

For a frozen time $t$:

$$ \frac{dx}{v_x} = \frac{dy}{v_y} \implies dx = \frac{U}{V} e^{-y/\delta}\cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) dy $$

Integrating with respect to $y$ from $y=0$ ($x_0=0$):

Instantaneous Streamline $x(y, t)$ $$ x(y, t) = -\frac{U\delta}{2V} e^{-y/\delta}\left[ \cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) + \sin\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) \right] + \frac{U\delta}{2V}\left[ \cos(\Omega t) + \sin(\Omega t) \right] $$

3 Vorticity and Velocity Potential

$$ \vec{\omega} = \nabla \wedge \vec{v} = \left(\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right)\vec{e}_z = -\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\vec{e}_z $$ $$ \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y} = U \left[ -\frac{1}{\delta}e^{-y/\delta}\cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) + \frac{1}{\delta}e^{-y/\delta}\sin\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) \right] $$
Vorticity Field $\vec{\omega}$ $$ \vec{\omega} = \frac{U}{\delta} e^{-y/\delta}\left[ \cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) - \sin\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) \right] \vec{e}_z \neq 0 $$

Since $\vec{\omega} \neq 0$, the flow is rotational and no velocity potential $\phi$ exists.

4 Rate of Expansion $\nabla \cdot \vec{v}$ and Stream Function $\psi$

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = 0 + 0 = 0 \implies \text{Incompressible Flow} $$

Being two-dimensional and incompressible, there exists a stream function $\psi$ such that $v_x = \partial\psi/\partial y$ and $v_y = -\partial\psi/\partial x$:

Stream Function $\psi(x, y, t)$ $$ \psi(x, y, t) = -Vx - \frac{U\delta}{2} e^{-y/\delta}\left[ \cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) + \sin\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) \right] + \psi_0 $$

5 Initial Fluid Line $x_0 = 0$

Fluid particles initially located along the vertical axis have $x_0 = 0$ and $y_0 = \lambda > 0$. Their vertical position evolves as $y(t) = \lambda + Vt \implies \lambda = y - Vt$. Substituting into the horizontal displacement equation:

Fluid Line $x(y, t)$ $$ x(y, t) = -\frac{U\delta}{2V} e^{-y/\delta}\left[ \cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) + \sin\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) \right] + \frac{U\delta}{2V} e^{-(y-Vt)/\delta}\left[ \cos\left(\frac{Vt-y}{\delta}\right) - \sin\left(\frac{Vt-y}{\delta}\right) \right] $$

6 Volume Flux $Q(t)$ Crossing $x = 0$ and Time-Average Value $Q_a$

Per unit width normal to the plane:

$$ Q(t) = \int_0^\infty v_x(0, y, t) dy = U \int_0^\infty e^{-y/\delta}\cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) dy = \frac{U\delta}{2}\left[\cos(\Omega t) - \sin(\Omega t)\right] $$

Averaging over a period $T = 2\pi/\Omega$:

Time-Averaged Volume Flux $$ Q_a = \frac{\Omega}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi/\Omega} Q(t) dt = \mathbf{0} $$

7 Flux Leaving a Sphere of Radius $R$

By Gauss's divergence theorem, since $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$ everywhere in the domain:

$$ \oint_S \vec{v} \cdot \vec{n} \, d\sigma = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{v}) dV = \mathbf{0} $$

8 Circulation $\Gamma$ around Square of Side $L$ and Verification via Stokes

The square has horizontal edges at $y = L/2$ and $y = 3L/2$, and vertical edges between $x$ and $x+L$:

$$ \Gamma = \oint \vec{v}\cdot d\vec{l} = \int_{x}^{x+L} v_x\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)dx + \int_{L/2}^{3L/2} v_y dy - \int_{x}^{x+L} v_x\left(\frac{3L}{2}\right)dx - \int_{L/2}^{3L/2} v_y dy $$
Circulation $\Gamma(t)$ $$ \Gamma(t) = U L \left\{ e^{-L/(2\delta)}\cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{L}{2\delta}\right) - e^{-3L/(2\delta)}\cos\left(\Omega t - \frac{3L}{2\delta}\right) \right\} $$

Integrating $\iint \vec{\omega}\cdot\vec{e}_z dx dy = L \int_{L/2}^{3L/2} \left(-\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right)dy = L[v_x(L/2) - v_x(3L/2)]$, identically verifying Stokes' theorem.

Problem K2

Plane Couette Flow and Strain / Rotation Analysis

Couette Vorticity Rate-of-Strain Tensor Principal Directions

Official Problem Statement

Consider the steady flow of a liquid confined between two parallel planar walls separated a distance $H$. If the top wall moves with constant velocity $U$, the resulting fluid motion exhibits a single velocity component $v_x = Uy/H$, where $y$ denotes the distance to the bottom wall, assumed to be at rest.

  1. Determine the streamlines and the trajectories.
  2. Obtain the vorticity field.
  3. Compute the circulation $\Gamma$ around a rectangular line of side $L$ oriented parallel to the walls and height $h$.
  4. Calculate the gradient of velocity, the rate-of-strain tensor and the expansion rate.
  5. Obtain the principal directions of strain as well as the associated principal strain rates.
  6. Investigate the evolution of a square fluid element of side $dl$ oriented parallel to the walls.

1 Streamlines and Trajectories

Since $v_y = 0$, the streamline equation $dx/v_x = dy/v_y$ directly implies $dy = 0 \implies \mathbf{y = y_0 = \text{const}}$.

For material particles: $dy/dt = 0 \implies y(t) = y_0$, and $dx/dt = Uy_0/H \implies \mathbf{x(t) - x_0 = \frac{U y_0}{H} t}$.

Kinematic Coincidence: Steady flow $\implies$ Streamlines $\equiv$ Trajectories $\equiv$ Streaklines ($y = y_0$).

2 Vorticity Field

$$ \vec{\omega} = \nabla \wedge \vec{v} = \left(\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right)\vec{e}_z = \left(0 - \frac{U}{H}\right)\vec{e}_z = \mathbf{-\frac{U}{H}\vec{e}_z} $$

The vorticity is uniform, spatially constant, and oriented along $-\vec{e}_z$ (clockwise rotation).

3 Circulation $\Gamma$ around a Rectangle

$$ \Gamma = \oint \vec{v}\cdot d\vec{l} = \int_{x}^{x+L} v_x(y)dx + 0 - \int_{x}^{x+L} v_x(y+h)dx - 0 = \frac{U y}{H}L - \frac{U(y+h)}{H}L = \mathbf{-\frac{U h L}{H}} $$

By Stokes' Theorem: $\Gamma = \iint (\vec{\omega}\cdot\vec{e}_z) d\sigma = -\frac{U}{H}(L h) = -\frac{U h L}{H}$.

4 Velocity Gradient and Kinematic Tensors

$$ \nabla\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ \frac{U}{H} & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \bar{\bar{T}}_d = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H} \\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H} & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \bar{\bar{T}}_r = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H} \\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H} & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Volumetric expansion rate: $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = \text{tr}(\bar{\bar{T}}_d) = 0$.

5 Principal Directions and Principal Strain Rates

$$ \det(\bar{\bar{T}}_d - \lambda \bar{\bar{I}}) = \lambda^2 - \left(\frac{U}{2H}\right)^2 = 0 \implies \lambda = \pm \frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H} $$
Principal Strain Modes
  • Tension (+45°): $\lambda_1 = +\frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H}, \quad \vec{n}_1 = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$
  • Compression (-45°): $\lambda_2 = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{U}{H}, \quad \vec{n}_2 = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$

6 Evolution of a Square Fluid Element of Side $dl$

$$ dl(1, 0) \to dl(1, 0) + dl(1, 0)\nabla\vec{v} dt = dl(1, 0) \quad (\text{horizontal side does not rotate or stretch}) $$ $$ dl(0, 1) \to dl(0, 1) + dl(0, 1)\nabla\vec{v} dt = dl\left(\frac{U}{H}dt, 1\right) \quad (\text{vertical side tilts}) $$

The distortion is the exact superposition of:

  1. Rigid-body rotation: Angular velocity $\Omega_z = -\frac{U}{2H}$ (angle $-\frac{U}{2H}dt$).
  2. Pure shear: Shear strain rate $\dot{\gamma} = \frac{U}{H}$ (symmetric angle $\frac{U}{2H}dt$).
Problem K3

Three-Dimensional Point Source at the Origin

Spherical Coordinates Fluid Line Streamtube Moving Observer

Official Problem Statement

A three-dimensional fluid source placed at the origin induces a radial motion given by $v_r = Q(t)/(4\pi r^2)$, with $v_\theta = v_\phi = 0$. Obtain:

  1. Streamlines, trajectories and paths.
  2. Equation for the fluid surface initially located at $r = R$.
  3. Equation for the fluid line that corresponds at the initial instant with a circle of radius $R$ intersecting the origin.
  4. Streamtube that intersects the circle defined by $r = R_1$ and $\theta = \theta_1$.
  5. The velocity components in cartesian coordinates, as well as the corresponding streamlines and trajectories.
  6. Equations for the streamlines and trajectories for an observer moving with velocity $U$.

1 Streamlines, Trajectories, and Pathlines

In spherical coordinates: $\frac{dr}{v_r} = \frac{r d\theta}{0} = \frac{r \sin\theta d\phi}{0} \implies \mathbf{\theta = \theta_0, \; \phi = \phi_0}$. They are radial rays diverging from the origin.

Integrating radially for the trajectory:

$$ \frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi r^2} \implies 4\pi r^2 dr = Q(t) dt \implies \mathbf{\frac{4\pi}{3}\left(r^3 - r_0^3\right) = \int_0^t Q(t') dt'} $$

2 Evolution of the Initial Fluid Surface $r = R$

Substituting $r_0 = R$ into the radial evolution law:

$$ \frac{4\pi}{3}\left(r^3 - R^3\right) = \int_0^t Q(t') dt' \implies \mathbf{r(t) = \left[ R^3 + \frac{3}{4\pi}\int_0^t Q(t') dt' \right]^{1/3}} $$

The fluid surface remains a perfect expanding sphere centered at the origin.

3 Evolution of an Initial Circle Passing through the Origin

Parametrizing the initial circle in $\phi_0 = 0$ as $r_0 = R \cos\lambda, \theta_0 = \lambda$:

$$ \frac{4\pi}{3}\left(r^3 - R^3\cos^3\lambda\right) = \int_0^t Q(t') dt', \qquad \theta = \lambda $$
Equation of the Fluid Line $$ \mathbf{\frac{4\pi}{3}\left(r^3 - R^3\cos^3\theta\right) = \int_0^t Q(t') dt'} $$

4 Streamtube through the Circle $r = R_1, \theta = \theta_1$

Formed by the bundle of streamlines passing through all points with $\theta = \theta_1$, and since each streamline is a ray of constant angle:

$$ \mathbf{\theta = \theta_1} \qquad \text{(Right circular cone of semi-angle } \theta_1 \text{ with apex at the origin)} $$

5 Cartesian Components

$$ v_x = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi}\frac{x}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}}, \quad v_y = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi}\frac{y}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}}, \quad v_z = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi}\frac{z}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}} $$

Cartesian streamlines: $\frac{dx}{x} = \frac{dy}{y} = \frac{dz}{z} \implies \mathbf{\frac{x}{x_0} = \frac{y}{y_0} = \frac{z}{z_0}}$ (straight lines through the origin).

6 Observer Moving with Velocity $U \vec{e}_x$

$$ v_x' = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi}\frac{x'}{(x'^2+y'^2+z'^2)^{3/2}} - U, \qquad v_y' = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi}\frac{y'}{r'^3}, \qquad v_z' = \frac{Q(t)}{4\pi}\frac{z'}{r'^3} $$
Problem K4

Flow Past a Rankine Half-Body

Rankine Stagnation Point Stream Function Saddle Point

Official Problem Statement

Consider the fluid flow that derives from the potential $\phi = \frac{Q}{2\pi}\ln r + U_\infty r\cos\theta$, corresponding to the superposition of a uniform stream of velocity $U_\infty$ and a source of volumetric flux $Q$.

  1. Determine the velocity components in cartesian and cylindrical coordinates.
  2. Obtain the stream lines.
  3. Compute the trajectories and paths.
  4. Calculate the stagnation points, sketching the stream lines that reach them.
  5. Consider the flow near the stagnation points, determining in particular the trajectories and the stream lines.

1 Velocity Components

In cylindrical/polar coordinates: $v_r = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r} = \mathbf{\frac{Q}{2\pi r} + U_\infty \cos\theta}, \quad v_\theta = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta} = \mathbf{-U_\infty \sin\theta}$.

In Cartesian coordinates: $v_x = \mathbf{\frac{Q}{2\pi}\frac{x}{x^2+y^2} + U_\infty}, \quad v_y = \mathbf{\frac{Q}{2\pi}\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}}$.

2 Streamlines

$$ \frac{dr}{\frac{Q}{2\pi r} + U_\infty \cos\theta} = \frac{r d\theta}{-U_\infty \sin\theta} \implies d\left( \frac{Q\theta}{2\pi} + U_\infty r\sin\theta \right) = 0 $$
Stream Function $\psi(r, \theta)$ $$ \mathbf{\psi(r, \theta) = \frac{Q\theta}{2\pi} + U_\infty r\sin\theta = \frac{Q\theta_0}{2\pi} + U_\infty r_0\sin\theta_0 = \text{const}} $$

3 Trajectories and Transit Time

Being a steady flow, trajectories coincide with streamlines ($\psi = \text{const}$).

$$ dt = -\frac{r d\theta}{U_\infty \sin\theta} \implies \mathbf{t = -\int_{\theta_0}^\theta \frac{\frac{Q}{2\pi}(\theta_0 - \theta') + U_\infty r_0 \sin\theta_0}{U_\infty^2 \sin^2\theta'} d\theta'} $$

4 Stagnation Point and Half-Body Contour

Setting $v_\theta = 0 \implies \theta = \pi$. In the radial direction $v_r = \frac{Q}{2\pi r} - U_\infty = 0 \implies \mathbf{r_0 = \frac{Q}{2\pi U_\infty}}$.

Evaluating the stream function at $(r_0, \pi) \implies \psi = Q/2$. Equating $\psi(r, \theta) = Q/2$:

Rankine Half-Body Contour $$ \mathbf{r(\theta) = \frac{Q(\pi - \theta)}{2\pi U_\infty \sin\theta}, \qquad 0 < \theta < 2\pi} $$

For $\theta \to 0$, $y = r\sin\theta \to \frac{Q}{2 U_\infty}$ (asymptotic half-thickness).

5 Flow near the Stagnation Point

Defining $\xi = (x + r_0)/r_0 \ll 1, \eta = y/r_0 \ll 1$, and $\tau = t U_\infty / r_0$:

$$ \mathbf{\xi \eta = \xi_0 \eta_0} \quad (\text{Hyperbolic streamlines}) $$ $$ \mathbf{\xi(\tau) = \xi_0 e^{-\tau}, \qquad \eta(\tau) = \eta_0 e^{\tau}} \quad (\text{Trajectories}) $$
Problem K5

Pulsating / Oscillating Plane Dipole

Dipole Lamb-Gromeka Acceleration Zero Circulation

Official Problem Statement

For the planar fluid motion deriving from the velocity potential $\phi = M\cos(\Omega t)\frac{\cos\theta}{r}$, obtain:

  1. Radial and azimuthal velocity components.
  2. Expansion rate $\nabla \cdot \vec{v}$.
  3. Vorticity $\nabla \wedge \vec{v}$.
  4. Acceleration of the fluid particle initially located at $r = 1$ and $\theta = \pi/2$.
  5. Initial value of the circulation around a circle of radius $R = 1/2$ centered at $(r = 1, \theta = \pi/2)$.
  6. Streamlines.
  7. Trajectories and particle paths.

1 Velocity Components

$$ v_r = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r} = \mathbf{-\frac{M\cos(\Omega t)\cos\theta}{r^2}}, \qquad v_\theta = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta} = \mathbf{-\frac{M\cos(\Omega t)\sin\theta}{r^2}} $$

2–3 Expansion Rate and Vorticity

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = \frac{1}{r}\left[\frac{\partial(rv_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta}\right] = \mathbf{0} \quad (\text{Incompressible}) $$ $$ \vec{\omega} = \nabla \wedge \vec{v} = \frac{1}{r}\left[\frac{\partial(rv_\theta)}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial\theta}\right]\vec{e}_z = \mathbf{0} \quad (\text{Irrotational}) $$

4 Acceleration at $(r = 1, \theta = \pi/2)$

With $\nabla \wedge \vec{v} = 0$, the acceleration is $\vec{a} = \frac{\partial\vec{v}}{\partial t} + \nabla(v^2/2)$. With $v^2/2 = \frac{M^2 \cos^2(\Omega t)}{2 r^4}$:

$$ \vec{a} = \left[ \frac{M\Omega\sin(\Omega t)\cos\theta}{r^2} - \frac{2 M^2 \cos^2(\Omega t)}{r^5} \right]\vec{e}_r + \left[ \frac{M\Omega\sin(\Omega t)\sin\theta}{r^2} \right]\vec{e}_\theta $$
Acceleration at $(1, \pi/2)$ $$ \mathbf{\vec{a}\left(1, \frac{\pi}{2}, t\right) = -2 M^2 \cos^2(\Omega t) \, \vec{e}_r + M \Omega \sin(\Omega t) \, \vec{e}_\theta} $$

5 Initial Circulation around Shifted Circle

The circle of radius $R=1/2$ centered at $(1, \pi/2)$ lies at distance $d - R = 1 - 1/2 = 1/2 > 0$ from the origin, enclosing no singularity. By Stokes' theorem:

$$ \mathbf{\Gamma = \iint \vec{\omega}\cdot\vec{n} d\sigma = 0} $$

6–7 Streamlines and Trajectories

$$ \frac{dr}{v_r} = \frac{r d\theta}{v_\theta} \implies \frac{dr}{r} = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} d\theta \implies \mathbf{r = r_0 \frac{\sin\theta}{\sin\theta_0}} $$

Circles tangent to the $x$-axis at the origin. Since the direction of the velocity vector is stationary in time, Streamlines $\equiv$ Pathlines.

Time Evolution of Angular Position $$ \mathbf{\frac{\theta - \theta_0}{2} - \frac{\sin(2\theta) - \sin(2\theta_0)}{4} = -\frac{M \sin^3\theta_0}{r_0^3 \Omega}\sin(\Omega t)} $$
Problem K6

Burgers 3D Vortex with Axial Stretching

Burgers Vortex Incompressibility Axial Stretching Cylindrical Coordinates

Official Problem Statement

The axisymmetric velocity field corresponding to a three-dimensional steady vortex of circulation $\Gamma$ is given in cylindrical coordinates by:

$$ v_r = -\frac{r}{\tau_r}, \qquad v_\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}\left\{ 1 - \exp\left[ -\left(\frac{r}{R_0}\right)^2 \right] \right\}, \qquad v_z = \frac{z}{\tau_z} $$
  1. Determine the condition required for the motion to be that of an incompressible fluid.
  2. Compute streamlines and trajectories.

1 Incompressibility Condition

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = \frac{1}{r}\left(-\frac{2r}{\tau_r}\right) + 0 + \frac{1}{\tau_z} = -\frac{2}{\tau_r} + \frac{1}{\tau_z} = 0 $$
$$ \mathbf{\tau_r = 2\tau_z} $$

2 Streamlines and Trajectories

Since the flow is steady, streamlines coincide with trajectories:

  • Radial evolution: $dr/dt = -r/\tau_r \implies \mathbf{r(t) = r_0 e^{-t/\tau_r}}$
  • Axial evolution: $dz/dt = z/\tau_z \implies \mathbf{z(t) = z_0 e^{t/\tau_z}}$
  • Meridional stream surface: Eliminating $t$ with $\tau_r = 2\tau_z$:
    $$ \frac{z}{z_0} = \left(\frac{r}{r_0}\right)^{-2} \implies \mathbf{z r^2 = z_0 r_0^2 = \text{const}} $$
  • Azimuthal evolution: $$ \mathbf{\theta - \theta_0 = -\frac{\Gamma \tau_r}{2\pi} \int_{r_0}^r \left\{ 1 - \exp\left[-\left(\frac{r'}{R_0}\right)^2\right] \right\} \frac{dr'}{r'^3}} $$
Problem K7

Plane Hyperbolic Stagnation Flow

Stagnation Flow Pure Strain Stream Function Infinite Time

Official Problem Statement

Consider the planar motion of velocity components $v_x = ax$ and $v_y = by$, where $a$ and $b$ are known constants.

  1. If the flow is irrotational compute the associated velocity potential.
  2. Compute the value of $b$ for which the flow corresponds to that of an incompressible fluid.
  3. Obtain the stream lines and represent the result schematically.
  4. Determine the trajectory and the path of a fluid particle initially located at $x_0 = 0$ and $y_0 = 1$. How long does it take to reach the origin?
  5. Calculate the acceleration of fluid particles located along the axis $y=0$.
  6. Find the stream function for the flow.
  7. Compute the rate-of-strain tensor.
  8. Investigate the evolution of a fluid element whose initial shape is that of a square of sides $dl$.

1 Velocity Potential

$$ \vec{\omega} = (0 - 0)\vec{e}_z = 0 \implies \mathbf{\phi = a \frac{x^2}{2} + b \frac{y^2}{2} + \phi_0} $$

2 Incompressibility

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = a + b = 0 \implies \mathbf{b = -a} \implies \vec{v} = ax\vec{e}_x - ay\vec{e}_y $$

3 Streamlines

$$ \frac{dx}{ax} = \frac{dy}{-ay} \implies \frac{dx}{x} = -\frac{dy}{y} \implies \mathbf{x y = x_0 y_0 = \text{const}} \quad (\text{Equilateral hyperbolas}) $$

4 Particle at $(0, 1)$ and Time to Origin

$x(t) = 0, y(t) = e^{-at}$. Pathline: vertical segment $x=0, 0 < y \le 1$.

Time to Reach the Origin $$ y(t) \to 0 \implies e^{-at} \to 0 \implies -at \to -\infty \implies \mathbf{t \to \infty} $$

An infinite time is required due to exponential deceleration approaching stagnation.

5 Acceleration along $y = 0$

$$ \vec{a} = (\vec{v}\cdot\nabla)\vec{v} = a^2 x \vec{e}_x + a^2 y \vec{e}_y \implies \mathbf{\left. \vec{a} \right|_{y=0} = a^2 x \, \vec{e}_x} $$

6 Stream Function

$$ v_x = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y} = ax, \quad v_y = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} = -ay \implies \mathbf{\psi(x, y) = axy + \psi_0} $$

7–8 Kinematic Tensors and Deformation of the Square

$$ \bar{\bar{T}}_d = \begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & -a \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \bar{\bar{T}}_r = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Evolution of the square's edges: $dl(1, 0) \to \mathbf{dl(1+a\,dt, 0)}$ and $dl(0, 1) \to \mathbf{dl(0, 1-a\,dt)}$.

State of pure strain without rotation: stretching along $x$, symmetric compression along $y$, area conservation.

Problem K8

Pulsating 3D Potential Flow and Spherical Deformation

3D Potential Material Surface Pulsating Ellipsoid Axial Symmetry

Official Problem Statement

The velocity potential of a three-dimensional flow field is given by $\phi = A \cos(\Omega t)(2x^2 - y^2 - z^2)$, where $A$ and $\Omega$ are known constants.

  1. Determine the velocity field, verifying that $\nabla \wedge \vec{v} = 0$ and $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$.
  2. Compute the streamlines, trajectories and particle paths.
  3. Obtain the fluid surface that at the initial instant is a sphere of radius $R$ centered at $(x, y, z) = (-R/2, 0, 0)$.

1 Velocity Field

$$ v_x = \mathbf{4 A \cos(\Omega t) x}, \qquad v_y = \mathbf{-2 A \cos(\Omega t) y}, \qquad v_z = \mathbf{-2 A \cos(\Omega t) z} $$

$\nabla \wedge \vec{v} = \mathbf{0}$ and incompressible: $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = A\cos(\Omega t)(4 - 2 - 2) = \mathbf{0}$.

2 Streamlines and Trajectories

$$ \frac{dx}{2x} = -\frac{dy}{y} = -\frac{dz}{z} \implies \mathbf{x y^2 = x_0 y_0^2, \qquad \frac{y}{z} = \frac{y_0}{z_0}} $$

Integrating trajectories over time:

$$ \frac{x(t)}{x_i} = \exp\left[ \frac{4A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t) \right], \quad \frac{y(t)}{y_i} = \exp\left[ -\frac{2A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t) \right], \quad \frac{z(t)}{z_i} = \exp\left[ -\frac{2A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t) \right] $$

Eliminating time recovers the exact streamlines (Streamlines $\equiv$ Trajectories).

3 Evolution of the Initial Sphere $(x_i + R/2)^2 + y_i^2 + z_i^2 = R^2$

Inverting the Lagrangian position: $x_i = x e^{-\frac{4A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)}, y_i = y e^{\frac{2A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)}, z_i = z e^{\frac{2A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)}$.

Deformed Fluid Surface (Pulsating Ellipsoid) $$ \mathbf{\left( x e^{-\frac{4A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)} + \frac{R}{2} \right)^2 + \left( y e^{\frac{2A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)} \right)^2 + \left( z e^{\frac{2A}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)} \right)^2 = R^2} $$
Problem K9

Oscillating Polar Flow and Fluid Line Deformation

Polar Coordinates Potential & Stream Function Fluid Line Acceleration

Official Problem Statement

For the planar velocity field $v_r = -A \sin(\Omega t) \frac{\sin\theta}{r^2}, v_\theta = A \sin(\Omega t) \frac{\cos\theta}{r^2}$, where $A$ and $\Omega$ are known constants,

  1. Determine the vorticity.
  2. Compute the expansion rate.
  3. Comment on the existence of velocity potential and stream function and, if they do exist, compute them.
  4. Find the acceleration for points along the line $\theta = 0$.
  5. Obtain the trajectories & streamlines.
  6. Determine the fluid line that at $t = 0$ is given by $\theta = 0$ and $0 < r < \infty$.

1–2 Vorticity and Expansion Rate

$$ \omega_z = \frac{1}{r}\left[ \frac{\partial(r v_\theta)}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial\theta} \right] = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = \frac{1}{r}\left[ \frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial\theta} \right] = \mathbf{0} $$

3 Velocity Potential $\phi$ and Stream Function $\psi$

$$ \mathbf{\phi(r, \theta, t) = A \sin(\Omega t) \frac{\sin\theta}{r} + \phi_0} $$ $$ \mathbf{\psi(r, \theta, t) = A \sin(\Omega t) \frac{\cos\theta}{r} + \psi_0} $$

4 Acceleration along the Polar Axis $\theta = 0$

With $v^2/2 = \frac{A^2 \sin^2(\Omega t)}{2 r^4}$:

$$ \mathbf{\left. a_r \right|_{\theta = 0} = -\frac{2 A^2 \sin^2(\Omega t)}{r^5}}, \qquad \mathbf{\left. a_\theta \right|_{\theta = 0} = \frac{A \Omega \cos(\Omega t)}{r^2}} $$

5 Streamlines and Trajectories

$$ \frac{dr}{v_r} = \frac{r d\theta}{v_\theta} \implies \frac{dr}{r} = -\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} d\theta \implies \mathbf{r = r_0 \frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\theta_0}} $$

Circles tangent to the $y$-axis at the origin. Streamlines $\equiv$ Pathlines.

$$ \mathbf{\frac{\theta - \theta_0}{2} + \frac{\sin(2\theta) - \sin(2\theta_0)}{4} = \frac{A \cos^3\theta_0}{r_0^3 \Omega}\left[1 - \cos(\Omega t)\right]} $$

6 Deformation of the Initial Fluid Line $\theta_0 = 0$

With $\theta_0 = 0, r_0 = \lambda$: $\lambda = r / \cos\theta$. Eliminating $\lambda$:

Fluid Line $r(\theta, t)$ $$ \mathbf{\frac{r^3}{2\cos^3\theta}\left(\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{A}{\Omega}\left[1 - \cos(\Omega t)\right]} $$
Problem K10

Plane Flow with Exponential Shear and Pure Strain

Quiz 2013 Irrotational Pure Shear Pure Strain

Official Problem Statement (UC3M Quiz Sept 30, 2013)

Given the planar motion described by the velocity components $v_x = A e^{\Omega t} y$ and $v_y = B e^{\Omega t} x$, where $A, B$, and $\Omega$ are constants.

  1. Determine the value of the volume dilation rate $\nabla \cdot \vec{v}$.
  2. Obtain the value of $B$ for which the resulting flow is irrotational. Adopt this particular value in what follows.
  3. Calculate the stream line that intersects the point $(x, y) = (x_0, y_0)$ at a given instant of time $t$. Sketch the resulting streamlines.
  4. Compute the trajectory of a fluid particle located initially at $(x, y) = (x_0, y_0)$.
  5. If they exist, calculate the velocity potential $\phi$ and the stream function $\psi$.
  6. Determine the rate-of-strain tensor $\bar{\bar{T}}_d$.
  7. Describe the deformation of a fluid element of square shape whose sides of length $dl$ are initially oriented parallel to the axes.

1 Volume Dilation Rate

$$ \nabla \cdot \vec{v} = \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = 0 + 0 = \mathbf{0} \quad (\text{Incompressible}) $$

2 Irrotationality Condition

$$ \vec{\omega} = \left(\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right)\vec{e}_z = (B e^{\Omega t} - A e^{\Omega t})\vec{e}_z = 0 \implies \mathbf{B = A} $$

3 Streamlines

$$ \frac{dx}{A e^{\Omega t} y} = \frac{dy}{A e^{\Omega t} x} \implies x dx = y dy \implies \mathbf{y^2 - x^2 = y_0^2 - x_0^2} \quad (\text{Equilateral hyperbolas}) $$

4 Trajectories

Pathlines $\equiv$ Streamlines ($y^2 - x^2 = y_0^2 - x_0^2$). Time integration:

$$ \mathbf{\ln\left( \frac{x + \sqrt{x^2 + y_0^2 - x_0^2}}{x_0 + y_0} \right) = \pm \frac{A}{\Omega}(e^{\Omega t} - 1)} $$

5 Velocity Potential and Stream Function

$$ \mathbf{\phi(x, y, t) = A e^{\Omega t} x y + \phi_0} $$ $$ \mathbf{\psi(x, y, t) = \frac{1}{2} A e^{\Omega t}(y^2 - x^2) + \psi_0} $$

6 Rate-of-Strain Tensor

$$ \mathbf{\bar{\bar{T}}_d = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & A e^{\Omega t} \\ A e^{\Omega t} & 0 \end{bmatrix}}, \qquad \mathbf{\bar{\bar{T}}_r = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}} $$

7 Deformation of a Square Element

The horizontal and vertical sides rotate inwards by an identical angle $d\alpha = d\beta = A e^{\Omega t} dt$:

$$ dl(1, 0) \to \mathbf{dl(1, A e^{\Omega t} dt)}, \qquad dl(0, 1) \to \mathbf{dl(A e^{\Omega t} dt, 1)} $$

Mean angular rotation velocity $\Omega_z = 0$. Shear strain rate $\dot{\gamma}_{xy} = 2 A e^{\Omega t}$. This represents a state of pure shear strain without rigid-body rotation.
