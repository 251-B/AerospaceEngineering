Problem FS1

Melting Ice Cube

Original statement · fluid_statics_1.pdf
ArchimedesFloating bodyMass conservation

Official Problem Statement

An ice cube of density $\rho_h$ is partially submerged in a container holding a fluid of density $\rho_f > \rho_h$. The total volume of the ice cube is $V_h$, while the submerged part of the cube occupies a volume $V_s$ to be determined. Calculate the volume of water displaced once the cube has melted, turning into water of density $\rho_a$, as a function of $\rho_f$, $\rho_h$, $\rho_a$ and $V_h$, knowing that $\rho_h < \rho_a < \rho_f$. Particularize for the case $\rho_f = \rho_a$.

Translated from the Spanish original; the figure of the sheet labels the cube density $\rho_b$, the same quantity as $\rho_h$.

Source: fluid_statics_1.pdf (page 1, sheet of 11-2-00). Notes.pdf, Chapter 5: Archimedes' principle, Eq. 5.15.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The fluid and the melt water are at rest and incompressible; the air above them is at the uniform pressure $p_a$.
  • The cube floats in equilibrium with part of its volume above the free surface (this requires $\rho_h < \rho_f$, given).
  • Mass is conserved when the ice melts; the melt water and the fluid do not change volume when they meet (the melt water either floats as a layer, since $\rho_a < \rho_f$, or mixes with additive volumes).
  • Data: $\rho_h$, $\rho_f$, $\rho_a$, $V_h$, $g$. Unknowns: the submerged volume $V_s$ and the volume $V_d$ that the liquid gains when the cube melts, that is the change of the volume of liquid below the free surface.
SymbolMeaningSI unit
$\rho_h$, $\rho_f$, $\rho_a$densities of ice, fluid and melt waterkg/m$^3$
$V_h$volume of the cubem$^3$
$V_s$submerged volume of the cubem$^3$
$V_a$volume of the melt waterm$^3$

The Floating Cube: Archimedes' Principle

The forces on the cube are its weight and the pressure of the fluid and of the air on its surface. The air pressure $p_a$ acts over the whole closed surface of the cube and adds up to zero ($\oint p_a\vec{n}\,d\sigma = 0$). The part below the free surface feels the gauge pressure of the fluid, and by Archimedes' principle (Eq. 5.15) applied to the submerged part, closed by the plane of the free surface where the gauge pressure is zero, its resultant is the weight of the displaced fluid, $\rho_f g V_s$, upward.

Swipe sideways to see the whole figure.
(a) The floating cube: weight $\rho_h g V_h$ at its centre of gravity, buoyancy $\rho_f g V_s$ at the centroid of the submerged part. (b) The cube displaced $V_s$; once melted, the same mass occupies $V_a = \rho_h V_h/\rho_a$; the difference $V_d = V_a - V_s$ is the volume the liquid gains
Open the figure full size

Vertical equilibrium of the cube (upward positive):

$$ \rho_f g V_s - \rho_h g V_h = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_s = \frac{\rho_h}{\rho_f}\,V_h $$

Since $\rho_h < \rho_f$, $V_s < V_h$: part of the cube emerges, as assumed.

Melting: Conservation of Mass

When the ice melts, its mass $\rho_h V_h$ is conserved and becomes water of density $\rho_a$:

$$ \rho_a V_a = \rho_h V_h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_a = \frac{\rho_h}{\rho_a}\,V_h $$

Before melting, the cube occupied the volume $V_s$ below the free surface; after melting, the melt water occupies $V_a$ in the liquid. The volume of liquid below the free surface therefore grows by

$$ V_d = V_a - V_s = \rho_h V_h\left(\frac{1}{\rho_a} - \frac{1}{\rho_f}\right) = \frac{\rho_h}{\rho_a}\,V_h\left(1 - \frac{\rho_a}{\rho_f}\right) $$

In a container of horizontal section $S$, the free surface rises by $V_d/S$.

Answers and Checks

  • Submerged volume: $V_s = (\rho_h/\rho_f)\,V_h$.
  • Extra volume after melting: $V_d = (\rho_h/\rho_a)\,V_h\,(1 - \rho_a/\rho_f)$. With $\rho_a < \rho_f$ it is positive: the level rises, because the melt water is lighter than the fluid it displaced.
  • Particular case $\rho_f = \rho_a$ (ice floating in its own water): $V_d = 0$. The melt water fills exactly the hole the cube had made, and the level does not change.
  • Limiting case $\rho_h \to \rho_f$: $V_s \to V_h$, the cube is just fully submerged.
  • Dimensions: $V_d$ is a ratio of densities times $V_h$, so m$^3$; both brackets are dimensionless.
  • Check against the official key: fluid_statics_1.pdf gives $V_s = (\rho_h/\rho_f)V_h$ and $V_d = (\rho_h/\rho_a)V_h(1 - \rho_a/\rho_f)$, the same results.
Problem FS2

Hemispherical Station on the Sea Floor

Original statement · fluid_statics_2.pdf

Official Problem Statement

A hemispherical experimental station of radius $R$ is to be installed on the ocean floor at a depth $H$. Calculate the force, indicating its direction and sense, exerted on the walls of the station, knowing that the pressure inside is equal to the atmospheric pressure, $p_a$.

Source: fluid_statics_2.pdf and fluid_statics_8.pdf (page 1, sheet of 29-01-01). Notes.pdf, Chapter 5: Eqs. 5.10, 5.11 and 5.14.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Sea water at rest, incompressible, density $\rho$; the free surface is at the atmospheric pressure $p_a$, the depth of the floor is $H > R$.
  • The walls form a thin hemispherical shell of radius $R$ resting on the floor; inside, the air is at $p_a$. The force asked for is the net pressure force on the shell (water outside plus air inside); the floor of the station is not part of the walls.
  • Data: $R$, $H$, $\rho$, $g$, $p_a$. Unknown: the force $\vec{F}$.
SymbolMeaningSI unit
$R$radius of the stationm
$H$depth of the sea floorm
$\rho$density of sea waterkg/m$^3$

Coordinates, Surface Element and Normal

Take the origin $O$ at the centre of the base of the hemisphere, $z$ vertical and pointing up, so the floor is $z = 0$ and the free surface $z = H$. A point of the shell is located by its elevation $\varphi$ above the floor plane ($0 \leq \varphi \leq \pi/2$) and its azimuth $\theta$ ($0 \leq \theta < 2\pi$). In the meridian section (right triangle with the radius $R$ as hypotenuse), the height $z = R\sin\varphi$ is the leg opposite to $\varphi$ and the distance to the axis $R\cos\varphi$ the adjacent leg.

The element of the sphere is a small rectangle with sides $R\,d\varphi$ (along the meridian) and $R\cos\varphi\,d\theta$ (along the parallel, a circle of radius $R\cos\varphi$):

$$ dS = R^2\cos\varphi\,d\theta\,d\varphi $$

The unit normal towards the water is the radial vector $\vec{e}_r = \cos\varphi\cos\theta\,\vec{e}_x + \cos\varphi\sin\theta\,\vec{e}_y + \sin\varphi\,\vec{e}_z$; its vertical component $n_z = \sin\varphi$ is the leg opposite to $\varphi$ of the unit right triangle.

The Pressure and the Net Elementary Force

By (5.10), at the height $z$ (depth $H - z$): $p = p_a + \rho g (H - z) = p_a + \rho g (H - R\sin\varphi)$.

The water pushes the outer face with $-p\,\vec{e}_r\,dS$; the air pushes the inner face with $-p_a(-\vec{e}_r)\,dS$, since the normal towards the air is $-\vec{e}_r$. Their sum is

$$ d\vec{F} = -(p - p_a)\,\vec{e}_r\,dS = -\rho g (H - R\sin\varphi)\,\vec{e}_r\,R^2\cos\varphi\,d\theta\,d\varphi $$

Integration

Horizontal components. $F_x$ contains $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta = [\sin\theta]_0^{2\pi} = 0$, and $F_y$ contains $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta = [-\cos\theta]_0^{2\pi} = 0$: by the axial symmetry the horizontal forces cancel.

Vertical component. With $n_z = \sin\varphi$, the $\theta$ integral of a function independent of $\theta$ gives $2\pi$:

$$ F_z = -\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\rho g (H - R\sin\varphi)\sin\varphi\,R^2\cos\varphi\,d\theta\,d\varphi = -2\pi R^2\rho g\int_0^{\pi/2}\left(H\sin\varphi\cos\varphi - R\sin^2\varphi\cos\varphi\right)d\varphi $$

Both terms are integrated with the substitution $u = \sin\varphi$, $du = \cos\varphi\,d\varphi$, with $u = 0$ at $\varphi = 0$ and $u = 1$ at $\varphi = \pi/2$:

$$ \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\,d\varphi = \int_0^1 u\,du = \left[\frac{u^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\varphi\cos\varphi\,d\varphi = \int_0^1 u^2\,du = \left[\frac{u^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{3} $$

Therefore

$$ F_z = -2\pi R^2\rho g\left(\frac{H}{2} - \frac{R}{3}\right) = -\pi R^2\rho g\left(H - \frac{2R}{3}\right) $$

Check with the Projection Rule

Projection rule (Eq. 5.14): $F_z$ is the weight of the water that would stand above the hemisphere, the cylinder $\pi R^2 H$ minus the hemisphere $\tfrac{2}{3}\pi R^3$; it points down
Open the figure full size

By (5.14), the vertical force on a surface with the water above it is the weight of the water column standing on it, down to the surface: the cylinder of radius $R$ and height $H$, $\pi R^2 H$, minus the volume of the hemisphere, $\tfrac{2}{3}\pi R^3$, which is not water:

$$ F_z = -\rho g\left(\pi R^2 H - \frac{2}{3}\pi R^3\right) = -\pi R^2\rho g\left(H - \frac{2R}{3}\right) $$

the same result. The air inside contributes nothing net because $p_a$ also acts at the top of the column.

Answers and Checks

  • $\vec{F} = -\pi R^2\rho g\,(H - 2R/3)\,\vec{e}_z$: vertical, downward (since $H > R > 2R/3$), pressing the station against the floor; no horizontal component.
  • Limiting case of a small station, $R \ll H$: $F_z \approx -\rho g H\,\pi R^2$, the gauge pressure at the floor times the base area, as for a flat lid; the term $2R/3$ comes from the hemisphere volume, which is not water.
  • Physical reading: the station is not lifted by Archimedes' principle, because no water reaches its base: the buoyancy formula (5.15) applies only to a surface closed by water.
  • Dimensions: $\rho g R^2 H$ is (kg m$^{-3}$)(m s$^{-2}$)(m$^3$) = N.
  • Check against the official key: fluid_statics_2.pdf (and its second scan fluid_statics_8.pdf) sets up the same integral, $F_z = -\int\!\!\int\rho g(H - R\sin\varphi)\sin\varphi\,R^2\cos\varphi\,d\theta\,d\varphi$, and reaches $-2\pi R^2\rho g[H\sin^2\varphi/2 - R\sin^3\varphi/3]_0^{\pi/2}$, which equals the result above.
Discrepancy with the official solution

The handwritten last line reads $-\pi R^2\rho g\,[H - 2R/3]\,\vec{e}_z$ preceded by a mark that looks like a struck-out 4 (reading of handwriting). The previous line of the key, $-2\pi R^2\rho g\,[H\sin^2\varphi/2 - R\sin^3\varphi/3]_0^{\pi/2}$, evaluates to $-2\pi R^2\rho g\,(H/2 - R/3) = -\pi R^2\rho g\,(H - 2R/3)$, coefficient $\pi$, as above.

Problem FS3

Hollow Cube with Trapped Air

Official Problem Statement

A hollow cube of side $L$ and weight $W < \rho g L^3$ is initially full of air at the ambient pressure $p_a$ and temperature $T_a$. At a certain instant, the cube is placed on the surface of a liquid of density $\rho$. The liquid enters the cube through an orifice in its base until an equilibrium position is reached, characterized by the lengths $H_1$ and $H_2$ of the attached figure. Assuming that the evolution of the air inside the cube is isothermal, determine the values of $H_1/L$ and $H_2/L$, as well as the final pressure inside the cube, $p_c$.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Liquid and air at rest; the liquid is incompressible; the outside air is at the uniform pressure $p_a$.
  • The trapped air has a uniform pressure $p_c$ (its weight and the change of its pressure over a height $L$ are negligible, Section 7 of Topic 5) and keeps the temperature $T_a$: isothermal process, $p\,V = \text{const}$ from the state equation (5.18) at constant $T$ and constant mass.
  • Thin walls: the volume of the walls is neglected; $W$ is the weight of the cube alone (the air weight is negligible).
  • The cube floats upright with its top face above the outer free surface (checked at the end).
  • Data: $L$, $W$, $\rho$, $g$, $p_a$. Unknowns: $H_1$ (depth of the inner interface below the outer free surface), $H_2$ (height of the inner liquid layer above the base) and $p_c$.

Coordinates and the Free-Body Diagram

Take the vertical axis pointing up. The system for the force balance is the shell of the cube alone (not the air, not the liquid inside). Its side walls are vertical, so the pressure on them is horizontal and adds up to zero by symmetry; its base has an orifice and is wetted by the same liquid on both faces at the same depth, so its net pressure force is zero (thin wall). Only the top face carries a net vertical pressure force: $p_a L^2$ downward from the outside air and $p_c L^2$ upward from the trapped air.

Equilibrium of the Shell

Vertical equilibrium of the shell (upward positive):

$$ p_c L^2 - p_a L^2 - W = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_c = p_a + \frac{W}{L^2} $$

The Hydrostatic Law between the Two Free Surfaces

The liquid inside the cube is connected with the outside liquid through the orifice, so (5.10) holds through both. Walking from a point of the outer free surface, where $p = p_a$, down a depth $H_1$ to the level of the inner interface (any path inside the connected liquid gives the same result), the pressure grows by $\rho g H_1$; at the interface it equals the air pressure (Section 1 of Topic 5, no surface tension):

$$ p_c = p_a + \rho g H_1 $$

Equating the two expressions of $p_c$:

$$ \rho g H_1 = \frac{W}{L^2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{H_1}{L} = \frac{W}{\rho g L^3} $$

The hypothesis $W < \rho g L^3$ gives $H_1 < L$.

The Trapped Air: Isothermal Compression

Initially the air fills the whole cube, $V_0 = L^3$, at $p_a$. At equilibrium it fills the part of the cube above the inner interface, a prism of base $L^2$ and height $L - H_2$, at $p_c$. With the same mass and the same temperature, $p\,V$ is constant:

$$ p_a L^3 = p_c L^2 (L - H_2) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{L - H_2}{L} = \frac{p_a}{p_c} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{H_2}{L} = 1 - \frac{p_a}{p_c} = \frac{p_c - p_a}{p_c} $$

and with $p_c - p_a = W/L^2$ and $p_c = p_a + W/L^2$, multiplying numerator and denominator by $L^2$:

$$ \frac{H_2}{L} = \frac{W/L^2}{p_a + W/L^2} = \frac{W}{p_a L^2 + W} $$

Answers and Checks

  • $H_1/L = W/(\rho g L^3)$, $H_2/L = W/(p_a L^2 + W)$, $p_c = p_a + W/L^2$.
  • Archimedes check. For the system cube plus trapped air, the displaced liquid is the air below the outer free surface, a prism of base $L^2$ and height $H_1$ (from the outer level down to the inner interface). Its weight $\rho g L^2 H_1 = W$ balances the cube, which is the same equation as above.
  • Limiting cases. $W \to 0$: $H_1 \to 0$, $H_2 \to 0$, $p_c \to p_a$ (a weightless box lets no liquid in). $p_a \to \infty$ (incompressible air): $H_2 \to 0$, the air keeps its volume.
  • Consistency. The top face is above the outer free surface when $H_1 + H_2 < L$, that is $W/(\rho g L^3) + W/(p_a L^2 + W) < 1$. For water and $L = 1$ m, $p_a/(\rho g L) \approx 10.3$, so $H_2 \ll H_1$: the air is barely compressed and the cube floats almost as a closed box.
  • Dimensions. $W/(\rho g L^3)$: N / (kg m$^{-3}$ m s$^{-2}$ m$^3$) = N/N, dimensionless; $W/(p_a L^2)$: N/(Pa m$^2$) = N/N; $W/L^2$ in Pa.
  • Check against the official key. fluid_statics_3.pdf gives $H_1 = W/(\rho g L^2)$, $p_c = p_a + W/L^2$ and $H_2/L = 1 - p_a/p_c = W/(p_a L^2 + W)$, the same results.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Water at rest, incompressible, density $\rho$; air at $p_a$ above the water and on the outer face of the valve, so only the gauge pressure counts (Section 4 of Topic 5).
  • The valve is a vertical square plate of side $L$ with its lower edge on the bottom of the tank and its top edge at the depth $h - L$ below the free surface ($h \geq L$).
  • The axis is horizontal, in the plane of the valve, through its centre; friction on the axis is neglected; the weight of the valve acts through the axis (homogeneous plate) and has no moment about it.
  • $F_A$ is horizontal and acts at the base of the valve, as drawn.
  • Data: $L$, $h$, $\rho$, $g$. Unknowns: $F$ and $F_A$.

Coordinates and the Surface Element

Take $z$ vertical, pointing up, with $z = 0$ at the bottom of the tank (the lower edge of the valve), so the valve covers $0 \leq z \leq L$ and the free surface is at $z = h$. The depth of a point is $h - z$, and (5.10) gives the gauge pressure

$$ p(z) - p_a = \rho g (h - z) $$

The strip of the valve between $z$ and $z + dz$ has the width $L$ (horizontal side of the square) and the area $dA = L\,dz$. The unit normal towards the water is $\vec{n} = -\vec{e}_x$ (the water is on the left, $x$ points to the right, out of the tank), so the elementary force $-(p - p_a)\vec{n}\,dA = (p - p_a)\,dA\,\vec{e}_x$ pushes the valve outward. The axis is at $z = L/2$; the strip is at the signed distance $L/2 - z$ below it.

(a) The Force on the Valve

$$ F = \int_0^L \rho g (h - z)\,L\,dz = \rho g L\left[hz - \frac{z^2}{2}\right]_0^L = \rho g L\left(hL - \frac{L^2}{2}\right) - 0 = \rho g L^2\left(h - \frac{L}{2}\right) $$

horizontal, pointing out of the tank ($+\vec{e}_x$). Check with the centroid (Section 4.1 of Topic 5): the centroid of the square is at the depth $h - L/2$, so $F = \rho g (h - L/2) L^2$, the same.

(b) The Moment about the Axis and the Holding Force

The strip below the axis ($z < L/2$) carries more pressure than the strip at the same distance above it, so the water tends to turn the valve with its lower edge outward. The moment about the axis, counted positive in that sense, is the force of each strip times its arm $L/2 - z$ (negative above the axis, where the strip turns the valve the other way):

$$ M = \int_0^L \left(\frac{L}{2} - z\right)\rho g (h - z)\,L\,dz $$

Expanding the integrand, $(L/2 - z)(h - z) = \tfrac{Lh}{2} - z\left(\tfrac{L}{2} + h\right) + z^2$, and integrating term by term with Barrow's rule between $0$ and $L$:

$$ M = \rho g L\left[\frac{Lh}{2}z - \frac{z^2}{2}\left(\frac{L}{2} + h\right) + \frac{z^3}{3}\right]_0^L = \rho g L\left(\frac{L^2 h}{2} - \frac{L^3}{4} - \frac{L^2 h}{2} + \frac{L^3}{3}\right) = \rho g L\cdot\frac{L^3}{12} = \frac{\rho g L^4}{12} $$

The terms in $h$ cancel: the moment does not depend on the water depth.

Moment balance of the valve about its axis: the axis reaction and the weight pass through the axis; $F_A$, at the base, has the arm $L/2$ and must turn the valve the other way:

$$ F_A\,\frac{L}{2} = \frac{\rho g L^4}{12} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F_A = \frac{\rho g L^3}{6} $$

pointing into the tank ($-\vec{e}_x$).

Check with the centre of pressure. The centre of pressure is below the centroid by $e = I_C/(s_C A)$ (Section 4.1, with $\theta = 90^\circ$, $s$ = depth): $I_C = L\cdot L^3/12$, $s_C = h - L/2$, $A = L^2$, so

$$ e = \frac{L^4/12}{(h - L/2)L^2} = \frac{L^2}{12\,(h - L/2)}, \qquad F\,e = \rho g L^2\left(h - \frac{L}{2}\right)\frac{L^2}{12\,(h - L/2)} = \frac{\rho g L^4}{12} $$

As the depth grows the centre of pressure approaches the centre ($e \to 0$) but the force grows in the same proportion, so the moment stays $\rho g I_C$ (the general result $\rho g\sin\theta\,I_C$ of Section 4.1).

Answers and Checks

  • (a) $F = \rho g L^2 (h - L/2)$, horizontal, out of the tank, at the depth $h - L/2 + L^2/(12(h - L/2))$.

Moments about the Hinge

Moment of liquid 1 (turning the gate towards liquid 2):

$$ M_1 = \int_0^{H_1}\rho_1 g (H_1 - z)\,z\,b\,dz = \rho_1 g b\left[\frac{H_1 z^2}{2} - \frac{z^3}{3}\right]_0^{H_1} = \rho_1 g b\left(\frac{H_1^3}{2} - \frac{H_1^3}{3}\right) = \frac{\rho_1 g b H_1^3}{6} $$

Moment of liquid 2 (turning it back), with the same integral and $H_2$ in place of $H_1$:

$$ M_2 = \int_0^{H_2}\rho_2 g (H_2 - z)\,z\,b\,dz = \frac{\rho_2 g b H_2^3}{6} $$

Equilibrium and the Critical Level

Moment balance about the hinge, with the stop reaction $R$ (pushing towards liquid 1) acting at the top, arm $H_1$:

$$ M_1 - M_2 - R\,H_1 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R = \frac{g b\,(\rho_1 H_1^3 - \rho_2 H_2^3)}{6 H_1} $$

$$ \rho_1 H_1^3 = \rho_2 H_c^3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_c = H_1\left(\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right)^{1/3} $$

For $H_2 > H_c$ the moment of liquid 2 wins, the gate leaves the stop and turns towards liquid 1.

  • Consistency with the statement: $\rho_2 > \rho_1$ gives $H_c < H_1$, so the critical level lies within the gate, as it must for $H_2 < H_1$.
  • Limiting case $\rho_2 = \rho_1$: $H_c = H_1$, two equal columns of the same liquid balance exactly.
  • Not the force balance: the horizontal forces are equal when $\rho_1 H_1^2 = \rho_2 H_2^2$, which gives a different level, $H_1(\rho_1/\rho_2)^{1/2}$; the gate turns about the hinge, so the moments decide (the difference is taken by the hinge reaction).
  • Dimensions: $H_c$ is $H_1$ times a dimensionless ratio, m; $R$ is (m s$^{-2}$)(m)(kg m$^{-3}$ m$^3$)/m = N.

Geometry: Similar Equilateral Triangles

The height of the equilateral triangle is $L\sqrt{3}/2$: in the right triangle formed by the height, half the base $L/2$ and a side $L$ (hypotenuse), Pythagoras gives $\sqrt{L^2 - L^2/4} = L\sqrt{3}/2$.

Equation 1: Isothermal Air

Initially the air fills the whole triangle, $A_0 = \sqrt{3}L^2/4$, at $p_a$; at equilibrium it fills the air triangle $A = a^2/\sqrt{3}$ at $p$. With constant mass and temperature, $p\,A = p_a A_0$:

$$ \frac{p}{p_a} = \frac{A_0}{A} = \frac{\sqrt{3}L^2/4}{a^2/\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3L^2}{4a^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,\frac{1}{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{h}{L} - \frac{H}{L}\right)^2} $$

Equation 2: the Hydrostatic Law

The water inside is connected with the outside through the orifice. From the outer free surface ($p = p_a$) down to the depth $h$ of the inner level, (5.10) adds $\rho g h$, and at the interface the water pressure equals the air pressure:

$$ p = p_a + \rho g h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{p}{p_a} = 1 + \frac{\rho g L}{p_a}\,\frac{h}{L} $$

$$ \frac{W}{\rho g L^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{h}{L} - \frac{H}{L}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{H}{L}\right)^2\right] $$

$$ \frac{p}{p_a} = \frac{3}{4\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{h}{L} - \frac{H}{L}\right)^2}, \qquad \frac{p}{p_a} = 1 + \frac{\rho g L}{p_a}\,\frac{h}{L}, \qquad \frac{W}{\rho g L^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{h}{L} - \frac{H}{L}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{H}{L}\right)^2\right] $$

  • Expanding the bracket of the third equation: $W/(\rho g L^2) = (h/L)\,[\sqrt{3} - 2H/L + h/L]/\sqrt{3}$, which vanishes for $h = 0$: with no compression there is no displaced air and no buoyancy.

Take $z$ vertical, pointing up, with $z = 0$ at the hinge; the gate covers $-h \leq z \leq l$, and the free surface is at $z = H + l$. The depth of a point is $H + l - z$, so by (5.10)

$$ p(z) - p_a = \rho g (H + l - z) $$

The strip between $z$ and $z + dz$ has the area $b\,dz$ and the horizontal force $(p - p_a)\,b\,dz$ towards the dry side; its arm about the hinge is $z$. Strips above the hinge ($z > 0$) turn the gate with the top edge out (opening); strips below it ($z < 0$) turn it with the lower edge out, against the stop.

Moment about the Hinge

Counting the opening sense as positive, the signed arm $z$ takes care of both parts at once:

$$ M = \int_{-h}^{l}\rho g (H + l - z)\,z\,b\,dz = \rho g b\left[(H + l)\frac{z^2}{2} - \frac{z^3}{3}\right]_{-h}^{l} $$

Barrow's rule, with $(-h)^2 = h^2$ and $(-h)^3 = -h^3$:

$$ M = \rho g b\left[(H + l)\frac{l^2 - h^2}{2} - \frac{l^3 + h^3}{3}\right] $$

Opening Condition

While $M < 0$ the water presses the lower edge against the stop and the gate stays closed. It opens when $M$ reaches zero and becomes positive; $M$ grows with $H$ because $l^2 - h^2 > 0$ ($h < l$). Setting $M = 0$:

$$ (H + l)\frac{l^2 - h^2}{2} = \frac{l^3 + h^3}{3} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H + l = \frac{2}{3}\,\frac{l^3 + h^3}{l^2 - h^2} $$

With $l^3 + h^3 = (l + h)(l^2 - lh + h^2)$ and $l^2 - h^2 = (l + h)(l - h)$:

$$ H = \frac{2(l^2 - lh + h^2)}{3(l - h)} - l = \frac{2l^2 - 2lh + 2h^2 - 3l^2 + 3lh}{3(l - h)} = \frac{(l + h)(2h - l)}{3(l - h)} $$

The gate opens for any water level above this value.

Interpretation with the centre of pressure. $M = 0$ means that the resultant passes through the hinge: the centre of pressure is at the hinge. As $H$ grows, the centre of pressure rises towards the centroid of the gate (Section 4.1 of Topic 5), which is $(l - h)/2$ above the hinge; the gate opens when the centre of pressure crosses the hinge from below.

Answers and Checks

  • $H + l = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\dfrac{l^3 + h^3}{l^2 - h^2}$, that is $H = \dfrac{(l + h)(2h - l)}{3(l - h)}$.
  • When is it positive? Only for $l/2 < h < l$. If $h \leq l/2$ the formula gives $H \leq 0$: even with the free surface at the top edge the centre of pressure is already above the hinge (for a triangular distribution it sits at a third of the height from the bottom, $(l + h)/3$ above the floor, which is above the hinge when $h < (l + h)/3$, i.e. $h < l/2$), so the gate cannot be kept closed. At $h = l/2$ the centre of pressure of the triangle is exactly at the hinge, $H = 0$.
  • Limiting case $h \to l$ (hinge at the centre): $H \to \infty$; the centre of pressure is always below the centroid, so a gate hinged at its centre never opens (compare with FS4).
  • Dimensions: $H$ is m (cubic over quadratic lengths).
  • Check against the official key: fluid_statics_9.pdf gives $M = \rho g[(H + l)(l^2 - h^2)/2 - (l^3 + h^3)/3]$ and $H + l = \tfrac{2}{3}(l^3 + h^3)/(l^2 - h^2)$, the same results.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Water at rest, incompressible, $\rho = 1000$ kg/m$^3$, $g = 9.81$ m/s$^2$; air at $p_a$ above both free surfaces and on the dry parts of the cylinder, so only gauge pressures count.
  • The cylinder, of diameter $D = 2R = 1$ m and length $L = 5$ m, lies on the floor with its axis horizontal and normal to the paper; end effects are neglected (the end faces are vertical, and their horizontal forces act along the axis, normal to the paper, so they do not enter the net force in the plane of the figure).
  • Water on the left up to $2R = 1$ m (the top of the cylinder); on the right up to $R = 0.5$ m (its centre).
  • Unknown: the net horizontal force $F_x$ in the plane of the figure.

Coordinates, Element and Normal

Take $y$ vertical and up from the floor, $x$ horizontal to the right, and measure the angle $\theta$ from the upward vertical through the centre $C$ (at $y = R$). In the right triangle with the radius $R$ as hypotenuse, the vertical leg $R\cos\theta$ is adjacent to $\theta$ and the horizontal leg $R\sin\theta$ opposite, so a point of the left half is at

$$ x = -R\sin\theta, \qquad y = R + R\cos\theta, \qquad 0 \leq \theta \leq \pi $$

and a point of the right half at $x = +R\sin\theta$, same $y$. The element of the surface is the arc $R\,d\theta$ times the length $L$: $dS = LR\,d\theta$. The unit normal towards the water is the outward radial vector, with the horizontal component $n_x = -\sin\theta$ on the left half and $n_x = +\sin\theta$ on the right half.

Left Half

The water on the left reaches $y = 2R$, so the depth of a point is $2R - y = R - R\cos\theta$ and, by (5.10), $p - p_a = \rho g R(1 - \cos\theta)$, over the whole left half ($0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$). The horizontal component of $-(p - p_a)\vec{n}\,dS$:

$$ F_1 = -\int_0^{\pi}\rho g R(1 - \cos\theta)(-\sin\theta)\,LR\,d\theta = \rho g R^2 L\int_0^{\pi}\left(\sin\theta - \sin\theta\cos\theta\right)d\theta $$

With $\int\sin\theta\,d\theta = -\cos\theta$ and $\int\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta = \sin^2\theta/2$ (substitution $u = \sin\theta$, $du = \cos\theta\,d\theta$), by Barrow's rule:

$$ F_1 = \rho g R^2 L\left[-\cos\theta - \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2}\right]_0^{\pi} = \rho g R^2 L\left[(1 - 0) - (-1 - 0)\right] = 2\rho g R^2 L $$

pointing to the right.

Right Half

The water on the right reaches $y = R$, so only the lower right quarter is wetted, $\pi/2 \leq \theta \leq \pi$, with the depth $R - y = -R\cos\theta$ (positive there, since $\cos\theta \leq 0$):

$$ F_2 = -\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi}\rho g(-R\cos\theta)(\sin\theta)\,LR\,d\theta = \rho g R^2 L\left[\frac{\sin^2\theta}{2}\right]_{\pi/2}^{\pi} = \rho g R^2 L\left(0 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = -\frac{\rho g R^2 L}{2} $$

pointing to the left.

Check with the Projection Rule

By (5.14), the horizontal force on each side is the force on the vertical projection of the wetted surface. On the left, the projection is a rectangle of height $2R$ and length $L$ with its centroid at the depth $R$: $\rho g R\cdot 2RL = 2\rho g R^2 L$. On the right, it is a rectangle of height $R$ with its centroid at the depth $R/2$: $\rho g (R/2)\cdot RL = \rho g R^2 L/2$. Both agree with the integrals.

Answers and Checks

$$ F_x = F_1 + F_2 = 2\rho g R^2 L - \frac{\rho g R^2 L}{2} = \frac{3}{2}\rho g R^2 L = \frac{3}{2}(1000)(9.81)(0.5)^2(5) = 18\,394\ \text{N} \approx 18.4\ \text{kN} $$

pointing to the right, from the deeper side to the shallower one.

  • Limiting case equal levels on both sides: the two projections are equal and $F_x = 0$.
  • Dimensions: $\rho g R^2 L$ is (kg m$^{-3}$)(m s$^{-2}$)(m$^3$) = N.
  • Beyond the statement: the vertical force is upward and equals the weight of the water displaced by each wetted part (a half disc on the left, a quarter disc on the right); it is shown in the simulator but not asked for.
  • Check against the official key: fluid_statics_10.pdf gives $F_1 = 2\rho g R^2 L$ and boxes $F_2 = -\rho g L R^2/2$ (the minus sign is the leftward sense, as above), then writes $F_T = F_1 - F_2 = \tfrac{3}{2}\rho g L R^2$ with $F_2$ taken as a magnitude; the net force is the same.
