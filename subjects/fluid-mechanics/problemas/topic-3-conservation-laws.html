Solved Problems: Official Collection CL1 to CL5

Worked solutions to the conservation-laws collection of the Department of Thermal and Fluid Engineering, Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (5 of the 22 problems so far; the rest of the collection is added in batches). Every problem restates the official statement, then follows four phases: (1) hypotheses, data and unknowns; (2) frames, control volume and its surfaces; (3) the balances; (4) interpretation, limits and dimensional check. The balances follow the method used in class: each one is written in full with the vanishing terms crossed out and the reason underneath, every surface integral is then evaluated surface by surface (the vertical line drawn under each integral), the wall terms are grouped (boxed) as the force on the body, and each result is boxed and numbered. Discrepancies found in the handwritten official solutions are flagged in the corresponding problem, and anything beyond the course material is marked as an extension. References to Notes.pdf are to Chapter 3 (Conservation laws).

Problem CL1

Airfoil Wake: Streamline Displacement and Drag

Original statement · CL1.pdf
Control volumeLinear momentumWakeDrag

Official Problem Statement

The experimental tests of an airfoil in a wind tunnel indicate that the velocity profile upstream remains uniform, whereas behind the airfoil there exists a wake region with significant velocity deficit whose thickness increases downstream, resulting in the flow field sketched in the figure of CL1.pdf. For the airfoil tested, the velocity measurements at a given downstream section reveal that the velocity in the wake varies linearly from the value $u = U/2$ at the centerline to reach the value $u = U$ at the edge of the wake, located at $y = y_0$. To analyze the problem, consider a control volume limited laterally by the streamlines that extend from $x \to -\infty$, $y = \pm y_{-\infty}$ ($y_{-\infty} < y_0$) to $x = x_0$, $y = \pm y_0$ and internally by the surface of the airfoil. Assuming that the air behaves as an incompressible fluid with density $\rho$ and that viscous stresses are negligible both in the wake and along the bounding streamlines, obtain the value of $y_{-\infty}$ as a function of $y_0$, as well as the value of the drag on the airfoil as a function of $\rho$, $y_0$ and $U$. In the analysis, assume that on the outer boundary of the control volume the pressure differences from the ambient value $p_\infty$ are negligible, and so is the effect of gravity on the air flow.

Source: CL1.pdf (page 1). Notes.pdf, Chapter 3: integral mass balance Eqs. 3.10-3.11, integral momentum balance Eqs. 3.35-3.36, force of the fluid on a body Eq. 3.32.

Worked solution (4-phase method)expand / collapse
Phase 1

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Steady, planar (everything per unit span), incompressible ($\rho$ constant); neglect $\bar{g}$.
  • Viscous stresses negligible in the wake and on the bounding streamlines; $p - p_\infty \simeq 0$ on the whole outer boundary of the control volume (given). On the airfoil surface pressure and viscous stresses are not neglected: they make the drag.
  • Wake profile at $x = x_0$ (symmetric about $y = 0$): $v_x = \tfrac{U}{2}\left(1 + \tfrac{|y|}{y_0}\right)$ for $|y| \le y_0$, and $v_x = U$ outside.
  • Data: $[\rho,\ U,\ y_0]$. Unknowns: $[y_{-\infty},\ D]$, with $D$ the drag per unit span.
SymbolMeaningSI unit
$U$uniform upstream velocitym/s
$y_0$half-width of the wake at $x = x_0$m
$y_{-\infty}$half-width of the stream tube far upstreamm
$\rho$air densitykg/m$^3$
$D$drag per unit spanN/m

Dimensional check of the unknown: $[\rho U^2 y_0] = (\text{kg/m}^3)(\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2)\,\text{m} = \text{N/m}$, a force per unit length, as required for a planar problem.

Phase 2

Frames, Control Volume and Surfaces

  • Inertial frame fixed to the airfoil and the tunnel; basis $(\bar{e}_x, \bar{e}_y, \bar{e}_z)$ with $\bar{e}_x$ along the stream. The control volume is fixed: $\bar{v}_c = 0$.
  • Surfaces of $\Sigma_c$ (outward normal $\bar{n}$): $\Sigma_1$, far upstream, $|y| < y_{-\infty}$, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_x$; $\Sigma_2$, the section $x = x_0$, $|y| < y_0$, $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_x$; $\Sigma_l$, the two bounding streamlines ($\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n} = 0$); $\Sigma_w$, the airfoil surface ($\bar{n}$ pointing into the airfoil).
  • Change of variable for the wake integrals: $s = 1 + y/y_0$, $dy = y_0\,ds$; $y \in [0, y_0]$ maps to $s \in [1, 2]$.

Force of the air on the airfoil (Notes Eq. 3.32), written on the wetted surface with the normal $\bar{n}$ of the control volume:

$$ \boxed{\,\bar{F} = \int_{\Sigma_w}(p - p_\infty)\,\bar{n}\,d\sigma - \int_{\Sigma_w}\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma\,} $$

Hence the $\Sigma_w$ terms of the momentum balance add up to $-\bar{F}$.

Phase 3

Balances, Surface by Surface

Mass (Notes Eq. 3.11)
$$ \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V_c}\rho\,dV}}_{0\ (\text{steady})} + \int_{\Sigma_c}\rho\,(\bar{v} - \underbrace{\cancel{\bar{v}_c}}_{0})\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma = 0 $$

Each surface integral, surface by surface (the vertical line drawn under each integral in class):

Surface$\int\rho\,\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma$
$\Sigma_1$$\rho\,U\,(-1)\,2y_{-\infty} = -2\rho U y_{-\infty}$
$\Sigma_2$$2\rho\int_0^{y_0}\tfrac{U}{2}\left(1 + \tfrac{y}{y_0}\right)dy = \tfrac{3}{2}\rho U y_0$
$\Sigma_l$$0$ (streamlines, $\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n} = 0$)
$\Sigma_w$$0$ (impermeable wall)
$$ -2\rho U y_{-\infty} + \frac{3}{2}\rho U y_0 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,y_{-\infty} = \frac{3}{4}\,y_0\,} \tag{1} $$

The $\Sigma_2$ entry in detail: the antiderivative of $1 + y/y_0$ is $y + y^2/(2y_0)$, and by Barrow's rule between $y = 0$ and $y = y_0$, $\rho U\left[y + \frac{y^2}{2y_0}\right]_0^{y_0} = \rho U\left(y_0 + \frac{y_0}{2}\right) = \frac{3}{2}\rho U y_0$ (the factor 2 of the table counts both halves of the wake).

Linear momentum (Notes Eq. 3.36, with $p_a = p_\infty$)
$$ \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V_c}\rho\bar{v}\,dV}}_{0\ (\text{steady})} + \int_{\Sigma_c}\rho\bar{v}\,[(\bar{v} - \underbrace{\cancel{\bar{v}_c}}_{0})\cdot\bar{n}]\,d\sigma = -\int_{\Sigma_c}(p - p_\infty)\,\bar{n}\,d\sigma + \int_{\Sigma_c}\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma + \underbrace{\cancel{\int_{V_c}\rho\bar{g}\,dV}}_{\text{neglect } \bar{g}} $$

Each surface integral, surface by surface (the vertical line drawn under each integral in class):

Surface$\int\rho\bar{v}\,(\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n})\,d\sigma$$-\int(p - p_\infty)\,\bar{n}\,d\sigma$$\int\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma$
$\Sigma_1$$\rho U\,(-U)\,2y_{-\infty}\,\bar{e}_x = -2\rho U^2y_{-\infty}\,\bar{e}_x$$0$ ($p \simeq p_\infty$)$0$ (uniform flow)
$\Sigma_2$$2\rho\int_0^{y_0}v_x^2\,dy\;\bar{e}_x = \tfrac{7}{6}\rho U^2 y_0\,\bar{e}_x$$0$ ($p \simeq p_\infty$)$0$ (given)
$\Sigma_l$$0$ ($\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n} = 0$)$0$ ($p \simeq p_\infty$)$0$ (given)
$\Sigma_w$$0$ (impermeable)$-\int_{\Sigma_w}(p - p_\infty)\,\bar{n}\,d\sigma$$\int_{\Sigma_w}\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma$

Boxed $\Sigma_w$ terms: the force of the airfoil on the air, $-\bar{F}$. The $\Sigma_1$ entry uses (1): $-2\rho U^2y_{-\infty} = -\tfrac{3}{2}\rho U^2 y_0$.

$$ -\frac{3}{2}\rho U^2y_0\,\bar{e}_x + \frac{7}{6}\rho U^2y_0\,\bar{e}_x = -\bar{F} \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,\bar{F} = D\,\bar{e}_x,\qquad D = \frac{1}{3}\,\rho\,U^2\,y_0\,} \tag{2} $$

The $\Sigma_2$ momentum entry in detail, with $s = 1 + y/y_0$, $dy = y_0\,ds$ and Barrow's rule between $s = 1$ and $s = 2$: $2\rho\frac{U^2}{4}\int_0^{y_0}\left(1 + \frac{y}{y_0}\right)^2dy = \frac{\rho U^2y_0}{2}\int_1^2 s^2\,ds = \frac{\rho U^2y_0}{2}\left[\frac{s^3}{3}\right]_1^2 = \frac{\rho U^2y_0}{2}\cdot\frac{8 - 1}{3} = \frac{7}{6}\rho U^2y_0$. The sum of the flux column is $\left(-\frac{9}{6} + \frac{7}{6}\right)\rho U^2y_0 = -\frac{1}{3}\rho U^2 y_0$.

For a wake symmetric about $y = 0$ (a symmetric airfoil at zero incidence, as the symmetric profile implies) the $y$ component of the momentum flux through $\Sigma_2$ cancels between the two halves, so $F_y = 0$: no lift.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • The wake carries less mass flux ($\tfrac{3}{2}\rho U y_0$) than the undisturbed stream through the same width ($2\rho U y_0$): the bounding streamlines are pushed outwards, from $y_{-\infty} = \tfrac{3}{4}y_0$ to $y_0$. The momentum deficit left in the wake is the drag.
  • Limit $y_0 \to 0$: no wake, $y_{-\infty} \to 0$ and $D \to 0$ (d'Alembert's paradox for a frictionless attached flow).
  • Dimensions (SI): $[y_{-\infty}] = \text{m}$; $[D] = [\rho U^2 y_0] = \text{N/m}$.
  • Numerical cross-check (midpoint quadrature of the defining integrals with $2\times10^5$ cells, $U = 1$ m/s, $\rho = 1.2$ kg/m$^3$, $y_0 = 0.35$ m): $y_{-\infty}/y_0 = 0.75000$ and $D = 0.140000$ N/m $= \rho U^2y_0/3$.
  • Agreement with the official solution: CL1.pdf obtains $y_{-\infty} = \tfrac{3}{4}y_0$ and $\bar{F}_{\text{airfoil}} = \tfrac{1}{3}\rho U^2 y_0\,\bar{e}_x$.
Extension (beyond the official solution)

With the momentum thickness $\theta = \int_{-y_0}^{y_0}\frac{v_x}{U}\left(1 - \frac{v_x}{U}\right)dy = 2y_0\int_0^1\frac{1 - \sigma^2}{4}\,d\sigma = \frac{y_0}{3}$ ($\sigma = y/y_0$), the result reads $D = \rho U^2\theta$, the classical momentum-deficit formula for the drag from a wake survey.

Problem CL2

Bore over a Stagnant Water Layer

Original statement · CL2.pdf
Moving frameMass and momentumHydrostatic pressureFroude number

Official Problem Statement

The bore sketched in the figure of CL2.pdf moves under the action of gravity with constant velocity $U$ over an initially stagnant water layer of thickness $h_0$, leaving behind a layer of thickness $h_1$ moving with velocity $U_1$. Assuming that in the air both viscous stresses and pressure variations with respect to the ambient value $p_a$ are negligible, and so are viscous stresses on the bottom wall, find the values of $U_1$ and $h_1$ as a function of $U$, $h_0$ and $g$. As a preliminary computation, obtain the pressure distributions both upstream and downstream from the bore, verifying that they can be expressed in the form $p = p_a + \rho g(h_0 - z)$ and $p = p_a + \rho g(h_1 - z)$, respectively, where $z$ represents the vertical distance to the bottom wall.

Source: CL2.pdf (page 1). Notes.pdf, Chapter 3: integral mass balance Eqs. 3.10-3.11, integral momentum balance Eqs. 3.35-3.36.

Worked solution (4-phase method)expand / collapse
Phase 1

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Planar flow (per unit width) of an incompressible liquid of density $\rho$, with gravity $\bar{g} = -g\,\bar{e}_z$ (here gravity is not neglected: it drives the bore).
  • The bore advances towards $-\bar{e}_x$ with constant velocity $U$ over the stagnant layer $h_0$; behind it the layer $h_1$ moves with $-U_1\bar{e}_x$. Far from the front the velocity is uniform and horizontal and the free surface is flat at $p = p_a$.
  • Viscous stresses negligible in the water sections and on the bottom wall (given); $\mu/(\rho U h) \ll 1$.
  • Data: $[U,\ h_0,\ g,\ \rho]$. Unknowns: $[h_1,\ U_1]$ and the pressure distributions.
SymbolMeaningSI unit
$U$velocity of the borem/s
$U_1$velocity of the water behind the borem/s
$h_0$, $h_1$layer thickness ahead of and behind the borem
$F = U^2/(gh_0)$Froude number of the bore1
Phase 2

Frames, Control Volume and Surfaces

  • Laboratory frame $L$, with $z$ measured from the bottom: unsteady flow, because the front moves.
  • Frame of the bore $B$, moving with $-U\bar{e}_x$ relative to $L$. It is inertial (constant velocity), so the balances keep their form, and the flow is steady in it. Velocities: $\bar{v}_B = \bar{v}_L + U\bar{e}_x$, so the water ahead has $U\bar{e}_x$ and the water behind $(U - U_1)\bar{e}_x$.
  • Control volume fixed in $B$ ($\bar{v}_c = 0$). Surfaces: $\Sigma_0$, vertical section ahead ($\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_x$, height $h_0$); $\Sigma_1$, vertical section behind ($\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_x$, height $h_1$); $\Sigma_b$, bottom wall ($\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_z$); $\Sigma_s$, free surface (steady material surface in $B$, $p = p_a$).
Phase 3

Balances, Surface by Surface

3.1 Preliminary: hydrostatic pressure in the uniform regions

Control volume: a column of width $\Delta x$ in the uniform region ahead, from height $z$ up to the free surface $z = h_0$. Surfaces: $\Sigma_{\text{bot}}$ (at height $z$, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_z$), $\Sigma_{\text{top}}$ (free surface, $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_z$) and two vertical faces $\Sigma_{\text{side}}$ ($\bar{n} = \mp\bar{e}_x$). Only the $z$ component is needed.

Momentum, z component, on the column (Notes Eq. 3.36)
$$ \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V_c}\rho v_z\,dV}}_{0} + \int_{\Sigma_c}\rho v_z\,(\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n})\,d\sigma = -\int_{\Sigma_c}p\,n_z\,d\sigma + \underbrace{\cancel{\int_{\Sigma_c}(\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n})_z\,d\sigma}}_{\text{negligible}} - \int_{V_c}\rho g\,dV $$

Each surface integral, surface by surface (the vertical line drawn under each integral in class):

Surface$\int\rho v_z\,(\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n})\,d\sigma$$-\int p\,n_z\,d\sigma$
$\Sigma_{\text{bot}}$$0$ ($v_z = 0$)$+p(z)\,\Delta x$
$\Sigma_{\text{top}}$$0$ ($v_z = 0$)$-p_a\,\Delta x$
$\Sigma_{\text{side}}$$0$ ($v_z = 0$)$0$ ($n_z = 0$)

Volume term: $-\int_{V_c}\rho g\,dV = -\rho g\,(h_0 - z)\,\Delta x$ (weight of the column).

$$ 0 = p(z)\,\Delta x - p_a\,\Delta x - \rho g\,(h_0 - z)\,\Delta x \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,p = p_a + \rho g\,(h_0 - z)\,} \tag{1} $$

The same column taken behind the bore gives $p = p_a + \rho g\,(h_1 - z)$ (1').

3.2 Mass and momentum in the frame of the bore

Mass (Notes Eq. 3.11), frame B
$$ \underbrace{\cancel{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V_c}\rho\,dV}}_{0\ (\text{steady})} + \int_{\Sigma_c}\rho\,(\bar{v} - \underbrace{\cancel{\bar{v}_c}}_{0})\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma = 0 $$

Each surface integral, surface by surface (the vertical line drawn under each integral in class):

Surface$\int\rho\,\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n}\,d\sigma$
$\Sigma_0$$\rho\,U\,(-1)\,h_0 = -\rho U h_0$
$\Sigma_1$$\rho\,(U - U_1)\,h_1$
$\Sigma_b$$0$ (wall)
$\Sigma_s$$0$ (material surface)
$$ \boxed{\,U\,h_0 = (U - U_1)\,h_1\,} \tag{2} $$

In the momentum balance $p$ may be replaced by $p - p_a$ because $\int_{\Sigma_c}p_a\bar{n}\,d\sigma = 0$ on a closed surface. The $x$ component is taken (gravity has no $x$ component). On $\Sigma_0$ and $\Sigma_1$ the pressure is (1) and (1'); the antiderivative of $\rho g(h - z)$ is $\rho g(hz - z^2/2)$, and Barrow's rule between $z = 0$ and $z = h$ gives $\rho g h^2/2$.

Each surface integral, surface by surface (the vertical line drawn under each integral in class):

Surface$\int\rho v_x\,(\bar{v}\cdot\bar{n})\,d\sigma$$-\int(p - p_a)\,n_x\,d\sigma$$\int(\bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n})_x\,d\sigma$
$\Sigma_0$$\rho U\,(-U)\,h_0 = -\rho U^2h_0$$+\int_0^{h_0}\rho g(h_0 - z)\,dz = \frac{\rho g h_0^2}{2}$$0$
$\Sigma_1$$\rho\,(U - U_1)^2\,h_1$$-\int_0^{h_1}\rho g(h_1 - z)\,dz = -\frac{\rho g h_1^2}{2}$$0$
$\Sigma_b$$0$$0$ ($n_x = 0$)$0$ (given)
$\Sigma_s$$0$$0$ ($p = p_a$)$0$ (air)
$$ \boxed{\,-\rho U^2 h_0 + \rho\,(U - U_1)^2\,h_1 = \frac{\rho g}{2}\left(h_0^2 - h_1^2\right)\,} \tag{3} $$

3.3 Solution for $h_1/h_0$ and $U_1/U$

From (2), $U - U_1 = Uh_0/h_1$, so $(U - U_1)^2h_1 = U^2h_0^2/h_1$. Substituting into (3) and dividing by $\rho$:

$$ U^2h_0\left(\frac{h_0}{h_1} - 1\right) = \frac{g}{2}(h_0 - h_1)(h_0 + h_1) \;\Longrightarrow\; U^2h_0\,\frac{h_0 - h_1}{h_1} = \frac{g}{2}(h_0 - h_1)(h_0 + h_1). $$

For a bore $h_1 \ne h_0$, so $h_0 - h_1$ cancels. With $r = h_1/h_0$ and $F = U^2/(gh_0)$, dividing by $gh_0^2$ gives $F/r = (1 + r)/2$:

$$ r^2 + r - 2F = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,\frac{h_1}{h_0} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8F} - 1\right)\,} \tag{4} $$

(the negative root is rejected, $h_1 > 0$). From (2), $U_1/U = 1 - h_0/h_1 = (r - 1)/r$; with $s = \sqrt{1 + 8F}$, $r = (s - 1)/2$ and

$$ \boxed{\,\frac{U_1}{U} = 1 - \frac{2}{s - 1} = \frac{\sqrt{1 + 8F} - 3}{\sqrt{1 + 8F} - 1}\,},\qquad F = \frac{U^2}{gh_0}. \tag{5} $$
Erratum in the official solution (reading of the handwriting)

CL2.pdf gives $h_1/h_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{1+8F} - 1)$, as in (4). For the velocity it gives, as legible in the scan, $U_1/U = \tfrac{3}{2}\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\sqrt{1+8F} - 1\right) = (\sqrt{1+8F} - 3)/2 = r - 1$. This contradicts its own continuity equation (2), which requires $U_1/U = (r - 1)/r$; the two coincide only for $r = 1$. Check: (2) gives $(U - U_1)h_1 = Uh_0 > 0$, hence $U_1 < U$, whereas $r - 1 > 1$ for $F > 3$ (for $F = 4$, $r - 1 = 1.3723$ against $U_1/U = 0.5785$ from (5)). The computed value is kept.

Phase 4

Interpretation, Limits and Dimensional Check

  • A bore exists only for $F > 1$: $r > 1 \iff s > 3 \iff F > 1$. $F$ compares the speed of the front with the shallow-water wave speed $\sqrt{gh_0}$.
  • Weak bore, $F = 1 + \epsilon$: $r \approx 1 + \tfrac{2}{3}\epsilon$ and $U_1/U \approx \tfrac{2}{3}\epsilon \to 0$ (an infinitesimal gravity wave travelling at $\sqrt{gh_0}$).
  • Strong bore, $F \gg 1$: $r \approx \sqrt{2F}$ and $U_1 \to U$.
  • Dimensions (SI): both sides of (3) are forces per unit width, $[\rho U^2h_0] = [\rho gh^2] = \text{N/m}$; $F$, $r$ and $U_1/U$ are dimensionless.
  • Numerical cross-check: $F = 4$, $h_0 = 0.5$ m, $g = 9.81$ m/s$^2$ give $U = 4.4294$ m/s, $h_1/h_0 = 2.3723$ ($h_1 = 1.1861$ m), $U_1/U = 0.57846$ ($U_1 = 2.5623$ m/s); the residuals of (2) and (3) are below $10^{-14}$ in SI units.
Extension (beyond the official solution)

Mechanical energy is not conserved across the bore, so Bernoulli's equation cannot be used between both sections. The head lost is the classical hydraulic-jump value $\Delta H = (h_1 - h_0)^3/(4h_0h_1)$, positive for $h_1 > h_0$.

Problem CL3

Double-Elbow Mouthpiece: Exit Velocity, Force and Moment

Original statement · CL3.pdf
Control volumeLinear momentumAngular momentumGauge pressure

Official Problem Statement

The double-elbow mouth piece of exit cross section $A_o$ shown in the figure of CL3.pdf is to be installed at the end of a circular pipe of cross section $A_p$ to redirect the outflow of water into the open atmosphere. Once the mouth piece is installed, the uniform values $U_p$ and $p_p$ are measured for the flow velocity and the pressure at the end of the pipe (i.e., just at the entrance of the mouth piece). Find the value of the uniform velocity at the exit section, $U_o$, as well as the force exerted on the mouth piece and its corresponding moment with respect to the entrance section. (Handwritten addition: neglect the effect of gravity.)

Source: CL3.pdf (page 1). Notes.pdf, Chapter 3: integral mass balance Eq. 3.11, integral momentum balance Eq. 3.36, integral angular momentum balance Eqs. 3.37-3.38, force and moment on a body Eqs. 3.32-3.33.

Phase 1

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Steady, incompressible (water); neglect $\bar{g}$ (handwritten addition); uniform properties at inlet and outlet; viscous stresses negligible on both sections.
  • Free jet at the exit: $p = p_a$ over $\Sigma_o$. The thin wall of the mouth piece has the atmosphere $p_a$ on its outer face; the force asked for is the net force of the water inside plus the atmosphere outside, as in the official solution. Ignoring the atmosphere would add $p_a(A_p - A_o)\,\bar{e}_x$.
  • Geometry (figure): S-shaped piece; the exit axis is a vertical distance $H$ below the entrance axis; both the entrance and the exit flows are along $+\bar{e}_x$.
  • Data: $[\rho,\ A_p,\ A_o,\ U_p,\ p_p,\ p_a,\ H]$. Unknowns: $[U_o,\ \bar{F}_{MP},\ \bar{M}_{MP}]$.

  • Dimensions (SI): $[\dot{m}(v_2 - v_1)] = \text{N}$; $[(\dot{Q} - \dot{W})/\dot{m}] = \text{J/kg} = \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 = [h] = [v^2]$.
  • Numerical illustration (hypothetical data): $A_1 = 0.5$ m$^2$, $A_2 = 0.3$ m$^2$, $v_1 = 150$ m/s, $p_1 = 0.9\times10^5$ Pa, $\rho_1 = 1.1$ kg/m$^3$ ($M_1 = 0.443$), $p_a = 10^5$ Pa, $F = 30$ kN, $\dot{W} = 2$ MW, $\dot{Q} = 30$ MW, $\gamma = 1.4$: $\dot{m} = 82.5$ kg/s and the two roots of the extension below, $v_2 = 355.515$ m/s, $p_2 = 126817$ Pa, $\rho_2 = 0.77353$ kg/m$^3$ ($M_2 = 0.742$), and $v_2 = 597.263$ m/s, $p_2 = 60336$ Pa, $\rho_2 = 0.46043$ kg/m$^3$ ($M_2 = 1.39$); residuals of (1) to (3) below $10^{-6}$ in SI units.
  • Agreement with the official and class solutions: the same equations (1), (2), (3) (CL5.pdf writes (2) as $F = \rho_1v_1A_1(v_2 - v_1) - (p_1 - p_a)A_1 + (p_2 - p_a)A_2$).
Extension (beyond the official solution): explicit solution

With $K = p_aA_2 + F + (p_1 - p_a)A_1 + \dot{m}v_1$, (2) gives $p_2A_2 = K - \dot{m}v_2$ and, with (1), $p_2/\rho_2 = (K - \dot{m}v_2)v_2/\dot{m}$. Substituting into (3) and using $\frac{1}{2} - \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} = -\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}$: $(\gamma + 1)v_2^2 - 2\gamma\frac{K}{\dot{m}}v_2 + 2(\gamma - 1)H_0 = 0$, with $H_0 = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{p_1}{\rho_1} + \frac{v_1^2}{2} + \frac{\dot{Q} - \dot{W}}{\dot{m}}$. The two roots are a subsonic and a supersonic exit state; for a subsonic entrance with heat addition the physical one is the subsonic (smaller) root, since reaching the other would require passing through sonic conditions inside the engine, which this overall balance cannot describe. For $F = 0$, $\dot{Q} = \dot{W} = 0$ and $A_1 = A_2$ one root is $v_2 = v_1$ and the product of the roots is $2(\gamma - 1)H_0/(\gamma + 1)$, the Prandtl relation of a normal shock.
