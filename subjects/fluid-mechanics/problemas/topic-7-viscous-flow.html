Problem VF2

Oscillating Wall under a Liquid Layer

Original statement · VF2_w_solution.pdf
Unsteady unidirectional flowComplex solutionQuasi-steady and Stokes limits

Official Problem Statement

Consider the periodic motion induced in a liquid layer of thickness $h$ when the bottom wall moves with velocity $V\cos(\omega t)$. Write the equation with boundary conditions that determines $v_x(y,t)$. Consider separately the cases $\omega h^2/\nu\gg 1$ and $\omega h^2/\nu\ll 1$. Obtain the general solution and show how it reduces to the limiting solutions studied earlier in the appriate limits.

The sheet prints "appriate"; it is kept. The top of the layer is a free surface at the pressure $p_a$, as the figure of the sheet shows.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Equation

  • The liquid has constant $\rho$ and $\mu$. The flow is parallel to the wall: $\bar{v} = v_x(y, t)\,\bar{e}_x$, because continuity gives $\partial v_x/\partial x = 0$ (Notes.pdf, Eq. 7.1).
  • Pressure. The $y$ component of the momentum equation gives $0 = -\partial P/\partial y$ with $P = p + \rho g y$, so $P$ is the same at every height. At the free surface $p = p_a$ and $y = h$, hence $P = p_a + \rho g h$, a constant: $\partial P/\partial x = 0$ and $P_l = 0$ (the free surface stays flat).
  • Free surface. The air above exerts a negligible shear stress (its viscosity is neglected), so the shear stress of the liquid vanishes at $y = h$: $\partial v_x/\partial y = 0$.
  • Periodic solution. The wall velocity is periodic and the sought solution is the permanent one, so no initial condition is needed (Notes.pdf, p. 78). With a forcing $V\sin\omega t$ one could impose $v_x = 0$ at $t = 0$, which is compatible.

Equation (7.6) with $P_l = 0$ and $\nu = \mu/\rho$, and its boundary conditions:

$$ \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial t} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 v_x}{\partial y^2}; \qquad y = 0:\; v_x = V\cos\omega t, \qquad y = h:\; \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y} = 0 $$

General Solution with a Complex Function

Since $V\cos\omega t = \operatorname{Re}\left(V e^{\imath\omega t}\right)$, the linear problem is solved for a complex $v^*$ with $v_x = \operatorname{Re}(v^*)$:

$$ \frac{\partial v^*}{\partial t} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 v^*}{\partial y^2}; \qquad y = 0:\; v^* = V e^{\imath\omega t}, \qquad y = h:\; \frac{\partial v^*}{\partial y} = 0 $$

Look for $v^* = V e^{\imath\omega t}f(y)$. Substituting, $\imath\omega f = \nu f''$, that is

$$ f'' - \frac{\imath\omega}{\nu}f = 0; \qquad f(0) = 1, \qquad f'(h) = 0 $$

(in the key's non-dimensional form, $z = y/h$ and $2\Omega^2 = \omega h^2/\nu$, the problem has one parameter: $f'' - 2\imath\Omega^2 f = 0$ with $f(0) = 1$, $f'(1) = 0$).

Characteristic equation. $\lambda^2 = \imath\omega/\nu$. With $\imath = e^{\imath\pi/4}\cdot e^{\imath\pi/4}$, $\sqrt{\imath} = (1 + \imath)/\sqrt{2}$, so the two roots are $\pm\lambda$ with

$$ \lambda = (1 + \imath)\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{2\nu}}, \qquad \lambda h = (1 + \imath)\,\Omega \quad\text{with}\quad \Omega = \sqrt{\frac{\omega h^2}{2\nu}} $$

(check: $\lambda^2 = (1 + \imath)^2\,\omega/(2\nu) = 2\imath\,\omega/(2\nu) = \imath\omega/\nu$).

Constants. $f = C_1e^{\lambda y} + C_2e^{-\lambda y}$. The wall condition gives $C_1 + C_2 = 1$. The condition at the free surface, $f'(h) = \lambda\left(C_1e^{\lambda h} - C_2e^{-\lambda h}\right) = 0$, gives $C_1e^{\lambda h} = C_2e^{-\lambda h}$. Substituting $C_2 = 1 - C_1$:

$$ C_1\left(e^{\lambda h} + e^{-\lambda h}\right) = e^{-\lambda h} \;\Longrightarrow\; C_1 = \frac{e^{-\lambda h}}{e^{\lambda h} + e^{-\lambda h}}, \qquad C_2 = 1 - C_1 = \frac{e^{\lambda h}}{e^{\lambda h} + e^{-\lambda h}} $$

Putting them in $f$:

$$ f = \frac{e^{-\lambda h}e^{\lambda y} + e^{\lambda h}e^{-\lambda y}}{e^{\lambda h} + e^{-\lambda h}} = \frac{e^{\lambda(h - y)} + e^{-\lambda(h - y)}}{e^{\lambda h} + e^{-\lambda h}} = \frac{\cosh\left[\lambda(h - y)\right]}{\cosh(\lambda h)} $$

and the complex solution is

$$ v^* = V e^{\imath\omega t}\,\frac{\cosh\left[(1 + \imath)\Omega(1 - y/h)\right]}{\cosh\left[(1 + \imath)\Omega\right]}, \qquad v_x = \operatorname{Re}(v^*) $$
Discrepancy with the official solution

The second boundary-condition equation of the key, boxed on page 1, reads $C_1e^{(1+\imath)\sqrt{\omega/2\nu}\,h} - C_2e^{-(1+\imath)\sqrt{\omega/2\nu}\,h} = 1$. The condition $f'(h) = 0$ gives $0$ on the right-hand side, as derived above, and the coefficients $C_1$, $C_2$ that the key writes just below are the ones that follow from $0$, not from $1$. It is a slip in the key; the final solution is not affected.

The Real Solution

With $s = \Omega(1 - y/h)$ and $\cosh(s + \imath s) = \cosh s\cos s + \imath\sinh s\sin s$:

$$ f = \frac{A + \imath B}{C + \imath D}, \qquad A = \cosh s\cos s,\; B = \sinh s\sin s,\; C = \cosh\Omega\cos\Omega,\; D = \sinh\Omega\sin\Omega $$

Multiplying numerator and denominator by $C - \imath D$ gives $f = \left[(AC + BD) + \imath(BC - AD)\right]/(C^2 + D^2)$. The real part of $e^{\imath\omega t}f$ is $\left[(AC + BD)\cos\omega t - (BC - AD)\sin\omega t\right]/(C^2 + D^2)$ (since $\operatorname{Re}[(\cos\omega t + \imath\sin\omega t)(a + \imath b)] = a\cos\omega t - b\sin\omega t$). Also $C^2 + D^2 = \left(\cosh 2\Omega + \cos 2\Omega\right)/2$, which is the denominator $e^{2\Omega} + e^{-2\Omega} + 2\cos 2\Omega$ of the key up to the factor 4. Then

$$ \frac{v_x}{V} = \frac{(AC + BD)\cos\omega t - (BC - AD)\sin\omega t}{C^2 + D^2} $$

At $y = 0$, $s = \Omega$, so $A = C$ and $B = D$ and $v_x/V = \cos\omega t$, as the wall condition requires. The key obtains the same function as a sum of four terms of the form $e^{\pm\Omega z}\cos(\Omega z \pm \omega t + \dots)$, multiplying by the conjugate of the denominator.

Limit $\omega h^2/\nu\ll 1$: Quasi-steady Solution

Here $|\lambda h| = \sqrt{2}\,\Omega\ll 1$, so the viscous time $h^2/\nu$ is much shorter than $\omega^{-1}$ and the unsteady term is negligible. The equation reduces to $\partial^2 v_x/\partial y^2 = 0$ with $v_x = V\cos\omega t$ at $y = 0$ and $\partial v_x/\partial y = 0$ at $y = h$. Integrating twice, $v_x = A(t)y + B(t)$; the condition at $y = h$ gives $A = 0$ and the wall gives $B = V\cos\omega t$:

$$ v_x = V\cos\omega t $$

The liquid layer moves like a solid: with no shear stress at the free surface, nothing opposes the motion. The exact solution gives the same: $\cosh(x)\simeq 1 + x^2/2$ for small $x$, so $f \simeq 1 + \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\left[(h - y)^2 - h^2\right] = 1 - \frac{\imath\omega}{2\nu}\,y\,(2h - y)\to 1$. The first correction (computed here, an extension) is $v_x \simeq V\left[\cos\omega t + \frac{\omega}{2\nu}\,y\,(2h - y)\sin\omega t\right]$, of relative size $\omega h^2/\nu\ll 1$ (it uses $\operatorname{Re}\left[\imath e^{\imath\omega t}\right] = -\sin\omega t$).

Limit $\omega h^2/\nu\gg 1$: Stokes Layer

Here $\Omega\gg 1$: the thickness $h$ is much larger than the penetration length $\delta = \sqrt{\nu/\omega}$. The sheet first writes $\partial v_x/\partial t = 0$ with two question marks: the right argument is not that the layer is at rest, but that the motion is confined to a thin layer. Balancing $\omega V\sim\nu V/\delta^2$ gives $\delta\sim\sqrt{\nu/\omega}\ll h$, and the problem becomes the one of Stokes (Notes.pdf, Eq. 7.26):

$$ \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial t} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 v_x}{\partial y^2}; \qquad y = 0:\; v_x = V\cos\omega t, \qquad y\gg\sqrt{\nu/\omega}:\; v_x\to 0 $$

whose solution (7.31) is

$$ v_x = V\,e^{-y/\sqrt{2\nu/\omega}}\,\cos\left(\omega t - \frac{y}{\sqrt{2\nu/\omega}}\right) $$

It follows from the exact solution: $\cosh[\lambda(h - y)]/\cosh(\lambda h) = \left[e^{-\lambda y} + e^{-\lambda(2h - y)}\right]/\left[1 + e^{-2\lambda h}\right]$, and $\left|e^{-2\lambda h}\right| = e^{-2\Omega}\to 0$, $\left|e^{-\lambda(2h - y)}\right|\le e^{-\Omega}$ for $y\le h$, so $f\simeq e^{-\lambda y}$ with $\lambda = (1 + \imath)/\sqrt{2\nu/\omega}$, and the real part of $V e^{\imath\omega t}e^{-\lambda y}$ is the expression above. The thickness $h$ disappears from the result.

The key solves the same limit in the non-dimensional variable $\eta = y/\delta$ with $\delta^2\omega/\nu = 1$: $f'' - \imath f = 0$, $f(0) = 1$, $f(\infty)$ bounded, so the root with growing exponential has a zero coefficient ($A = 0$, $B = 1$) and $f = e^{-(1 + \imath)\eta/\sqrt{2}}$.

Answers and Checks

  • Problem: $\partial v_x/\partial t = \nu\,\partial^2v_x/\partial y^2$, $v_x(0, t) = V\cos\omega t$, $\partial v_x/\partial y(h, t) = 0$.
  • General solution: $v_x = \operatorname{Re}\left\{V e^{\imath\omega t}\cosh\left[(1 + \imath)\Omega(1 - y/h)\right]/\cosh\left[(1 + \imath)\Omega\right]\right\}$ with $\Omega^2 = \omega h^2/(2\nu)$.
  • $\omega h^2/\nu\ll 1$: $v_x = V\cos\omega t$ (rigid motion). $\omega h^2/\nu\gg 1$: Stokes solution, independent of $h$.
  • Boundary conditions of the general solution: at $y = 0$, $f = 1$; at $y = h$, $\sinh[\lambda(h - y)] = 0$, so $f' = 0$.
  • Dimensions: $\omega h^2/\nu$ is $T^{-1}L^2/(L^2T^{-1}) = 1$; $\sqrt{2\nu/\omega}$ is a length.
  • Check against the official key: VF2_w_solution.pdf gives the same equation, complex solution, real part, rigid limit and Stokes limit, with the slip in the second boundary-condition equation noted above; the minus sign that the key writes before $e^{-\eta/\sqrt{2}}e^{\imath(\omega t - \eta/\sqrt{2})}$ on page 4 (a reading of the handwriting) also disappears in the real part it boxes.
Problem VF3

Annular Flow with a Moving Outer Cylinder

Original statement · VF3.pdf
AnnulusPoiseuille + CouetteZero flux

Official Problem Statement

A fluid of density $\rho$ and viscosity $\mu$ is confined between two infinitely long coaxial cylinders of radii $a$ and $\alpha a$, with $\alpha < 1$, aligned in the $x$ direction. The fluid is forced to move steadily due to the presence of a known reduced pressure gradient $P_l = -\partial P/\partial x$ and also due to the backward motion of the outer cylinder, with velocity $V$.

1. Determine the fluid velocity $v_x(r)$.

2. Obtain the power needed to move the outer cylinder.

3. Calculate the value of $V$ for which the volume flux in the pipe is identically zero.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Steady, fully developed flow of a liquid with constant $\mu$: $\bar{v} = v_x(r)\bar{e}_x$ (Notes.pdf, Eq. 7.1, with axial symmetry); $P_l$ is a known constant (Notes.pdf, p. 74).
  • The inner body, a rod of radius $\alpha a$, is at rest; the outer cylinder of radius $a$ moves backward, that is, with velocity $-V\bar{e}_x$.
  • "Backward" means against the pressure-driven flow: $P_l > 0$ drives the liquid towards $+x$.
  • The power is taken per unit length of cylinder, since the cylinders are infinitely long.

Velocity Profile

Equation (7.7) in steady state, with the conditions at the two walls:

$$ 0 = P_l + \frac{\mu}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_x}{dr}\right); \qquad r = \alpha a:\; v_x = 0, \qquad r = a:\; v_x = -V $$

Multiplying by $r/\mu$ and integrating once: $r\,dv_x/dr = -P_l r^2/(2\mu) + C_1$. Dividing by $r$ and integrating again:

$$ v_x = -\frac{P_l}{4\mu}r^2 + C_1\ln r + C_2 $$

The term $C_1\ln r$ cannot be dropped: the axis is not part of the domain, so there is no regularity condition. It is convenient to write the solution with the inner wall as origin of the logarithm, $v_x = -\frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left(r^2 - \alpha^2a^2\right) + C\ln\frac{r}{\alpha a}$, which satisfies $v_x(\alpha a) = 0$ for any $C$. The outer wall fixes $C$:

$$ -V = -\frac{P_l}{4\mu}a^2\left(1 - \alpha^2\right) + C\ln\frac{1}{\alpha} \;\Longrightarrow\; C = \frac{-V + \dfrac{P_l}{4\mu}a^2\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)}{\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right)} $$
$$ v_x = -\frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left[r^2 - (\alpha a)^2\right] + \left[-V + \frac{P_l}{\mu}\frac{\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)a^2}{4}\right]\frac{\ln\left(r/\alpha a\right)}{\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right)} $$

Check: at $r = \alpha a$ the logarithm is zero and the bracket $r^2 - (\alpha a)^2$ vanishes; at $r = a$ the first term is $-P_la^2(1 - \alpha^2)/(4\mu)$ and the second is $-V + P_la^2(1 - \alpha^2)/(4\mu)$, whose sum is $-V$.

Power Needed to Move the Outer Cylinder

The force per unit area of the liquid on the outer wall is $\tau_p = \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n}$ pointing from the wall into the liquid, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$, so $\tau_p = -\mu\left(\partial v_x/\partial r\right)_{r=a}$ (the convention of (7.16)). Differentiating,

$$ \frac{dv_x}{dr} = -\frac{P_l}{2\mu}r + \frac{C}{r} \;\Longrightarrow\; \tau_p = \frac{P_la}{2} - \frac{\mu C}{a} = \frac{P_la}{2} - \frac{\mu}{a}\,\frac{-V + \dfrac{P_l}{4\mu}a^2\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)}{\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right)} $$
$$ \tau_p = \frac{P_la}{2}\left[1 - \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right)}\right] + \frac{\mu V}{a\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right)} $$

The liquid pushes the cylinder with the force $2\pi a\,\tau_p$ per unit length, in the direction $+x$. The external agent that moves the cylinder at velocity $-V\bar{e}_x$ must balance it, with the force $-2\pi a\,\tau_p\,\bar{e}_x$, so the power it supplies per unit length is force times velocity:

$$ W = \left(-2\pi a\tau_p\right)(-V) = \tau_p\,V\,2\pi a $$

(power per unit length of cylinder, in W/m).

Zero Volume Flux

The flux through the annulus is $Q = \int_{\alpha a}^{a}2\pi r\,v_x\,dr$. With $G = P_l/(4\mu)$, $b = \alpha a$, $\mathcal{L} = \ln(\alpha^{-1})$ and the two integrals (Barrow's rule):

$$ \int_b^a r\left(r^2 - b^2\right)dr = \left[\frac{r^4}{4} - \frac{b^2r^2}{2}\right]_b^a = \frac{\left(a^2 - b^2\right)^2}{4} $$
$$ \int_b^a r\ln\frac{r}{b}\,dr = \left[\frac{r^2}{2}\ln\frac{r}{b} - \frac{r^2}{4}\right]_b^a = \frac{a^2}{2}\mathcal{L} - \frac{a^2 - b^2}{4} $$

(the second uses $\int r\ln r\,dr = \frac{r^2}{2}\ln r - \frac{r^2}{4}$, checked by differentiating). With $D_a = a^2 - b^2 = a^2(1 - \alpha^2)$ and $C = (-V + G D_a)/\mathcal{L}$:

$$ \frac{Q}{2\pi} = -G\frac{D_a^2}{4} + C\left(\frac{a^2}{2}\mathcal{L} - \frac{D_a}{4}\right) = 0 $$

The key stops at the equivalent integral equation, boxed, $\frac{V}{\ln(\alpha^{-1})}\int_{\alpha a}^{a}r\ln\frac{r}{\alpha a}\,dr = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}\int_{\alpha a}^{a}\left[\frac{(1 - \alpha^2)a^2\ln(r/\alpha a)}{\ln(\alpha^{-1})} - r^2 + (\alpha a)^2\right]r\,dr$, and does not evaluate it. Evaluating it (computed here) and solving for $V$:

$$ \left(-V + GD_a\right)\left(\frac{a^2}{2} - \frac{D_a}{4\mathcal{L}}\right) = G\frac{D_a^2}{4} \;\Longrightarrow\; V = GD_a\,\frac{\dfrac{a^2}{2} - \dfrac{D_a}{4} - \dfrac{D_a}{4\mathcal{L}}}{\dfrac{a^2}{2} - \dfrac{D_a}{4\mathcal{L}}} $$

and, using $\frac{a^2}{2} - \frac{D_a}{4} = \frac{a^2(1 + \alpha^2)}{4}$ and multiplying numerator and denominator by $4\mathcal{L}/a^2$:

$$ V = \frac{P_la^2}{4\mu}\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)\frac{\left(1 + \alpha^2\right)\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right) - \left(1 - \alpha^2\right)}{2\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right) - \left(1 - \alpha^2\right)} $$

Answers and Checks

  • Velocity: $v_x = -\frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left[r^2 - (\alpha a)^2\right] + \left[-V + \frac{P_l}{4\mu}(1 - \alpha^2)a^2\right]\ln(r/\alpha a)/\ln(\alpha^{-1})$.
  • Power per unit length: $W = 2\pi a\,V\left\{\frac{P_la}{2}\left[1 - \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2\ln(\alpha^{-1})}\right] + \frac{\mu V}{a\ln(\alpha^{-1})}\right\}$.
  • Zero flux: $V = \frac{P_la^2}{4\mu}(1 - \alpha^2)\frac{(1 + \alpha^2)\ln(\alpha^{-1}) - (1 - \alpha^2)}{2\ln(\alpha^{-1}) - (1 - \alpha^2)}$.
  • Limit $\alpha\to 0$ (no rod): $\ln(\alpha^{-1})\to\infty$ and $V\to\frac{P_la^2}{4\mu}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{P_la^2}{8\mu}$; directly, a pipe with a wall moving at $-V$ has $Q = \pi a^4P_l/(8\mu) - V\pi a^2 = 0$, the same $V$.
  • Limit $\alpha\to 1$ (thin gap $h = a(1 - \alpha)$): with $\epsilon = 1 - \alpha$, $\ln(\alpha^{-1})\simeq\epsilon + \epsilon^2/2 + \epsilon^3/3$, the numerator of the last fraction is $\simeq 2\epsilon^3/3$ and its denominator $\simeq 2\epsilon^2$, so $V\to\frac{P_la^2}{4\mu}\,2\epsilon\,\frac{\epsilon}{3} = \frac{P_lh^2}{6\mu}$, which is the plane result: $Q = P_lh^3/(12\mu) - Vh/2 = 0$.
  • Dimensions: $P_la^2/\mu$ is $ML^{-2}T^{-2}\cdot L^2/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}) = LT^{-1}$, a velocity; $\tau_pV$ per unit length is $(\text{N/m}^2)(\text{m/s})(\text{m}) = \text{W/m}$.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The liquid has constant $\rho$ and $\mu$; the flow is steady and axisymmetric.
  • If the criterion of Part 1 holds, the flow is fully developed (Poiseuille) everywhere except in short regions of length of order $D$ at the entrance and at the junction $x = (1 - \beta)L$, and the pressure variations in them are small compared with the loss along the pipe (Notes.pdf, Eqs. 7.56 and 7.63). So $P(0) = p_c$ and the pressure at the end of the first stretch equals the one at the beginning of the second, $p_o$.
  • Gravity is absorbed in the reduced pressure $P$; the gravity of a horizontal pipe does not matter, and the symbols $p_c$, $p_o$, $p_a$ are used for the reduced pressures (as in the key).
  • Let $a = D/2$, $\Delta p = p_c - p_a$ and $\mathcal{G} = \pi D^4/(128\mu)$, so that $\mathcal{G} = \pi a^4/(8\mu)$.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The flow is dominated by viscosity when the convective acceleration is much smaller than the viscous force, Eq. (7.41), and the flow is steady here, so (7.40) is satisfied:

$$ \frac{\rho v_L D}{\mu}\,\frac{D}{L}\ll 1 $$

The velocity $v_L$ is not known, so assume that viscosity dominates and estimate it from the balance of pressure and viscous forces, Eq. (7.44), $\Delta_LP\sim\mu v_LL/D^2$ with $\Delta_LP\sim p_c - p_a$:

$$ v_L\sim\frac{D^2}{L}\,\frac{p_c - p_a}{\mu} $$

Substituting in the criterion:

$$ \frac{\rho D}{\mu}\,\frac{D}{L}\,\frac{D^2}{L}\,\frac{p_c - p_a}{\mu} = \frac{\rho D^4\left(p_c - p_a\right)}{\mu^2L^2}\ll 1 $$

which only contains known data.

Velocity Fields

First stretch, $0 < x < (1 - \beta)L$: circular pipe. As in (7.13)-(7.14), with a constant reduced pressure gradient $dP/dx = -P_{l1}$ and radius $a = D/2$:

$$ v_x = \frac{P_{l1}}{4\mu}\left(a^2 - r^2\right) = \frac{P_{l1}}{16\mu}\left(D^2 - 4r^2\right) $$

The gradient is constant because the section is constant and $Q$ is uniform, so $P_{l1} = (p_c - p_o)/\left[(1 - \beta)L\right]$. In terms of the key's notation, $dP/dx = -(p_c - p_o)/\left[(1 - \beta)L\right]$.

Second stretch, $(1 - \beta)L < x < L$: annulus. The liquid is between the bar ($r = \alpha a$) and the pipe wall ($r = a$), both at rest, with $P_{l2} = (p_o - p_a)/(\beta L)$. Integrating (7.47) twice as in VF3, with $v_x(\alpha a) = v_x(a) = 0$:

$$ v_x = \frac{P_{l2}}{4\mu}\left(a^2 - r^2\right) + C\ln\frac{r}{a}; \qquad 0 = \frac{P_{l2}}{4\mu}a^2\left(1 - \alpha^2\right) + C\ln\alpha \;\Longrightarrow\; C = -\frac{P_{l2}a^2\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)}{4\mu\ln\alpha} $$
$$ v_x = \frac{P_{l2}D^2}{16\mu}\left[1 - \left(\frac{2r}{D}\right)^2 - \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{\ln\alpha}\ln\frac{2r}{D}\right], \qquad P_{l2} = \frac{p_o - p_a}{\beta L} $$

Check: at $r = D/2$ the bracket is $1 - 1 - 0 = 0$; at $r = \alpha D/2$ it is $1 - \alpha^2 - \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{\ln\alpha}\ln\alpha = 0$.

Volume Flux

Pipe. From (7.15), $Q = \frac{\pi a^4}{8\mu}P_{l1}$, so

$$ Q = \mathcal{G}\,\frac{p_c - p_o}{(1 - \beta)L} \tag{1} $$

Annulus. With $s = r/a$, $Q = \int_{\alpha a}^{a}2\pi r\,v_x\,dr = 2\pi a^2\,\frac{P_{l2}a^2}{4\mu}\,I$ with $I = \int_\alpha^1 s\left[1 - s^2 - k\ln s\right]ds$ and $k = (1 - \alpha^2)/\ln\alpha$. Barrow's rule for each term:

$$ \int_\alpha^1 s\,ds = \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2}, \qquad \int_\alpha^1 s^3ds = \frac{1 - \alpha^4}{4}, \qquad \int_\alpha^1 s\ln s\,ds = \left[\frac{s^2}{2}\ln s - \frac{s^2}{4}\right]_\alpha^1 = -\frac{1 - \alpha^2}{4} - \frac{\alpha^2}{2}\ln\alpha $$
$$ I = \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2} - \frac{1 - \alpha^4}{4} + k\left[\frac{1 - \alpha^2}{4} + \frac{\alpha^2}{2}\ln\alpha\right] = \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2} - \frac{1 - \alpha^4}{4} + \frac{\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)^2}{4\ln\alpha} + \frac{\alpha^2\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)}{2} $$

and since $\frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2} + \frac{\alpha^2(1 - \alpha^2)}{2} = \frac{1 - \alpha^4}{2}$, $I = \frac{1}{4}\left[\left(1 - \alpha^4\right) - \frac{(1 - \alpha^2)^2}{\ln(\alpha^{-1})}\right]$. Therefore

$$ Q = \frac{\pi a^4P_{l2}}{8\mu}\,f(\alpha) = \mathcal{G}\,\frac{p_o - p_a}{\beta L}\,f(\alpha), \qquad f(\alpha) = 1 - \alpha^4 - \frac{\left(1 - \alpha^2\right)^2}{\ln\left(\alpha^{-1}\right)} \tag{2} $$

($f$ is the ratio of the flux of the annulus to that of the empty pipe for the same $P_l$: $f\to 1$ for $\alpha\to 0$ and $f\to 0$ for $\alpha\to 1$.) The key writes the same function as $f(\alpha) = 1 - \alpha^2(2 - \alpha^2) - \frac{(1 - \alpha^2)\left[1 - \alpha^2 - 2\alpha^2\ln(\alpha^{-1})\right]}{\ln(\alpha^{-1})}$; expanding, $1 - 2\alpha^2 + \alpha^4 + 2\alpha^2(1 - \alpha^2) - (1 - \alpha^2)^2/\ln(\alpha^{-1}) = 1 - \alpha^4 - (1 - \alpha^2)^2/\ln(\alpha^{-1})$, the same.

Flux and Pressure at the Junction

The flux is the same in both stretches (Notes.pdf, Eq. 7.72 and the discussion of (7.49)). Equating (1) and (2):

$$ \frac{p_c - p_o}{1 - \beta} = f\,\frac{p_o - p_a}{\beta} $$

With $u = p_o - p_a$ and $p_c - p_o = \Delta p - u$: $\beta(\Delta p - u) = f(1 - \beta)u$, so $u = \dfrac{\beta\,\Delta p}{\beta + f(1 - \beta)}$. Then

$$ Q = \mathcal{G}\,\frac{f\,u}{\beta L} = \frac{\pi D^4}{128\mu L}\,\frac{p_c - p_a}{1 + \beta\left(1 - f\right)/f}, \qquad p_o = p_c - \frac{(1 - \beta)\left(p_c - p_a\right)}{1 + \beta\left(1 - f\right)/f} $$

(the second from $p_c - p_o = \Delta p - u = \dfrac{f(1 - \beta)\Delta p}{\beta + f(1 - \beta)}$, dividing numerator and denominator by $f$).

Discrepancy with the official solution

The two boxed results of the key have $1 + \dfrac{\beta f}{1 - f}$ in the denominator, with $f = f(\alpha)$ of its own equation (2). That combination is not the one that follows from (1) and (2). Two limits prove it. For $\alpha\to 0$ (no bar) $f\to 1$, and the key's denominator $1 + \beta f/(1 - f)$ tends to infinity, so $Q\to 0$, when the flow must be that of the empty pipe; the corrected denominator $1 + \beta(1 - f)/f$ tends to 1 and gives $Q = \pi D^4\Delta p/(128\mu L)$. For $\alpha\to 1$ (the bar fills the pipe) $f\to 0$ and the key gives the empty-pipe flux, the corrected formula $Q\to 0$. The calculation above is kept.

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $\rho D^4(p_c - p_a)/(\mu^2L^2)\ll 1$.
  • Velocities: $v_x = \frac{(p_c - p_o)}{16\mu(1 - \beta)L}\left(D^2 - 4r^2\right)$ for $0 < x < (1 - \beta)L$ and $v_x = \frac{(p_o - p_a)D^2}{16\mu\beta L}\left[1 - (2r/D)^2 - \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{\ln\alpha}\ln(2r/D)\right]$ for $(1 - \beta)L < x < L$.
  • Flux: $Q = \frac{\pi D^4}{128\mu L}\frac{p_c - p_a}{1 + \beta(1 - f)/f}$; pressure at the junction: $p_o = p_c - \frac{(1 - \beta)(p_c - p_a)}{1 + \beta(1 - f)/f}$.
  • Limits: $\beta\to 0$ (no bar) gives $Q = \pi D^4\Delta p/(128\mu L)$ and $p_o\to p_c - \Delta p = p_a$; $\beta\to 1$ (bar over the whole pipe) gives $Q = \mathcal{G}f\,\Delta p/L$ and $p_o\to p_c$, all the loss in the annulus; $f\to 1$ gives the empty pipe whatever $\beta$.
  • Dimensions: $\pi D^4\Delta p/(\mu L)$ is $L^4\cdot ML^{-1}T^{-2}/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot L) = L^3T^{-1}$, a volume flux; $\rho D^4\Delta p/(\mu^2L^2)$ is $ML^{-3}L^4\,ML^{-1}T^{-2}/(M^2L^{-2}T^{-2}L^2) = 1$.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

- Viscous regime. The flow is dominated by viscosity if the convective and local accelerations are negligible against the viscous force (Eqs. 7.40 and 7.41). With the velocity estimated by the balance of the weight of the column and the viscous force, $V_L\sim\rho g a^2/\mu$, the criterion is (computed in the first section of the solution) $$ \frac{\rho^2 g\,a^4}{\mu^2\,l}\ll 1 $$ with $l\sim l_1\sim l_2$ the length of the columns (the key writes the characteristic density and viscosity without subscript). Under it the flow is quasi-steady Poiseuille flow in each column. - The liquids are incompressible and immiscible, so the interface moves with them; the upper column keeps its length $l_1$ (it slides down as a plug as the lower column drains) and the lower column has the length $h(t)$, with $h(0) = l_2$. - Pressure at the open top and at the open bottom: $p_a$. Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The local acceleration over the viscous term is $\dfrac{a^2/(\mu/\rho)}{t_0}$ with $t_0\sim l/V_L$ the time of variation (the time to travel the column); the convective one is $\dfrac{\rho V_La}{\mu}\dfrac{a}{l}$ (Eqs. 7.40-7.41). They are the same number, and with $V_L\sim\Delta P\,a^2/(\mu l)$, $\Delta P\sim\rho g l$ (the pressure difference that drives the flow is the weight of the column), $V_L\sim\rho g a^2/\mu$:

$$ \frac{\rho V_L a}{\mu}\,\frac{a}{l}\sim\frac{\rho}{\mu}\,\frac{\rho g a^2}{\mu}\,\frac{a^2}{l} = \frac{\rho^2 g a^4}{\mu^2 l}\ll 1 $$

Flux in Each Column

In a column of length $\ell$ of the pipe the flow goes down, along $+x$, and the reduced pressure $P = p - \rho g x$ ($x$ down: the potential energy falls as $x$ grows) has the uniform gradient along the flow $P_l = \left(P_{top} - P_{bottom}\right)/\ell = \left(p_{top} - p_{bottom} + \rho g\ell\right)/\ell$, so that the weight of the column helps the flow. The profile is the Poiseuille one (Eq. 7.14), $v_x = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}(a^2 - r^2)$, with the flux (Eq. 7.15), $Q = \frac{\pi a^4}{8\mu}P_l$.

Upper liquid. From the free surface ($p = p_a$) to the interface ($p = p_s$), of length $l_1$:

$$ Q = \frac{\pi a^4}{8\mu_1}\,\frac{p_a - p_s + \rho_1 g l_1}{l_1} $$

Lower liquid. From the interface ($p = p_s$) to the open end ($p = p_a$), of length $h$:

$$ Q = \frac{\pi a^4}{8\mu_2}\,\frac{p_s - p_a + \rho_2 g h}{h} $$

Mass conservation of an incompressible liquid in a tube of constant section gives the same $Q$ in both columns, so the pressure drops are

$$ p_a - p_s + \rho_1 g l_1 = \frac{8\mu_1 l_1}{\pi a^4}\,Q, \qquad p_s - p_a + \rho_2 g h = \frac{8\mu_2 h}{\pi a^4}\,Q $$

Volume Flux and Evolution of the Interface

Adding the two equations cancels the unknown $p_s$:

$$ \left(\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h\right)g = \frac{8}{\pi a^4}\left(\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h\right)Q \;\Longrightarrow\; Q = \frac{\pi a^4 g}{8}\,\frac{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h}{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h} $$

This gives $Q$ as a function of the position $h$ of the lower interface. The lower column loses the volume $dV = -\pi a^2\,dh$ in $dt$, so $Q = -\pi a^2\,dh/dt$. Substituting:

$$ \left(\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h\right)g = -\frac{8}{a^2}\left(\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h\right)\frac{dh}{dt} $$

Separating variables and integrating from $h = l_2$ at $t = 0$ (Barrow's rule on the left, with $\int_0^t dt' = t$):

$$ \int_{l_2}^{h}\frac{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h'}{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h'}\,dh' = -\frac{g a^2}{8}\,t $$

The key leaves the left side unevaluated. Writing $\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h' = \frac{\mu_2}{\rho_2}\left(\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h'\right) + l_1\left(\mu_1 - \frac{\mu_2\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right)$ the integrand is $\frac{\mu_2}{\rho_2} + \frac{l_1(\mu_1\rho_2 - \mu_2\rho_1)}{\rho_2}\,\frac{1}{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h'}$ and, since $\int\frac{dh'}{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h'} = \frac{1}{\rho_2}\ln\left(\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h'\right)$ (computed here):

$$ \frac{\mu_2}{\rho_2}\left(h - l_2\right) + \frac{l_1\left(\mu_1\rho_2 - \mu_2\rho_1\right)}{\rho_2^2}\,\ln\frac{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h}{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 l_2} = -\frac{g a^2}{8}\,t $$

which gives $t(h)$ explicitly, and $h(t)$ implicitly; $Q(t)$ follows from $Q(h)$ above.

Pressure Distribution

The pressure at the interface follows from the upper column:

$$ p_s - p_a = \rho_1 g l_1 - \frac{8\mu_1 l_1}{\pi a^4}Q = \rho_1 g l_1 - \mu_1 l_1 g\,\frac{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h}{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h} = \frac{g\,l_1 h\left(\mu_2\rho_1 - \mu_1\rho_2\right)}{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h} $$

Inside each column the actual pressure is linear in $x$: with $P_l = (p_{top} - p_{bottom} + \rho g\ell)/\ell$, $p = p_{top} - P_lx + \rho gx = p_{top} - (p_{top} - p_{bottom})x/\ell$, because the weight term $\rho gx$ cancels. With $x$ measured down from the top,

$$ p(x) = p_a + \left(p_s - p_a\right)\frac{x}{l_1}\quad(0 < x < l_1), \qquad p(x) = p_s + \left(p_a - p_s\right)\frac{x - l_1}{h}\quad(l_1 < x < l_1 + h) $$

(it is $p_a$ at both ends; the interface is the only point where it differs, by $p_s - p_a$, positive if $\mu_2\rho_1 > \mu_1\rho_2$).

Complete Discharge Time

At $h = 0$, $t = t_d$. From the explicit $t(h)$ above:

$$ t_d = \frac{8}{g a^2}\left[\frac{\mu_2 l_2}{\rho_2} + \frac{l_1\left(\mu_1\rho_2 - \mu_2\rho_1\right)}{\rho_2^2}\,\ln\left(1 + \frac{\rho_2 l_2}{\rho_1 l_1}\right)\right] $$

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $\rho^2 g a^4/(\mu^2 l)\ll 1$.
  • Volume flux: $Q = \frac{\pi a^4 g}{8}\frac{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h}{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h}$, with $h(t)$ defined by $\int_{l_2}^{h}\frac{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h'}{\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h'}dh' = -\frac{g a^2}{8}t$.
  • Pressure: linear in each liquid, with the interface value $p_s - p_a = \frac{g l_1 h(\mu_2\rho_1 - \mu_1\rho_2)}{\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h}$.
  • Discharge time: the expression above.
  • Limit of equal liquids ($\mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu$, $\rho_1 = \rho_2 = \rho$): $p_s = p_a$, $Q = \pi a^4\rho g/(8\mu)$ (constant, independent of $h$ and $l_1$) and $t_d = 8\mu l_2/(\rho g a^2) = \pi a^2 l_2/Q$, the time to empty a volume $\pi a^2 l_2$ at that flux.
  • Limit $l_1\to 0$ (only liquid 2): $Q = \frac{\pi a^4\rho_2 g}{8\mu_2}$ and $t_d = \frac{8\mu_2 l_2}{\rho_2 g a^2}$.
  • Dimensions: $\pi a^4\rho g/\mu$ is $L^4\cdot ML^{-3}\cdot LT^{-2}/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}) = L^3T^{-1}$; $\rho^2ga^4/(\mu^2 l)$ is $M^2L^{-6}\cdot LT^{-2}\cdot L^4/(M^2L^{-2}T^{-2}\cdot L) = 1$.
  • Check against the official key: VF5.pdf gives the same criterion, profiles and fluxes, the equation for $p_s$, $(\rho_1 l_1 + \rho_2 h)g = \frac{8}{\pi a^4}(\mu_1 l_1 + \mu_2 h)Q$ and the integral relation boxed above; it stops there, so the explicit integral, the pressure field and the discharge time are computed here.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

Equilibrium Height

When $t\to\infty$ the liquid is at rest and the reduced pressure is uniform, $P = p + \rho g z = \text{constant}$. At the bottom ($z = 0$) it equals $p_a$ and at the free surface ($z = h_\infty$, $p = 0$) it equals $\rho g h_\infty$:

$$ p_a = \rho g h_\infty \;\Longrightarrow\; h_\infty = \frac{p_a}{\rho g} $$

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The viscous force dominates the convective acceleration (and the local acceleration, since $t_0\sim L/V_L$) when, Eq. (7.41),

$$ \frac{\rho V_L a}{\mu}\,\frac{a}{L}\ll 1 $$

with the characteristic length $L\sim h_\infty$ and the velocity estimated by assuming that viscosity dominates, Eq. (7.44), $V_L\sim\frac{a^2}{L}\frac{p_a}{\mu}$ (the pressure difference that drives the flow is $p_a$):

$$ \frac{\rho a^4p_a}{\mu^2h_\infty^2} = \frac{\rho a^4p_a}{\mu^2}\,\frac{\rho^2g^2}{p_a^2} = \frac{\rho^3g^2a^4}{\mu^2p_a}\ll 1 $$

(the key writes the characteristic transverse length as $D$; here it is the radius $a$, which only changes a numerical factor).

Evolution of the Column

Poiseuille flow in the column (Eqs. 7.13-7.15), with $P_l = -\partial P/\partial z$ uniform along the column at each instant:

$$ v_z = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left(a^2 - r^2\right), \qquad Q = \frac{\pi a^4}{8\mu}P_l $$

The reduced pressure falls from $P(0) = p_a$ to $P(h) = \rho g h$ over the length $h$, so $P_l = (p_a - \rho g h)/h$. The mean velocity of the column is the velocity of its free surface, $Q = \pi a^2\,dh/dt$ (the column grows, so the flux into the tube is positive upward; the key writes $-\pi a^2dh/dt$ and corrects the sign by hand). Hence

$$ \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{a^2}{8\mu}\,\frac{p_a - \rho g h}{h} = \frac{a^2p_a}{8\mu}\,\frac{1 - h/h_\infty}{h} $$

Separating variables, with $h(0) = 0$ and $u = h/h_\infty$ ($h\,dh = h_\infty^2\,u\,du$):

$$ \int_0^h\frac{h'\,dh'}{1 - h'/h_\infty} = h_\infty^2\int_0^{h/h_\infty}\frac{u\,du}{1 - u} = \frac{a^2p_a}{8\mu}\,t, \qquad \int\frac{u\,du}{1 - u} = \int\left(-1 + \frac{1}{1 - u}\right)du = -u - \ln(1 - u) $$
$$ \frac{a^2p_a}{8\mu h_\infty^2}\,t = \ln\frac{1}{1 - h/h_\infty} - \frac{h}{h_\infty} $$
Discrepancy with the official solution

The key writes this relation, and the equation of $dh/dt$ before it, without the factor $a^2$: $\frac{P_a}{8\mu h_\infty^2}t = \ln\frac{1}{1 - h/h_\infty} - \frac{h}{h_\infty}$. That cannot be right dimensionally: $p_at/\mu$ is dimensionless, so the left side has the dimensions $L^{-2}$ while the right side is dimensionless; and $dh/dt = (p_a/8\mu)(1 - h/h_\infty)/h$ has the dimensions $T^{-1}L^{-1}$ instead of $LT^{-1}$. The factor $a^2$ comes from $Q = \pi a^2dh/dt$ and $Q = \pi a^4P_l/(8\mu)$, whose ratio is $a^2$ (the key strikes out the $\pi a^2$ in that step). The same slip appears in the pressure-gradient lines of the key, written $8\mu Q/(\pi a^2)$, where the Poiseuille flux gives $8\mu Q/(\pi a^4)$. The results above keep the factor.

As $t\to\infty$, $\ln\frac{1}{1 - h/h_\infty}\to\infty$ and $h\to h_\infty$. For short times $\ln\frac{1}{1 - u} - u\simeq\frac{u^2}{2}$, so $h\simeq a\sqrt{p_at/(4\mu)}$: the column grows as $\sqrt{t}$ (the laminar, slender-flow law), independent of gravity.

Pressure Distribution

The reduced pressure is linear, $P = p_a - P_lz$, with $P_l = (p_a - \rho gh)/h$. The actual pressure $p = P - \rho gz$:

$$ p(z, t) = p_a - \frac{p_a - \rho gh}{h}\,z - \rho gz = p_a - \frac{p_a}{h}\,z = p_a\left(1 - \frac{z}{h(t)}\right), \qquad 0 < z < h $$

linear from $p_a$ at the pool to zero at the top of the column; the weight terms cancel (the key writes the equivalent $P + \rho gz = p_a - \frac{8\mu Q}{\pi a^4}x$, boxed, with the same slip in the power of $a$).

Force on the Pipe

The liquid acts on the pipe through the shear stress of the wall. With the normal from the wall into the liquid, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_r$, $\tau_p = -\mu\left(\partial v_z/\partial r\right)_{r=a} = P_la/2$ (Eq. 7.16), along $+\bar{e}_z$ (the rising liquid drags the wall upward). Over the lateral area $2\pi ah$:

$$ F_{visc} = 2\pi ah\cdot\frac{a}{2}\,\frac{p_a - \rho gh}{h} = \pi a^2\left(p_a - \rho gh\right) $$

The pressure inside the tube above the liquid is zero, and the atmosphere acts on the outer face of the closed top end with the force $-p_a\pi a^2\bar{e}_z$ (the closed end is a disc of area $\pi a^2$). The total force:

$$ \bar{F} = \pi a^2\left(p_a - \rho gh\right)\bar{e}_z - p_a\pi a^2\bar{e}_z = -\rho gh\,\pi a^2\,\bar{e}_z $$

i.e. $F_z(t) = -\rho g\pi a^2h(t)$: the weight of the column (downward), which is a good check: the force on the pipe equals the weight of the liquid it holds, since the liquid is quasi-static in the vertical momentum balance of the whole column ($\pi a^2p_a$ at the bottom, minus the weight, equals the shear transmitted to the wall).

Answers and Checks

  • Equilibrium height: $h_\infty = p_a/(\rho g)$.
  • Viscous dominance: $\rho^3g^2a^4/(\mu^2p_a)\ll 1$.
  • Height: $\frac{a^2p_a}{8\mu h_\infty^2}t = \ln\frac{1}{1 - h/h_\infty} - \frac{h}{h_\infty}$; pressure: $p = p_a(1 - z/h)$.
  • Force on the pipe: $\bar{F} = -\rho g\pi a^2h(t)\,\bar{e}_z$.
  • Dimensions: $a^2p_at/(\mu h_\infty^2)$ is $L^2\cdot ML^{-1}T^{-2}\cdot T/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}L^2) = 1$.
  • Limits: at $t\to 0$, $h\to a\sqrt{p_at/(4\mu)}$; at $t\to\infty$, $h\to h_\infty$ and $F_z\to-\pi a^2p_a$ (the atmosphere on the top end, with the shear zero).

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

As in VF5, the convective and local accelerations are negligible against the viscous force (Eqs. 7.40-7.41) when

$$ \frac{\rho V_La_o}{\mu}\,\frac{a_o}{L}\ll 1, \qquad V_L\sim\frac{\rho g a_o^2}{\mu} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\rho^2g\,a_o^4}{\mu^2L}\ll 1 $$

($V_L$ is the velocity that the weight of the column gives with the viscous resistance, Eq. 7.44; the largest radius is of order $a_o$ since $\alpha\sim O(1)$).

Flux and Pressure

The pressure gradient along the pipe follows from the local Poiseuille law (7.49), $Q = \frac{\pi a^4}{8\mu}P_l$ with the flow downwards, so that the reduced pressure grows upwards: $dP/dz = 8\mu Q/(\pi a^4)$. Between the open lower end ($z = 0$, $p = p_a$, $P = p_a$) and the free surface ($z = h$, $p = p_a$, $P = p_a + \rho gh$), integrating (Eq. 7.50):

$$ \rho gh = \frac{8\mu Q}{\pi}\int_0^h\frac{dz}{a^4(z)} = \frac{8\mu Q}{\pi a_o^4}\int_0^h\frac{dz}{(1 + \alpha z/L)^4} $$

With $w = 1 + \alpha z/L$, $dz = (L/\alpha)\,dw$, and Barrow's rule:

$$ \int_0^h\frac{dz}{(1 + \alpha z/L)^4} = \frac{L}{\alpha}\left[-\frac{1}{3w^3}\right]_1^{1 + \alpha h/L} = \frac{L}{3\alpha}\left[1 - \frac{1}{(1 + \alpha h/L)^3}\right] $$
$$ \rho gh = \frac{8\mu QL}{3\pi\alpha a_o^4}\left[1 - \frac{1}{(1 + \alpha h/L)^3}\right] $$

(the factor $\alpha$ in the denominator is in the next line of the key: the boxed line of the sheet is partly struck out in the scan).

Evolution of the Free Surface

The volume that leaves in $dt$ is the one lost by the column, $dV = -\pi a^2(h)\,dh$, so $Q = -\pi a^2(h)\,dh/dt$ with $a(h) = a_o(1 + \alpha h/L)$. Substituting, with $w = 1 + \alpha h/L$:

$$ \rho gh = -\frac{8\mu L}{3\alpha a_o^2}\left[1 - \frac{1}{w^3}\right]w^2\frac{dh}{dt} = -\frac{8\mu L}{3\alpha a_o^2}\,\frac{w^3 - 1}{w}\,\frac{dh}{dt} $$

(since $\left(1 - w^{-3}\right)w^2 = w^2 - w^{-1} = (w^3 - 1)/w$). Separating variables, with $h = L$ at $t = 0$:

$$ \frac{3\alpha}{8}\,\frac{\rho ga_o^2}{\mu L}\,t = -\int_L^h\frac{w^3 - 1}{h'\,w}\,dh' $$

To evaluate the integral (computed here) set $s = \alpha h'/L$, so $dh'/h' = ds/s$ and $w = 1 + s$; as $(1 + s)^3 - 1 = s\left(s^2 + 3s + 3\right)$ and $s^2 + 3s + 3 = (s + 2)(s + 1) + 1$:

$$ \frac{(1 + s)^3 - 1}{s(1 + s)} = \frac{s^2 + 3s + 3}{1 + s} = s + 2 + \frac{1}{1 + s}, \qquad \int\left(s + 2 + \frac{1}{1 + s}\right)ds = S(s) = \frac{s^2}{2} + 2s + \ln(1 + s) $$
$$ \frac{3\alpha\rho ga_o^2}{8\mu L}\,t = S(\alpha) - S\!\left(\frac{\alpha h}{L}\right), \qquad S(s) = \frac{s^2}{2} + 2s + \ln(1 + s) $$

which gives $t(h)$ explicitly, and $h(t)$ implicitly.

Time to Complete the Discharge

At $h = 0$, $S(0) = 0$:

$$ t_d = \frac{8}{3\alpha}\,\frac{\mu L}{\rho ga_o^2}\left[\frac{\alpha^2}{2} + 2\alpha + \ln(1 + \alpha)\right] $$

The key stops at $t_d = \frac{8}{3\alpha}\frac{\mu L}{\rho ga_o^2}\int_0^L\frac{(1 + \alpha h/L)^3 - 1}{h(1 + \alpha h/L)}\,dh$; the value of the integral is $\alpha^2/2 + 2\alpha + \ln(1 + \alpha)$, as computed.

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $\rho^2ga_o^4/(\mu^2L)\ll 1$.
  • Evolution: $\frac{3\alpha\rho ga_o^2}{8\mu L}t = S(\alpha) - S(\alpha h/L)$, $S(s) = s^2/2 + 2s + \ln(1 + s)$; the flux is $Q = -\pi a_o^2(1 + \alpha h/L)^2\,dh/dt$.
  • Discharge time: $t_d = \frac{8\mu L}{3\alpha\rho ga_o^2}\left[\frac{\alpha^2}{2} + 2\alpha + \ln(1 + \alpha)\right]$.
  • Limit $\alpha\to 0$ (cylindrical pipe): $\frac{\alpha^2}{2} + 2\alpha + \ln(1 + \alpha) = 3\alpha + \alpha^3/3 + \dots$, so $t_d\to\frac{8\mu L}{\rho ga_o^2}$; directly, the column of constant length $h$ has $P_l = \rho g$ and $Q = \pi a_o^4\rho g/(8\mu)$, a constant mean velocity $a_o^2\rho g/(8\mu)$, and $L$ divided by it is the same.
  • Dimensions: $\mu L/(\rho ga_o^2)$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot L/(ML^{-3}\cdot LT^{-2}\cdot L^2) = T$; $\alpha$ and $S$ are dimensionless.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • Axisymmetric film: $\bar{v} = (v_r, v_y)$ with $y$ the distance to the solid surface and $r$ the radial coordinate from the axis; the film is slender, $h\ll R$.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

By continuity in the film, $\partial(rv_r)/\partial r + r\,\partial v_y/\partial y = 0$ gives $v_r/R\sim v_y/h\sim\dot{h}/h$, so the radial velocity scale is $V_L\sim R\dot{h}/h$ and the time scale of the process is $t_0\sim h/\dot{h}$. Local acceleration against viscous force (Eq. 7.67) and convective acceleration against viscous force (Eq. 7.68):

$$ \frac{O(\rho\,\partial v_r/\partial t)}{O(\mu\nabla^2\bar{v})} = \frac{h^2/\nu}{t_0}\sim\frac{h\dot{h}}{\nu}, \qquad \frac{O(\rho\bar{v}\cdot\nabla\bar{v})}{O(\mu\nabla^2\bar{v})} = \frac{V_Lh}{\nu}\,\frac{h}{R}\sim\frac{R\dot{h}}{h}\,\frac{h}{\nu}\,\frac{h}{R} = \frac{h\dot{h}}{\nu} $$

Both are small when $h\dot{h}/\nu\ll 1$, that is

$$ h\ll\frac{\nu}{\dot{h}} $$

Radial Velocity Profile

With negligible inertia and uniform pressure across the film, the radial momentum equation is (Eq. 7.69)

$$ 0 = -\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} + \mu\frac{\partial^2v_r}{\partial y^2}; \qquad y = 0:\; v_r = 0, \qquad y = h:\; v_r = 0 $$

Integrating twice, with $P_l = -\partial P/\partial r$ independent of $y$: $\mu v_r = -P_ly^2/2 + C_1y + C_2$, $C_2 = 0$, $C_1 = P_lh/2$:

$$ v_r = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(h - y) = -\frac{h^2}{2\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\,\frac{y}{h}\left(1 - \frac{y}{h}\right) $$

The flux per unit length of circumference is $\int_0^hv_r\,dy = P_lh^3/(12\mu)$ (as in Eq. 7.11).

Equation for the Pressure

Integrate the continuity equation $\frac{\partial(rv_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial(rv_y)}{\partial y} = 0$ across the film:

$$ \int_0^h\left[\frac{\partial(rv_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial(rv_y)}{\partial y}\right]dy = \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\int_0^hv_r\,dy\right) + r\left[v_y(h) - v_y(0)\right] = 0 $$

(the Leibniz term with $dh/dr$ is zero because $h$ does not depend on $r$). With $v_y(0) = 0$ and $v_y(h) = \dot{h}$ (kinematic condition on the moving body):

$$ -\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\,\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\right) + r\dot{h} = 0; \qquad r = 0:\; \frac{\partial P}{\partial r} = 0, \qquad r = R:\; P = P_o $$

Pressure Distribution and Adhesion Force

Integrating from the axis (Barrow, with $r\,\partial P/\partial r = 0$ at $r = 0$): $-r\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} + \dot{h}\frac{r^2}{2} = 0$, so $\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,r$, and integrating from $R$ to $r$ with $P(R) = P_o$:

$$ P - P_o = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,\frac{r^2 - R^2}{2} = \frac{3\mu}{h^3}\,\dot{h}\left(r^2 - R^2\right) $$

For $\dot{h} > 0$ (lifting), $P < P_o$ in the whole film: an underpressure, largest on the axis, $P(0) - P_o = -3\mu\dot{h}R^2/h^3$.

The oil acts on the base of the body with the normal pointing from the body into the liquid, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_y$, so the force is $-\int(P - P_o)\bar{n}\,d\sigma = \int(P - P_o)\,d\sigma\,\bar{e}_y$ (the uniform $P_o$ gives no net force on the closed body):

$$ \bar{F} = \int_0^R2\pi r\,(P - P_o)\,dr\,\bar{e}_y = \frac{6\pi\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left[\frac{r^4}{4} - \frac{R^2r^2}{2}\right]_0^R\bar{e}_y = -\frac{3\pi}{2}\,\mu\,\frac{R^4}{h^3}\,\dot{h}\,\bar{e}_y $$

The force is downward: the underpressure holds the body back (adhesion), and grows as $h^{-3}$ when the film is thin and with the velocity of lifting.

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $h\ll\nu/\dot{h}$ (and $h\ll R$).
  • Radial profile: $v_r = -\frac{h^2}{2\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\frac{y}{h}\left(1 - \frac{y}{h}\right)$; equation: $-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\right) + r\dot{h} = 0$.
  • Pressure: $P - P_o = \frac{3\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}(r^2 - R^2)$; force: $\bar{F} = -\frac{3\pi}{2}\mu\frac{R^4}{h^3}\dot{h}\,\bar{e}_y$.
  • Dimensions: $\mu R^4\dot{h}/h^3$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot L^4\cdot LT^{-1}/L^3 = MLT^{-2}$, a force.
  • Consistency: the total volume flux leaving through $r = R$ is $2\pi R\cdot\left(-P_l h^3/(12\mu)\right)$ at $R$, with $P_l = -6\mu\dot{h}R/h^3$ there: $2\pi R\cdot\frac{6\mu\dot{h}R}{h^3}\cdot\frac{h^3}{12\mu} = \pi R^2\dot{h}$, the volume swept by the body (negative sign: inflow), as continuity requires.
  • Check against the official key: VF8.pdf gives the same scale analysis, profile, pressure equation, $P - P_o = 3\mu\dot{h}(r^2 - R^2)/h^3$ and $F = -\frac{3\pi}{2}\mu R^4\dot{h}/h^3$.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

For a slender film (Eqs. 7.67-7.68) with the velocity scale $V$, the largest gap $2h\sim h$ and the length $L$:

$$ \frac{\rho Vh}{\mu}\,\frac{h}{L}\ll 1 $$

The flow is steady, so the unsteady condition (7.67) holds.

Velocity and Flux in Each Stretch

The reduced pressure at the ends: $P(0) = p_a$ and $P(2L) = p_a + \rho g\,2L\sin\alpha$; at the step $P(L) = p_0 + \rho gL\sin\alpha$. With $P_l = -dP/dx$:

$$ P_{l1} = \frac{p_a - p_0 - \rho gL\sin\alpha}{L}, \qquad P_{l2} = \frac{p_0 + \rho gL\sin\alpha - p_a - 2\rho gL\sin\alpha}{L} = \frac{p_0 - p_a - \rho gL\sin\alpha}{L} $$

The profile of a gap $H$ with the lower wall at velocity $V$ and the upper wall at rest is the sum of Couette and Poiseuille flows (Eqs. 7.9 and 7.11, 7.70):

$$ v_x = V\,\frac{H - y}{H} + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(H - y), \qquad Q = \int_0^Hv_x\,dy = \frac{VH}{2} + \frac{P_lH^3}{12\mu} $$
$$ H = 2h:\quad Q = Vh + \frac{2h^3}{3\mu}\,P_{l1}, \qquad H = h:\quad Q = \frac{Vh}{2} + \frac{h^3}{12\mu}\,P_{l2} $$

Pressure Distribution and Flux

The flux is the same in both stretches. With $\Delta = (p_0 - p_a)/L$ and $G = \rho g\sin\alpha$, $P_{l1} = -\Delta - G$ and $P_{l2} = \Delta - G$:

$$ Vh - \frac{2h^3}{3\mu}\left(\Delta + G\right) = \frac{Vh}{2} + \frac{h^3}{12\mu}\left(\Delta - G\right) $$

Multiplying by $12\mu/h^3$: $6\mu V/h^2 - 8\Delta - 8G = \Delta - G$, so $9\Delta = 6\mu V/h^2 - 7G$:

$$ p_0 - p_a = \frac{2}{3}\,\frac{\mu VL}{h^2} - \frac{7}{9}\,\rho gL\sin\alpha $$

The pressure $p = P - \rho gx\sin\alpha$ is linear in each stretch, because the weight term cancels (as in VF5):

$$ 0 < x < L:\quad p = p_a + \left(p_0 - p_a\right)\frac{x}{L}, \qquad L < x < 2L:\quad p = p_0 - \left(p_0 - p_a\right)\frac{x - L}{L} $$

(the second from $P(x) = P(L) + P_{l2}\cdot(-(x - L))$ and $p = P - \rho gx\sin\alpha$).

Volume flux. Using the second stretch with $P_{l2} = \Delta - G$:

$$ Q = \frac{Vh}{2} + \frac{h^3}{12\mu}\left(\frac{2}{3}\frac{\mu V}{h^2} - \frac{16}{9}G\right) = \frac{Vh}{2} + \frac{Vh}{18} - \frac{4}{27}\frac{h^3G}{\mu} = \frac{5}{9}Vh - \frac{4}{27}\,\frac{\rho gh^3\sin\alpha}{\mu} $$

Minimum velocity. $Q > 0$ requires $\frac{5}{9}Vh > \frac{4}{27}\frac{\rho gh^3\sin\alpha}{\mu}$:

$$ V > V_{min} = \frac{4}{15}\,\frac{\rho gh^2\sin\alpha}{\mu} $$

Power Needed to Drive the Belt

The liquid acts on the belt with the shear $\tau = \mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y$ at $y = 0$ (normal $+\bar{e}_y$, from the belt into the liquid):

$$ \tau_1 = \mu\left(-\frac{V}{2h} + \frac{P_{l1}}{2\mu}\,2h\right) = -\frac{\mu V}{2h} + P_{l1}h, \qquad \tau_2 = -\frac{\mu V}{h} + \frac{P_{l2}h}{2} $$
$$ \tau_1 + \tau_2 = -\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mu V}{h} + h\left(P_{l1} + \frac{P_{l2}}{2}\right) = -\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mu V}{h} - h\left(\frac{\Delta}{2} + \frac{3G}{2}\right) $$

The force of the liquid on the belt (per unit width) is $L(\tau_1 + \tau_2)\bar{e}_x$, negative: the liquid resists. The key's boxed $W = VL(\tau_1 + \tau_2)$ is the power of this force (negative); the power that the drive must supply is its opposite:

$$ W_{drive} = -VL\left(\tau_1 + \tau_2\right) = VL\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mu V}{h} + \frac{3}{2}\rho gh\sin\alpha + \frac{h}{2}\,\frac{p_0 - p_a}{L}\right] $$

and with $\Delta = \frac{2}{3}\frac{\mu V}{h^2} - \frac{7}{9}G$ ($\frac{h\Delta}{2} = \frac{1}{3}\frac{\mu V}{h} - \frac{7}{18}Gh$):

$$ W_{drive} = VL\left[\frac{11}{6}\,\frac{\mu V}{h} + \frac{10}{9}\,\rho gh\sin\alpha\right] $$

Energy check (computed here, horizontal case $G = 0$): the viscous dissipation of a gap $H$ per unit length is $\int_0^H\mu\,(\partial v_x/\partial y)^2dy = \mu V^2/H + P_l^2H^3/(12\mu)$ (the cross term integrates to zero). With $P_{l1} = -\Delta = -\frac{2}{3}\mu V/h^2$ and $P_{l2} = \Delta$: stretch 1 gives $\frac{43}{54}\mu V^2/h$ and stretch 2 gives $\frac{28}{27}\mu V^2/h = \frac{56}{54}\mu V^2/h$; adding them and multiplying by $L$ gives $\frac{99}{54}\mu V^2L/h = \frac{11}{6}\mu V^2L/h$, which is $W_{drive}$ for $G = 0$.

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $(\rho Vh/\mu)(h/L)\ll 1$.
  • Pressure: $p = p_a + (p_0 - p_a)x/L$ for $0 < x < L$ and $p = p_0 - (p_0 - p_a)(x - L)/L$ for $L < x < 2L$, with $p_0 - p_a = \frac{2}{3}\mu VL/h^2 - \frac{7}{9}\rho gL\sin\alpha$.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Equation

  • Incompressible liquid of constant $\rho$ and $\mu$ ($\nu = \mu/\rho$) in a pipe of constant section, so the flow is strictly unidirectional, $v_x(r, t)$ (Eq. 7.1), and the reduced pressure gradient $P_l(t) = A\cos\omega t$ is uniform along the pipe (Eq. 7.4).
  • The solution sought is the periodic one, so no initial condition is needed.
  • Aorta: $\omega\simeq 2\pi$ s$^{-1}$ (heart rate 1 Hz), $\nu\simeq 4\times 10^{-2}$ cm$^2$/s, $a\simeq 1.2$ cm.

Equation (7.7) with the forcing and its conditions (symmetry on the axis and no slip at the wall), with the scale of each term below it:

$$ \underbrace{\rho\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial t}}_{\rho V_L\omega} = A\cos\omega t + \underbrace{\frac{\mu}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial r}\right)}_{\mu V_L/a^2}; \qquad r = 0:\; \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial r} = 0, \qquad r = a:\; v_x = 0 $$

The ratio of the local acceleration to the viscous term is $\rho V_L\omega\big/(\mu V_L/a^2) = a^2\omega/\nu$, the square of the Womersley parameter.

Exact Solution by Separation of Variables

With $A\cos\omega t = \operatorname{Re}\left(Ae^{\imath\omega t}\right)$, solve for a complex $v^*$ with $v_x = \operatorname{Re}(v^*)$:

$$ \rho\frac{\partial v^*}{\partial t} = Ae^{\imath\omega t} + \frac{\mu}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial v^*}{\partial r}\right), \qquad v^* = Ae^{\imath\omega t}f(r) $$

Substituting (the factor $e^{\imath\omega t}$ cancels, the variables separate):

$$ \imath\omega\rho f = 1 + \frac{\mu}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{df}{dr}\right); \qquad f'(0) = 0, \qquad f(a) = 0 $$

This is a linear ordinary equation with a constant forcing. A particular solution is the constant $f_p = 1/(\imath\omega\rho)$ (the pressure gradient balances the acceleration). The homogeneous equation $f'' + f'/r - \kappa^2f = 0$ with $\kappa^2 = \imath\omega/\nu$, $\kappa = (1 + \imath)\sqrt{\omega/(2\nu)}$, is the modified Bessel equation of order zero (outside the notes, an extension), whose solution regular on the axis is $I_0(\kappa r)$. The wall condition fixes its coefficient:

$$ f = \frac{1}{\imath\omega\rho}\left[1 - \frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa a)}\right], \qquad v_x = \operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac{A}{\imath\omega\rho}\left[1 - \frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa a)}\right]e^{\imath\omega t}\right\} $$

Check: since $I_0'' + I_0'/x = I_0$, $f'' + f'/r = -\frac{\kappa^2}{\imath\omega\rho}\frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa a)}$, and $\imath\omega\rho f - 1 = -\frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa a)}$ while $\frac{\mu}{r}\left(rf'\right)' = \mu\left(f'' + \frac{f'}{r}\right) = -\frac{\mu\kappa^2}{\imath\omega\rho}\frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa a)} = -\frac{I_0(\kappa r)}{I_0(\kappa a)}$, so the equation holds; $f(a) = 0$ and $f'(0) = 0$ because $I_0'(0) = 0$.

Limit $a^2\omega/\nu\ll 1$: Quasi-steady Poiseuille Flow

The viscous time $a^2/\nu$ is much shorter than $\omega^{-1}$, so the unsteady term is negligible and the profile adjusts at every instant:

$$ 0 = A\cos\omega t + \frac{\mu}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial r}\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; v_x = \frac{A\cos\omega t}{4\mu}\left(a^2 - r^2\right) $$

(the integration of (7.13) with $P_l = A\cos\omega t$). From the exact solution: $I_0(x)\simeq 1 + x^2/4$, so $1 - I_0(\kappa r)/I_0(\kappa a)\simeq\kappa^2(a^2 - r^2)/4$, and $f\simeq\frac{1}{\imath\omega\rho}\frac{\imath\omega}{4\nu}\left(a^2 - r^2\right) = \frac{a^2 - r^2}{4\mu}$.

Limit $a^2\omega/\nu\gg 1$: Uniform Flow and Stokes Layer

Viscosity is negligible in most of the section, and the equation reduces to the balance of acceleration and pressure force, $\rho\,\partial v_x/\partial t = A\cos\omega t$. Integrating in time (the mean value is zero for the periodic solution):

$$ v_x = \frac{A}{\rho\omega}\sin\omega t $$

a uniform flow (the same velocity at every radius) that lags the pressure gradient by a quarter of a period. It does not satisfy the no-slip condition: near the wall there is a thin layer where viscosity matters. The balance $\rho V_L/\omega^{-1}\sim\mu V_L/\delta^2$ gives $\delta\sim\sqrt{\nu/\omega}\ll a$. In it let $\eta = (a - r)/\sqrt{\nu/\omega}$ (the distance to the wall), $\tau = \omega t$ and $\tilde{v} = \frac{A}{\rho\omega}\sin\omega t - v_x$:

$$ \frac{\partial\tilde{v}}{\partial\tau} = \frac{\partial^2\tilde{v}}{\partial\eta^2}; \qquad \eta = 0:\; \tilde{v} = \frac{A}{\rho\omega}\sin\omega t, \qquad \eta\to\infty:\; \tilde{v}\to 0 $$

(the curvature of the pipe is negligible in a layer of thickness $\delta\ll a$). It is the Stokes problem (7.26) for the wall velocity $\frac{A}{\rho\omega}\sin\omega t$, whose solution (7.31), with $\sin$ in place of $\cos$, gives (computed here)

$$ v_x = \frac{A}{\rho\omega}\left[\sin\omega t - e^{-\zeta}\sin(\omega t - \zeta)\right], \qquad \zeta = \frac{a - r}{\sqrt{2\nu/\omega}} $$

which is zero at the wall ($\zeta = 0$) and tends to the uniform flow away from it.

Blood Flow in the Aorta

$$ \frac{a^2\omega}{\nu} = \frac{(1.2\ \text{cm})^2\,2\pi\ \text{s}^{-1}}{4\times 10^{-2}\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}} = \frac{1.44\times 6.283}{0.04} = 226, \qquad a\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{\nu}} = \sqrt{226} = 15 $$

The Womersley parameter is about 15, so the flow in the aorta is in the limit $a^2\omega/\nu\gg 1$: a uniform core with a thin viscous layer of thickness $\sqrt{\nu/\omega} = \sqrt{0.04/6.283}\ \text{cm} = 0.0798\ \text{cm}\simeq 0.8$ mm.

Radius for Poiseuille flow. Viscosity dominates the whole section, and the profile is the quasi-steady Poiseuille one, when $a^2\omega/\nu\lesssim 1$, that is

$$ a\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\omega}}\simeq 0.8\ \text{mm}, \qquad a\ll 0.8\ \text{mm for a good approximation} $$

(about the size of the smallest arteries and arterioles).

Velocity Profile

The wall at $y = 0$ moves with $-V$ and the arm at $y = h$ is at rest, so the Couette and Poiseuille flows of Eqs. (7.9) and (7.11) add (Eq. 7.12):

$$ v_x = -V\,\frac{h - y}{h} + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(h - y) $$

The channel is closed at $x = 0$ and the flux is uniform along it (Eq. 7.72), so the flux is zero:

$$ Q = \int_0^hv_x\,dy = -\frac{Vh}{2} + \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; P_l = \frac{6\mu V}{h^2} $$

and the profile is $v_x = -V\frac{h - y}{h} + \frac{3V}{h^2}\,y\,(h - y)$: the liquid is dragged to the left near the wall and returns to the right near the arm.

Pressure Distribution

$P_l = -dp/dx = (p_0 - p_a)/L$ is constant with $p(L) = p_a$:

$$ p_0 = p_a + \frac{6\mu VL}{h^2}, \qquad p - p_a = \frac{6\mu V}{h^2}\,(L - x) $$

an overpressure, largest at the closed end.

Force Needed to Move the Wiper

Take the wiper as the arm plus the vertical leg. The force of the liquid on it (normal pointing from the wiper into the liquid) has two parts in the $x$ direction.

Pressure on the leg (area $h$ per unit width, normal $+\bar{e}_x$ on its inner face, force $-(p - p_a)\bar{n}$): $-(p_0 - p_a)h = -6\mu VL/h$. The pressure on the underside of the arm is vertical.

Shear on the underside of the arm ($y = h$, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_y$): $\bar{\tau}\cdot\bar{n} = -\mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y$, and $\partial v_x/\partial y = V/h + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}(h - 2y)$ gives at $y = h$: $V/h - \frac{P_lh}{2\mu} = V/h - 3V/h = -2V/h$. So the force per unit area is $+2\mu V/h$ and, over the length $L$, $+2\mu VL/h$.

$$ F_{liquid,x} = -\frac{6\mu VL}{h} + \frac{2\mu VL}{h} = -\frac{4\mu VL}{h} $$

The liquid opposes the motion. The force that must be applied at $A$ is the opposite:

$$ F_x = \frac{4\mu VL}{h} $$

(the key writes the sum of both contributions on one line, "$-\mu(\partial v_x/\partial y)_{y=h}L\,\bar{e}_x = -4\mu VL/h\,\bar{e}_x$", which is the total and not the viscous term alone; it then boxes $F_x = 4\mu VL/h$).

Moment about the End Point $A$

The pressure on the underside of the arm acts upward, $+(p - p_a)\,dx\,\bar{e}_y$, at the point $x$, whose position with respect to $A$ is $(x - L)\bar{e}_x$:

$$ \bar{M}_A = \int_0^L(x - L)\,(p - p_a)\,dx\;\bar{e}_z = -\frac{6\mu V}{h^2}\int_0^L(L - x)^2dx\;\bar{e}_z = -\frac{6\mu V}{h^2}\,\frac{L^3}{3}\,\bar{e}_z = -\frac{2\mu VL^3}{h^2}\,\bar{e}_z $$

(since $\bar{e}_x\times\bar{e}_y = \bar{e}_z$ and $\int_0^L(L - x)^2dx = \left[-(L - x)^3/3\right]_0^L = L^3/3$). The shear forces on the arm act at the level of $A$ and give no moment; the pressure on the leg and the shear on it give a moment of order $\mu VL/h$, a factor $(h/L)^2$ smaller than the one above, and are neglected (the key strikes out the viscous integral).

Critical Velocity

The wiper is subjected to its weight $W$ (at the middle, $x = L/2$), the pressure load of the film on the arm, the force at $A$ and the reaction $R$ of the wall on its end $x = 0$ (upward). Moments about $A$, with $\bar{M}_A$ the moment of the liquid:

$$ 0 = -\frac{2\mu VL^3}{h^2} + W\frac{L}{2} - RL \;\Longrightarrow\; R = \frac{W}{2} - \frac{2\mu VL^2}{h^2} $$

The end $x = 0$ rises when $R = 0$ (the film lifts it):

$$ V_c = \frac{Wh^2}{4\mu L^2} $$

For $V < V_c$ the end rests on the wall and the liquid is retained; for $V > V_c$ it rises and lets the liquid flow out.

  • Force: $F_x = 4\mu VL/h$. Moment about $A$: $\bar{M}_A = -\frac{2\mu VL^3}{h^2}\bar{e}_z$. Critical velocity: $V_c = Wh^2/(4\mu L^2)$.
  • Dimensions: $\mu VL/h$ per unit width is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot LT^{-1}\cdot L/L = MT^{-2}$, a force per unit width; $Wh^2/(\mu L^2)$ is a velocity ($W$ per unit width $\sim MT^{-2}$).

Gap Thickness

Place the centre of the casing at $C$ and the centre of the shaft at $O$, a distance $e$ from $C$, with $\theta$ the angle at $O$ from the line $OC$ direction; the radial line from $O$ at the angle $\theta$ meets the casing at a point at the distance $R + h$ from $O$. In the triangle $O$, $C$, point of the casing, the sides are $R + h$ (from $O$), $e$ (from $C$ to $O$) and $R_b$ (from $C$), with the angle $\theta$ at $O$ between the sides $R + h$ and $e$; the law of cosines gives (the legs of the key's sketch, $(R + h)\cos\theta - e$ and $(R + h)\sin\theta$, give the same):

$$ R_b^2 = (R + h)^2 - 2e(R + h)\cos\theta + e^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; R + h = e\cos\theta + \sqrt{R_b^2 - e^2\sin^2\theta} $$

The square root is $R_b\left[1 - e^2\sin^2\theta/(2R_b^2) + \dots\right]$, and the second term is a factor $e/R_b\ll 1$ smaller than $e$, so $R + h\simeq R_b + e\cos\theta$:

$$ h(\theta) = R_b - R + e\cos\theta = (R_b - R)\left[1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta\right] $$

The gap is largest at $\theta = 0$ and smallest at $\theta = \pi$.

Velocity Profile, Flux and Pressure

The shaft moves with $\omega R$ and the casing is at rest: Couette plus Poiseuille (Eq. 7.70), with the pressure gradient along $x = R\theta$:

$$ v_x = \omega R\,\frac{h - y}{h} - \frac{y(h - y)}{2\mu}\,\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta}, \qquad Q = \int_0^hv_x\,dy = \frac{\omega Rh}{2} - \frac{h^3}{12\mu R}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta} $$

The flux is the same at every $\theta$ (Eq. 7.72), so $\partial P/\partial\theta = \frac{12\mu R}{h^3}\left(\frac{\omega Rh}{2} - Q\right)$, and integrating from $0$ to $\theta$ (Eq. 7.74):

$$ P(\theta) - P(0) = 6\mu\omega R^2\int_0^\theta\frac{d\theta'}{h^2} - 12\mu RQ\int_0^\theta\frac{d\theta'}{h^3} $$

Periodicity. $P(2\pi) = P(0)$ gives $Q = \frac{\omega R}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{h^2}\Big/\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{h^3}$. With $h = c(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)$ and the known integrals (computed here, with $|\varepsilon|<1$)

$$ \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^2} = \frac{2\pi}{(1 - \varepsilon^2)^{3/2}}, \qquad \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^3} = \frac{\pi\left(2 + \varepsilon^2\right)}{(1 - \varepsilon^2)^{5/2}} $$
$$ Q = \frac{\omega Rc}{2}\,\frac{2\pi/(1 - \varepsilon^2)^{3/2}}{\pi(2 + \varepsilon^2)/(1 - \varepsilon^2)^{5/2}} = \omega Rc\,\frac{1 - \varepsilon^2}{2 + \varepsilon^2} $$

Pressure. Substituting $Q$:

$$ \frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta} = \frac{6\mu\omega R^2}{c^2}\left[\frac{1}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^2} - \frac{2(1 - \varepsilon^2)}{(2 + \varepsilon^2)(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^3}\right] = \frac{6\mu\omega R^2}{c^2(2 + \varepsilon^2)}\,\frac{\varepsilon\left(2\cos\theta + 3\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2\cos\theta\right)}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^3} $$

and this is the derivative of

$$ P(\theta) - P(0) = \frac{6\mu\omega R^2}{(R_b - R)^2}\,\frac{\varepsilon\,(2 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)\sin\theta}{(2 + \varepsilon^2)(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^2} $$

(check: with $N = (2 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)\sin\theta$, $N' = 2\cos\theta - \varepsilon + 2\varepsilon\cos^2\theta$ and $\frac{d}{d\theta}\frac{N}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^2} = \frac{N'(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta) + 2\varepsilon\sin\theta\,N}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^3} = \frac{2\cos\theta + 3\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2\cos\theta}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^3}$, which has the factor $\varepsilon$ of the previous line; and the expression vanishes at $\theta = 0$). The pressure is antisymmetric, $P(\theta) - P(0) = -[P(-\theta) - P(0)]$: overpressure on one side of the line of centres and underpressure on the other, which gives a force perpendicular to it.

Force and Torque on the Shaft

Torque. The shear on the shaft (normal from the shaft into the liquid, $+\bar{e}_y$) is $\tau_p = \mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y|_{y=0} = -\frac{\mu\omega R}{h} - \frac{h}{2R}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta}$; with $\partial P/\partial\theta = 6\mu\omega R^2/h^2 - 12\mu RQ/h^3$, $\tau_p = -\frac{4\mu\omega R}{h} + \frac{6\mu Q}{h^2}$. The torque per unit length is $M = \int_0^{2\pi}\tau_pR\cdot R\,d\theta$:

$$ M = -4\mu\omega R^3\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{h} + 6\mu QR^2\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{h^2}, \qquad \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}} $$
$$ M = -\frac{8\pi\mu\omega R^3}{c\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}} + \frac{12\pi\mu\omega R^3}{c(2 + \varepsilon^2)\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}} = -\frac{4\pi\mu\omega R^3}{R_b - R}\,\frac{1 + 2\varepsilon^2}{\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}\,(2 + \varepsilon^2)} $$

(the torque of the liquid on the shaft, opposing the rotation; the torque the motor applies is its opposite).

Force. The pressure force on the shaft is $\bar{F} = -\int(P - P(0))\,\bar{n}\,R\,d\theta$, with $\bar{n}$ the outward normal of the shaft, $n_z = \sin\theta$ and $n_\parallel = \cos\theta$ (the uniform $P(0)$ gives no net force). The component along the line of centres is zero because $P - P(0)$ is odd in $\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ is even. The perpendicular one, integrating by parts ($P(0) = P(2\pi)$ so the boundary term vanishes): $\int_0^{2\pi}(P - P(0))\sin\theta\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta}\,d\theta = \frac{6\mu\omega R^2\varepsilon}{c^2(2 + \varepsilon^2)}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta\left(2\cos\theta + 3\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2\cos\theta\right)}{(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^3}d\theta$, and the last integral equals $2\pi/\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}$ (verified numerically in the checks of this note). Hence

$$ F_z = -R\int_0^{2\pi}(P - P(0))\sin\theta\,d\theta = -\frac{12\pi\mu\omega R^3}{(R_b - R)^2}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}\,(2 + \varepsilon^2)} $$

on the shaft (downward in the figure), and the opposite on the bearing (upward). The shear forces contribute to the force on the shaft at a relative order $c/R\ll 1$ and are neglected.

Limit of Large Eccentricity and Cavitation

When $\varepsilon\to 1$ (the shaft touches the casing, $h_{min} = c(1 - \varepsilon)\to 0$) the force grows without bound, $F_z\propto(1 - \varepsilon^2)^{-1/2}$: a bearing can in principle bear an infinite load. In practice it fails earlier: the underpressure side of $P(\theta) - P(0)$ reaches the vapour pressure $p_v$ and the liquid cavitates when $P_{min} - P(0) = p_v - P(0)$. The minimum of $P$ is where $\partial P/\partial\theta = 0$, that is $\cos\theta_{min} = -3\varepsilon/(2 + \varepsilon^2)$ with $\sin\theta_{min}<0$ ($\theta_{min}\simeq 3\pi/2$ for small $\varepsilon$, as the key notes).

Answers and Checks

  • Velocity: $v_x = \omega R\frac{h - y}{h} - \frac{y(h - y)}{2\mu R}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta}$; flux: $Q = \omega R(R_b - R)\frac{1 - \varepsilon^2}{2 + \varepsilon^2}$.
  • Pressure: $P - P(0) = \frac{6\mu\omega R^2}{(R_b - R)^2}\frac{\varepsilon(2 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)\sin\theta}{(2 + \varepsilon^2)(1 + \varepsilon\cos\theta)^2}$.
  • Torque: $M = -\frac{4\pi\mu\omega R^3}{R_b - R}\frac{1 + 2\varepsilon^2}{\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}(2 + \varepsilon^2)}$; force: $F_z = -\frac{12\pi\mu\omega R^3}{(R_b - R)^2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1 - \varepsilon^2}(2 + \varepsilon^2)}$ on the shaft, opposite on the bearing.

  • Dimensions: $\mu\omega R^3/(R_b - R)$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot T^{-1}\cdot L^3/L = MLT^{-2}$, a torque per unit length (N m/m = N); $\mu\omega R^3/(R_b - R)^2$ is a force per unit length.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The scales are $h_c\sim L\alpha$ (largest gap) and $t_0 = \alpha/\omega$ (time to change the angle by its own value). The continuity equation $\partial v_x/\partial x + \partial v_y/\partial y = 0$ with $v_y\sim\omega L$ gives $V_L\sim\omega L^2/h_c = \omega L/\alpha$. Local acceleration and convective acceleration against the viscous force (Eqs. 7.67-7.68):

$$ \frac{h_c^2/\nu}{t_0}\sim\frac{L^2\alpha^2\omega}{\nu\alpha} = \frac{\omega L^2\alpha}{\nu}, \qquad \frac{V_Lh_c}{\nu}\frac{h_c}{L}\sim\frac{\omega L}{\alpha}\frac{L\alpha}{\nu}\,\alpha = \frac{\omega L^2\alpha}{\nu} $$

Both are the same number, and viscosity dominates if it is much smaller than one:

$$ \alpha\ll\alpha_c = \frac{\nu}{\omega L^2} $$

(and of course $\alpha\ll 1$).

Velocity Profile and Flux

Equation (7.69) with the no-slip conditions at the wall ($y = 0$) and at the plate ($y = h$), both at rest in this approximation (the normal motion of the plate is the continuity term), integrated as for (7.11):

$$ v_x = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(h - y), \qquad Q = \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu} = -\frac{\alpha^3x^3}{12\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x} $$

Continuity and Pressure

Integrate $\partial v_x/\partial x + \partial v_y/\partial y = 0$ across the gap:

$$ \int_0^h\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}\,dy + \int_0^h\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y}\,dy = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{dh}{dx}\,v_x(y = h) + v_y(h) - v_y(0) = 0 $$

The term $\frac{dh}{dx}v_x(y = h)$ vanishes because $v_x(h) = 0$ (no slip on the plate), $v_y(0) = 0$ on the wall and $v_y(h) = \partial h/\partial t = \omega x$:

$$ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} + \omega x = 0 $$

Integrating with $Q = 0$ at $x = 0$: $Q = -\omega x^2/2$ (negative: inflow towards the hinge). Equating with the flux of the profile:

$$ -\frac{\alpha^3x^3}{12\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x} = -\frac{\omega x^2}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} = \frac{6\mu\omega}{\alpha^3}\,\frac{1}{x} $$

(the constant of integration of the first step is zero because $x^3P_l\to 0$ as $x\to 0$). Integrating from $L$ to $x$ with $P(L) = P_\infty$:

$$ P - P_\infty = \frac{6\mu\omega}{\alpha^3}\,\ln\frac{x}{L} $$

negative for $x < L$: an underpressure that grows without bound near the hinge (logarithmically) and tends to hold the plate down.

Torque

The moment of the pressure on the plate about the hinge, per unit width, with the pressure acting on its underside (the force is vertical to this order, at the position $x$):

$$ M_{liquid} = \int_0^L(P - P_\infty)\,x\,dx\;\bar{e}_z = \frac{6\mu\omega}{\alpha^3}\int_0^Lx\ln\frac{x}{L}\,dx\;\bar{e}_z, \qquad \int_0^Lx\ln\frac{x}{L}dx = L^2\int_0^1u\ln u\,du = L^2\left[\frac{u^2}{2}\ln u - \frac{u^2}{4}\right]_0^1 = -\frac{L^2}{4} $$
$$ M_{liquid} = -\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{\mu\omega L^2}{\alpha^3}\,\bar{e}_z $$

The liquid opposes the rotation (the rotation is counterclockwise, $+\bar{e}_z$), so the torque that must be applied is the opposite, positive:

$$ \bar{M} = \frac{3}{2}\,\frac{\mu\,\omega\,L^2}{\alpha^3}\,\bar{e}_z $$

It grows as $\alpha^{-3}$ at the start of the motion (small $\alpha$). The shear on the plate gives a moment smaller by a factor $\alpha$.

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $\alpha\ll\nu/(\omega L^2)$.
  • Profile and flux: $v_x = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}y(h - y)$, $Q = \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu} = -\frac{\omega x^2}{2}$.
  • Pressure: $P - P_\infty = \frac{6\mu\omega}{\alpha^3}\ln(x/L)$. Torque: $M = \frac{3}{2}\mu\omega L^2/\alpha^3$.
  • Dimensions: $\mu\omega L^2/\alpha^3$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot T^{-1}\cdot L^2 = MT^{-2}\cdot L$, a torque per unit width (N); $\nu/(\omega L^2)$ is dimensionless.
  • Consistency: the volume flowing in through $x = L$ is $|Q(L)| = \omega L^2/2$ per unit width, equal to the area of the wedge swept per unit time, $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}L\cdot\alpha L\right) = \omega L^2/2$.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The arm moves vertically with the velocity $\dot{h} = dh/dt$ (negative when it approaches the wall); the channel, $0 < x < L$, $0 < y < h(t)$, is closed at $x = 0$ by the vertical leg and open at $x = L$, where the pressure is $P_\infty$.
  • Slender film ($h\ll L$), pressure uniform across it; gravity is absorbed in the reduced pressure.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

By continuity $V_L\sim L\dot{h}/h$ and $t_0\sim h/|\dot{h}|$. The convective and local accelerations against the viscous force (Eqs. 7.67-7.68):

$$ \frac{\rho V_Lh}{\mu}\frac{h}{L}\sim\frac{L\dot{h}}{h}\frac{h}{\nu}\frac{h}{L} = \frac{h\dot{h}}{\nu}\ll 1, \qquad \frac{h^2/\nu}{t_0}\sim\frac{h\dot{h}}{\nu}\ll 1 $$

So $h\,|\dot{h}|/\nu\ll 1$.

Velocity, Flux and Pressure

Equation (7.69) with no slip on the wall and on the arm ($v_x = 0$ at $y = 0$ and $y = h$) gives the Poiseuille profile (7.11):

$$ 0 = P_l + \mu\frac{\partial^2v_x}{\partial y^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; v_x = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(h - y), \qquad Q = \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu} = -\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\frac{h^3}{12\mu} $$

Integrating the continuity equation across the channel, with $v_x(h) = 0$, $v_y(0) = 0$ and $v_y(h) = \dot{h}$ (the arm moves with $\dot{h}$):

$$ \int_0^{h}\left(\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y}\right)dy = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} + \dot{h} = 0 $$

so $Q = -\dot{h}x$ (zero at the closed end $x = 0$; positive for an approaching arm). Then $-\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\frac{h^3}{12\mu} = -\dot{h}x$, that is, the first integration of the key, $\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\frac{h^3}{12\mu} = \dot{h}x$ (the key's boxed line carries a sign that is the opposite of its next line, which is the one used):

$$ \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} = \frac{12\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,x \;\Longrightarrow\; P = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,x^2 + C_2 $$

With $P(L) = P_\infty$:

$$ P(x, t) = P_\infty + \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left(x^2 - L^2\right), \qquad P_0(t) = P_\infty - \frac{6\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^3} $$

For an approaching arm ($\dot{h}<0$) there is an overpressure, largest at the closed end.

Force on the Arm

Vertical force. The liquid below the arm pushes it upward with the excess pressure (the pressure $P_\infty$ acts on the whole surface of the arm and gives no net force):

$$ F_y = \int_0^L(P - P_\infty)\,dx = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left[\frac{x^3}{3} - L^2x\right]_0^L = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left(-\frac{2L^3}{3}\right) = -\frac{4\mu\dot{h}L^3}{h^3} $$

positive for $\dot{h}<0$: the liquid resists the approach (it is a force of the order of $\mu|\dot{h}|L^3/h^3$, very large for a thin film).

Horizontal force. Two contributions: the pressure on the inner face of the vertical leg (area $h$, normal $+\bar{e}_x$ from the arm into the liquid; the force of the liquid is $-(P_0 - P_\infty)h$) and the shear on the underside of the arm ($\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_y$, force $-\mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y$ per unit area):

$$ F_x = -(P_0 - P_\infty)\,h - \int_0^L\mu\left.\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right|_{y=h}dx, \qquad -(P_0 - P_\infty)h = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2} $$

With $\partial v_x/\partial y = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}(h - 2y)$, $P_l = -\partial P/\partial x = -12\mu\dot{h}x/h^3$: at $y = h$, $\mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y = -\frac{P_lh}{2} = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}x}{h^2}$, and

$$ -\int_0^L\frac{6\mu\dot{h}x}{h^2}\,dx = -\frac{3\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2}, \qquad F_x = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2} - \frac{3\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2} = \frac{3\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2} $$
$$ \bar{F} = \left(\frac{3\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2},\; -\frac{4\mu\dot{h}L^3}{h^3}\right), \qquad |F_x|/|F_y|\sim h/L\ll 1 $$

Torque about the End Point $A$

The pressure on the underside of the arm gives the moment about $A$ ($x = L$) of a vertical force at the distance $x - L$ (arm positive along $\bar{e}_x\times\bar{e}_y = \bar{e}_z$):

$$ \bar{M} = -\int_0^L(P - P_\infty)(L - x)\,dx\;\bar{e}_z = -\frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\int_0^L\left(x^2 - L^2\right)(L - x)\,dx\;\bar{e}_z $$
$$ \int_0^L\left(x^2 - L^2\right)(L - x)\,dx = \int_0^L\left(Lx^2 - x^3 - L^3 + L^2x\right)dx = L\frac{L^3}{3} - \frac{L^4}{4} - L^4 + \frac{L^4}{2} = L^4\left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4} - 1 + \frac{1}{2}\right) = -\frac{5}{12}L^4 $$
$$ \bar{M} = \frac{5}{2}\,\frac{\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,L^4\;\bar{e}_z $$

The moment of the pressure on the leg is of order $\mu\dot{h}L^2/h$ (the pressure $\sim\mu\dot{h}L^2/h^3$ acting on the area $h$ with the arm $\sim h$) and the moment of the shear on the arm is $\sim\mu\dot{h}L^2/h$: a factor $(h/L)^2$ smaller than the one above and neglected, as the key does.

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $h|\dot{h}|/\nu\ll 1$.
  • Profile and flux: $v_x = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}y(h - y)$, $Q = -\dot{h}x$; pressure: $P = P_\infty + \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}(x^2 - L^2)$, $P_0 = P_\infty - \frac{6\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^3}$.
  • Force: $\bar{F} = \left(\frac{3\mu\dot{h}L^2}{h^2}, -\frac{4\mu\dot{h}L^3}{h^3}\right)$; torque about $A$: $\bar{M} = \frac{5}{2}\frac{\mu\dot{h}L^4}{h^3}\bar{e}_z$.
  • Dimensions: $\mu\dot{h}L^3/h^3$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot LT^{-1}\cdot L^3/L^3 = MLT^{-2}\cdot L^{-1}$, a force per unit width.
  • Limits: $\dot{h}\to 0$ gives zero force, torque and overpressure; $h\to 0$ makes them grow as $h^{-3}$ (and the film does not drain in finite time at a constant $\dot{h}$ without infinite force).

Characteristic Velocity and Criterion for Pumping

The balance of the pressure and viscous forces in the gap, $\Delta P/L\sim\mu V_{\Delta P}/h^2$, gives the velocity induced by the overpressure (which pushes the liquid backward, from high to low pressure):

$$ V_{\Delta P}\sim\frac{h^2\Delta P}{\mu L}, \qquad \Lambda = \frac{V_{\Delta P}}{V_l + V_u} = \frac{h^2\Delta P}{\mu L\left(V_l + V_u\right)} $$

The belts drag the liquid forward with a velocity of order $V_l + V_u$: the pump only works if this velocity beats the backflow, $V_{\Delta P}$ sufficiently smaller than $V_l + V_u$, that is, $\Lambda$ small enough (Part 6 shows that the flux vanishes for a finite value of $\Lambda$).

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The slender-flow condition (Eq. 7.41) is $(\rho V h/\mu)(h/L)\ll 1$ with the largest velocity of the problem. Depending on which one dominates:

$$ \Lambda\ll 1\ (V\sim V_l + V_u):\quad\frac{\rho\left(V_l + V_u\right)h^2}{\mu L}\ll 1; \qquad\Lambda\gg 1\ (V\sim V_{\Delta P}):\quad\frac{\rho\,\Delta P\,h^4}{\mu^2L^2}\ll 1 $$

The pump only works for $\Lambda$ small, so the first condition is the one that matters. The flow is steady, so the unsteady condition holds.

Velocity Profile and Flux

With $P_l = -\partial P/\partial x$ and Eq. (7.12) extended to two moving walls:

$$ 0 = P_l + \mu\frac{\partial^2v_x}{\partial y^2}; \qquad y = 0:\; v_x = V_l, \qquad y = h:\; v_x = V_u $$

$v_x = -\frac{P_ly^2}{2\mu} + Ay + B$ with $B = V_l$ and $A = \frac{V_u - V_l}{h} + \frac{P_lh}{2\mu}$:

$$ v_x = V_l + \frac{V_u - V_l}{h}\,y + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(h - y), \qquad q = \int_0^hv_x\,dy = \frac{(V_l + V_u)h}{2} + \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu} $$

Pressure Gradient and Pressure

Integrating the continuity equation across the gap: $\partial q/\partial x = v_y(0) - v_y(h) = 0$ (the belts do not move normally to themselves), so $q$ is uniform and so is $P_l$:

$$ \frac{\partial^2P}{\partial x^2} = 0,\quad P(0) = P_0,\quad P(L) = P_0 + \Delta P \;\Longrightarrow\; P = P_0 + \Delta P\,\frac{x}{L}, \qquad P_l = -\frac{\Delta P}{L} $$

Forces on the Belts and Power

The shear stress is $\tau'_{xy} = \mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y = \mu\frac{V_u - V_l}{h} - \frac{\Delta P}{2L}(h - 2y)$ (with $P_l = -\Delta P/L$). The force of the liquid on a belt is $\bar{\tau}'\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n}$ from the belt into the liquid ($+\bar{e}_y$ on the lower belt, $-\bar{e}_y$ on the upper belt); only the $x$ components are considered (the pressure is normal to the belts):

$$ F_{lx} = \int_0^L\tau'_{xy}\big|_{y=0}dx = \frac{\mu\left(V_u - V_l\right)L}{h} - \frac{\Delta P\,h}{2}, \qquad F_{ux} = -\int_0^L\tau'_{xy}\big|_{y=h}dx = -\frac{\mu\left(V_u - V_l\right)L}{h} - \frac{\Delta P\,h}{2} $$

The external forces that keep the belts at constant velocity are the opposite:

$$ F^{EXT}_{u,x} = \frac{\Delta P\,h}{2} + \frac{\mu\left(V_u - V_l\right)L}{h}, \qquad F^{EXT}_{l,x} = \frac{\Delta P\,h}{2} - \frac{\mu\left(V_u - V_l\right)L}{h} $$

and the power per unit spanwise length is the sum of force times velocity:

$$ \dot{W} = F^{EXT}_{l,x}V_l + F^{EXT}_{u,x}V_u = \frac{\left(V_l + V_u\right)h\,\Delta P}{2} + \frac{\left(V_u - V_l\right)^2\mu L}{h} $$

The first term is the work against the pressure difference (the force $\Delta P\,h/2$ on each belt), the second is the viscous dissipation of the Couette part.

Efficiency

$$ \eta = \frac{q\,\Delta P}{\dot{W}} = \frac{\dfrac{(V_l + V_u)h\,\Delta P}{2} - \dfrac{h^3\Delta P^2}{12\mu L}}{\dfrac{(V_l + V_u)h\,\Delta P}{2} + \dfrac{(V_u - V_l)^2\mu L}{h}} $$

Dividing numerator and denominator by $(V_l + V_u)h\,\Delta P/2$, with $\frac{h^2\Delta P}{6\mu L(V_l + V_u)} = \frac{\Lambda}{6}$ and $\frac{2\mu L(V_u - V_l)^2}{h^2\Delta P(V_u + V_l)} = \frac{2}{\Lambda}\left(\frac{V_u - V_l}{V_u + V_l}\right)^2$:

$$ \eta = \frac{1 - \Lambda/6}{1 + \dfrac{2}{\Lambda}\left(\dfrac{V_u - V_l}{V_u + V_l}\right)^2} $$

The flux $q = \frac{(V_l + V_u)h}{2}\left(1 - \frac{\Lambda}{6}\right)$ vanishes at $\Lambda = 6$: the pump works for $\Lambda<6$.

a) $V_l = V_u$. The ratio of velocities is zero and $\eta = 1 - \Lambda/6$ (no shear dissipation: the walls move together).

b) $V_l = 0$. The ratio is 1, so $\eta = \dfrac{1 - \Lambda/6}{1 + 2/\Lambda} = \dfrac{\Lambda(6 - \Lambda)}{6(\Lambda + 2)}$, which is zero at $\Lambda = 0$ and at $\Lambda = 6$. The maximum: $\frac{d}{d\Lambda}\frac{6\Lambda - \Lambda^2}{6\Lambda + 12} = 0$ gives $(6 - 2\Lambda)(\Lambda + 2) - (6\Lambda - \Lambda^2) = 0$, that is $\Lambda^2 + 4\Lambda - 12 = 0$, $(\Lambda + 6)(\Lambda - 2) = 0$:

$$ \Lambda_{max} = 2, \qquad \eta_{max} = \frac{2\cdot 4}{6\cdot 4} = \frac{1}{3} $$

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The film between the piston and the bottom is slender ($h\ll R$), axisymmetric, with $z$ the height above the bottom and $r$ the radius. The velocity at the piston is $v_z = \dot{h}$ ($<0$ while it falls), at the bottom $v_z = 0$.
  • The liquid has no weight effects, so $P\approx p$; the pressure at the hole edge is $p_a$ (the non-slender turning region has a negligible pressure drop).
  • The piston has its weight $W$ downward and the atmospheric pressure on its upper face; the liquid pressure acts on its lower face.

Velocity Profile

Radial momentum (Eq. 7.69 in cylindrical coordinates) with no slip at the bottom and the piston:

$$ 0 = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} + \mu\frac{\partial^2v_r}{\partial z^2} = P_l + \mu\frac{\partial^2v_r}{\partial z^2}; \qquad z = 0:\; v_r = 0, \qquad z = h:\; v_r = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v_r = \frac{P_l(r, t)}{2\mu}\,z\,(h - z) $$

Pressure

Integrate the continuity equation $\frac{\partial(rv_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial(rv_z)}{\partial z} = 0$ across the gap:

$$ \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[r\int_0^hv_r\,dz\right] + r\left[v_z(h) - v_z(0)\right] = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[r\,\frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu}\right] + r\dot{h} = 0 $$

and with $P_l = -\partial p/\partial r$: $\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}\right) = \frac{12\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,r$. First integration: $r\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}r^2 + A$. At the container wall $v_r = 0$ for every $z$, and since $v_r\propto P_l$ this means $\partial p/\partial r = 0$ at $r = R$:

$$ A = -\frac{6\mu\dot{h}R^2}{h^3}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left(r - \frac{R^2}{r}\right) $$

Second integration, with $p(\alpha R) = p_a$:

$$ p = \frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left(\frac{r^2}{2} - R^2\ln r\right) + B \;\Longrightarrow\; p - p_a = -\frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left[R^2\ln\frac{r}{\alpha R} - \frac{r^2 - (\alpha R)^2}{2}\right] $$

For $\dot{h}<0$ it is an overpressure.

Force on the Piston

The excess force is $F = 2\pi\int_{\alpha R}^{R}(p - p_a)\,r\,dr$ (the part $r<\alpha R$, in front of the hole, is at about $p_a$). With $r = R\eta$, $r\,dr = R^2\eta\,d\eta$:

$$ F = -\frac{12\pi\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\,R^4f(\alpha), \qquad f(\alpha) = \int_\alpha^1\eta\left[\ln\eta - \ln\alpha - \frac{\eta^2 - \alpha^2}{2}\right]d\eta $$

Evaluating the three pieces with Barrow's rule (using the hint $\int\eta\ln\eta\,d\eta = \frac{\eta^2}{2}\left(\ln\eta - \frac{1}{2}\right)$):

$$ \int_\alpha^1\eta\ln\eta\,d\eta = -\frac{1}{4} - \frac{\alpha^2}{2}\ln\alpha + \frac{\alpha^2}{4}, \qquad -\ln\alpha\int_\alpha^1\eta\,d\eta = -\frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2}\ln\alpha, \qquad -\frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^1\left(\eta^3 - \alpha^2\eta\right)d\eta = -\frac{1 - \alpha^4}{8} + \frac{\alpha^2(1 - \alpha^2)}{4} $$
$$ f(\alpha) = -\frac{1}{2}\ln\alpha - \frac{3}{8} + \frac{\alpha^2}{2} - \frac{\alpha^4}{8} $$
Discrepancy with the official solution

The key gives "$f(\alpha) = -\frac{1}{2}\ln\alpha - \frac{5}{12} + \frac{3}{4}\alpha^2 - \frac{1}{3}\alpha^3$" after "computing the integral". That expression is not the value of the integral above (it is not even a function of $\alpha^2$, as the integral is): at $\alpha = 0.5$ the integral is $0.0888$ and the key's polynomial gives $0.0757$. Both vanish at $\alpha = 1$ and both behave as $-\frac{1}{2}\ln\alpha$ for $\alpha\to 0$. The value above is used from here on; its derivative is $f'(\alpha) = -(1 - \alpha^2)^2/(2\alpha)$, which is negative: $f$ decreases from infinity to zero as the hole grows.

The force is positive (upward) for $\dot{h}<0$: the liquid resists the fall.

Equation of the Piston and Solution

Newton's second law for the piston in the vertical direction (the weight $W$ downward, the force $F$ of the liquid and of the atmosphere upward; $h$ upward):

$$ \frac{W}{g}\,\ddot{h} = F - W, \qquad h(0) = h_0,\quad\dot{h}(0) = 0 $$

(with $F = -12\pi\mu\dot{h}R^4f(\alpha)/h^3$, a second-order ODE).

Negligible inertia, $|\ddot{h}|\ll g$. Then $F = W$ and the equation is of first order, with only $h(0) = h_0$:

$$ \frac{dh}{dt} = -\frac{W}{12\pi\mu R^4f(\alpha)}\,h^3 \equiv -k\,h^3 $$

Separating variables, $\frac{dh}{h^3} = -k\,dt$, and integrating (Barrow's rule): $\left[-\frac{1}{2h^2}\right]_{h_0}^h = -kt$, so $\frac{1}{h^2} = \frac{1}{h_0^2} + 2kt$:

$$ \bar{h} = \frac{h}{h_0} = (1 + 2\tau)^{-1/2}, \qquad \tau = \frac{Wh_0^2}{12\pi\mu R^4f(\alpha)}\,t $$

Volume flux through the hole. The liquid expelled equals the volume swept by the piston, $Q = -\pi R^2\dot{h} = \pi R^2kh^3$ (computed here):

$$ Q(t) = \frac{W\,h^3}{12\mu R^2f(\alpha)} = \frac{Wh_0^3}{12\mu R^2f(\alpha)}\left(1 + 2\tau\right)^{-3/2} $$

The piston never reaches the bottom in a finite time: $h\to 0$ as $t^{-1/2}$ (it needs to squeeze an ever thinner film).

Criteria

Viscous dominance. The scale of the velocity of the piston follows from the time scale $t_c\sim\mu R^4/(Wh_0^2)$ (the factor of $\tau$): $\dot{h}_c\sim h_0/t_c = Wh_0^3/(\mu R^4)$. The slender-film conditions (Eqs. 7.67-7.68) reduce, as in VF14, to

$$ \frac{h_0\dot{h}_c}{\nu}\ll 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\rho\,Wh_0^4}{\mu^2R^4}\ll 1 $$

(besides $h_0\ll R$).

Negligible inertia of the piston. The acceleration scale is $\ddot{h}_c\sim h_0/t_c^2 = h_0\left(\dfrac{Wh_0^2}{\mu R^4}\right)^2 = \dfrac{W^2h_0^5}{\mu^2R^8}$, so the condition $\ddot{h}_c\ll g$ is

$$ \frac{W^2h_0^5}{\mu^2R^8g}\ll 1 $$

Answers and Checks

  • Velocity: $v_r = \frac{P_l}{2\mu}z(h - z)$; pressure: $p - p_a = -\frac{6\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}\left[R^2\ln\frac{r}{\alpha R} - \frac{r^2 - (\alpha R)^2}{2}\right]$.
  • Force: $F = -\frac{12\pi\mu\dot{h}}{h^3}R^4f(\alpha)$, $f(\alpha) = -\frac{1}{2}\ln\alpha - \frac{3}{8} + \frac{\alpha^2}{2} - \frac{\alpha^4}{8}$.
  • Equation: $\frac{W}{g}\ddot{h} = F - W$, $h(0) = h_0$, $\dot{h}(0) = 0$.
  • Quasi-steady piston: $\bar{h} = (1 + 2\tau)^{-1/2}$, $\tau = \frac{Wh_0^2t}{12\pi\mu R^4f(\alpha)}$; $Q(t) = \frac{Wh^3}{12\mu R^2f(\alpha)}$.
  • Criteria: $\rho Wh_0^4/(\mu^2R^4)\ll 1$ and $W^2h_0^5/(\mu^2R^8g)\ll 1$.
  • Dimensions: $Wh_0^2/(\mu R^4)$ is $MLT^{-2}\cdot L^2/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}L^4) = T^{-1}$, so $\tau$ is dimensionless; $Wh^3/(\mu R^2)$ is $L^3T^{-1}$.
  • Limit $\alpha\to 1$ (a hole as wide as the piston): $f\to 0$ and $F\to 0$, so the liquid offers no resistance, as it can escape freely; $\alpha\to 0$ (a tiny hole): $f\simeq-\frac{1}{2}\ln\alpha\to\infty$ and the fall is slowed down.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

In the half-channel of height $h$ and length $L$ with the velocity scale $U$ (Eq. 7.41):

$$ \frac{\rho Uh}{\mu}\,\frac{h}{L}\ll 1 $$

The flow is steady. (If the pressure-driven velocity, computed below, is of the order of $U$ the same criterion holds.)

Velocity Field in the Plate Region

In the upper half-channel, $0 < y < h$, with the plate at rest ($y = 0$) and the wall moving with $U$ ($y = h$), the profile is Couette plus Poiseuille (Eq. 7.12):

$$ v_x = U\,\frac{y}{h} + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,y\,(h - y), \qquad q_{half} = \int_0^hv_x\,dy = \frac{Uh}{2} + \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu} $$

The lower half is the mirror image, $v_x(-y) = v_x(y)$.

Pressure Drop and Pressure Gradient

The flux is conserved along the channel. Far from the plate it is $2h\,U$ (uniform velocity). Over the plate it is the sum of the two halves:

$$ 2hU = 2\left[\frac{Uh}{2} + \frac{P_lh^3}{12\mu}\right] = Uh + \frac{P_lh^3}{6\mu} \;\Longrightarrow\; P_l = \frac{6\mu U}{h^2} $$

The couette flux alone ($Uh$) is only half of what must pass, so the pressure must push: $P_l>0$, the pressure falls in the direction of the flow. The gradient is uniform (the section is constant), so

$$ P_{-\infty} - P_\infty = P_lL = \frac{6\mu UL}{h^2} $$

(the losses at the ends of the plate are of order $\mu U/h$, a factor $h/L$ smaller). With this value the velocity is

$$ v_x = U\frac{y}{h} + \frac{3U}{h^2}\,y\,(h - y) = \frac{U\,y}{h^2}\,(4h - 3y), \qquad 0\le y\le h $$

which is $U$ at the wall, zero at the plate and has a maximum $\frac{4}{3}U$ at $y = \frac{2}{3}h$ (the jet seen in the sketch of the sheet).

Forces and Power

The shear stress is $\mu\,\partial v_x/\partial y = \mu\left[\frac{U}{h} + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}(h - 2y)\right] = \frac{\mu U}{h^2}\left(4h - 6y\right)$. The force of the liquid on a surface is $\bar{\tau}'\cdot\bar{n}$ with $\bar{n}$ pointing from the surface into the liquid.

Walls. On the upper wall ($y = h$, $\bar{n} = -\bar{e}_y$): $\tau = -\mu\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\Big|_h = -\frac{\mu U}{h^2}(4h - 6h) = \frac{2\mu U}{h}$ in $+x$: the liquid in the gap moves faster than the wall and drags it forward. Over the length $L$, for each wall (the walls outside the plate region are in uniform flow, with no shear):

$$ F_w = \frac{2\mu UL}{h} \;\text{ on each wall},\quad\text{in the direction of its motion}; \qquad\text{both walls: }\frac{4\mu UL}{h} $$

Plate. On each face ($y = 0^{\pm}$, $\bar{n} = \pm\bar{e}_y$): $\tau = \mu\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\Big|_0 = \frac{4\mu U}{h}$ along $+x$. Two faces and the length $L$:

$$ F_p = 2\cdot\frac{4\mu U}{h}\,L = \frac{8\mu UL}{h} $$

(the drag on the plate, in the direction of the flow).

Check by momentum. For the liquid in the plate region (no change of momentum flux, uniform $U$ in and out), the forces on it add to zero: the pressure difference on the section $2h$, $2h(P_{-\infty} - P_\infty) = 12\mu UL/h$, minus the reactions on the two walls, $4\mu UL/h$, minus the reaction on the plate, $8\mu UL/h$: $12 - 4 - 8 = 0$.

Power. The liquid pushes the walls forward with the total force $4\mu UL/h$, so to keep them at the constant velocity $U$ an external force $-F_w$ must hold each one back. The power that the external agent supplies to the walls is

$$ \dot{W}_{walls} = -2F_wU = -\frac{4\mu U^2L}{h} $$

negative: the walls do not have to be driven, they receive the power $4\mu U^2L/h$ from the liquid (which is being pushed through the plate region by the imposed pressure drop); a brake has to absorb it. Its magnitude is $2F_wU$.

Temperature Increase

Control volume: the liquid between two sections far upstream and far downstream, bounded by the walls and the plate (all insulated: no heat). Steady energy balance in integral form: net outflow of $\rho(e + v^2/2)$ plus the work of the pressure at the two sections equals the work of the viscous forces on the moving boundaries (the plate is at rest, the walls move with $U$). The kinetic energy flux is the same in and out (uniform $U$); with $e = cT$:

$$ \rho c\,(2hU)\left(T_\infty - T_{-\infty}\right) = \underbrace{2hU\left(P_{-\infty} - P_\infty\right)}_{\text{work of the pressure}} - \underbrace{2F_wU}_{\text{work done on the walls}} = \frac{12\mu U^2L}{h} - \frac{4\mu U^2L}{h} = \frac{8\mu U^2L}{h} $$

(the work of the force that the walls exert on the liquid is $-F_w U$ per wall; the reduced pressure is used, since the flow is horizontal and the heights are the same). Hence

$$ T_\infty - T_{-\infty} = \frac{8\mu U^2L/h}{2\rho c\,hU} = \frac{4\mu UL}{\rho c\,h^2} $$

Check with the dissipation: $\Phi = \mu(\partial v_x/\partial y)^2 = \mu U^2(4h - 6y)^2/h^4$, and $\int_0^h\Phi\,dy = \frac{\mu U^2}{h^4}\left[-\frac{(4h - 6y)^3}{18}\right]_0^h = \frac{\mu U^2}{h^4}\,\frac{64h^3 + 8h^3}{18} = \frac{4\mu U^2}{h}$; two halves and the length $L$: $\frac{8\mu U^2L}{h}$, the same, as it must be (all the mechanical power that is not stored becomes heat).

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $(\rho Uh/\mu)(h/L)\ll 1$.
  • Velocity over the plate: $v_x = \frac{U\,y}{h^2}(4h - 3y)$ for $0\le y\le h$ (symmetric in $y$); $P_l = 6\mu U/h^2$; $P_{-\infty} - P_\infty = 6\mu UL/h^2$.
  • Forces: $F_w = 2\mu UL/h$ on each wall (forward), $F_p = 8\mu UL/h$ on the plate (forward). Power at the walls: $2F_wU = 4\mu U^2L/h$, received from the liquid.
  • Temperature: $T_\infty - T_{-\infty} = 4\mu UL/(\rho ch^2)$.
  • Dimensions: $\mu UL/h$ per unit width is $MT^{-2}$, a force per unit width; $\mu UL/(\rho ch^2)$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot LT^{-1}\cdot L/(ML^{-3}\cdot L^2T^{-2}\Theta^{-1}\cdot L^2) = \Theta$.
  • Limit $L\to 0$: all the quantities vanish, as they should without a plate.
  • Check against the official key: none; the sheet has no solution, so all the results of this note are computed here and checked by the momentum, flux and energy balances above.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The wall ($y = 0$) moves up with $V$; the plate ($y = d$) is at rest. $z$ is measured upwards from the free surface of the vessel; the gap extends from the lower end of the plate, $z = -H$, up to the top of the column, $z = h(t)$, with $h(0) = 0$.
  • The free surface of the vessel and the top of the column are at $p_a$. The hydrostatic pressure at the lower end of the plate: $p = p_a + \rho gH$.
  • The flow is slender and quasi-steady (viscosity dominates), so the profile is Couette plus Poiseuille at each instant, with the reduced pressure uniform across the gap and a uniform gradient $P_l(t)$ along it.

$V$velocity of the wallm/s
$d$, $H$width of the gap, depth of the lower end of the platem
$h(t)$height of the column above the free surfacem
$Q(t)$volume flux entering the gap (per unit width)m$^2$/s

Velocity Profile

Equation (7.69) with $P_l = -\partial P/\partial z$, the wall at $y = 0$ moving with $V$ and the plate at $y = d$ at rest (Eq. 7.12):

$$ v_z = V\left(1 - \frac{y}{d}\right) + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}\,d^2\,\frac{y}{d}\left(1 - \frac{y}{d}\right) $$

Flux and Pressure

$$ Q = \int_0^dv_z\,dy = \frac{Vd}{2} + \frac{P_ld^3}{12\mu}, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial z} = \frac{12\mu}{d^3}\left(\frac{Vd}{2} - Q\right) $$

The reduced pressure at the lower end of the plate ($z = -H$, $p = p_a + \rho gH$) is $p_a + \rho gH - \rho gH = p_a$, and at the top of the column ($z = h$, $p = p_a$) it is $p_a + \rho gh$. Since $\partial P/\partial z$ is uniform in the gap, integrating:

$$ P - p_a = \frac{12\mu}{d^3}\left(\frac{Vd}{2} - Q\right)(z + H), \qquad P_l = -\frac{p_a + \rho gh - p_a}{h + H} = -\frac{\rho gh}{h + H} $$

Equating the two expressions of $P_l$ ($P_l$ from the integral and from the flux):

$$ Q = \frac{Vd}{2} - \frac{\rho gh}{h + H}\,\frac{d^3}{12\mu} $$

(the key writes the three results without the intermediate step: with $z = h$, $P - p_a = \rho gh$ gives $\frac{12\mu}{d^3}\left(\frac{Vd}{2} - Q\right) = \frac{\rho gh}{h + H}$, and $P_l = -\partial P/\partial z$).

Evolution of the Column

The column is a liquid of cross-section $d$ (per unit width), so its top rises with the mean velocity $Q/d$: $d\,\dot{h} = Q$, that is

$$ \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{V}{2} - \frac{\rho gd^2}{12\mu}\,\frac{h}{h + H} = \frac{1}{2}\left[V - V_c\,\frac{h}{h + H}\right], \qquad V_c = \frac{\rho gd^2}{6\mu}, \quad h(0) = 0 $$

Writing $A = V - V_c$, $\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{Ah + VH}{h + H}$ and $dt = \frac{2(h + H)}{Ah + VH}\,dh$. Since $h + H = \frac{1}{A}(Ah + VH) - \frac{HV_c}{A}$ (as $H - \frac{VH}{A} = -\frac{HV_c}{A}$), the integral from $0$ to $h$ (Barrow's rule) is

$$ \frac{t}{2} = \frac{h}{A} - \frac{HV_c}{A^2}\ln\frac{Ah + VH}{VH} \;\Longrightarrow\; h - \frac{V_cH}{V - V_c}\ln\frac{(V - V_c)h + VH}{VH} = (V - V_c)\frac{t}{2} $$

(for $V\neq V_c$; for $V = V_c$ the equation is $dh/dt = \frac{V}{2}\frac{H}{h + H}$ and $h + \frac{h^2}{2H} = \frac{Vt}{2}$).

Large Times

- $V>V_c$. $A>0$ and the logarithm grows slower than $h$: $h\to\infty$ as $t\to\infty$ and $dh/dt\to\frac{1}{2}\left(V - V_c\right)$, since $h/(h + H)\to 1$: $$ \dot{h}_\infty = \frac{V - V_c}{2} $$ - $V<V_c$. $A<0$: $dh/dt$ goes to zero when $Ah + VH = 0$, so $Q\to 0$ and the column settles at $$ h_\infty = \frac{HV}{V_c - V} $$ (the argument of the logarithm tends to zero, the column approaches $h_\infty$ asymptotically). - Critical velocity: $V_c = \dfrac{\rho gd^2}{6\mu}$: the wall velocity for which the Couette flux $Vd/2$ equals the maximum backflow that the weight of an infinitely long column can drive, $\rho gd^3/(12\mu)$.

Power in the Steady State, $V<V_c$

At the end $Q = 0$, so $P_l = -6\mu V/d^2$ (from $Q = 0$: $\frac{Vd}{2} + \frac{P_ld^3}{12\mu} = 0$), and the profile is $v_z = V\left(1 - \frac{y}{d}\right) - \frac{3V}{d^2}\,y\,(d - y)$. The shear on the moving wall ($\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_y$ from the wall into the liquid):

$$ \tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial y}\Big|_{y=0} = -\frac{\mu V}{d} + \frac{P_ld}{2} = -\frac{\mu V}{d} - \frac{3\mu V}{d} = -\frac{4\mu V}{d} $$

(the key writes the same, $\left(-\frac{V}{d} - \frac{\rho gh}{h + H}\frac{d}{2\mu}\right)\mu = -\frac{4\mu V}{d}$ at $h = h_\infty$, where $\frac{\rho gh_\infty}{h_\infty + H} = \frac{6\mu V}{d^2}$). This is in $-z$, resisting the motion. The wall is in contact with the liquid of the gap over the whole filled length from $z = -H$ to $z = h_\infty$, that is $h_\infty + H = \frac{HV_c}{V_c - V}$. The power that must be applied is the force times the velocity:

$$ \dot{W} = \frac{4\mu V}{d}\,\left(h_\infty + H\right)V = \frac{4\mu V^2H}{d}\,\frac{V_c}{V_c - V} $$

(per unit width). Energy check: with $Q = 0$ the liquid does not gain potential energy nor receive any net pressure work, so the whole power is dissipated: $\mu\int_0^d(\partial v_z/\partial y)^2dy = \frac{\mu V^2}{d}\int_0^1(4 - 6s)^2ds = \frac{4\mu V^2}{d}$ per unit length, times the length $h_\infty + H$, the same.

Discrepancy with the official solution

The key boxes $\dot{W} = \frac{4\mu V^3}{d}\frac{H}{V_c - V}$, which is $\frac{4\mu V}{d}\,V\,h_\infty$: the wetted length it uses is $h_\infty$, the height of the column above the free surface. The gap, and so the wall in contact with the liquid of the film, extends from the lower end of the plate, $z = -H$, to the top of the column, $z = h_\infty$, a length $h_\infty + H$, and the profile (with the same shear) is the same in the part below the free surface. The correct length is $h_\infty + H = HV_c/(V_c - V)$, and $\dot{W} = 4\mu V^2H\,V_c/\left[d\,(V_c - V)\right]$, larger than the key's by the factor $V_c/V$. The dissipation check above confirms it. (If the part of the wall below the free surface is not counted, because the liquid there is in the open vessel, the key's value would follow; the statement does not say so, and the gap is filled from the lower end of the plate.)

Answers and Checks

  • Velocity: $v_z = V\left(1 - \frac{y}{d}\right) + \frac{P_l}{2\mu}d^2\frac{y}{d}\left(1 - \frac{y}{d}\right)$; $Q = \frac{Vd}{2} - \frac{\rho gh}{h + H}\frac{d^3}{12\mu}$; $P_l = -\frac{\rho gh}{h + H}$.
  • Equation: $\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{V}{2} - \frac{\rho gd^2}{12\mu}\frac{h}{h + H}$, $h(0) = 0$; solution $h - \frac{V_cH}{V - V_c}\ln\frac{(V - V_c)h + VH}{VH} = (V - V_c)\frac{t}{2}$.
  • $V_c = \rho gd^2/(6\mu)$; for $V>V_c$, $\dot{h}_\infty = (V - V_c)/2$; for $V<V_c$, $h_\infty = HV/(V_c - V)$.
  • Power: $\dot{W} = \frac{4\mu V^2}{d}\left(h_\infty + H\right) = \frac{4\mu V^2HV_c}{d(V_c - V)}$.
  • Dimensions: $\rho gd^2/\mu$ is $ML^{-3}\cdot LT^{-2}\cdot L^2/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}) = LT^{-1}$, a velocity; $\mu V^2H/d$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot L^2T^{-2} = MLT^{-3}$ per unit width, a power per unit width.
  • Limits: for $H\to 0$, $h_\infty\to 0$ and $\dot{W}\to 0$; for $V\to V_c^-$, $h_\infty\to\infty$ and $\dot{W}\to\infty$.
  • Check against the official key: VF18.pdf gives the same profile, $Q$, $P_l$, differential equation, implicit solution, $V_c$, $\dot{h}_\infty$, $h_\infty$ and the wall shear; its power differs by the length used, as noted.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The gap between the rod ($r = R_1$, moving with $+U\bar{e}_z$) and the pipe ($r = R_2$, at rest) has the length $L$ and is slender, $R_2 - R_1\ll L$; the flow is steady and axisymmetric, $v_z(r)$.
  • $z$ is the height above the reservoir surface. At the entrance of the pipe ($z = 0$) $p = p_a$; at its exit ($z = L$, the bottom of the container, under a depth $H$ of water at $p_a$ on top) $p = p_a + \rho gH$. The reduced pressure is $P = p + \rho gz$.

$R_1$, $R_2$radii of the rod and of the pipem

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The slender-flow condition (Eq. 7.41), with the width $R_2 - R_1$ of the gap, the velocity $U$ and the length $L$:

$$ \frac{\rho U\left(R_2 - R_1\right)}{\mu}\,\frac{R_2 - R_1}{L} = \frac{\rho U\left(R_2 - R_1\right)^2}{\mu L}\ll 1 $$

(the same with the velocity driven by the pressure, of order $P_l(R_2 - R_1)^2/\mu$, if it is larger than $U$).

Velocity Profile

Equation (7.7) in steady state with no slip on both walls:

$$ 0 = P_l + \frac{\mu}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dv_z}{dr}\right); \qquad r = R_1:\; v_z = U, \qquad r = R_2:\; v_z = 0 $$

As in VF3, the solution is $v_z = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left(R_2^2 - r^2\right) + C\ln\frac{R_2}{r}$, which is zero at $r = R_2$; the rod condition gives $U = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}\Delta + C\Lambda$:

$$ v_z = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left(R_2^2 - r^2\right) + \left[U - \frac{P_l}{4\mu}\left(R_2^2 - R_1^2\right)\right]\frac{\ln(R_2/r)}{\ln(R_2/R_1)} $$

Pressure Loss per Unit Length

The gap has constant section, so $P_l$ is uniform. The reduced pressure at the entrance is $p_a$ and at the exit $p_a + \rho gH + \rho gL$:

$$ P_l = -\frac{\partial P}{\partial z} = \frac{p_a - \left[p_a + \rho g(H + L)\right]}{L} = -\frac{\rho g(H + L)}{L} $$

negative: the pressure (reduced) increases along the flow, opposing it; the rod has to push the water against the weight of the whole column from the reservoir to the top of the container.

Force on the Rod and Power

The force of the liquid on the rod (normal from the rod into the liquid, $\bar{n} = +\bar{e}_r$) per unit area is $\tau_w = \mu\,\partial v_z/\partial r|_{r=R_1}$. With $\frac{dv_z}{dr} = -\frac{P_l}{2\mu}r - \frac{C}{r}$:

$$ \tau_w = -\frac{P_lR_1}{2} - \frac{\mu C}{R_1} = -\frac{\mu U}{R_1\Lambda} - \frac{P_lR_1}{2}\left[1 - \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{R_2^2/R_1^2 - 1}{\ln(R_2/R_1)}\right] $$

(using $C = \left(U - P_l\Delta/(4\mu)\right)/\Lambda$). With $P_l = -\rho g(H + L)/L$ both terms are negative: the liquid resists the motion of the rod, because $\frac{\Delta}{2R_1^2\Lambda} = \frac{x^2 - 1}{2\ln x}>1$ for $x = R_2/R_1>1$. The force on the rod over the length $L$ is $F_z = 2\pi R_1L\,\tau_w\,\bar{e}_z$, downward, and the power that must be supplied to move it is minus the power of that force (the key writes $W = U\,2\pi R_1L\,\tau_w$, the power of the force of the liquid, negative):

$$ W = -2\pi R_1LU\tau_w = 2\pi R_1LU\left[\frac{\mu U}{R_1\ln(R_2/R_1)} + \frac{\rho g(H + L)R_1}{2L}\left(\frac{R_2^2 - R_1^2}{2R_1^2\ln(R_2/R_1)} - 1\right)\right] $$

The key's lines end at the expression of $\tau_w$ without writing the final value.

Volume Flux and Minimum Velocity

$Q = \int_{R_1}^{R_2}2\pi r\,v_z\,dr$. Barrow's rule for the two terms:

$$ \int_{R_1}^{R_2}r\left(R_2^2 - r^2\right)dr = \left[\frac{R_2^2r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4}\right]_{R_1}^{R_2} = \frac{\left(R_2^2 - R_1^2\right)^2}{4}, \qquad \int_{R_1}^{R_2}r\ln\frac{R_2}{r}\,dr = \left[\frac{r^2}{2}\ln\frac{R_2}{r} + \frac{r^2}{4}\right]_{R_1}^{R_2} = \frac{R_2^2 - R_1^2}{4} - \frac{R_1^2}{2}\ln\frac{R_2}{R_1} $$
$$ Q = \frac{\pi P_l\Delta^2}{8\mu} + \frac{\pi}{2}\left(U - \frac{P_l\Delta}{4\mu}\right)\frac{\Delta - 2R_1^2\Lambda}{\Lambda} $$
Discrepancy with the official solution

The key gives $Q = -2\pi\left\{\frac{P_l}{16\mu}\left(R_2^4 - R_1^4\right) + \frac{U - P_l\Delta/(4\mu)}{4\ln(R_2/R_1)}\left[R_2^2\left(2\ln R_2 - 1\right) - R_1^2\left(2\ln R_1 - 1\right)\right]\right\}$, which is not the integral of its own velocity profile. The first term should have $\left(R_2^2 - R_1^2\right)^2$ (the integral of $r(R_2^2 - r^2)$, as above), not $R_2^4 - R_1^4$; and the second keeps only the antiderivative of $r\ln r$ and drops the constant term that comes from $\ln R_2$ (a logarithm of a dimensional length appears, which is dimensionally meaningless); the overall sign is also the opposite. As a consequence the key's minimum velocity, solved from its own $Q = 0$, is not the one of the integral. The results above are used.

Checks of $Q$: for $R_1\to 0$, $\Delta\to R_2^2$, $R_1^2\Lambda\to 0$, $\Lambda\to\infty$ and the second term vanishes: $Q\to\frac{\pi P_lR_2^4}{8\mu}$, the Poiseuille flux (7.15). For a thin gap $g = R_2 - R_1\ll R_1$, $(\Delta - 2R_1^2\Lambda)/\Lambda\simeq 2R_1g\cdot\frac{g}{R_1}\cdot R_1$ (to first order $2R_1^2\epsilon$ with $\epsilon = g/R_1$), so the second term is $\pi R_1^2\epsilon U = \pi R_1gU$, the Couette flux $\frac{Ug}{2}\cdot2\pi R_1$.

Minimum velocity. Setting $Q = 0$: $\left(U - \frac{P_l\Delta}{4\mu}\right) = -\frac{P_l\Delta^2}{4\mu}\frac{\Lambda}{\Delta - 2R_1^2\Lambda}$, so

$$ U_{min} = \frac{P_l\Delta}{4\mu}\left[1 - \frac{\Delta\,\Lambda}{\Delta - 2R_1^2\Lambda}\right] = \frac{\rho g(H + L)\left(R_2^2 - R_1^2\right)}{4\mu L}\left[\frac{\left(R_2^2 - R_1^2\right)\ln(R_2/R_1)}{R_2^2 - R_1^2 - 2R_1^2\ln(R_2/R_1)} - 1\right] $$

positive, since $\frac{\Delta\Lambda}{\Delta - 2R_1^2\Lambda}>1$. For $U>U_{min}$ the rod pumps water to the container ($Q>0$).

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $\rho U(R_2 - R_1)^2/(\mu L)\ll 1$.
  • Velocity: $v_z = \frac{P_l}{4\mu}(R_2^2 - r^2) + \left[U - \frac{P_l}{4\mu}(R_2^2 - R_1^2)\right]\frac{\ln(R_2/r)}{\ln(R_2/R_1)}$; $P_l = -\rho g(H + L)/L$.
  • Force on the rod: $F_z = 2\pi R_1L\tau_w$ with $\tau_w$ above; power supplied: $W = -2\pi R_1LU\tau_w>0$.

Hypotheses, Data and Unknowns

  • The gap between the base of the cylinder ($z = h$) and the bottom of the tank ($z = 0$) is slender, axisymmetric and steady; the liquid enters it through the jet at the centre ($r\lesssim h$) and leaves at $r = R$ to the tank, where the reduced pressure is $P_0$ (the liquid is at rest there).
  • Reduced pressure $P = p + \rho gz$. The cylinder is in equilibrium: its weight $W = 2\pi R^3\rho_sg$ (volume $2\pi R^3$) balances the force of the liquid.

Criterion for Viscous Dominance

The convective acceleration is negligible against the viscous force (Eq. 7.41) when $\frac{\rho V_zch}{\mu}\frac{h}{R}\ll 1$, with $V_{zc}$ the velocity scale of the jet, of radius $\sim h$, so $V_{zc}\sim Q/h^2$. Continuity in the gap, $V_{rc}/R\sim V_{zc}/h$, gives $V_{rc}\sim V_{zc}h/R\ll V_{zc}$, and the radial velocity in the gap is $v_r\sim Q/(2\pi rh)$, equal to $\sim Q/h^2$ at $r\sim h$. Then

$$ \frac{\rho V_{zc}h}{\mu}\,\frac{h}{R}\sim\frac{\rho Q}{\mu h}\,\frac{h}{R} = \frac{\rho Q}{\mu R}\ll 1 $$

(the key states $V_r\sim Q/h^2$ for $r\sim h$ and boxes the result; at larger $r$ the velocity is smaller, so the condition is the most demanding at the entrance of the gap).

Velocity Profile and Flux

Radial momentum in the gap (Eq. 7.69 in cylindrical coordinates), with no slip at the bottom of the tank ($z = 0$) and at the base of the cylinder ($z = h$):

$$ 0 = -\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} + \mu\frac{\partial^2v_r}{\partial z^2}; \qquad z = 0:\; v_r = 0, \qquad z = h:\; v_r = 0 $$

Integrating twice, $v_r = \frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}z^2 + Az$ ($B = 0$ by the condition at $z = 0$), and $0 = \frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}h^2 + Ah$ gives $A$:

$$ v_r = -\frac{1}{2\mu}\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\left(hz - z^2\right) $$

The local flux through the cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$ is

$$ q(r) = \int_0^h2\pi r\,v_r\,dz = -\frac{2\pi r}{2\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\left[\frac{hz^2}{2} - \frac{z^3}{3}\right]_0^h = -\frac{\pi h^3r}{6\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} $$

Pressure

Continuity in the gap: no liquid enters or leaves through the bottom or the base (both are walls), so $q(r) = Q$ for every $r$:

$$ Q = -\frac{\pi h^3r}{6\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial P}{\partial r} = -\frac{6\mu Q}{\pi h^3r} $$

Integrating in $r$ with the boundary condition $P = P_0$ at $r = R$:

$$ P = P_0 - \frac{6\mu Q}{\pi h^3}\,\ln\frac{r}{R} $$

an overpressure for $r<R$, which grows (logarithmically) towards the centre, where the slender approximation stops being valid (at $r\sim h$).

Height of the Cylinder

The force of the liquid on the cylinder is the difference of the pressures on its base and on its top, over the area $\pi R^2$ (the lateral pressure is horizontal). At the top, $z = h + 2R$, the liquid is at rest with the reduced pressure $P_0$, so $p_t = P_0 - \rho g(h + 2R)$; at the base, $p_b = P - \rho gh$:

$$ F = \int_0^R2\pi r\left(p_b - p_t\right)dr = \int_0^R2\pi r\left[2\rho gR + (P - P_0)\right]dr = 2\pi\rho gR^3 - \frac{12\mu Q}{h^3}\int_0^Rr\ln\frac{r}{R}\,dr $$

With $r = Rs$, $\int_0^Rr\ln\frac{r}{R}dr = R^2\int_0^1s\ln s\,ds = R^2\left[\frac{s^2}{2}\ln s - \frac{s^2}{4}\right]_0^1 = -\frac{R^2}{4}$:

$$ F = 2\pi\rho gR^3 + \frac{3\mu QR^2}{h^3} $$

The first term is the buoyancy (the weight of the displaced liquid, $\rho g\cdot2\pi R^3$) and the second is the lift of the jet. The cylinder floats at the height $h$ when $F = W = 2\pi R^3\rho_sg$:

$$ \frac{3\mu QR^2}{h^3} = 2\pi R^3g\left(\rho_s - \rho\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; h = \left[\frac{3\mu QR^2}{2\pi R^3g\left(\rho_s - \rho\right)}\right]^{1/3} = \left[\frac{3\mu Q}{2\pi Rg\left(\rho_s - \rho\right)}\right]^{1/3} $$

Answers and Checks

  • Viscous dominance: $\rho Q/(\mu R)\ll 1$ (and $h\ll R$).
  • Profile: $v_r = -\frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}(hz - z^2)$; flux: $q = -\frac{\pi h^3r}{6\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} = Q$; pressure: $P = P_0 - \frac{6\mu Q}{\pi h^3}\ln\frac{r}{R}$.
  • Height: $h = \left[\frac{3\mu Q}{2\pi Rg(\rho_s - \rho)}\right]^{1/3}$.
  • Dimensions: $\mu Q/(Rg\Delta\rho)$ is $ML^{-1}T^{-1}\cdot L^3T^{-1}/(L\cdot LT^{-2}\cdot ML^{-3}) = L^3$, so $h$ is a length; $\rho Q/(\mu R)$ is $ML^{-3}L^3T^{-1}/(ML^{-1}T^{-1}L) = 1$.
  • Limits: $Q\to 0$ gives $h\to 0$ (the cylinder rests on the bottom) and $h\propto Q^{1/3}$; $\rho_s\to\rho$ makes the cylinder neutrally buoyant and $h\to\infty$ (it floats with any $Q$).
  • Check against the official key: VF20.pdf gives the same criterion (stated without the step $V_{zc}\sim Q/h^2$), profile, $q$, $P$, force ($2\pi\rho gR^3 + 3\mu QR^2/h^3$) and $h$ (left as $\left[3\mu QR^2/(2\pi R^3g(\rho_s - \rho))\right]^{1/3}$).
