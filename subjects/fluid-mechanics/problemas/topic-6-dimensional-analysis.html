Problem DA1

Power of a Marine Freighter

Original statement · DA1.pdf
Π theoremFroude similarityRe ≫ 1

Official Problem Statement

We want to estimate the driving power $W$ necessary to move at a peak velocity $V = 10$ m/s a marine freighter of length $l = 100$ m and total mass at full load $m = 1000$ Tm. For that purpose, a small model of length $l_e = 1$ m, geometrically similar to the real ship, is to be tested on a hydraulic channel. To guide the design of the experiment, follow these steps:

1. Use the dimensional analysis to reduce the parametric dependence of the required power $W$.

2. Write the three equations that need to be satisfied to guarantee physical similarity between the experiment and the full-scale prototype. Verify that it is not possible to find the values of the mass $m_e$ and velocity $V_e$ of the experiment that satisfy simultaneously all three equations, so that complete physical similarity cannot be attained.

3. Assuming that the effect of viscosity on the fluid motion is negligible, determine the values of $m_e$ and $V_e$. Check that the corresponding values of the Reynolds number for both the real prototype and the experiment are indeed sufficiently large for the assumption of negligible viscosity to be a reasonable approximation.

4. If the experimental measurement of the power obtained with the experimental model is $W_e = 10$ W, obtain the value of the driving power $W$ needed to operate the real prototype at the required conditions.

Source: DA1.pdf (page 1, statement and handwritten key). Notes.pdf, Chapter 6: Π theorem, Eq. 6.25; physical similarity, Eqs. 6.57-6.59.

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Dimensional Table

  • The ship moves at constant velocity on the free surface of water, which is the same liquid in the channel and in the sea: $\rho_e = \rho$, $\mu_e = \mu$, so $\nu_e = \nu = \mu/\rho \simeq 10^{-6}$ m$^2$/s.
  • The power goes into the waves made at the free surface (gravity $g$ matters, since the free surface is not flat; Notes.pdf, p. 66) and into the viscous drag ($\mu$ matters, a priori).
  • The mass $m$ fixes how deep the hull floats: by Archimedes, the submerged volume is $m/\rho$.
  • "Tm" is the metric ton: $m = 1000\ \text{t} = 10^6$ kg.

Application of the Π Theorem

$$ W = W(V, g, m, l, \rho, \mu) $$

There are $n = 6$ governing parameters. Exponent matrix, rows $V$, $l$, $\rho$; columns $L$, $M$, $T$:

$$ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -3 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det = 1\,(0\cdot 0 - 0\cdot 1) - 0 + (-1)\,(1\cdot 1 - 0\cdot(-3)) = -1 \neq 0 $$

so $(V, l, \rho)$ have independent dimensions and $k = 3$: there are $n - k = 3$ dimensionless parameters. $(V, l, \rho)$ is chosen because velocity, size and the inertia of the water clearly matter (Notes.pdf, p. 68).

Power. $[W] = [\rho]^a[V]^b[l]^c$:

$$ M:\; 1 = a, \qquad T:\; -3 = -b, \qquad L:\; 2 = -3a + b + c $$

so $a = 1$, $b = 3$ and $c = 2 + 3 - 3 = 2$: $\Pi_0 = W/(\rho V^3 l^2)$.

Gravity. $[g] = [V]^b[l]^c$ (no mass, so no $\rho$): $T:\; -2 = -b$, $L:\; 1 = b + c$, so $b = 2$, $c = -1$ and $g/(V^2/l)$ is dimensionless; its inverse is the square of the Froude number, $V^2/(gl)$.

Mass. $[m] = [\rho]^a[V]^b[l]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$, $T:\; 0 = -b$, $L:\; 0 = -3a + b + c$, so $a = 1$, $b = 0$, $c = 3$: $m/(\rho l^3)$.

Viscosity. $[\mu] = [\rho]^a[V]^b[l]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$, $T:\; -1 = -b$, $L:\; -1 = -3a + b + c$, so $a = 1$, $b = 1$, $c = 1$: $\mu/(\rho V l)$, the inverse of the Reynolds number.

$$ \frac{W}{\rho V^3 l^2} = f\left(\frac{V^2}{g l}, \frac{m}{\rho l^3}, \frac{\rho V l}{\mu}\right) $$
  • $V^2/(gl)$ (Froude number squared): inertia against gravity, that is the waves at the free surface.
  • $m/(\rho l^3)$: by Archimedes $m/\rho$ is the submerged volume, so this group is proportional to the ratio of the submerged volume to the total volume of the ship.
  • $\rho V l/\mu$: the Reynolds number.

Physical Similarity

Model (subscript $e$) and prototype are physically similar when the three governing groups are equal. With the same liquid and the same $g$:

$$ \frac{V^2}{g l} = \frac{V_e^2}{g l_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; V_e = \left(\frac{l_e}{l}\right)^{1/2} V $$
$$ \frac{V l}{\nu} = \frac{V_e l_e}{\nu} \;\Longrightarrow\; V_e = \frac{l}{l_e}\,V $$
$$ \frac{m}{\rho l^3} = \frac{m_e}{\rho l_e^3} \;\Longrightarrow\; m_e = \left(\frac{l_e}{l}\right)^3 m $$

The third equation fixes $m_e$. The first two ask for two different velocities: with $l_e/l = 10^{-2}$, Froude asks for $V_e = 10^{-1}V$ and Reynolds for $V_e = 10^{2}V$. Both hold only if $(l_e/l)^{1/2} = l/l_e$, that is $(l_e/l)^{3/2} = 1$, $l_e = l$: with the same liquid, no scaled model can be fully similar (Notes.pdf, p. 71: partial similarity).

Negligible Viscosity

If viscosity has a negligible effect ($\mathrm{Re}\gg 1$), the group $\rho V l/\mu$ drops out of the law (Notes.pdf, Eq. 6.55):

$$ \frac{W}{\rho V^3 l^2} = f\left(\frac{V^2}{g l}, \frac{m}{\rho l^3}\right) $$

and only the Froude and mass conditions remain:

$$ V_e = \left(\frac{l_e}{l}\right)^{1/2} V = \left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^{1/2}\times 10 = 1\ \text{m/s}, \qquad m_e = \left(\frac{l_e}{l}\right)^3 m = 10^{-6}\times 10^{6} = 1\ \text{kg} $$

Check of the assumption, with $\nu = 10^{-6}$ m$^2$/s:

$$ \mathrm{Re} = \frac{V l}{\nu} = \frac{10\times 100}{10^{-6}} = 10^{9} \gg 1, \qquad \mathrm{Re}_e = \frac{V_e l_e}{\nu} = \frac{1\times 1}{10^{-6}} = 10^{6} \gg 1 $$

Both are very large, so neglecting viscosity is reasonable in the model and in the ship, even though the two Reynolds numbers differ by a factor $10^3$.

Power of the Prototype

With the two remaining groups equal, $\Pi_0$ is equal too (Notes.pdf, Eq. 6.58):

$$ \frac{W}{\rho V^3 l^2} = \frac{W_e}{\rho V_e^3 l_e^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; W = \left(\frac{V}{V_e}\right)^3\left(\frac{l}{l_e}\right)^2 W_e $$
$$ W = (10)^3\,(100)^2 \times 10\ \text{W} = 10^{3}\times 10^{4}\times 10 = 10^{8}\ \text{W} = 100\ \text{MW} $$

Answers and Checks

  • Reduced law: $W/(\rho V^3 l^2) = f\left(V^2/(gl),\, m/(\rho l^3),\, \rho V l/\mu\right)$.
  • Full similarity needs $V_e = (l_e/l)^{1/2}V$ and $V_e = (l/l_e)V$ at once: impossible for $l_e \neq l$.
  • With negligible viscosity: $V_e = 1$ m/s, $m_e = 1$ kg; $\mathrm{Re} = 10^9$, $\mathrm{Re}_e = 10^6$.
  • Prototype power: $W = 100$ MW.
  • Dimensions: $\rho V^3 l^2$ has $ML^{-3}\cdot L^3T^{-3}\cdot L^2 = ML^2T^{-3}$, a power.
  • Scaling check: under Froude similarity $V \propto l^{1/2}$, so $W \propto V^3 l^2 \propto l^{7/2}$, and $100^{7/2} = 10^7$ times 10 W is $10^8$ W, the same.
  • Check against the official key: DA1.pdf gives $V_e = 1$ m/s, $m_e = 1$ kg, $\mathrm{Re}\simeq 10^9$, $\mathrm{Re}_e = 10^6$ and $W = 100$ MW, the same results.

Application of the Π Theorem

Critical velocity

$$ U_c = U_c(\rho, \mu, R) $$

Rows $\rho$, $\mu$, $R$; columns $L$, $M$, $T$:

$$ \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det = 1\cdot\left(1\cdot(-1) - 0\cdot 1\right) = -1 \neq 0 $$

(expanding along the third row). So $k = n = 3$ and no dimensionless parameter is left: $U_c$ divided by the combination with its dimensions is a constant (Notes.pdf, Eqs. 6.6-6.7). With $[U_c] = [\rho]^a[\mu]^b[R]^c$:

$$ M:\; 0 = a + b, \qquad T:\; -1 = -b, \qquad L:\; 1 = -3a - b + c $$

so $b = 1$, $a = -1$ and $c = 1 + 3(-1) + 1 = -1$:

$$ \frac{U_c}{\mu/(\rho R)} = \frac{U_c R}{\nu} = C_1 $$

Shedding starts at a fixed critical Reynolds number.

Period and force

$$ T = T(\rho, U, \mu, R), \qquad \bar{F} = \bar{F}(\rho, U, \mu, R) $$

$n = 4$; $(\rho, U, R)$ have independent dimensions ($\det$ of rows $U(1,0,-1)$, $R(1,0,0)$, $\rho(-3,1,0)$ is $-1$, as in DA1), so $k = 3$ and one group is left, which is $\mu/(\rho U R) = 1/\mathrm{Re}$ (exponents as for the sphere, Notes.pdf, Eq. 6.33).

Period. $[T] = [\rho]^a[U]^b[R]^c$: $M:\; 0 = a$; $T:\; 1 = -b$; $L:\; 0 = -3a + b + c$; so $a = 0$, $b = -1$, $c = 1$, the time scale $R/U$.

Force. $[\bar{F}] = MT^{-2} = [\rho]^a[U]^b[R]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -2 = -b$; $L:\; 0 = -3a + b + c$; so $a = 1$, $b = 2$, $c = 3 - 2 = 1$, the scale $\rho U^2 R$.

$$ \frac{T}{R/U} = f_T\left(\frac{\rho U R}{\mu}\right), \qquad \frac{\bar{F}}{\rho U^2 R} = f_F\left(\frac{\rho U R}{\mu}\right) $$

Large velocities

$U \gg \nu/R$ means $\mathrm{Re} = UR/\nu \gg 1$. With $(\rho, U, R)$ as the independent set (the natural choice when viscosity is not dominant, Notes.pdf, p. 68), the dependence on $\mathrm{Re}$ disappears (Notes.pdf, Eq. 6.55):

$$ \frac{T}{R/U} = C_T, \qquad \frac{\bar{F}}{\rho U^2 R} = C_F $$

Physical Similarity: from the 1 mm Wire to the 2 mm Wire

Both wires are in air (same $\rho$, $\mu$, $\nu$). Subscript 1 is the tested wire ($R_1 = 1$ mm), subscript 2 the new one ($R_2 = 2$ mm).

Critical velocity. $U_c R/\nu$ is the same constant for both:

$$ \frac{U_{c1}}{\nu/R_1} = \frac{U_{c2}}{\nu/R_2} \;\Longrightarrow\; U_{c2} = \frac{R_1}{R_2}\,U_{c1} = \frac{1}{2}\times 0.2 = 0.1\ \text{m/s} $$

Each measured point. A point of the table describes the 2 mm wire when the Reynolds numbers are equal:

$$ \frac{\rho U_1 R_1}{\mu} = \frac{\rho U_2 R_2}{\mu} \;\Longrightarrow\; U_2 = \frac{R_1}{R_2}\,U_1 = \frac{U_1}{2} $$

Then $T/(R/U)$ and $\bar{F}/(\rho U^2 R)$ are equal:

$$ \frac{T_1}{R_1/U_1} = \frac{T_2}{R_2/U_2} \;\Longrightarrow\; T_2 = \frac{R_2}{R_1}\,\frac{U_1}{U_2}\,T_1 = 2\times 2\times T_1 = 4\,T_1 $$
$$ \frac{\bar{F}_1}{\rho U_1^2 R_1} = \frac{\bar{F}_2}{\rho U_2^2 R_2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \bar{F}_2 = \left(\frac{U_2}{U_1}\right)^2\frac{R_2}{R_1}\,\bar{F}_1 = \frac{1}{4}\times 2\times\bar{F}_1 = \frac{\bar{F}_1}{2} $$

  • Critical velocity: $U_c R/\nu = C_1$; for $R = 2$ mm, $U_c = 0.1$ m/s.

Application of the Π Theorem

$$ W = W(\Omega, g, h, D, V, \rho, \mu) $$

$n = 7$. The key takes $(\mu, \Omega, h)$ as the independent set, natural for a very viscous liquid (Notes.pdf, p. 68). Rows $\Omega$, $h$, $\mu$; columns $L$, $M$, $T$:

$$ \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det = (-1)\cdot\left(1\cdot 1 - 0\cdot(-1)\right) = -1 \neq 0 $$

(expanding along the first row), so $k = 3$ and $n - k = 4$ groups remain.

Power. $[W] = [\mu]^a[\Omega]^b[h]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -3 = -a - b$; $L:\; 2 = -a + c$; so $a = 1$, $b = 2$, $c = 3$: $\Pi_0 = W/(\mu\Omega^2 h^3)$.

Gravity. $[g] = [\Omega]^b[h]^c$: $T:\; -2 = -b$; $L:\; 1 = c$: the group $g/(\Omega^2 h)$, an inverse Froude number squared.

Geometry. $D/h$ and $V/h^3$.

Density. $[\rho] = [\mu]^a[\Omega]^b[h]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; 0 = -a - b$, so $b = -1$; $L:\; -3 = -a + c$, so $c = -2$: $\rho/(\mu\Omega^{-1}h^{-2}) = \rho\Omega h^2/\mu$, a Reynolds number.

$$ \frac{W}{\mu\Omega^2 h^3} = f\left(\frac{g}{\Omega^2 h}, \frac{D}{h}, \frac{V}{h^3}, \frac{\rho\Omega h^2}{\mu}\right) $$

Physical Similarity with Water

All four groups must be equal in model ($e$, water) and prototype ($t$, tar), with the same $g$:

$$ \frac{g}{\Omega^2 h} = \frac{g}{\Omega_e^2 h_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{h_e}{h} = \left(\frac{\Omega}{\Omega_e}\right)^2 $$
$$ \frac{\Omega h^2}{\nu_t} = \frac{\Omega_e h_e^2}{\nu_w} \;\Longrightarrow\; \left(\frac{h_e}{h}\right)^2 = \frac{\Omega}{\Omega_e}\,\frac{\nu_w}{\nu_t} $$
$$ \frac{D}{h} = \frac{D_e}{h_e}, \qquad \frac{V}{h^3} = \frac{V_e}{h_e^3} $$

The first two fix the scale and the speed. From the first, $\Omega/\Omega_e = (h_e/h)^{1/2}$; into the second:

$$ \left(\frac{h_e}{h}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{h_e}{h}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\nu_w}{\nu_t} \;\Longrightarrow\; \left(\frac{h_e}{h}\right)^{3/2} = \frac{\nu_w}{\nu_t} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{h_e}{h} = \left(\frac{\nu_t}{\nu_w}\right)^{-2/3} = \left(10^{3}\right)^{-2/3} = 10^{-2} $$
$$ h_e = 10^{-2}\times 1 = 1\ \text{cm}, \qquad \frac{\Omega_e}{\Omega} = \left(\frac{h}{h_e}\right)^{1/2} = \left(\frac{\nu_t}{\nu_w}\right)^{1/3} = 10 \;\Longrightarrow\; \Omega_e = 10\ \text{s}^{-1} $$
$$ D_e = \frac{h_e}{h}\,D = 10^{-2}\times 10 = 10\ \text{cm}, \qquad V_e = \left(\frac{h_e}{h}\right)^3 V = 10^{-6}\times 1000 = 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3 = 1\ \text{litre} $$

This is the way out of the Reynolds-Froude incompatibility of DA1: the model fluid must satisfy $\nu_w/\nu_t = (h_e/h)^{3/2}$, and water does it for a 1:100 model of a tank of tar.

Check of both groups: prototype $\Omega h^2/\nu_t = 1\times 1/10^{-3} = 10^3$, model $\Omega_e h_e^2/\nu_w = 10\times 10^{-4}/10^{-6} = 10^3$; prototype $g/(\Omega^2 h) = 9.81$, model $9.81/(100\times 0.01) = 9.81$.

Power Ratio

With all groups equal, $\Pi_0$ is equal:

$$ \frac{W}{\mu_t\Omega^2 h^3} = \frac{W_e}{\mu_w\Omega_e^2 h_e^3} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{W}{W_e} = \frac{\mu_t}{\mu_w}\left(\frac{\Omega}{\Omega_e}\right)^2\left(\frac{h}{h_e}\right)^3 = \frac{\rho_t}{\rho_w}\,\frac{\nu_t}{\nu_w}\left(\frac{\Omega}{\Omega_e}\right)^2\left(\frac{h}{h_e}\right)^3 $$
$$ \frac{W}{W_e} = 2\times 10^{3}\times\left(10^{-1}\right)^2\times\left(10^{2}\right)^3 = 2\times 10^{3}\times 10^{-2}\times 10^{6} = 2\times 10^{7} $$

Answers and Checks

Application of the Π Theorem

$$ u_t = u_t(D, m, g, \rho, \mu) $$

$n = 5$. The key takes $(D, g, \rho)$: rows $D(1, 0, 0)$, $g(1, 0, -2)$, $\rho(-3, 1, 0)$ in $(L, M, T)$, with $\det = 1\cdot(0\cdot 0 - (-2)\cdot 1) - 0 + 0 = 2 \neq 0$, so $k = 3$ and two groups remain.

Velocity. $[u_t] = [D]^a[g]^b$: $T:\; -1 = -2b$, so $b = 1/2$; $L:\; 1 = a + b$, so $a = 1/2$: the scale $\sqrt{gD}$.

Viscosity. $[\mu] = [\rho]^a[g]^b[D]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -1 = -2b$, so $b = 1/2$; $L:\; -1 = -3a + b + c = -3 + \tfrac{1}{2} + c$, so $c = 3/2$: the group $\mu/(\rho g^{1/2}D^{3/2}) = \nu/(g^{1/2}D^{3/2})$.

$$ \frac{u_t}{\sqrt{g D}} = f\left(\frac{m}{\rho D^3}, \frac{\mu}{\rho g^{1/2} D^{3/2}}\right) $$

Physical Similarity in Glycerine

Subscript $s$ for the slime particle in water ($\rho_w$, $\nu_w$), $e$ for the experiment in glycerine ($\rho_g$, $\nu_g$); same $g$. Both groups must be equal.

Viscosity group:

$$ \frac{\nu_w}{g^{1/2}D_s^{3/2}} = \frac{\nu_g}{g^{1/2}D_e^{3/2}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \left(\frac{D_e}{D_s}\right)^{3/2} = \frac{\nu_g}{\nu_w} \;\Longrightarrow\; D_e = \left(\frac{\nu_g}{\nu_w}\right)^{2/3}D_s $$

$680^{2/3} = \exp\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\ln 680\right) = \exp(4.348) = 77.33$, so

$$ D_e = 77.33\times 30\ \mu\text{m} = 2320\ \mu\text{m} = 2.32\ \text{mm} $$

Mass group:

$$ \frac{m_s}{\rho_w D_s^3} = \frac{m_e}{\rho_g D_e^3} \;\Longrightarrow\; m_e = \frac{\rho_g}{\rho_w}\left(\frac{D_e}{D_s}\right)^3 m_s = 1.25\times 77.33^3\times 0.2\ \mu\text{g} $$

$77.33^3 = 4.624\times 10^{5}$, so $m_e = 1.25\times 4.624\times 10^{5}\times 0.2\ \mu\text{g} = 1.156\times 10^{5}\ \mu\text{g} = 0.116$ g. The experimental particle is a sphere of 2.3 mm and about 0.12 g, easy to handle.

Terminal Velocity of the Slime

$$ \frac{u_t}{\sqrt{g D_s}} = \frac{u_e}{\sqrt{g D_e}} \;\Longrightarrow\; u_t = \left(\frac{D_s}{D_e}\right)^{1/2}u_e = \left(\frac{1}{77.33}\right)^{1/2}\times 10^{-3}\ \text{m/s} $$
$$ u_t = 0.1137\times 10^{-3}\ \text{m/s} = 0.1137\ \text{mm/s} $$

$$ \frac{F}{\rho g h^2} = f_2\left(\frac{\mu}{\rho g^{1/2}h^{3/2}}, \frac{H}{h}\right) $$

Negligible viscosity

The group $\mu/(\rho g^{1/2}h^{3/2}) = \nu/(g^{1/2}h^{3/2})$ is the inverse of a Reynolds number built with the velocity $\sqrt{gh}$ and the length $h$. If

$$ \frac{\mu}{\rho g^{1/2}h^{3/2}} \ll 1 $$

$$ \frac{U}{\sqrt{g h}} = f_1 = \text{constant}, \qquad \frac{F}{\rho g h^2} = f_2\left(\frac{H}{h}\right) $$

$$ \frac{H}{h} = \frac{H'}{h'} \;\Longrightarrow\; h' = \frac{H'}{H}\,h = \frac{0.3}{3}\times 2 = 0.2\ \text{m} $$

$$ \frac{F}{\rho g h^2} = \frac{F'}{\rho g h'^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; F = \left(\frac{h}{h'}\right)^2 F' = 100\times 1500 = 1.5\times 10^{5}\ \text{N/m} = 150\ \text{kN/m} $$

Areas. $A_e/A_s$.

$$ \frac{E}{\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}^{2/3}A_s^{1/3}} = f\left(\frac{A_e}{A_s}, \frac{\rho^{2/3}A_s^{1/6}\dot{W}^{1/3}}{\mu}\right) $$

Check that the last group is a Reynolds number: a velocity scale built from the power is $U_s = (\dot{W}/(\rho A_s))^{1/3}$ (kinetic-energy flux $\rho U^3 A \sim \dot{W}$), and $\rho U_s A_s^{1/2}/\mu = \rho^{2/3}\dot{W}^{1/3}A_s^{1/2 - 1/3}/\mu = \rho^{2/3}\dot{W}^{1/3}A_s^{1/6}/\mu$, the same group.

Negligible Viscosity

If

$$ \frac{\rho^{2/3}A_s^{1/6}\dot{W}^{1/3}}{\mu} \gg 1 $$

viscosity is negligible and the group drops out (Notes.pdf, Eq. 6.55):

$$ \frac{E}{\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}^{2/3}A_s^{1/3}} = f\left(\frac{A_e}{A_s}\right) $$

Justification with the test data (water, $\rho = 10^3$ kg/m$^3$, $\mu = 10^{-3}$ kg/(m s)): the model area is not given, but any reasonable outlet, e.g. $A_s \sim 10^{-2}$ m$^2$, with $\dot{W} = 100$ W gives $\rho^{2/3}A_s^{1/6}\dot{W}^{1/3}/\mu = 100\times 0.46\times 4.64/10^{-3} \simeq 2\times 10^{5} \gg 1$ (an estimate, since the size is assumed).

Physical Similarity and Prototype Power

Geometric similarity gives the same $A_e/A_s$, so the dimensionless thrust is the same; with the same water:

$$ \frac{E_p}{\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}^{2/3}A_{s}^{1/3}} = \frac{E_e}{\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}_e^{2/3}A_{se}^{1/3}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \dot{W}^{2/3} = \frac{E_p}{E_e}\left(\frac{A_{se}}{A_s}\right)^{1/3}\dot{W}_e^{2/3} $$

and raising to $3/2$:

$$ \dot{W} = \left(\frac{E_p}{E_e}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{A_{se}}{A_s}\right)^{1/2}\dot{W}_e $$

Scale 1/10 in length means $A_{se}/A_s = 1/100$:

$$ \dot{W} = \left(\frac{12500}{125}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^{1/2}\times 100\ \text{W} = 100^{3/2}\times\frac{1}{10}\times 100 = 1000\times\frac{1}{10}\times 100 = 10^{4}\ \text{W} $$

Answers and Checks

  • Reduced law: $E/(\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}^{2/3}A_s^{1/3}) = f\left(A_e/A_s,\, \rho^{2/3}A_s^{1/6}\dot{W}^{1/3}/\mu\right)$.
  • With $\rho^{2/3}A_s^{1/6}\dot{W}^{1/3}/\mu \gg 1$: $E/(\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}^{2/3}A_s^{1/3}) = f(A_e/A_s)$.
  • Prototype power: $\dot{W} = 10^4$ W = 10 kW.
  • Dimensions: $\rho^{1/3}\dot{W}^{2/3}A_s^{1/3}$ is $M^{1/3}L^{-1}\cdot M^{2/3}L^{4/3}T^{-2}\cdot L^{2/3} = MLT^{-2}$, a force.
  • Physical sense: at fixed geometry $E \propto \dot{W}^{2/3}$; a hundred times the thrust with a hundred times the area needs $(100)^{3/2}/(100)^{1/2} = 100$ times the power.
  • Check against the official key: DA7.pdf gives $\dot{W} = (E/E_e)^{3/2}(A_{se}/A_s)^{1/2}\dot{W}_e = 10^4$ W, the same result.

Application of the Π Theorem

$$ F = F(f, L, \rho, \mu) $$

$n = 4$; $(f, L, \rho)$ have independent dimensions (rows $f(0,0,-1)$, $L(1,0,0)$, $\rho(-3,1,0)$, $\det = -1\cdot(1\cdot 1 - 0\cdot(-3)) = -1 \neq 0$ expanding along the first row), so one group remains.

Force. $[F] = [\rho]^a[f]^b[L]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -2 = -b$; $L:\; 1 = -3 + c$; so $\rho f^2 L^4$.

Viscosity. $[\mu] = [\rho]^a[f]^b[L]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -1 = -b$; $L:\; -1 = -3 + c$; so $\rho f L^2$, and the group is $\rho L^2 f/\mu$, a Reynolds number with the velocity scale $fL$.

$$ \frac{F}{\rho L^4 f^2} = \Pi\left(\frac{\rho L^2 f}{\mu}\right) $$

Viscosity of the Oil

Physical similarity requires equal Reynolds numbers:

$$ \frac{\rho L^2 f}{\mu} = \frac{\rho_e L_e^2 f_e}{\mu_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mu_e = \mu\,\frac{\rho_e}{\rho}\left(\frac{L_e}{L}\right)^2\frac{f_e}{f} $$
$$ \mu_e = 1.8\times 10^{-5}\times\frac{800}{1.2}\times\left(\frac{250}{2.5}\right)^2\times\frac{0.2}{200} = 1.8\times 10^{-5}\times 666.7\times 10^{4}\times 10^{-3} = 0.12\ \text{kg/(m s)} $$

Lift Curve of the Fly

Each measured point, with $\mu_e = 0.12$ kg/(m s), is similar to the fly state with the same Reynolds number:

$$ \frac{\rho L^2 f}{\mu} = \frac{\rho_e L_e^2 f_e}{\mu_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; f = \frac{\rho_e}{\rho}\left(\frac{L_e}{L}\right)^2\frac{\mu}{\mu_e}\,f_e = 666.7\times 10^{4}\times 1.5\times 10^{-4}\,f_e = 1000\,f_e $$

and then equal dimensionless lift:

$$ \frac{F}{\rho L^4 f^2} = \frac{F_e}{\rho_e L_e^4 f_e^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; F = \frac{\rho}{\rho_e}\left(\frac{L}{L_e}\right)^4\left(\frac{f}{f_e}\right)^2 F_e = \frac{1.2}{800}\times 10^{-8}\times 10^{6}\,F_e = 1.5\times 10^{-5}\,F_e $$

The same factor follows in the key's form: substituting $f/f_e$ from the first relation, $F = (\rho_e/\rho)(\mu/\mu_e)^2 F_e = 666.7\times(1.5\times 10^{-4})^2 F_e = 1.5\times 10^{-5}F_e$.

Official Problem Statement

Worked solutionexpand / collapse

Hypotheses, Data and Dimensional Table

  • Air is a compressible gas: its compressibility enters through the speed of sound $c$ (Notes.pdf, p. 66). Gravity is negligible.
  • Prototype at $z = 5000$ m: $\rho = 0.736$ kg/m$^3$, $\mu = 1.630\times 10^{-5}$ kg/(m s), $c = 320.5$ m/s. Model at $z = 0$: $\rho_e = 1.225$ kg/m$^3$, $\mu_e = 1.775\times 10^{-5}$ kg/(m s), $c_e = 340.3$ m/s.
ParameterDimensions
$W$ (unknown)$ML^2T^{-3}$
$\rho$$ML^{-3}$
$\mu$$ML^{-1}T^{-1}$
$c$, $V$$LT^{-1}$
$D$$L$
$\Omega$$T^{-1}$

Application of the Π Theorem

$$ W = W(\rho, \mu, c, V, D, \Omega) $$

$n = 6$; $(\rho, V, D)$ have independent dimensions ($\det = -1$, as in DA1), so $n - k = 3$ groups remain.

  • Power. $[W] = [\rho][V]^3[D]^2$ (exponents as in DA1): $\Pi_0 = W/(\rho V^3 D^2)$.
  • Viscosity. $[\mu] = [\rho][V][D]$: the Reynolds number $\rho V D/\mu$.
  • Speed of sound. $[c] = [V]$: the inverse Mach number $V/c$ is the group.
  • Rotation. $[\Omega] = [V][D]^{-1}$: the group $\Omega D/V$ (the inverse of the advance ratio).
$$ \frac{W}{\rho V^3 D^2} = f\left(\frac{\rho V D}{\mu}, \frac{V}{c}, \frac{\Omega D}{V}\right) $$

Physical Similarity at Sea Level

The three groups must be equal. The Mach number fixes the tunnel speed:

$$ \frac{V}{c} = \frac{V_e}{c_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; V_e = \frac{c_e}{c}\,V = \frac{340.3}{320.5}\times 300 = 1.0618\times 300 = 318.5\ \text{km/h} \simeq 319\ \text{km/h} $$

The Reynolds number then fixes the diameter:

$$ \frac{\rho V D}{\mu} = \frac{\rho_e V_e D_e}{\mu_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; D_e = \frac{\mu_e}{\mu}\,\frac{\rho}{\rho_e}\,\frac{V}{V_e}\,D = \frac{\mu_e}{\mu}\,\frac{\rho}{\rho_e}\,\frac{c}{c_e}\,D $$
$$ D_e = \frac{1.775}{1.630}\times\frac{0.736}{1.225}\times\frac{320.5}{340.3}\times 1.5 = 1.0890\times 0.6008\times 0.9418\times 1.5 = 0.924\ \text{m} $$

The advance ratio fixes the rotation:

$$ \frac{\Omega D}{V} = \frac{\Omega_e D_e}{V_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; \Omega_e = \frac{D}{D_e}\,\frac{V_e}{V}\,\Omega = \frac{1.5}{0.924}\times 1.0618\times 2500 = 1.6229\times 1.0618\times 2500 = 4308\ \text{rpm} $$

The ratios $V/V_e$, $\Omega/\Omega_e$ are dimensionless, so km/h and rpm can be kept.

Power of the Airplane

With the three groups equal, $W/(\rho V^3 D^2)$ is equal:

$$ \frac{W}{\rho V^3 D^2} = \frac{W_e}{\rho_e V_e^3 D_e^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; W = \frac{\rho}{\rho_e}\left(\frac{V}{V_e}\right)^3\left(\frac{D}{D_e}\right)^2 W_e $$
$$ W = 0.6008\times(0.9418)^3\times(1.6229)^2\times 6\times 10^{7} = 0.6008\times 0.8354\times 2.6338\times 6\times 10^{7} = 7.93\times 10^{7}\ \text{W} $$

Answers and Checks

  • Reduced law: $W/(\rho V^3D^2) = f\left(\rho VD/\mu,\, V/c,\, \Omega D/V\right)$.
  • Model: $D_e = 0.924$ m, $V_e = 319$ km/h, $\Omega_e = 4308$ rpm.
  • Airplane power: $W = 7.9\times 10^7$ W.
  • Dimensions: as in DA1, $\rho V^3D^2$ is a power; $V/c$ and $\Omega D/V$ are pure numbers.
  • Why the model is smaller: sea-level air is denser ($\rho_e/\mu_e = 6.9\times 10^4$ s/m$^2$ against $4.5\times 10^4$ at 5000 m), so the same Reynolds number needs a smaller diameter; the faster tunnel speed (same Mach in warmer air) helps too.
  • On the data: the given $W_e = 6\times 10^7$ W is far above the real power of a small propeller (of order $10^5$ W); the conversion factor $W/W_e = 1.32$ does not depend on it.
  • Check against the official key: DA9.pdf gives $D_e = 0.924$ m, $V_e = 319$ km/h, $\Omega_e = 4308$ rpm and $W = 7.9\times 10^7$ W, the same results.
