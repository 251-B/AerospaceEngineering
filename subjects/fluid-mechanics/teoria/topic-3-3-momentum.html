Subchapter 3.3 · Momentum Dynamics

Conservation of Linear Momentum & Reaction Forces

Master 5-term balance breakdown, the atmospheric gauge pressure cancellation trick, fluid vs solid reactions, and the 5-stage exam algorithm.

1. The Master Linear Momentum Equation

Newton's Second Law for a closed material fluid system $V_f(t)$ states that the rate of change of linear momentum equals the resultant vector sum of all external forces (surface forces and body forces) acting on the fluid:

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{V_f(t)} \rho\vec{v} \, dV\right] = -\int_{\Sigma_f(t)} p\vec{n} \, d\sigma + \int_{\Sigma_f(t)} \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\vec{n} \, d\sigma + \int_{V_f(t)} \rho\vec{f}_m \, dV $$
[Eq. 3.34] Newton's second law for a closed material fluid system.

Substituting intensive linear momentum $\vec{b} = \vec{v}$ into the universal Reynolds Transport Theorem (Eq. 3.8) yields the Master Linear Momentum Conservation Equation for any moving control volume $V_c(t)$:

$$ \mathbf{\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{V_c(t)} \rho\vec{v} \, dV\right] + \int_{\Sigma_c(t)} \rho\vec{v}[(\vec{v}-\vec{v}_c)\cdot\vec{n}] \, d\sigma = -\int_{\Sigma_c(t)} p\vec{n} \, d\sigma + \int_{\Sigma_c(t)} \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\vec{n} \, d\sigma + \int_{V_c(t)} \rho\vec{f}_m \, dV} $$
[Eq. 3.35] Master Integral Linear Momentum Equation for an arbitrary moving control volume.
Rigorous Term-by-Term Physical Anatomy
  1. $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V_c} \rho\vec{v} dV$: Unsteady accumulation rate of linear momentum stored inside the geometric boundaries of $V_c$. (Identically zero for steady flows).
  2. $\int_{\Sigma_c} \rho\vec{v}[(\vec{v}-\vec{v}_c)\cdot\vec{n}] d\sigma$: Net convective flux of linear momentum carried out of the control volume across $\Sigma_c$ by the relative velocity $(\vec{v}-\vec{v}_c)$.
  3. $-\int_{\Sigma_c} p\vec{n} d\sigma$: Net normal pressure force exerted on the control surface. (The negative sign reflects the fact that pressure compresses inward, opposite to outward normal $\vec{n}$).
  4. $\int_{\Sigma_c} \bar{\bar{\tau}}'\cdot\vec{n} d\sigma$: Net viscous shear force (skin friction and viscous stresses) exerted on $\Sigma_c$. Often negligible across open flow apertures at high Reynolds numbers.
  5. $\int_{V_c} \rho\vec{f}_m dV$: Net volumetric body force acting on fluid elements inside $V_c$ (gravity $\rho\vec{g}$, plus inertial forces if non-inertial reference frame).

2. The Gauge Pressure Trick & Solid vs Fluid Conventions

In almost every practical engineering problem, the fluid system operates under ambient atmospheric pressure $p_a$. If we evaluate absolute pressure $p$, we must integrate large constants over complex geometries. The Gauge Pressure Trick eliminates this burden entirely.

Mathematical Proof

Consider a closed control surface $\Sigma_c$. By Gauss's Divergence Theorem, integrating any uniform constant scalar field $p_a$ over a closed boundary yields:

$$ \oint_{\Sigma_c} p_a \vec{n} \, d\sigma = \int_{V_c} \nabla(p_a) \, dV = \vec{0} \qquad (\text{since } \nabla p_a = \vec{0}) $$

Because this closed integral is strictly zero, we can subtract it from the absolute pressure integral without altering the net force balance in any way:

$$ -\int_{\Sigma_c} p\vec{n} \, d\sigma = -\int_{\Sigma_c} p\vec{n} \, d\sigma + \oint_{\Sigma_c} p_a \vec{n} \, d\sigma = -\int_{\Sigma_c} (p - p_a)\vec{n} \, d\sigma = \mathbf{-\int_{\Sigma_c} p_{\text{gauge}} \vec{n} \, d\sigma} $$
The Game-Changing Practical Advantage
At any open discharge aperture or free liquid surface exposed to the atmosphere, $p = p_a \implies p_{\text{gauge}} = 0$. Consequently, the pressure integral across all free atmospheric jets drops to zero automatically! Only pressurized boundaries ($p > p_a$) contribute to the surface integral.

Forces on Fluid vs Forces on Solid (Newton's 3rd Law)

When our control surface cuts across solid anchoring bolts, pipe flanges, or engine test stand mounts, the momentum equation calculates the reaction force exerted by the solid mounting structure on the fluid ($\vec{R}_{\text{solid}\to\text{fluid}}$):

$$ \sum \vec{F}_{\text{on fluid}} = \vec{R}_{\text{solid}\to\text{fluid}} - \int_{\Sigma_c} p_{\text{gauge}}\vec{n} \, d\sigma + \int_{V_c}\rho\vec{g} \, dV $$

By Newton's Third Law of Action-Reaction, the holding force exerted by the fluid on the solid structure / anchor is the exact opposite:

$$ \mathbf{\vec{F}_{\text{fluid}\to\text{structure}} = -\vec{R}_{\text{solid}\to\text{fluid}}} $$

3. The 5-Stage Algorithm for ANY Momentum Exam Problem

Follow this exact chronological blueprint to solve every linear momentum exam problem cleanly:

1

Define Control Volume $V_c$ & Coordinate System

Place inlet and outlet boundaries perpendicular to the flow streamlines (so $\vec{v}\parallel\vec{n}$). Cut across structural flanges where reaction forces $\vec{R}$ act. Set up a clear Cartesian coordinate frame $(x, y, z)$.

2

Establish Continuity (Mass Conservation)

Relate inlet and outlet velocities, cross-sectional areas, and mass flow rates: $\dot{m} = \rho v_1 A_1 = \rho v_2 A_2$. Solve for unknown velocities before touching forces.

3

Compute Net Convective Momentum Flux

For 1D streams, evaluate the vector sum: $\sum \dot{m}_{\text{out}}\vec{v}_{\text{out}} - \sum \dot{m}_{\text{in}}\vec{v}_{\text{in}}$. Respect signs carefully according to your coordinate axes.

4

Inventory All External Forces (Using Gauge Pressures)

Include gauge pressure forces on cut fluid surfaces ($p_{g} A \vec{n}_{\text{inward}}$), unknown structural reaction vector $\vec{R} = (R_x, R_y, R_z)$, and fluid weight $\vec{W} = -m_f g \hat{j}$ if non-negligible.

5

Project onto Axes & Solve for Unknown Reactions

Equate $\sum F_x = \dot{M}_{\text{out}, x} - \dot{M}_{\text{in}, x}$ and $\sum F_y = \dot{M}_{\text{out}, y} - \dot{M}_{\text{in}, y}$. Invert for $R_x, R_y$. Finally, invert signs for forces acting on the solid body!

Canonical Walkthrough 1 · CL3 Setup

Reducing 90° Pipe Elbow & Mouthpiece

Problem: Water ($\rho$) flows steadily through a horizontal reducing 90° elbow. Inlet 1 has area $A_1$, velocity $v_1\hat{i}$, and gauge pressure $p_{g1}$. Exit 2 has area $A_2 < A_1$, points vertically $+y$ ($v_2\hat{j}$), and discharges into the atmosphere ($p_{g2} = 0$). Fluid mass inside the bend is $m_f$. Determine the anchoring force $\vec{R}$ required to hold the elbow stationary.

1 & 2 Continuity & Momentum Fluxes

From mass conservation: $\dot{m} = \rho A_1 v_1 = \rho A_2 v_2 \implies v_2 = v_1 \frac{A_1}{A_2}$.

Net convective momentum flux:

  • $\vec{v}_{\text{in}} = v_1 \hat{i}$, with $\dot{m}_{\text{in}} = \dot{m}$
  • $\vec{v}_{\text{out}} = v_2 \hat{j}$, with $\dot{m}_{\text{out}} = \dot{m}$
  • Convective flux: $\sum \dot{m}_{\text{out}}\vec{v}_{\text{out}} - \sum \dot{m}_{\text{in}}\vec{v}_{\text{in}} = -\dot{m} v_1 \hat{i} + \dot{m} v_2 \hat{j}$
3 & 4 Forces Inventory & Projection

External forces acting on the fluid volume:

  • Inlet gauge pressure: $\vec{F}_{p1} = -p_{g1} A_1 (-\hat{i}) = +p_{g1} A_1 \hat{i}$ (compresses inward into $V_c$).
  • Exit gauge pressure: $\vec{F}_{p2} = 0$ (atmospheric discharge).
  • Anchoring reaction from pipe bolts: $\vec{R} = R_x \hat{i} + R_y \hat{j}$.
  • Weight of fluid: $\vec{W} = -m_f g \hat{j}$.

Projecting onto Cartesian axes:

$$ x\text{-axis}: \quad p_{g1} A_1 + R_x = -\dot{m} v_1 \implies \mathbf{R_x = -p_{g1} A_1 - \dot{m} v_1} $$ $$ y\text{-axis}: \quad R_y - m_f g = \dot{m} v_2 \implies \mathbf{R_y = \dot{m} v_2 + m_f g} $$

The force exerted by the fluid on the elbow is $\vec{F}_{\text{fluid}\to\text{elbow}} = -\vec{R} = (p_{g1} A_1 + \dot{m} v_1)\hat{i} - (\dot{m} v_2 + m_f g)\hat{j}$.

Canonical Walkthrough 2 · CL5 Setup

Testbench Rocket / Turboreactor Thrust

Problem: A jet engine is mounted on a horizontal test bench. Air enters at mass flow rate $\dot{m}_a$ with speed $v_a$ at atmospheric pressure $p_a$. Fuel is burned at rate $\dot{m}_f$. Combustion gases exhaust through nozzle area $A_e$ at speed $v_e$ with exit static pressure $p_e \neq p_a$. Determine the horizontal thrust $F_T$ transmitted to the test stand anchor.

AnalysisControl Volume & Momentum Balance

Select a control volume tightly enclosing the engine outer casing, cutting across the inlet (area $A_a$), nozzle exit (area $A_e$), and the structural load cell pin $R_x$ holding the engine against thrust.

Continuity: $\dot{m}_e = \dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_f$.

Linear momentum balance along the thrust axis $+x$:

$$ \sum F_x = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_a $$

External forces acting on the fluid/engine system (using ambient pressure $p_a$ as reference):

$$ \sum F_x = R_x - (p_e - p_a)A_e $$

Equating forces to momentum flux:

$$ R_x - (p_e - p_a)A_e = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_a \implies R_x = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_a + (p_e - p_a)A_e $$

The net forward propulsive thrust delivered to the aircraft mount is:

$$ \mathbf{F_{\text{thrust}} = \dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_a + (p_e - p_a)A_e} $$

Composed of momentum thrust $\dot{m}_e v_e - \dot{m}_a v_a$ and pressure thrust $(p_e - p_a)A_e$.

Canonical Walkthrough 3 · CL1 Setup

Airfoil Drag Evaluation from Wake Deficit (Momentum Integral)

Problem: Using the downstream wake deficit profile from Subchapter 3.2 ($v(y) = v_\infty[1 - \Delta(1 - |y|/b)]$ for $|y| \le b$), compute the total aerodynamic drag force $D$ per unit span acting on the airfoil.

DerivationThe Wake Momentum-Deficit Drag Integral

Select a large rectangular control volume of length $2L$ and height $2H > 2b$. Far upstream and downstream, static pressure has recovered to free stream $p_\infty$, so net pressure forces vanish ($\oint (p-p_\infty)\vec{n}d\sigma = \vec{0}$).

The drag force $D$ is the horizontal force exerted by the airfoil on the fluid, opposing motion (pushes $-x$ on fluid):

$$ -D = \int_{\Sigma_c} \rho v_x (\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}) \, d\sigma $$

Convective flux splits into inlet ($-x$), outlet ($+x$), and lateral outflow escaping through top/bottom at speed $v_\infty$:

$$ \int_{\Sigma_{\text{out}}} \rho v_x^2 dy + \dot{m}_{\text{lateral}} v_\infty - \int_{\Sigma_{\text{in}}} \rho v_\infty^2 dy = -D $$

Using continuity $\dot{m}_{\text{lateral}} = \dot{m}_{\text{in}} - \dot{m}_{\text{wake}} = \int_{-H}^H \rho v_\infty dy - \int_{-b}^b \rho v(y) dy$, the uniform outer portions cancel identically, yielding the universal Wake Deficit Drag Integral:

$$ \mathbf{D = \rho \int_{-b}^b v(y) [v_\infty - v(y)] \, dy} $$

Substitute $v(y) = v_\infty[1 - \delta]$ where $\delta(y) = \Delta(1 - y/b)$:

$$ D = 2\rho v_\infty^2 \int_0^b [1 - \delta(y)] \delta(y) \, dy = 2\rho v_\infty^2 \left[ \int_0^b \delta(y) dy - \int_0^b \delta^2(y) dy \right] $$

Evaluating each integral with variable substitution $\xi = 1 - y/b$ ($dy = -b d\xi$):

$$ \int_0^b \delta(y) dy = \Delta b \int_0^1 \xi d\xi = \frac{\Delta b}{2}, \qquad \int_0^b \delta^2(y) dy = \Delta^2 b \int_0^1 \xi^2 d\xi = \frac{\Delta^2 b}{3} $$

Combining the integrals yields the exact analytical drag per unit span:

$$ \mathbf{D = 2\rho v_\infty^2 b \left( \frac{\Delta}{2} - \frac{\Delta^2}{3} \right) = \rho v_\infty^2 b \Delta \left(1 - \frac{2}{3}\Delta\right)} $$
