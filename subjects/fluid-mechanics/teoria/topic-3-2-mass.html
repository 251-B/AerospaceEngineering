Subchapter 3.2 · Continuity Balances

Conservation of Mass (Continuity) in Problem Solving

Global mass balances, 1D streamtube formulations, unsteady tank drainage, and upstream streamtube capture width determination.

1. The Master Integral Continuity Equation

In classical fluid mechanics, matter is neither created nor destroyed. For any closed material fluid system $V_f(t)$, the rate of change of mass is identically zero:

$$ \frac{d M_{\text{sys}}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{V_f(t)} \rho \, dV\right] = 0 $$
[Eq. 3.9] Axiom of mass conservation for a material fluid system.

Setting the intensive property $b = 1$ in the universal Reynolds Transport Theorem (Eq. 3.8) yields the Master Integral Mass Conservation Equation for any moving control volume $V_c(t)$:

$$ \mathbf{\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{V_c(t)} \rho \, dV\right] + \int_{\Sigma_c(t)} \rho [(\vec{v} - \vec{v}_c)\cdot\vec{n}] \, d\sigma = 0} $$
[Eq. 3.10] Integral continuity equation for an arbitrary moving control volume.

For a fixed, rigid control volume ($V_0, \vec{v}_c = \vec{0}$), this simplifies to the Eulerian storage and convective flux balance:

$$ \int_{V_0} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} \, dV + \int_{\Sigma_0} \rho (\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}) \, d\sigma = 0 $$
[Eq. 3.11] Continuity equation for a fixed spatial control volume.

2. Incompressible Fluids & 1D Streamtubes

When the fluid density is spatially and temporally constant ($\rho = \text{const}$, valid for liquids and for gas flows where the Mach number $Ma = v/a < 0.3$), the density factors out of both integrals. For a fixed control volume:

$$ \int_{\Sigma_0} \vec{v}\cdot\vec{n} \, d\sigma = 0 \implies \sum_k Q_{\text{in}, k} = \sum_j Q_{\text{out}, j} $$

where $Q = \int_A (\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}) dA$ is the volumetric flow rate ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$).

1D Streamtube Approximation & Average Velocity

Across any duct or streamtube cross-section $A$, the mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ ($\text{kg/s}$) is:

$$ \dot{m} = \int_A \rho (\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}) \, dA = \rho \bar{v} A $$

where the area-averaged bulk velocity $\bar{v}$ is defined by:

$$ \bar{v} = \frac{1}{A} \int_A v(\vec{x}) \, dA $$
Velocity Profiles & Bulk Velocity

In laminar circular pipe flow (Poiseuille), $v(r) = v_{\max}[1 - (r/R)^2]$, so the bulk velocity is $\bar{v} = \frac{1}{2}v_{\max}$. In turbulent pipe flow, the profile is fuller ($1/7\text{th}$ power law), yielding $\bar{v} \approx 0.817 v_{\max}$. Always integrate the profile explicitly when given!

3. Unsteady Storage: Filling & Emptying Tanks

When the mass inside the control volume changes with time, the unsteady storage term must be evaluated by tracking the moving liquid level $h(t)$.

Consider a tank of horizontal cross-sectional area $A_T$ with an inlet flow rate $Q_{\text{in}}$ and a bottom discharge orifice of area $A_e \ll A_T$. Defining a control volume that encloses the liquid inside the tank up to the free surface $z = h(t)$:

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{V_c} \rho \, dV\right] = \rho \frac{d}{dt}\left[A_T h(t)\right] = \rho A_T \frac{dh}{dt} $$

Net mass flux across the boundaries:

$$ \int_{\Sigma_c} \rho (\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}) \, d\sigma = \rho Q_{\text{out}} - \rho Q_{\text{in}} $$

Applying Torricelli's law for inviscid discharge from the bottom orifice, $v_e(t) = \sqrt{2gh(t)}$, so $Q_{\text{out}} = A_e \sqrt{2gh(t)}$. Substituting into the continuity equation:

$$ A_T \frac{dh}{dt} = Q_{\text{in}} - A_e \sqrt{2gh(t)} $$

For a draining tank with no inflow ($Q_{\text{in}} = 0$) and initial height $H_0$, separating variables and integrating yields the classical draining time:

$$ \int_{H_0}^0 \frac{dh}{\sqrt{h}} = -\frac{A_e \sqrt{2g}}{A_T} \int_0^{t_{\text{drain}}} dt \implies \mathbf{t_{\text{drain}} = \frac{2 A_T \sqrt{H_0}}{A_e \sqrt{2g}}} $$

Airfoil Wake Deficit: Determining Upstream Capture Width

Physical Setup: An airfoil is tested in a uniform wind tunnel flow of velocity $v_\infty$ and density $\rho$. Far downstream, boundary layer skin friction and wake mixing generate a symmetric velocity deficit over a wake band of width $2b$ ($|y| \le b$):

$$ v(y) = v_\infty \left[ 1 - \Delta \left(1 - \frac{|y|}{b}\right) \right] \quad \text{for } |y| \le b; \qquad v(y) = v_\infty \quad \text{for } |y| > b $$

where $0 < \Delta < 1$ is the centerline fractional velocity defect. Far upstream where the flow is completely uniform ($v = v_\infty$), all the fluid that eventually exits through the wake $[-b, b]$ passed through a capture band $[-y_{-\infty}, y_{-\infty}]$.

Objective: Determine the upstream capture semi-width $y_{-\infty}$ from conservation of mass.

Stage 1 Select the Wake Streamtube Control Volume

Define a 2D control volume bounded by:

  • Upstream inlet face ($\Sigma_{\text{in}}$): Located at $x = -\infty$, extending across $y \in [-y_{-\infty}, y_{-\infty}]$ where velocity is uniform $v_x = v_\infty$.
  • Downstream exit face ($\Sigma_{\text{out}}$): Located at $x = +\infty$, spanning the exact wake thickness $y \in [-b, b]$ with velocity profile $v(y)$.
  • Upper and lower boundaries ($\Sigma_{\text{streamline}}$): The limiting streamlines that pass through $(\pm b)$ at downstream infinity. By definition, no fluid crosses a streamline: $(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}) \equiv 0$.
Stage 2 Evaluate Upstream Inflow Mass Rate

For unit span ($W = 1$) into the page, integrating over the upstream aperture:

$$ \dot{m}_{\text{in}} = \int_{-y_{-\infty}}^{y_{-\infty}} \rho v_\infty \, dy = 2\rho v_\infty y_{-\infty} $$
Stage 3 Evaluate Downstream Wake Outflow Mass Rate

Exploiting symmetry between upper and lower halves of the wake:

$$ \dot{m}_{\text{out}} = \int_{-b}^b \rho v(y) \, dy = 2\rho v_\infty \int_0^b \left[ 1 - \Delta \left(1 - \frac{y}{b}\right) \right] dy $$

Evaluating the spatial integral across the shear layer:

$$ \int_0^b \left[ 1 - \Delta + \Delta \frac{y}{b} \right] dy = \left[ (1 - \Delta)y + \Delta \frac{y^2}{2b} \right]_0^b = (1 - \Delta)b + \frac{\Delta b}{2} = b \left(1 - \frac{\Delta}{2}\right) $$

Therefore, the total wake mass flow rate is:

$$ \dot{m}_{\text{out}} = 2\rho v_\infty b \left(1 - \frac{\Delta}{2}\right) $$
Stage 4 Solve for Upstream Width $y_{-\infty}$

Since the flow is steady and no mass escapes through the bounding streamlines, $\dot{m}_{\text{in}} = \dot{m}_{\text{out}}$:

$$ 2\rho v_\infty y_{-\infty} = 2\rho v_\infty b \left(1 - \frac{\Delta}{2}\right) $$

Dividing by $2\rho v_\infty$ gives the exact analytical result:

$$ \mathbf{y_{-\infty} = b \left(1 - \frac{\Delta}{2}\right)} $$
Aerospace Exam Warning: The Lateral Leakage Trap
Notice that because $\Delta > 0$, we have $y_{-\infty} < b$! Because fluid in the wake has been slowed down by aerodynamic drag, each fluid package moves slower, requiring a larger cross-sectional area to carry the same mass flux. Consequently, streamlines diverge around and behind the airfoil.

If you choose a rigid rectangular control volume of fixed height $2H > 2b$ instead of streamline boundaries, fluid must leak outward through the top and bottom surfaces with mass rate $\dot{m}_{\text{lateral}} = 2\rho v_\infty (b - y_{-\infty}) = \rho v_\infty b \Delta$. Failing to include this lateral mass escape is the most frequent exam mistake in wake momentum balances!
