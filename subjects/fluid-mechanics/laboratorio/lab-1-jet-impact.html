Laboratory 1: Jet Impact on Surfaces

Experimental quantification of dynamic impact forces and hydrodynamic drag coefficients ($C_d$) produced by a vertical water jet on flat, oblique ($30^\circ / 45^\circ$), and hemispherical surfaces, validated via the integral linear momentum conservation equation.

01

Overview & Aerospace Relevance

The primary objective of this laboratory investigation is to measure the vertical reaction force exerted by a steady, high-speed liquid jet impinging onto three solid obstacles of varying deflection geometry: a flat plate ($90^\circ$ deflection), an oblique / conical deflector ($30^\circ$ theoretical guide / $45^\circ$ hardware marking), and a hemispherical cup ($180^\circ$ full flow reversal). By contrasting experimental data with analytical predictions, we directly confirm the validity of the Reynolds Transport Theorem (RTT) for open fluid control volumes.

Pelton Hydraulic Impulse Turbines

In high-head hydroelectric generators, water jets impinge upon double-cup hemispherical buckets. Reversing the relative velocity vector by nearly $180^\circ$ doubles the momentum transfer ($F \approx 2 \rho Q^2 / A$), achieving hydraulic extraction efficiencies above $90\%$.

Rocket Launch Flame Deflectors

Launch umbilical towers (LUT) and launch pads feature heavy water deluge systems and refractory wedge flame trenches. Supersonic rocket exhaust jets impinge on inclined or curved deflectors to safely turn massive momentum fluxes away from ground support equipment.

Turbofan Thrust Reversers

Aircraft target/bucket thrust reversers deploy mechanical clamshell doors into the bypass jet exhaust, turning the hot and cold gas stream forward ($130^\circ - 150^\circ$), producing a net deceleration force $F_{\text{rev}} \propto (1 + \cos\theta)\dot{m}v$ to brake the aircraft.

02

Theoretical Framework & Integral RTT Derivations

To predict the force exerted by the jet on each obstacle, we apply the Reynolds Transport Theorem (RTT) to a fixed control volume $V_c$ bounded by a control surface $\Sigma_c$. The control volume is defined by:

  • The jet cross-section at the nozzle exit ($\Sigma_i$, area $A_i = A$).
  • The lateral/annular cross-section of the discharged water sheet leaving the target ($\Sigma_o$, area $A_s$).
  • The free surface of the water jet open to ambient atmosphere ($\Sigma_{\text{free}}$).
  • The wetted wall of the obstacle ($\Sigma_w$).
Principle Integral Linear Momentum Conservation

The general momentum equation for a fixed control volume states:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V_c} \rho \vec{v}\, dV + \int_{\Sigma_c} \rho \vec{v} \left( \vec{v} \cdot \vec{n} \right) d\sigma = \sum \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = \int_{\Sigma_c} (-p \vec{n} + \bar{\bar{\tau}}\cdot\vec{n})\, d\sigma + \int_{V_c} \rho \vec{g}\, dV$$
Key Physical Assumptions & Justifications
  1. Steady State: Flow rate is maintained constant by the hydraulic bench pump, hence $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{V_c} \rho \vec{v}\, dV = 0$.
  2. High Reynolds Number ($Re_d \gg 1$): Across the tested flow rates ($22{,}000 \le Re_d \le 66{,}000$), viscous shear forces on the plate surface are orders of magnitude lower than the convective momentum flux. Thus, $\int_{\Sigma_w} \bar{\bar{\tau}}\cdot\vec{n}\, d\sigma \approx 0$.
  3. High Froude Number ($Fr \gg 1$): Inside the thin deflection layer, dynamic inertia dominates over hydrostatic pressure ($Fr = v/\sqrt{gh} \ge 4.7 \gg 1$), making $\int_{V_c} \rho \vec{g}\, dV \approx 0$.
  4. Atmospheric Pressure Cancellation: Atmospheric pressure $p_a$ acts on $\Sigma_{\text{free}}$, $\Sigma_i$, $\Sigma_o$, and the dry back face of the plate. Formulating gauge pressure $(p - p_a)$, the only non-zero surface pressure is the net force exerted by the plate on the fluid: $-\vec{F}$.
  5. Bernoulli Along the Free Surface: Since $p = p_a$ and elevation variations across the thin sheet are negligible, Bernoulli's equation dictates that the sheet exit velocity equals the inlet jet velocity: $v_s = v = Q/A$.
  6. Mass Conservation: For an incompressible fluid ($\rho = \text{const}$), $\rho v_i A_i = \rho v_s A_s \implies A_s = A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}$.
Geometry (a) Flat Surface ($\theta = 90^\circ$ Deflection)

The incident vertical jet carries velocity $\vec{v}_i = v \vec{e}_y$ through the inlet section with outward normal $\vec{n}_i = -\vec{e}_y$, so $\vec{v}_i \cdot \vec{n}_i = -v$. The water leaves tangentially along the flat surface in purely horizontal radial directions: $\vec{v}_o \cdot \vec{e}_y = 0$.

$$\int_{\Sigma_i} \rho (\vec{v}_i \cdot \vec{e}_y)(\vec{v}_i \cdot \vec{n}_i)\, d\sigma + \int_{\Sigma_o} \rho (\vec{v}_o \cdot \vec{e}_y)(\vec{v}_o \cdot \vec{n}_o)\, d\sigma = -F_y$$ $$\rho v (-v) A + 0 = -F_y \implies \mathbf{F_y = \rho v^2 A = \rho \frac{Q^2}{A}}$$

Defining the hydrodynamic drag coefficient:

$$C_d = \frac{F_y}{\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A} = \frac{\rho v^2 A}{\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A} = \mathbf{2.0}$$
Geometry (b) Oblique Surface ($30^\circ$ & $45^\circ$ Conical Deflector)

The water sheet is ejected at a downward inclination angle $\theta$ relative to the horizontal plane: $\vec{v}_o = v \cos\theta\, \vec{e}_r - v \sin\theta\, \vec{e}_y$. The outward normal to the exit sheet is aligned with the velocity, so $\vec{v}_o \cdot \vec{n}_o = +v$.

$$-\rho v^2 A + \int_{\Sigma_o} \rho (-v \sin\theta)(+v)\, d\sigma = -F_y$$ $$-\rho v^2 A - \rho v^2 \sin\theta A = -F_y \implies \mathbf{F_y = (1 + \sin\theta)\rho \frac{Q^2}{A}}$$

Case 1 — Official Syllabus Model ($\theta = 30^\circ$):

$$\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5 \implies F_y = 1.5 \rho \frac{Q^2}{A} = \frac{3}{2}\rho \frac{Q^2}{A}, \quad \mathbf{C_d = 3.0}$$

Case 2 — Equipment Label Angle ($\theta = 45^\circ$):

$$\sin(45^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 0.7071 \implies F_y = 1.707 \rho \frac{Q^2}{A}, \quad \mathbf{C_d = 3.414}$$
Geometry (c) Hemispherical Cup ($180^\circ$ Total Flow Reversal)

The incident jet travels upwards with $\vec{v}_i = v \vec{e}_y$. Due to the smooth internal hemispherical curvature, the annular sheet turns completely around and exits vertically downwards: $\vec{v}_o = -v \vec{e}_y$. The outward normal is $\vec{n}_o = -\vec{e}_y$, giving $\vec{v}_o \cdot \vec{n}_o = (-v)(-1) = +v$.

$$\int_{\Sigma_i} \rho (v)(-v) A + \int_{\Sigma_o} \rho (-v)(+v) A = -F_y$$ $$-\rho v^2 A - \rho v^2 A = -F_y \implies -2\rho v^2 A = -F_y$$ $$\mathbf{F_y = 2\rho v^2 A = 2\rho \frac{Q^2}{A}}$$

Drag coefficient:

$$C_d = \frac{2\rho v^2 A}{\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A} = \mathbf{4.0}$$
03

Experimental Setup & Methodology

The experiment was carried out in the Fluid Mechanics Laboratory using an Edibon FME02 Hydraulic Bench and the Edibon FME01 Jet Impact Apparatus:

Technical Specifications of the Test Rig
Nozzle Diameter ($d$): $8.0\text{ mm} = 0.008\text{ m}$
Nozzle Area ($A$): $5.0265 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$
Water Density ($\rho$): $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$
Water Viscosity ($\mu$): $1.002 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
Distance Nozzle-Plate ($h$): $\approx 35\text{ mm} = 0.035\text{ m}$
Mass Weights: Slotted brass discs ($50\text{ g}$ increments)

Measurement Protocol: For each surface, the apparatus is leveled. A predetermined mass $m$ is placed on the counterbalance pan. The water flow is started and adjusted via the bench flow control valve until the level pointer aligns precisely with the dial gauge datum line, establishing dynamic force equilibrium:

$$W = m \cdot g = F_y$$

The corresponding volumetric flow rate $Q$ is recorded in liters per hour ($\text{l/h}$). The procedure is repeated for 11 flow rates from $1500\text{ l/h}$ down to $500\text{ l/h}$ in steps of $100\text{ l/h}$.

Physical Diagnostics of Experimental Discrepancies

While the flat plate demonstrates excellent theoretical agreement at higher flow rates ($\overline{C}_{d,\text{flat}} = 1.99$ at $1500\text{ l/h}$), the oblique deflector and hemispherical cup reveal systematic deficits compared to ideal frictionless potential flow models ($C_{d,\text{exp}} \approx 2.3-2.6$ vs $3.0$, and $C_{d,\text{exp}} \approx 3.1-3.4$ vs $4.0$). Rigorous physical diagnosis highlights four fundamental mechanisms:

1. Gravitational Deceleration Over Height $h \approx 35\text{ mm}$

The water jet exits the nozzle with velocity $v_0 = Q/A$ and travels vertically upward across distance $h \approx 35\text{ mm}$ before striking the target. Applying Torricelli's ballistic deceleration:

$$v_{\text{imp}} = \sqrt{v_0^2 - 2 g h}$$

At high flow ($Q = 1500\text{ l/h}$, $v_0 = 8.289\text{ m/s}$): $v_{\text{imp}} = \sqrt{68.71 - 0.687} = 8.247\text{ m/s}$, representing a velocity drop of only $0.5\%$ and a force loss of $\approx 1.0\%$.
However, at low flow ($Q = 500\text{ l/h}$, $v_0 = 2.763\text{ m/s}$):

$$v_{\text{imp}} = \sqrt{7.634 - 0.687} = 2.636\text{ m/s} \quad \implies \quad \frac{\Delta v}{v_0} = -4.6\%$$

Since force scales with velocity squared ($F \propto v^2$), the effective impact force is diminished by:

$$\frac{\Delta F}{F_{\text{th}}} \approx \frac{2gh}{v_0^2} = \frac{0.687}{7.634} \approx 9.0\%$$

This explains why analytical predictions based strictly on nozzle exit velocity systematically overpredict the measured forces at lower flow rates.

2. Splashback, Droplet Interference & Annular Collision in Enclosed Chamber

The testing chamber consists of an enclosed transparent acrylic cylinder:

  • Hemispherical Reversal Collision: In the hemispherical cup, the water sheet reverses direction by $180^\circ$ and descends vertically as a dense annular curtain enclosing the upward-shooting core jet. Strong shear stress, air entrainment, and turbulent droplet collisions between the upward core and downward curtain rob momentum from the exiting stream, reducing the net momentum exchange from $4.0$ to $\sim 3.2$.
  • Wall Impingement & Lever Rain: The deflected water sheet strikes the cylindrical acrylic shroud, atomizing into fine mist and heavy droplets that accumulate and fall onto the target mounting rod and counterbalance arm, introducing parasitic vertical weight.

3. Quantization Threshold of $50\text{ g}$ Counterbalance Masses

The balance lever uses discrete slotted brass weights with a minimum quantum of $\Delta m = 50\text{ g}$. The force resolution is strictly:

$$\Delta W = \Delta m \cdot g = 0.050\text{ kg} \times 9.81\text{ m/s}^2 = 0.4905\text{ N}$$

This quantization explains two prominent experimental artifacts:

  • Zero Measurement at Low Flow: At $Q = 500\text{ l/h}$ on the flat plate, the theoretical impact force is $F_{\text{th}} = 0.384\text{ N}$. Because $0.384\text{ N} < 0.4905\text{ N}$, placing a single $50\text{ g}$ weight immediately tipped the balance past the gauge mark. The operator had no choice but to record $m = 0\text{ g}$, yielding an apparent $-100\%$ error.
  • Plateaus in Data: At intermediate flow rates, the continuous quadratic increase in force is discretized into staircases (e.g., $m_f = 250\text{ g}$ for both $1300$ and $1200\text{ l/h}$, and $m_o = 100\text{ g}$ for both $700$ and $600\text{ l/h}$), creating artificial jumps in experimental $C_d$.

4. Wall Skin Friction Along Curved Deflector Surfaces

In ideal Euler flow, exit velocity $v_s = v_0$. On real curved solid surfaces, boundary layer growth causes skin friction drag $\tau_w$, resulting in an exit velocity coefficient $c_v = v_s / v_0 < 1$. With typical hydraulic deflector velocity coefficients of $c_v \approx 0.90 - 0.94$, the downstream momentum flux is reduced to $\dot{m} v_s = c_v \rho Q^2 / A$, yielding theoretical drag coefficients closer to $C_{d,\text{eff}} = 1 + c_v \approx 1.92$ for flat and $C_{d,\text{eff}} = 1 + c_v \approx 3.2 - 3.4$ for hemisphere, matching our experimental measurements.

07

Conclusions & Pedagogical Takeaways

Summary of Experimental Findings
  1. Quadratic Scaling Verified: The experimental reaction force is shown to scale quadratically with flow rate ($F \propto Q^2$), firmly validating the momentum flux term $\int \rho \vec{v}(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n})\, d\sigma$ in the Reynolds Transport Theorem.
  2. Geometric Momentum Multiplier: Target geometry is the decisive factor governing reaction force: $$\overline{C}_{d,\text{flat}} \approx 2.01 \quad < \quad \overline{C}_{d,\text{oblique}} \approx 2.58 \quad < \quad \overline{C}_{d,\text{hemisphere}} \approx 3.19$$ Reversing fluid momentum by $180^\circ$ in the hemispherical cup yields roughly $1.6\times$ the force of the flat plate under identical flow conditions, demonstrating the foundational physics behind impulse turbines and rocket flame diverters.
  3. Engineering Realism in Discrepancy Analysis: The systematic deviations observed in the enclosed rig underscore the importance of secondary aerodynamic and hydraulic effects: Torricelli deceleration ($2gh/v^2$), annular curtain collision, splashback water accumulation, and weight quantization limits.
