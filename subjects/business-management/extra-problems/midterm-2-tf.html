F01 — Investment selection criteria and the cost of capital

Ten statements on investment selection (part ii), written for this portal in the style of the True/False exercise of the course's example exam. State whether each one is true (T) or false (F) and justify it briefly.

Statement 1 · What the NPV adds up

The NPV of a project is the sum of its net cash flows minus the initial outlay, with every flow counted at its nominal value whatever the year in which it arrives.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

That sum is only the NPV at a discount rate of 0%. The NPV discounts each flow to year 0, $NPV = -A + \sum_t Q_t/(1+k)^t$, so a flow received later is worth less today.

Source.

3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercise 5 (a); Examples PDF, Exercise 5 (a).

Statement 2 · The rate that cancels the NPV

The internal rate of return of a project is the discount rate at which its NPV is equal to zero.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

This is the definition used in the key: the IRR is the rate that makes the NPV equal to 0, which is why a known IRR lets one solve for the initial outlay.

Source.

3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercises 4 and 6.

Statement 3 · Ranking two projects below the cost of capital

Two mutually exclusive projects have IRRs of 9% and 7%, and the cost of capital is 9.5%. Under the IRR criterion the company should undertake the project with the 9% IRR, because it ranks first.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Ranking is not enough: a project is accepted only if its IRR exceeds the cost of capital. Both IRRs are below 9.5%, so both NPVs at 9.5% are negative and neither project is accepted.

Source.

Examples PDF, Exercise 6 (b); 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf, Exercise 8.

Statement 4 · IRR above the discount rate

A project with conventional cash flows (an outlay followed by positive flows) has an IRR of 10%. If it is valued at a discount rate of 7%, its NPV is positive.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The NPV of such a project falls as the rate rises and is zero at the IRR. At 7%, below the 10% IRR, it is therefore positive (IRR > k).

Source.

3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercise 6.

Statement 5 · Flows after the recovery

The payback period takes into account every cash flow of the project, including those received after the initial outlay has been recovered.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

The payback is the time it takes to recover the initial investment, so it stops counting at that moment and ignores every later flow. Two projects with the same payback can differ greatly in what they return afterwards.

Source.

3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercises 3 and 5 (b).

Statement 6 · Fisher's rate of one project

Fisher's rate of a project is the discount rate at which the NPV of that project becomes zero.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

That is the IRR. Fisher's rate belongs to a pair of projects: it is the discount rate at which the two NPVs are equal, and it marks where the preferred project changes.

Source.

3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercise 9 (b); Examples PDF, Exercise 5 (c).

Statement 7 · The weighted average cost of capital

The capital cost of a company is the average of the cost of its equity and the cost of its liabilities, each weighted by its share in the financial structure.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Topic 3 defines $K_{WACC} = K_{EQ}\,E/(E+L) + K_L\,L/(E+L)$; the key of Exercise 8 uses this weighted cost as the cut-off rate for the IRR.

Source.

Topic 3, Slide 37; 3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercise 8.

Statement 8 · Is equity free

Equity has no cost for the company, because the company does not pay interest on the funds contributed by its owners.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Every financial resource has a cost: liabilities have an explicit cost and equity an implicit (opportunity) cost, which enters the weighted average cost of capital.

Source.

Topic 3, Slide 37; 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf, Exercise 8 (cost of equity 15%).

Statement 9 · Raising the discount rate

For a project made of an initial outlay followed by positive cash flows, a higher discount rate gives a lower NPV.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Each positive flow is divided by $(1+k)^t$, which grows with $k$, while the outlay at year 0 is not discounted; the NPV therefore decreases as the rate rises.

Source.

3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, Exercise 6 (b); Examples PDF, Exercise 5 (c), table of NPVs at 0% and 8%.

Statement 10 · Equal NPVs at a zero rate

If two projects have the same NPV at a discount rate of 0% (the same sum of net cash flows minus the outlay), then 0% is a Fisher's rate of that pair.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Fisher's rate is the rate at which both NPVs are equal; at 0% both NPVs equal their undiscounted sums, which are the same, so $r = 0\%$ solves the equation.

Source.

Examples PDF, Exercise 5 (c), pair A–B ($x = 1$, FR = 0%), and Exercise 6 (c).

F02 — Production systems, costs and operating leverage

Ten statements on the production function, written for this portal in the style of the True/False exercise of the course's example exam. State whether each one is true (T) or false (F) and justify it briefly.

Statement 1 · What a Gantt chart shows

A Gantt chart is a bar chart that normally shows the dependency relationships between the activities of a project, which makes it the preferred tool for planning complex projects.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

The Gantt chart shows the scheduled start and stop dates of each step, but it normally does not show the dependency relationships between activities and it is used for non-complex projects.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 31.

Statement 2 · Flexibility and cost

Flexibility and cost are two objectives of the production function that can contradict each other.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The four objectives (cost, quality, time of delivery and flexibility) can conflict, and flexibility - cost is one of the pairs named by the course: making many different products in small amounts loses the savings of standard, large-scale production.

Source.

Topic 4, Slides 5 and 6.

Statement 3 · Characteristics of rigid production

Rigid (continuous) production is characterized by flexible volumes of production, highly qualified workers and equipment for general use.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Those are the characteristics of flexible or intermittent production. Rigid production has big volumes, standard products sold at a low price and is capital intensive.

Source.

Topic 4, Slides 10 and 14.

Statement 4 · Flexible versus rigid below the crossing volume

Below the volume at which the total cost lines of the two systems cross, a flexible production system is cheaper than a rigid one, because the rigid system starts with higher fixed costs.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The rigid system's total cost starts higher (more fixed cost) and grows more slowly (less variable cost per unit), so at low volumes (zone A) the flexible system is cheaper and at high volumes (zone B) the rigid one is.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 17.

Statement 5 · Bottlenecks in a product layout

In a product layout the pace of the line is set by the slowest machine, and a problem in one machine affects the whole process.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Both are disadvantages of the product layout listed by the course: time is set by the slowest machine (bottlenecks must be avoided) and a failure of one machine stops the whole flow.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 25.

Statement 6 · Inventories under Just in Time

Just in Time production relies on large safety inventories of finished products so that every customer request can be served at once.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Just in Time is based on requests and eliminates inventories; production is pulled by the orders, not served from stock.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 18.

Statement 7 · Labour productivity

Labour productivity is a total productivity index, because labour is the main factor of production of most firms.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Labour productivity (goods and services divided by labour) relates the output to one factor only, so it is a partial productivity index; the total index divides by labour, capital, raw materials and energy together.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 34.

Statement 8 · A higher price and the break-even point

If the selling price rises while the fixed costs and the variable cost per unit stay the same, the break-even point falls.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

$Q_0 = FC/(P - VC)$: a higher price raises the unit margin $m = P - VC$, and the same fixed costs are covered with fewer units.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 37.

Statement 9 · Sensitivity just above the break-even point

A firm that sells slightly more than its break-even volume sees its operating profit change by much more than 1% when its sales change by 1%.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

$OL = Q/(Q - Q^*)$: when $Q$ is just above $Q^*$ the denominator is small, so the operating leverage is large and the effect on profit is more than proportional.

Source.

Topic 4, Slides 40 and 42.

Statement 10 · Margin of safety in monetary units

The margin of safety in monetary units is the difference between the actual volume and the break-even volume multiplied by the selling price.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

In monetary units the margin of safety is $(Q - Q^*)\,m$: the units above the break-even point multiplied by the unit margin, not by the price, because each extra unit adds only its margin to the profit.

Source.

Topic 4, Slide 43.

F03 — Marketing concepts, the marketing mix and elasticity

Ten statements on marketing and sales, written for this portal in the style of the True/False exercise of the course's example exam. State whether each one is true (T) or false (F) and justify it briefly.

Statement 1 · Where segmentation, positioning and price belong

Market segmentation and the positioning of the product are formulated within strategic marketing, whereas setting the price of the product is a decision of operative marketing.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Segmentation and positioning are the strategies formulated in the last phase of strategic marketing (the analysis system), while price is one of the 4 Ps that operative marketing (the action system) combines in the marketing mix.

Source.

Topic 5, Slides 6-8.

Statement 2 · One-way and two-way processes

Marketing differs from the sale in that marketing is a one-way process in which the company places its goods and services in front of the customer.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

The one-way process is the sale. Marketing is a two-way process: goods and services go to the customer, and information about what the customer wants comes back to the company.

Source.

Topic 5, Slide 5.

Statement 3 · A new version inside an existing line

When an avionics firm adds a new model to one of its existing product lines, the extension (width) of its product range increases.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

The extension, or width (breadth), of the range is the number of product lines, which does not change. A new model inside an existing line increases the depth of that line (products per line) and the length of the range (total number of products).

Source.

Topic 5, Slide 24; Examples PDF, Exercise 4, statement 3.

Statement 4 · Raising the price of a product with inelastic demand

If the demand for a product is inelastic, a rise in its price increases the income of the firm that sells it.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

With $\lvert E_d \rvert < 1$ a 1% price rise lowers the quantity demanded by less than 1%, so almost the same quantity is sold at a higher price and income $I = P \cdot Q$ rises.

Source.

topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 3.

Statement 5 · Several segments, several marketing mixes

A firm that targets two or more segments and develops a distinct product and a separate marketing mix for each of them follows a differentiated concentrated (niche) strategy.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

That is the wide differentiated (multisegment) strategy. The differentiated concentrated (niche) strategy aims the product and the marketing message at a single, well-defined segment.

Source.

Topic 5, Slide 12.

Statement 6 · Who the wholesaler sells to

A wholesaler is an intermediary that sells to other intermediaries or to organisational buyers, but not to final consumers.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

This is the course's definition of the wholesaler; the intermediary that sells consumer goods to final consumers for their personal or household use is the retailer.

Source.

Topic 5, Slide 35.

Statement 7 · The sign of the price elasticity coefficient

By the law of demand the coefficient of price elasticity of demand is negative, which is why its absolute value is used to classify the demand as elastic or inelastic.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions, so the ratio of their percentage changes is negative; the course classifies the demand with $\lvert E_d \rvert$ compared with 1.

Source.

topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2; Topic 5, Slide 17.

Statement 8 · Influencer campaigns and the product

A campaign on social networks with technology influencers is one of the product attributes of a firm, because it builds the image of the product.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

The campaign belongs to the promotion (communication) policy of the marketing mix. The psychosocial attributes of the product are brand, design, packaging and quality, together with attributes such as warranty and post-sale service.

Source.

Topic 5, Slides 19, 23 and 29; Examples PDF, Exercise 2 (b).

Statement 9 · A negative cross elasticity

If the cross elasticity of demand between two goods is negative, the two goods are complementary.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Cross elasticity is negative for complementary goods (a fall in the price of one raises the consumption of both) and positive for substitute goods.

Source.

topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 5.

Statement 10 · How agents and brokers are paid

Merchandise agents and brokers take title to the products they distribute and earn their income from the margin between their buying and selling prices.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Agents and brokers bring buyers and sellers together without owning title to the products, and they are paid a commission for their services.

Source.

F04 — Decisions, structures and control

Ten statements on the management function, written for this portal in the style of the True/False exercise of the course's example exam. State whether each one is true (T) or false (F) and justify it briefly.

Statement 1 · What management is

Management is the process of setting and achieving goals through four basic functions, planning, organizing, leading and controlling, that use human, financial and material resources.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

This is the course's definition of management, and the four managerial functions are exactly planning, organizing, leading and controlling.

Source.

Topic 6, Slides 3-4.

Statement 2 · Innovative decisions

An innovative decision is taken when the problem is known and well defined and the alternative solutions are planned in advance.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

A known, well-defined problem with known alternatives calls for a routine (planned) decision. An innovative (not planned) decision faces an unusual and ambiguous problem with ambiguous, unplanned alternatives.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 5.

Statement 3 · Order of the decision-making process

In the decision-making process, the manager chooses an alternative first and only then analyses the alternatives that were considered.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Analysing the alternatives is step 4 and choosing an alternative is step 5: the choice comes after the analysis, never before it.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 6.

Statement 4 · Conceptual skills along the hierarchy

Conceptual skills, the ability to see the firm as a whole, weigh more in top management than in first-line supervision.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The share of conceptual skills grows as one moves up the management hierarchy, while technical skills weigh most in low management; interpersonal skills are needed at every level.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 8.

Statement 5 · Publishing a job offer

When an avionics firm publishes a job offer on an engineering job board to attract applicants, it is carrying out the selection of personnel.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Attracting applicants is recruitment, a Human Resources activity that comes before selection; selection is the later step that chooses among the candidates already recruited.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 23; Examples PDF, Exercise 4, statement 4.

Statement 6 · The key part of the division organization

In a division organization the middle line is the key part of the structure, and each autonomous division is itself organized as a bureaucracy.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The divisions are autonomous, the middle line is the key part, top management coordinates their performance and outputs, and each division is a bureaucracy.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 20.

Statement 7 · Analysts who design processes

The analysts who design the systems and processes of the firm belong to the technostructure, not to the support staff.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The technostructure is formed by the analysts who design systems and processes; the support staff is the part that lies outside the operating workflow.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 14.

Statement 8 · Decision-making in a machine bureaucracy

A machine bureaucracy combines decentralized decision-making with few hierarchical layers and a wide span of control.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

A machine bureaucracy has centralized decision-making and a highly elaborated administrative structure with many layers and a low span of control. Decentralization belongs to the professional bureaucracy and the adhocracy.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 16.

Statement 9 · The weekly shift roster

Drawing up next week's shift roster of an engine-assembly line is a strategic decision that belongs to top management.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

A weekly shift roster is an operational decision of low management (first-line supervisors). Top management takes the strategic, general decisions that affect the firm as a whole.

Source.

Topic 6, Slide 7; Examples PDF, Exercise 4, statement 5.

Statement 10 · What controlling involves

Controlling involves establishing standards, measuring actual performance, identifying deviations and taking corrective action if required, which is why planning and controlling are inseparable.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

These are the four activities of controlling in the course, and the standards it measures against come from planning, so the two functions cannot be separated.

Topic 7 · Entrepreneurship and innovation 1 block

True/False block · Topic 7 · 10 items · +0.15 right, -0.15 wrong · Practice item, not an official exercise

F05 — Innovation types, S-curves and the dominant design

Ten statements on entrepreneurship and innovation, written for this portal in the style of the True/False exercise of the course's example exam. State whether each one is true (T) or false (F) and justify it briefly.

Statement 1 · Creativity of an organization

The creativity of an organization depends not only on the creativity of its individuals but also on the social processes and contextual factors that shape the way those individuals interact.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The course defines organizational creativity as a function of the creativity of the individuals and of the social processes and contextual factors that shape their interaction; it is not a simple sum, because structure, routines and incentives can thwart or amplify individual creativity.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 5.

Statement 2 · A minor change called radical

A new coating that slightly extends the life of an existing turbine blade, without changing the blade or the engine, is a radical innovation, because it improves an aerospace product.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

A relatively minor change to existing practices is an incremental innovation; a radical innovation is very new and different from prior solutions. Improving a product does not make an innovation radical: the size of the departure from prior solutions does.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 12.

Statement 3 · Same innovation, two firms

The same innovation can be competence-enhancing for one firm and competence-destroying for another firm of the same industry.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

Whether an innovation enhances or destroys competences depends on whose perspective is taken: it enhances the competences of a firm whose existing knowledge base it builds on, and destroys those of a firm whose competences it does not use or renders obsolete.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 12.

Statement 4 · A new display in the same panel

Replacing the analogue altimeter of a light aircraft with a digital one that fits the same slot of the instrument panel, without changing how the other systems of the aircraft interact, is an architectural innovation.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

A change to one component that does not significantly affect the overall configuration of the system is a component (modular) innovation. An architectural innovation changes the overall design of the system or the way its components interact with each other, which this change does not do.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 13.

Statement 5 · Where the S-curve is steepest

On the S-curve of technology performance, performance improves fastest when the first units of effort are invested in a new technology, and then grows more and more slowly.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Plotted against effort, performance improves slowly at first, then quickly, and finally flattens as it approaches the limit of the technology. The steepest part is in the middle of the curve, not at its beginning.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 14.

Statement 6 · Effort near the limit

When a technology reaches the flat upper part of its S-curve, additional effort brings only small improvements in performance, because the technology is approaching its limit.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The upper part of the S-curve flattens towards the dotted line of the limit of the technology drawn on the slide, so each additional unit of effort yields a smaller gain in performance.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 14.

Statement 7 · A new knowledge base

A discontinuous technology fulfils a similar market need to the existing technology by building on an entirely new knowledge base, and at first its performance may be lower than that of the technology it later overtakes.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

This is the course's definition of a discontinuous technology; the second S-curve of the slide may start below the first technology and later cross it and reach a higher performance.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 15.

Statement 8 · What the diffusion curve plots

The S-curve of technology diffusion plots the performance of a technology against the effort invested in developing it.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

Performance against effort is the S-curve of technology performance. The diffusion S-curve plots the cumulative share of adopters against time (2.5%, 16%, 50%, 84% and 100% at the boundaries of the adopter categories), because diffusion is the spread of a technology through a population.

Source.

Topic 7, Slides 14-16.

Statement 9 · The two majorities

The early majority and the late majority each account for about 34% of the adopters of a technology, so together they make up about two thirds of the market.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: True (T)

The bell-shaped curve of market share gives 34% to the early majority and 34% to the late majority, 68% in total; innovators (2.5%), early adopters (13.5%) and laggards (16%) make up the rest.

Source.

Topic 7, Slide 16.

Statement 10 · How a design becomes dominant

A design becomes dominant only when a regulator or standards body officially enforces it; learning effects and network externalities play no part in its selection.

T / F

Answer and reasoning

Answer: False (F)

A dominant design is a de facto standard: it dominates its product category (usually 50% or more of the market) even if it is not officially enforced. The course gives learning effects and network externalities (installed base and complementary goods) as the reasons why dominant designs are selected.

Source.
