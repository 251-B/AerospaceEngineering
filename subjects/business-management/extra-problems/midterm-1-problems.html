P11 — FRIGOLEV: an incomplete 2017 balance, a cooperative discount and the 2018 statements

The newly appointed financial director of LOGÍSTICA FRIGORÍFICA DEL LEVANTE S.L. (FRIGOLEV), a company located in the port area of Castellón and dedicated to the manufacturing of isothermal containers and cold-chain storage equipment for agricultural exporters, is working on the closing of its financial statements (balance sheet and Income Statement) as of 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018).

The company was originally incorporated on 1 January 2016 (01/01/2016) by four partners, with an initial contribution of each of 1.500 m.u. disbursed fully in cash at that time, and no further capital modifications took place until fiscal year 2018. Due to the sudden departure of the previous chief accountant, the financial director only has an incomplete balance of the company as of 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017), which is included below with certain missing figures marked as (?), along with the audit notes detailing the operations that have taken place during the period from 1 January 2018 (01/01/2018) to 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018):

I. On 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017), the company had as depreciable fixed assets only the industrial refrigeration chambers purchased on a certain date during 2016, which have a useful life of 10 years and are amortized linearly with a residual value of 10% of their acquisition cost. In addition, the balance sheet at 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017) reflected an industrial plot of land acquired in 2016 and financial shares of other companies acquired as a permanent investment.

II. During the year 2018, the company has sold 3.600 units of isothermal containers at a catalog price of 25 m.u. / unit (every month the same amount, 300 u.). The general payment condition agreed with the customers implies that this will occur 60 days after the sale. However, a very important agricultural cooperative (representing 25% of the physical units sold each month), which has bought an equal amount every month, has been given a discount of 8% on the catalog price of their purchases in exchange for paying strictly in cash in the same month of the sale.

III. At 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017) there was a stock (stock in stock) of 400 tons of insulating raw material valued at 2,00 m.u. / ton. On 1 April 2018 (01/04/2018), the company purchased 5.000 tons of raw material at a cost of 2,00 m.u. / ton. The payment condition agreed with the suppliers was 40% paid in cash on the date of purchase, 30% payable to 90 days, and the remaining 30% deferred until 1 February 2019 (01/02/2019). At 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018), after conducting the warehouse count, there remains a final stock (stock in stock) of 900 tons.

IV. During the first quarter of 2018, the unpaid taxes and the outstanding supplier balances from the previous year were paid in full. Regarding the customer debt outstanding from 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017), 75% was charged in cash during the first semester of 2018, except 200 m.u. from a customer who requested and was granted a deferral of payment until 15 March 2019 (15/03/2019), and 100 m.u. corresponding to a client declared definitively insolvent on 1 June 2018 (01/06/2018), which must be recorded as an unrecoverable bad debt loss for the period.

V. Fixed personnel expenses have been 2.200 m.u. / month (to which 600 m.u. / month must be added for Social Security) from 1 January 2018 (01/01/2018). To respond to seasonal peaks in container assembly, production staff also received variable wages that amounted in 2018 to 3.620 m.u. Fixed and variable wages are paid in the month in which they are produced, whereas Social Security contributions (starting from January 2018) are paid on the 15th day of the following month. General expenses and utilities (electricity, phone, insurance, etc.) amounted to 1.100 m.u. / month and are paid two months after being made.

VI. The company carried out a renting contract on 1 March 2018 (01/03/2018) (with a duration of 2 years) with a specialized company for electric warehouse forklifts, for a value of 350 m.u. monthly. The contract establishes a condition of payment to 90 days (payment at the end of each month).

VII. On 1 April 2018 (01/04/2018), the company made a capital increase by issuing 1.000 new shares of 2,00 m.u. nominal value each, fully disbursed in cash and with an issue premium of 50% over the nominal value. On the same date, 1 April 2018 (01/04/2018), it bought two refrigerated delivery vehicles that cost a total of 4.800 m.u., of which it paid 50% in cash, postponing the payment of the remainder in two equal installments: half on 1 October 2018 (01/10/2018) and the other half on 1 April 2019 (01/04/2019). These vehicles will be amortized linearly in 6 years with a total residual value of 480 m.u. Furthermore, on 1 October 2018 (01/10/2018), it bought a software license for cold-chain tracking for a value of 1.600 m.u., payable to 120 days, which is amortized linearly in 4 years with residual value zero.

VIII. The long-term bank loan appearing on the balance sheet at 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017) was granted on 1 January 2017 (01/01/2017) at an annual interest rate of 6%. Each 31 December, the company must pay the accrued annual interest calculated from the loan value at the beginning of each year, and starting on 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018) it must also return to the bank an annual principal amount of 500 m.u. until completing the repayment on 31 December 2021 (31/12/2021). Additionally, on 1 July 2018 (01/07/2018), the company obtained a new short-term bank credit of 1.500 m.u. at 8% annual interest, agreeing to return 50% of the principal each semester for the next year. Both the principal installment and the semiannual interest calculated on the amount actually due in that semester will be paid the day after the end of each semester (i.e., on 1 January 2019 (01/01/2019) and 1 July 2019 (01/07/2019)).

IX. The financial results of the company are declared at 23:59 hrs on 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018). Discussion. The profit is higher with the discount (18.000 against 17.831,25): removing the discount gains $2$ m.u. on each of the 810 units the cooperative still buys ($810 \times 2 = 1.620$ m.u.), but the 90 units it stops buying carried a margin over raw material of $23 - 2{,}50 = 20{,}50$ m.u. each ($90 \times 20{,}50 = 1.845$ m.u.); the net effect before taxes is $1.620 - 1.845 = -225$ m.u. Interpretation and checks

  • Dimensional check: units × m.u./unit and tons × m.u./ton give m.u.; m.u./year × months/12 gives m.u.; ratios are dimensionless.
  • Why ROA and ROE are so high: the company's annual sales (88.200 m.u.) are ten times its 2017 assets; the ratios are measured on the year-end balance, which already includes most of the year's profit.

  • Typical mistakes: depreciating the vehicles for 12 months, forgetting the residual values, treating the issue premium as capital, and putting the whole short-term credit or the 200 m.u. deferred receivable outside current items.

P12 — AGRODRON: the second year of a drone start-up that closed its first year with a loss

The company AGRODRON LEVANTE S.L. was incorporated on 1 January 2017 (01/01/2017) by a group of agricultural engineers with a registered paid-in capital of 4.500 m.u., having an objective of providing precision crop-monitoring services by drone and distributing organic fertilizers in the region of Murcia.

The following information applies to fiscal year 2017:

To finance the operations of the company, on 1 January 2017 (01/01/2017) a bank granted the company a loan of 1.500 m.u. at 6% annual interest, with a duration of 5 years, i.e. until 31 December 2021 (31/12/2021). Each 31 December, the company must return to the bank an equal principal amount of 300 m.u. and should also pay the due annual interest (6%) calculated from the loan value at the beginning of each year.

During 2017, the company invested in a fleet of agricultural drones worth 2.400 m.u. The useful life of the drones purchased was 8 years (amortized linearly, with no residual value) and at the end of 2017 the company recorded in accumulated depreciation an amount of 75 m.u. for those assets. Also, a further investment of 400 m.u. in mapping software licenses was made on 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017) to process aerial images. They use these licenses for 4 years (linear amortization with residual value zero). In addition, the company spent 800 m.u. to purchase a plot of rural land for its flight testing base. Most of 2017 was devoted to obtaining civil aviation permits and only the actual business work was started in the final months of the year. At 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017), its balance sheet reflected a loss of 220 m.u., there was no stock (stock in stock) nor pending customer or supplier balances, and the cash in hand and bank account was 1.955 m.u.

  • The company purchased supplies of organic fertilizers during 2018 which amounted to 500 m.u. Of these purchases, 70% was paid immediately in cash and the payment of the remaining 30% was deferred to the following year. Year 2018 sales consumed 80% of the purchased goods.

Fixed salaries and Social Security costs (contribution of the company to the Social Security) of the permanent employees amounted to 60 m.u. per month (from 1 January 2018 (01/01/2018)) and were paid in full in 2018. To respond to increased demand during the harvest campaign, the company hired external pilots whose variable wages amounted in 2018 to 192 m.u. (paid in cash).

Step 3. Which statement the error distorts

The bank balance is the same: the 1.200 m.u. were paid either way. The error is an accrual error: it charges to 2022 nine months of insurance that belong to 2023 [Slide 8]. The Income Statement of 2022 understates the profit by 630 m.u. (and that of 2023 will overstate it), and the balance sheet omits a resource the company still owns, the cover of January–September 2023. It also understates the tax and the dividend, so the partners receive 189 m.u. less than they should.

Key results
  • Insurance expense 1.200 (+900); profit before taxes 4.420; tax 1.326 (−270); net profit 3.094 (−630).
  • Dividend 928,20 (−189); reserves 3.665,80 (−441).
  • Prepaid insurance disappears (−900); bank unchanged.

Interpretation and checks

  • Dimensional check: m.u. × rate × months/12 gives m.u.; ratios dimensionless.
  • Ledger logic: every closing balance follows from opening + increases − decreases; the customers account alone gives revenue (increase), collections and the bad debt (decreases) and the receivable (closing balance).
  • Profit vs cash: net profit 3.724 m.u., while the bank rose by 5.315 m.u.: $3.724 + 2.100$ (depreciation) $+ 3.000$ (credit line) $- 1.000$ (principal) $- 900$ (prepaid insurance) $- 2.600$ (rise in customers) $- 400$ (rise in parts) $+ 300 + 250 + 796 + 45$ (more owed to suppliers, Social Security, tax and interest) $= 5.315$.
  • Typical mistakes: expensing the whole insurance, charging the credit-line interest on the 4.000 m.u. limit instead of the amount drawn, treating the dividend decided as already paid, and forgetting the bad debt in the customers account.
