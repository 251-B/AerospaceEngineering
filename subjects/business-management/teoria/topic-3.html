Financial Management (I): Financial Statements & Capital Structure

Official Course Sources: Lecture Slides Topic 3 (Prof. Carmen Paz-Aparicio) & Practice Exercises T3 • UC3M Aerospace Engineering

1. Introduction: Economic Structure vs Financial Structure

Corporate financial management manages the fundraising and deployment of capital across two foundational structural dimensions:

Economic Structure (Assets)

"What is owned?" The total investments carried out by the company. It represents the use of funds in terms of goods, productive machinery, inventories, and legal rights.

Objective: Maximize Profitability.

Financial Structure (Liabilities + Equity)

"What is owed?" The totality of financial resources gathered to fund those investments (shareholders' equity, bank debts, bonds, supplier credit).

Objective: Minimize the Cost of Capital.

The Fundamental Value Creation Rule
$$\mathbf{\text{Profitability of Assets} > \text{Cost of Capital}}$$

An enterprise maximizes economic value when the rate of return generated by its productive investments exceeds the aggregate cost of funding them.

2. The Income Statement (Profit & Loss — P&L)

The Income Statement reports business economic performance over a specified fiscal period. It operates strictly on the accrual principle: revenues and expenses are recognized when value is generated or consumed, regardless of when cash is physically collected or paid.

Accounting Step Formula / Mathematical Line Economic Meaning
Gross Sales Revenue $\text{Gross Units Sold} \times \text{Price}$ Total invoice value of goods and services delivered.
(-) Commercial Discounts Discounts to high-volume clients Reductions granted to strategic client tiers.
= Net Sales Revenue $\text{Gross Sales} - \text{Discounts}$ Real commercial revenue generated.
(-) Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) $\text{Initial Stock} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Stock}$ Direct inventory and raw materials consumed in production.
= Gross Profit $\text{Net Sales} - \text{COGS}$ Trading margin before operational overheads.
(-) Operating Expenses $\text{Salaries} + \text{Social Security} + \text{Utilities} + \text{Depreciation}$ General operational, labor, and equipment depreciation costs.
= Operating Income (EBIT) $\text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses}$ Pure operating profit generated by core business activities.
(±) Non-Operating Items $\text{Interest Income} - \text{Interest Expense} \pm \text{Asset Gains}$ Net financial debt servicing and extraordinary asset sales.
= Earnings Before Taxes (EBT) $\text{EBIT} \pm \text{Non-Operating Items}$ Pre-tax taxable profit.
(-) Corporate Income Taxes $t \times \text{EBT} \quad (t = 30\%)$ Fiscal tax liability.
= Net Income (Net Profit) $\text{EBT} - \text{Taxes}$ Final profit available for distribution or reinvestment.

Final Net Income is allocated between Dividends distributed to shareholders and Retained Earnings reinvested to expand equity.

3. The Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet represents an instantaneous financial snapshot of the company at a specific date, adhering to the universal identity:

$$\mathbf{\text{Assets}} \equiv \mathbf{\text{Liabilities}} + \mathbf{\text{Stockholders' Equity}}$$

Assets (Use of Capital)

  • Non-Current Assets ($> 1\text{ yr}$):
    • Intangible Assets: Patents, software.
    • Tangible Fixed Assets: Land (non-depreciable), buildings, machinery, equipment.
    • (-) Accumulated Depreciation: Linear asset wear.
  • Current Assets ($\le 1\text{ yr}$):
    • Inventories: Raw materials, WIP, finished goods.
    • Accounts Receivable: Pending customer invoices.
    • Cash & Bank: Liquid treasury deposits.

Liabilities & Equity (Sources of Funds)

  • Stockholders' Equity (Own Funds):
    • Paid-in Capital: Subscribed shares.
    • (-) Uncalled Capital: Subscribed unpaid shares.
    • Retained Earnings: Cumulative retained profits.
  • Non-Current Liabilities ($> 1\text{ yr}$):
    • Long-term bank loans, corporate bonds.
  • Current Liabilities ($\le 1\text{ yr}$):
    • Accounts payable (suppliers), short-term bank debt.
Linear (Straight-Line) Depreciation Formulation
$$\text{Annual Depreciation Expense } (D) = \frac{\text{Acquisition Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Useful Lifespan in Years } (n)}$$ $$\text{Net Book Value (NBV)} = \text{Acquisition Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}$$

4. Corporate Financing Instruments

Category Instrument Key Economic Mechanism
Equity (Own Funds) Internal Equity (Self-Financing) Retained earnings generated organically. Measured by $\text{Retention Ratio} = 1 - \text{Pay-out}$.
External Equity (Shares / Stocks) Fractional ownership stakes. No fixed maturity; dividends are non-compulsory; junior claim in liquidation.
Long-Term Debt ($> 1\text{ yr}$) Corporate Bonds Debt securities issued on capital markets. Firm pays periodic coupons and returns principal at maturity.
Bank Loans vs Credit Lines Loans disburse a fixed lump-sum with interest on the total balance; credit lines allow revolving draws with interest paid only on drawn amounts.
Leasing (Finance vs Operating) Asset rental with a purchase option. Finance leases transfer all ownership risks; operating leases are short-term service rentals.
Short-Term Debt ($\le 1\text{ yr}$) Trade Credit (Accounts Payable) Suppliers allow deferred payment (30–90 days) without explicit loan paperwork.
Commercial Bill Discount Endorsing promissory notes to banks before maturity; the bank deducts interest and fees upfront.
Factoring Selling accounts receivable portfolios to a financial factor who assumes administration and default risk.

5. Financial Leverage & Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

Financial Structure Metrics

Let $E$ denote Total Equity and $L$ denote Total Liabilities (Total Debt):

$$\% \text{ Equity} = \frac{E}{E + L}, \qquad \% \text{ Debt} = \frac{L}{E + L}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Debt-to-Equity Ratio (Financial Leverage)}} = \frac{L}{E}$$

A higher $L/E$ ratio magnifies return on equity during expansions but heightens default risk during downturns.

Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

Capital providers demand compensation for their funds: an explicit contractual interest rate on debt ($K_L$) and an implicit opportunity cost on equity ($K_{EQ}$):

Standard Course Formula (Slide 37)
$$\mathbf{K_{WACC} = K_{EQ} \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E + L}\right) + K_L \cdot \left(\frac{L}{E + L}\right)}$$

With tax-deductible interest in extended corporate finance:

$$\mathbf{K_{WACC} = K_{EQ} \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E + L}\right) + K_L(1 - t) \cdot \left(\frac{L}{E + L}\right)}$$

The WACC serves as the universal benchmark hurdle rate: capital expenditure projects create value if and only if $\text{Project Return} > K_{WACC}$.
