Problem 1

Problem 1 — Capitalization of a deferred debt

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf

One person, Mr. Smith, is able to invest his available liquid capital at a 5 percent rate per year. Currently, another person, Mr. Martinez, must pay Mr. Smith the amount of USD 1,000, but he is going through a period of scarce liquidity and asks him to wait. How much money will Mr. Smith require if it is delayed a year? What if it is a two-year delay? What if it were three years of deferment?

Data and model

SymbolMeaningValue
$C_0$debt today1,000
$i$rate at which Mr. Smith can invest5% per year
$n$years of delay1, 2, 3

Mr. Smith must receive the amount that, invested at 5%, equals having the 1,000 today: the future (capitalized) value with compound interest,

$$C_n = C_0\,(1 + i)^n$$

This is the capitalization rule used in the key; the course decks on disk do not state it.

Solution

Step 1. One, two and three years

$$\begin{aligned} C_1 &= 1{,}000 \times 1.05 = 1{,}050 \\ C_2 &= 1{,}000 \times 1.05^2 = 1{,}000 \times 1.1025 = 1{,}102.50 \\ C_3 &= 1{,}000 \times 1.05^3 = 1{,}000 \times 1.157625 = 1{,}157.63 \end{aligned}$$

Each year the interest is earned on the previous year's balance, so the yearly increase grows: 50, then 52.50, then 55.13.

Checks and official key

  • Check: $C_3 = C_2 \times 1.05 = 1{,}102.50 \times 1.05 = 1{,}157.63$. ✓
  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, p. 1): 1,050, 1,102.5 and 1,157.62. Same results ($1{,}157.625$ is truncated to 1,157.62 in the key and rounded to 1,157.63 here).
Problem 2

Problem 2 — Alfa, S.A.: new debt with a constant financial structure

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf

In year 0 the company Alfa S.A. has the following financial structure: capital 6,000,000; reserves 4,800,000; debt 7,200,000; total 18,000,000. If this company can retain profits of 1,200,000 euros at the end of year 1, what is its ability to obtain new debt in year 1 (how much more can it borrow in year 1 compared to year 0) without changing its financial structure?

Data and model

ItemYear 0 (€)
Capital6,000,000
Reserves4,800,000
Debt7,200,000
Total18,000,000
Retained profit of year 11,200,000

Equity is capital plus reserves, and the financial structure is the share of equity and of debt in the total [Slides 33-35 of Topic 3]. Retained profit increases the reserves, so equity grows; keeping the structure means keeping those shares.

Solution

Step 1. Structure in year 0

$$E_0 = 6{,}000{,}000 + 4{,}800{,}000 = 10{,}800{,}000 \qquad \frac{E_0}{18{,}000{,}000} = 0.60 \qquad \frac{D_0}{18{,}000{,}000} = \frac{7{,}200{,}000}{18{,}000{,}000} = 0.40$$

Step 2. Equity and total in year 1

$$E_1 = 10{,}800{,}000 + 1{,}200{,}000 = 12{,}000{,}000$$

Equity must still be 60% of the total:

$$0.60 = \frac{12{,}000{,}000}{T_1} \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_1 = 20{,}000{,}000$$

Step 3. Debt in year 1 and new debt

$$D_1 = 0.40 \times 20{,}000{,}000 = 8{,}000{,}000 \qquad \text{New debt} = D_1 - D_0 = 8{,}000{,}000 - 7{,}200{,}000 = 800{,}000 \ \text{€}$$

Checks and official key

  • Check of the ratio: debt-to-equity stays $7{,}200{,}000/10{,}800{,}000 = 8{,}000{,}000/12{,}000{,}000 = 0.667$. ✓
  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, pp. 1-2): equity 60%, debt 40%, total 20,000,000, debt 8,000,000 and new debt 800,000 €. Same results.

Solution

Step 1. Cumulative cash flows

Step 2. Payback periods

The key's figure is what an outlay of 1,200 would give: $5 + (1{,}200 - 1{,}000)/250 = 5.8$ years, about 5 years and 10 months. With the stated data X and Z tie, and the key's preference for X is not supported by payback alone.

Problem 4

Problem 4 — Maximum investment from the IRR

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf

What is the maximum amount we should invest in a project at time 0 if the estimated annual cash inflow is €30,000 over a period of 4 years starting from year 1? Assume that the estimated internal rate of return is 8%.

Data and model

Cash inflows $CF_1 = \dots = CF_4 = 30{,}000$ €; IRR 8%. The net present value of a project with outlay $I_0$ is

$$NPV = -I_0 + \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + k)^t}$$

and the internal rate of return is the rate that makes $NPV = 0$. The maximum outlay is the one that leaves the NPV at zero when discounted at 8%: any higher outlay would give a return below 8%.

Solution

Step 1. NPV equal to zero at 8%

$$0 = -I_0 + \frac{30{,}000}{1.08} + \frac{30{,}000}{1.08^2} + \frac{30{,}000}{1.08^3} + \frac{30{,}000}{1.08^4}$$

Step 2. Each discounted flow

$$\frac{30{,}000}{1.08} = 27{,}777.78 \qquad \frac{30{,}000}{1.1664} = 25{,}720.16 \qquad \frac{30{,}000}{1.259712} = 23{,}814.96 \qquad \frac{30{,}000}{1.36048896} = 22{,}050.90$$

Step 3. Maximum outlay

$$I_0 = 27{,}777.78 + 25{,}720.16 + 23{,}814.96 + 22{,}050.90 = 99{,}363.81 \ \text{€}$$

Checks and official key

  • Check with the annuity factor: $30{,}000 \times \dfrac{1 - 1.08^{-4}}{0.08} = 30{,}000 \times 3.312127 = 99{,}363.81$ €. ✓
  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, pp. 2-3): $I_0 = 99{,}363.81$ €. Same result.
Problem 5

Problem 5 — NPV and payback of three projects

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf

Consider these projects (cash flows in euros) and answer the questions:

ProjectC0C1C2C3C4C5
A−1,000+1,0000000
B−2,000+1,000+1,000+4,000+1,000+1,000
C−3,000+1,000+1,0000+1,000+1,000

a) If the interest rate is 10%, which projects have a positive NPV? b) Calculate the payback period for each project. c) Which project(s) would be accepted by a company that uses the payback period model if the maximum period is three years?

Data and model

$NPV = \sum_{t=0}^{5} C_t/(1.10)^t$ with the discount factors $1.1^{-1} = 0.909091$, $1.1^{-2} = 0.826446$, $1.1^{-3} = 0.751315$, $1.1^{-4} = 0.683013$, $1.1^{-5} = 0.620921$. Payback: the time the cumulative flows take to recover the outlay.

Solution

Step 1. Part (a): NPV at 10%

$$NPV_A = -1{,}000 + 1{,}000 \times 0.909091 = -90.91 \ \text{€}$$
$$\begin{aligned} NPV_B &= -2{,}000 + 909.09 + 826.45 + 4{,}000 \times 0.751315 + 683.01 + 620.92 \\ &= -2{,}000 + 909.09 + 826.45 + 3{,}005.26 + 683.01 + 620.92 = 4{,}044.73 \ \text{€} \end{aligned}$$
$$NPV_C = -3{,}000 + 909.09 + 826.45 + 0 + 683.01 + 620.92 = 39.47 \ \text{€}$$

B and C have a positive NPV; A does not.

Step 2. Part (b): payback

YearA cumulativeB cumulativeC cumulative
11,0001,0001,000
21,0002,0002,000
31,0006,0002,000
41,0007,0003,000

A recovers its 1,000 in 1 year, B its 2,000 in 2 years and C its 3,000 in 4 years.

Step 3. Part (c): maximum payback of three years

A and B are accepted, C is rejected. The payback model accepts A although its NPV is negative and rejects C although its NPV is positive, because it ignores the time value of money and the flows after the cut-off. Between the two accepted projects, the NPV favours B.

Checks and official key

  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, p. 3): $NPV_A = -90.91$, $NPV_B = 4{,}044.7$, $NPV_C = 39.47$; payback 1, 2 and 4 years; A and B accepted, and B chosen by NPV. Same results.
Problem 6

Problem 6 — NPV from a known IRR

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf, p. 2

Assuming an interest rate of 7%, consider a three-year investment period generating a positive cash flow of €10,000 per year with an internal rate of return of 10%. Calculate the NPV of this investment.

Data and model

$CF_1 = CF_2 = CF_3 = 10{,}000$ €, IRR 10%, discount rate $k = 7\%$. The outlay $I_0$ is not given: it follows from the IRR, the rate at which the NPV is zero. Then the NPV is computed at 7%.

Solution

Step 1. Outlay from the IRR

$$0 = -I_0 + \frac{10{,}000}{1.1} + \frac{10{,}000}{1.1^2} + \frac{10{,}000}{1.1^3} = -I_0 + 9{,}090.91 + 8{,}264.46 + 7{,}513.15$$
$$I_0 = 24{,}868.52 \ \text{€}$$

Step 2. NPV at 7%

$$NPV = -24{,}868.52 + \frac{10{,}000}{1.07} + \frac{10{,}000}{1.1449} + \frac{10{,}000}{1.225043} = -24{,}868.52 + 9{,}345.79 + 8{,}734.39 + 8{,}162.98 = 1{,}374.64 \ \text{€}$$

The NPV is positive because the discount rate (7%) is below the IRR (10%).

Checks and official key

  • Check: at $k = $ IRR the NPV would be zero; a lower rate gives a positive NPV, as found.
  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, pp. 3-4): $I_0 = 24{,}868$ € and $NPV = 1{,}375$ €. Same results. The key also adds a part (b), not in the statement: at $k = 9\%$ the NPV is smaller but still positive, since $9\% < 10\%$; its value is $1{,}374.64 - 930.21 = 444.43$ €.

Problem 8

Problem 8 — GOFRES, S.A.: return against the cost of capital

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf, p. 2

The director of the financial department of GOFRES, S.A. raises the possibility of investing in a project with a profitability of 8.5%. GOFRES, S.A. is funded 30% with equity capital, whose cost is estimated at 15%, and the rest with external funds, with an average cost of 8%. What would be your advice to this executive: to carry out the project or not to invest, and why? How does your answer change if you were offered another investment project with a yield of 18.5%?

Data and model

SourceShareCost
Equity30%15%
Liabilities (external funds)70%8%

The cost of capital is the weighted average cost, $K_{WACC} = K_{EQ}\,\frac{E}{E+L} + K_L\,\frac{L}{E+L}$ [Slide 37 of Topic 3]. A project creates value when its return is above that cost (profitability > cost, Slide 5 of Topic 3).

Solution

Step 1. Cost of capital

$$K = 0.30 \times 0.15 + 0.70 \times 0.08 = 0.045 + 0.056 = 0.101 = 10.1\%$$

Step 2. Project with 8.5%

$8.5\% < 10.1\%$: the project returns less than the funds cost. Advice: do not invest.

Step 3. Project with 18.5%

$18.5\% > 10.1\%$: the project returns more than the funds cost. Advice: invest.

Checks and official key

  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, p. 5): $K = 0.3 \times 0.15 + 0.7 \times 0.08 = 10.1\%$; reject at 8.5%, accept at 18.5%. Same results.
Problem 9

Problem 9 — OSOPANDA, S.L.: payback, IRR and the Fisher rate

Original statement · 3_II_NPV IRR.pdf, p. 2

Company OSOPANDA, S.L. is considering an investment, for which it has two offers. The first offer (Project A) has an initial payment of €16,653 and generates cash flows of €6,500 and €13,000 during the first and second years (two-year period), respectively. The second offer (Project B) means a much higher initial payment, €71,428. It will end in a year, and it is expected to generate a positive cash flow of €76,428 by the end of that year. Answer the following questions and account for your answers:

a) Which project would the company choose if it uses the payback period model?

b) Analyze both alternatives in terms of internal rate of return (IRR).

Data and model

Project$I_0$ (€)$CF_1$ (€)$CF_2$ (€)
A16,6536,50013,000
B71,42876,428—

Payback with flows spread evenly over each year (30-day months). IRR: the rate that makes $NPV = 0$. The Fisher rate is the discount rate at which both projects have the same NPV.

Solution

Step 1. Part (a): payback

A recovers 6,500 in year 1; the remaining $16{,}653 - 6{,}500 = 10{,}153$ € come from the 13,000 of year 2 after $10{,}153/13{,}000 = 0.781$ years $= 9.37$ months: 1 year, 9 months and 11 days (0.37 × 30 days), recovered during the 12th day.

B recovers 71,428 out of 76,428 after $71{,}428/76{,}428 = 0.9346$ years $= 11.21$ months: 11 months and 6 days (0.21 × 30), recovered during the 7th day.

B has the shorter payback, so the payback model chooses B.

Step 2. Part (b): IRR of B

$$0 = -71{,}428 + \frac{76{,}428}{1 + r_B} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 1 + r_B = \frac{76{,}428}{71{,}428} = 1.0700 \quad\Rightarrow\quad r_B = 7.00\%$$

Step 3. Part (b): IRR of A

$$0 = -16{,}653 + \frac{6{,}500}{1 + r_A} + \frac{13{,}000}{(1 + r_A)^2}$$

With $x = 1 + r_A$ and multiplying by $x^2$: $16{,}653\,x^2 - 6{,}500\,x - 13{,}000 = 0$, so

$$x = \frac{6{,}500 + \sqrt{6{,}500^2 + 4 \times 16{,}653 \times 13{,}000}}{2 \times 16{,}653} = \frac{6{,}500 + \sqrt{908{,}206{,}000}}{33{,}306} = \frac{6{,}500 + 30{,}136.46}{33{,}306} = 1.1000$$

$r_A = 10.00\%$ (the negative root has no economic meaning). By IRR, A is better (10% > 7%).

Step 4. Fisher rate and the decision by the cost of capital

The two criteria disagree because the projects differ in size and duration. Setting $NPV_A(i) = NPV_B(i)$,

$$-16{,}653 + \frac{6{,}500}{1 + i} + \frac{13{,}000}{(1 + i)^2} = -71{,}428 + \frac{76{,}428}{1 + i}$$

whose root between 0 and 10% is $i \approx 5.08\%$. At $i = 0$, $NPV_A = 2{,}847$ € and $NPV_B = 5{,}000$ € (B better); at $i = 8\%$, $NPV_A = 510.92$ € and $NPV_B = -661.33$ € (A better). So:

  • cost of capital below about 5.08%: choose B (higher NPV);
  • between 5.08% and 10%: choose A;
  • above 10%: neither project has a positive NPV.

Checks and official key

  • Check of $r_A$: $6{,}500/1.1 + 13{,}000/1.21 = 5{,}909.09 + 10{,}743.80 = 16{,}652.89 \approx 16{,}653$. ✓
  • Check against the official key (3_II_NPV_NPV IRR_solutions.pdf, p. 6): payback A "1 year, 9 months and days (12...)" and B "11 months and 7 days", B preferred; IRR(A) ≈ 10%, IRR(B) ≈ 7%; Fisher rate ≈ 5% (0.0507); B below 5%, A between 5% and 10%, neither above 10%. Same results; the key counts the day in which the outlay is recovered (12th and 7th), which is why it gives one day more than the completed days above.
