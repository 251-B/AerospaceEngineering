Method used in every solution

Central idea: every operation of an exam problem is read three times: does it create a revenue or expense of the period (income statement)? does cash enter or leave before the closing date (cash account)? what asset, liability or equity item remains open at the closing date (balance sheet)? If the three readings are done for every operation, the balance sheet closes by itself.

The full step-by-step method (classification table, depreciation and interest rules, cash account and balance-sheet layout) is at the top of the exam-type problems page. Each solution below follows the same four phases: setup, frames (period and classification of every operation), derivation, interpretation.

Exercise 1

Exercise 1 — Revenues vs collections, expenses vs payments, and depreciation

Original statement · T3_Financial Statements_EXERCISES.pdf

A company started activity on 1 January of the present year. At 31 March it has the following goods, acquired on 31 December of the previous year:

  • factory building 1,000 m.u., duration 25 years;
  • office building 1,500 m.u., duration 50 years;
  • machinery 300 m.u., duration 10 years;
  • transport equipment 100 m.u., duration 5 years.

Information for the first quarter:

  • personnel salaries of the quarter 450 m.u., of which 75 m.u. are pending to be paid after 31/3;
  • materials purchased and used in production 700 m.u., of which 125 m.u. are pending to be paid after 31/3;
  • energy consumption estimated at 230 m.u., nothing paid to date;
  • sales invoiced to customers 1,930 m.u., of which 315 m.u. had not been collected at 31/3; the rest was collected in due time. In manufacturing all the units sold, all the purchased materials were consumed.

Ignore corporation taxes.

a) At 31/3, find the income, expenses, collections (cash inflows), payments (cash outflows), investments and depreciation of the quarter (linear depreciation along the lifetime of the goods).

b) How does the answer change if only half of the purchased materials had been consumed in manufacturing the units sold?

Phase 1 — Setup: data and hypotheses

Period: first quarter (1/01 to 31/03), $\frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$ of a year. The fixed assets were bought on 31/12 of the previous year, so they are used during the whole quarter.

The exercise trains the distinction of [Slide 8]: a revenue is recorded when the sale is made, whether or not it is collected; an expense when the good or service is received or consumed, whether or not it is paid. Collections and payments are the cash side of the same operations.

Step 2. Part (a)

  • Income (revenues): the sales invoiced, 1,930 m.u.
  • Expenses: $450 + 700 + 230 + 30 = $ 1,410 m.u. (so the profit of the quarter is $1{,}930 - 1{,}410 = 520$ m.u.).
  • Collections: $1{,}930 - 315 = $ 1,615 m.u.
  • Payments: $(450 - 75) + (700 - 125) + 0 = 375 + 575 = $ 950 m.u. Depreciation is not a payment.
  • Investments of the quarter: 0 m.u. The fixed assets (total $1{,}000 + 1{,}500 + 300 + 100 = 2{,}900$ m.u.) were acquired on 31/12 of the previous year, before the quarter began. If the question is read as "the investment the company works with", the answer is that 2,900 m.u.; state the reading you use in the exam.
  • Depreciation: 30 m.u.

Step 3. Part (b): only half of the materials consumed

Only the material consumed is an expense [Slide 8, Slide 10]: $700 / 2 = 350$ m.u. The other 350 m.u. stay in the warehouse as inventory, a current asset [Slide 14].

Phase 4 — Interpretation and checks

  • Profit is not cash: in (a) the profit is 520 while the net cash generated is $1{,}615 - 950 = 665$. The difference, $665 - 520 = 145$, is explained item by item: $+30$ (depreciation, an expense without payment) $+75 + 125 + 230$ (expenses not yet paid) $- 315$ (revenue not yet collected) $= 145$.
  • Part (b) raises the profit by 350 without any extra cash: unconsumed material is an asset, not an expense.
  • Check of depreciation: the annual total 120 times $\frac{1}{4}$ gives 30.

Exercise 2 — JUGA S.A., opening balance sheet

Original statement · T3_Financial Statements_EXERCISES.pdf

The JUGA S.A. firm is started with a shareholders' contribution of 60,000 €. Of those, 30,000 € are paid in and deposited in a bank account, and the remaining 30,000 € correspond to the transfer of a patent. With the cash of the bank account, raw materials are purchased for 10,500 € and machinery for 15,000 €. Detail the balance sheet at the start of operations.

Phase 1 — Setup: data and hypotheses

  • The capital contributed is 60,000 €: 30,000 € in money and 30,000 € in kind (a patent).
  • The purchases are paid with the bank account (no debt is created).
  • It is the start of operations: nothing has been produced or sold yet, so there are no revenues or expenses and no income statement.

Phase 2 — Frames: effect of each operation on the balance sheet

OperationAssetsLiabilitiesEquity

Patent contributedPatent +30,000Capital +30,000
Raw materials boughtRaw materials +10,500; bank −10,500
Machinery boughtMachinery +15,000; bank −15,000

Each row keeps Assets = Liabilities + Equity [Slide 12]: the contributions increase both sides; the purchases only change the composition of the assets.

Phase 3 — Derivation

Step 1. Bank balance

$$\text{Bank} = 30{,}000 - 10{,}500 - 15{,}000 = 4{,}500 \text{ €}$$

Step 2. Classification

  • Patent: non-current asset [Slide 15].
  • Machinery: non-current asset (equipment) [Slide 15].
  • Raw materials: inventory, a current asset [Slide 14]; not consumed yet, so not an expense.
  • Bank: cash, a current asset [Slide 14].
  • Share capital: contributed capital [Slide 17].

Step 3. Balance sheet at the start of operations

Machinery15,000Equity60,000

Phase 4 — Interpretation and checks

  • Balance: $45{,}000 + 15{,}000 = 60{,}000 = 60{,}000 + 0$.
  • Typical mistake: keeping the bank at 30,000 € while also adding the machinery and the raw materials gives assets of 85,000 € against 60,000 € of equity. Spending cash on an asset never changes the total.
  • Financial structure [Slide 36]: the company is financed 100% with equity ($L/E = 0$).

  • Typical mistakes: retained earnings are equity, not a liability; accounts receivable (owed to the company) are an asset, accounts payable (owed by the company) a liability.

The statement mixes the three kinds of income-statement lines of [Slide 9]: items of the primary operation (sales, goods, wages, rent, utilities), non-operating items (interests, the sale of property) [Slide 11], and the tax. The goods sold are those available (initial inventory + purchases) minus those still in stock at the end:

$$\text{COGS} = 600 + 84{,}000 - 500 = 84{,}100 \text{ €}$$

The inventory fell by $600 - 500 = 100$ €: those 100 € of goods were also sold, so they add to the purchases.

Exercise 5 — Income statement 2017 with discounts, payroll, depreciation, taxes and dividends

Original statement · T3_Financial Statements_EXERCISES.pdf, p. 2

Prepare the income statement of a company with the following information for 2017:

  • Gross sales during the year reached 2,002 million €. The company's trade policy gives a 3% discount to its Type A customers, those whose annual purchases from the company exceed 120 million €. Type A customers account for 40% of sales. Assume total sales = gross sales − discount.
  • Purchases of raw materials amounted to 962 million €.
  • The workforce consists of 7,800 employees. The average salary is 30,000 € per year, and the average spending on Social Security is 30% of the salary.
  • Payments for other operating expenses amounted to 457 million €.
  • In recent years the company has invested a total of 1,200 million € in fixed assets with an average lifespan of 10 years (linear depreciation, no residual value).
  • The company obtained an international loan of 225 million € in 2015, at 6% interest along the whole year; the whole principal will be repaid in 2020.
  • The corporate tax rate is 30%. The company has agreed to a dividend of 50%.
  • Assume no change in stocks of raw materials or finished products during the year.

Phase 1 — Setup: units and hypotheses

All amounts are expressed in millions of euros (M€). Salaries are given per employee in €, so they must be converted: $1 \text{ M€} = 10^6$ €.

Hypotheses:

  1. "No change in stocks" means initial inventory = final inventory, so $\text{COGS} = \text{Purchases}$.
  2. The 120 M€ threshold only defines who is a Type A customer; the statement already says these customers make 40% of the sales, so the discount applies to that 40%.
  3. The loan principal is not an expense: only its interest enters the income statement [Slide 11].
  4. The dividend of 50% is applied to the net income [Slide 23].

Step 1. Net sales

$$\text{Net sales} = 2{,}002 - 24.024 = 1{,}977.976 \text{ M€}$$

Step 2. Personnel expense

$$\text{Salaries} = 7{,}800 \times 30{,}000 = 234{,}000{,}000 \text{ €} = 234 \text{ M€}$$
$$\text{Social Security} = 0.30 \times 234 = 70.2 \text{ M€}, \qquad \text{Personnel} = 234 + 70.2 = 304.2 \text{ M€}$$

Step 3. Depreciation and interest

$$D = \frac{1{,}200 - 0}{10} = 120 \text{ M€/year}, \qquad I = 225 \times 0.06 = 13.5 \text{ M€}$$

  • Applying the discount to all the sales would deduct $0.03 \times 2{,}002 = 60.06$ M€ instead of 24.024: the 40% restriction matters.
  • The loan repayment in 2020 does not appear in 2017: repaying principal is a payment that is never an expense.
  • Exact values: income taxes 36.3828 M€ and net income 84.8932 M€ (shown rounded to three decimals in the table).
  • Dividends come after taxes and are not an expense: the income statement ends at the net income, and the 50/50 split is shown separately.

Exercise 6 — Company X, first-year balance sheet and partly paid capital

Original statement · T3_Financial Statements_EXERCISES.pdf, p. 3

Company X was incorporated on 1/01/2017 with a subscription of shares worth 100 m.u., fully paid in cash. On 1/01/2017 it bought, with cash: a) an area (land) for 20 m.u.; b) equipment with a life of 10 years (straight-line depreciation) and a purchase price of 20 m.u.; c) raw materials for 20 m.u.

The company hires an employee for a total annual cost (salaries, Social Security, etc.) of 10 m.u. per year. This employee processes half of the raw material and sells it for a total of 50 m.u. Sales are the same every day of the year. The average collection period is 3 months.

The single tax rate on the profits of the company is 30% (paid on 31/12/2017). The company follows a strict self-financing strategy.

  1. Prepare the balance sheet at 31/12/2017, justifying and explaining the intermediate calculations.
  2. Explain how the accounting statements would change if, instead of 100%, the shareholders had paid only 90% of the 100 m.u. subscribed.

Phase 1 — Setup: data and hypotheses

Period: calendar year 2017. Closing: 31/12/2017.

Hypotheses:

  1. The employee's cost is paid during the year.
  2. Land has no useful life given and is not depreciated; the equipment has no residual value given (zero).
  3. Strict self-financing [Slide 23]: the whole net income is retained (pay-out 0%).
  4. The tax is paid on 31/12, so no tax liability remains.

Step 2. Receivables from the collection period

Sales are spread evenly over the year and customers pay 3 months after buying. At 31/12 the sales of the last 3 months are still unpaid:

$$\text{Receivables} = 50 \times \frac{3}{12} = 12.5 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \text{Collected} = 50 - 12.5 = 37.5 \text{ m.u.}$$

Phase 3b — Derivation (requirement 2: only 90% paid)

Step 6. What changes

The shareholders subscribed 100 m.u. but paid in only $0.90 \times 100 = 90$ m.u. The remaining 10 m.u. is capital not yet paid (uncalled capital): the shareholders still owe it to the company.

  • Income statement: no change. Revenues and expenses do not depend on how the capital was paid; there is no interest involved. Net income stays 19.6.
  • Cash: 10 m.u. less: $59.1 - 10 = 49.1$ m.u. All the purchases (60) can still be paid in cash, since the company receives 90.
  • Equity: the contributed capital is what the stockholders actually provided [Slide 13, Slide 17], so the subscribed capital is shown minus the unpaid part: $100 - 10 = 90$.

Phase 4 — Interpretation and checks

  • Balance: $38 + 81.6 = 119.6 = 100 + 19.6$ (requirement 1); $38 + 71.6 = 109.6 = 90 + 19.6$ (requirement 2).
  • Equity bridge: equity = capital paid + net income retained: $100 + 19.6$ and $90 + 19.6$.
  • Profit vs cash: after the purchases of 1/01, the cash generated during the year is $37.5 - 10 - 8.4 = 19.1$, while the profit is 19.6. Reconciliation: $19.6 + 2 + 10 - 12.5 = 19.1$, that is, profit + depreciation (expense, never a payment) + materials consumed (expense whose payment was made on 1/01 with the purchase) − sales not collected yet.
  • Presentation note: some textbooks show the 10 m.u. owed by shareholders as an asset instead of deducting it from capital; then both totals stay at 119.6. The deduction used here is the one consistent with "contributed capital" in [Slide 17]. Both readings give the same net equity provided (109.6) and leave the income statement unchanged.
