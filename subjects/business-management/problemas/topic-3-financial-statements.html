Exercise 1

Accrual vs Cash Basis, Linear Depreciation & Inventory Sensitivity

A specific company has started activity on the 1st of January of the present year. To this date (31st of March), it has the following goods that were acquired on 31st of December of the previous year:
• Factory building: 1.000 m.u. (Duration 25 years)
• Office building: 1.500 m.u. (Duration 50 years)
• Machinery: 300 m.u. (Duration 10 years)
• Transport equipment: 100 m.u. (Duration 5 years)
Information corresponding to the 1st quarter is as follows: personnel salaries of the quarter 450 m.u. (75 m.u. pending after 31/3), materials purchases and used in production 700 m.u. (125 m.u. pending after 31/3), energy consumption estimated in 230 m.u. (nothing paid till date), sales invoiced to customers 1.930 m.u. (315 m.u. pending after 31/3). In manufacturing all sold units, the entire purchased materials have been consumed. Ignore corporation taxes.
a) Calculate at 31/3: income, expenses, collections/cash inflows, payments/cash outflows, investments and depreciation/amortization of the quarter.
b) How will the response change if only half of the purchased materials had been consumed?

Phase 1: Quarterly Linear Depreciation Computation

The time elapsed is the 1st Quarter ($t = 3 \text{ months} = \frac{1}{4} \text{ year}$). The straight-line quarterly depreciation is:

$$D_{\text{quarter}} = \frac{\text{Acquisition Cost}}{4 \times n}$$
  • Factory building: $\frac{1,000}{4 \times 25} = \frac{1,000}{100} = 10.0 \text{ m.u.}$
  • Office building: $\frac{1,500}{4 \times 50} = \frac{1,500}{200} = 7.5 \text{ m.u.}$
  • Machinery: $\frac{300}{4 \times 10} = \frac{300}{40} = 7.5 \text{ m.u.}$
  • Transport equipment: $\frac{100}{4 \times 5} = \frac{100}{20} = 5.0 \text{ m.u.}$
  • Total Depreciation for the Quarter: $10.0 + 7.5 + 7.5 + 5.0 = \mathbf{30.0 \text{ m.u.}}$

Phase 2: Resolution of Part (a) — 100% Consumed

  1. Income (Revenues): Under the accrual principle, revenue is recognized upon commercial invoicing regardless of collection: $$\mathbf{\text{Income}} = \mathbf{1,930.0 \text{ m.u.}}$$
  2. Expenses: Consumed factor inputs plus equipment depreciation: $$\mathbf{\text{Expenses}} = 450 \text{ (Salaries)} + 700 \text{ (Materials)} + 230 \text{ (Energy)} + 30 \text{ (Depreciation)} = \mathbf{1,410.0 \text{ m.u.}}$$ *(Net Income $= 1,930 - 1,410 = 520.0 \text{ m.u.}$).*
  3. Collections (Cash Inflows): Physical cash collected from customers during the quarter: $$\mathbf{\text{Collections}} = 1,930 - 315 \text{ (uncollected)} = \mathbf{1,615.0 \text{ m.u.}}$$
  4. Payments (Cash Outflows): Physical disbursements from treasury: $$\mathbf{\text{Payments}} = (450 - 75) + (700 - 125) + 0 \text{ (Energy)} = 375 + 575 = \mathbf{950.0 \text{ m.u.}}$$ *(Depreciation is a non-cash expense and requires no payment).*
  5. Investments: Total capital goods acquired on 31/12 were $1,000 + 1,500 + 300 + 100 = 2,900.0 \text{ m.u.}$ No new fixed asset investments were made during Q1 ($\text{Q1 Capex} = 0 \text{ m.u.}$).
  6. Depreciation: $\mathbf{30.0 \text{ m.u.}}$

Phase 3: Resolution of Part (b) — 50% Consumed

If only half of the materials are consumed in production ($700 / 2 = 350 \text{ m.u.}$), the remaining $350 \text{ m.u.}$ is stored in warehouse as an inventory asset:

  • Expenses: Decrease by $350 \text{ m.u.}$: $$\mathbf{\text{New Expenses}} = 450 + 350 + 230 + 30 = \mathbf{1,060.0 \text{ m.u.}} \quad (\text{Net Profit rises to } 1,930 - 1,060 = \mathbf{870.0 \text{ m.u.}})$$
  • Payments (Cash Outflows): Completely unchanged at $950.0 \text{ m.u.}$! Supplier payments are governed by raw materials purchased ($700 - 125 = 575 \text{ m.u.}$), regardless of physical shop-floor consumption.
  • Incomes and Collections: Unchanged at $1,930.0 \text{ m.u.}$ and $1,615.0 \text{ m.u.}$ respectively.
Pedagogical Conclusion
Unconsumed purchased materials represent a Balance Sheet Asset (Inventory), not an Income Statement expense. While Net Income increases by $350 \text{ m.u.}$, treasury cash flow remains identical.
Exercise 2

JUGA S.A. Opening Balance Sheet

The JUGA S.A. Firm is started with a shareholders contribution of 60.000 Euros. Of those 30.000 are paid in and deposited in a Bank account and the remaining are corresponding to the transfer of a patent for a value of 30.000 Euros. With the cash of the Bank account, raw materials have been purchased for a total amount of 10.500 Euros and machinery for 15.000 Euros. Please detail the balance sheet at the operations start.

Phase 1: Journaling Cash Flows & Balances

  1. Initial capital contributed: Subscribed Share Capital = €60,000 (Monetary: €30,000 cash; In-kind: €30,000 industrial patent).
  2. Purchases funded by bank cash: $$\text{Cash Outflows} = 10,500 \text{ (Raw Materials)} + 15,000 \text{ (Machinery)} = 25,500 \text{ \euro}$$
  3. Residual bank cash balance: $$\text{Bank Balance} = 30,000 - 25,500 = \mathbf{4,500 \text{ \euro}}$$

Phase 2: The Formal Opening Balance Sheet

ASSETS (Activo) Amount (€) LIABILITIES & EQUITY (Pasivo y PN) Amount (€)
NON-CURRENT ASSETS 45,000 STOCKHOLDERS' EQUITY 60,000
• Intangible Assets: Industrial Patent 30,000 • Subscribed Share Capital 60,000
• Tangible Fixed Assets: Machinery 15,000
CURRENT ASSETS 15,000 NON-CURRENT LIABILITIES 0
• Inventories: Raw Materials 10,500 CURRENT LIABILITIES 0
• Cash & Cash Equivalents (Bank account) 4,500
TOTAL ASSETS 60,000 TOTAL LIABILITIES & EQUITY 60,000
Equilibrium Verification
$$\mathbf{\text{Assets } (60,000 \text{ \euro}) \equiv \text{Liabilities } (0 \text{ \euro}) + \text{Equity } (60,000 \text{ \euro})}$$
Exercise 3

Libby's Company Balance Sheet Classification & Working Capital

Prepare the BALANCE SHEET of Libby's Company using the following figures (in euros):
Short-term loan debts 15.500 | Long-term loan debts 3.000 | Money on its bank account 3.000 | Cash 500 | Paid-in capital 12.000 | Accounts receivable 3.500 | Accounts payable 1.500 | Equipment 13.000 | Raw materials (purchased this year but not used yet) 12.500 | Furniture 1.500 | Retained earnings from the year 2.000.

Phase 1: Standard Account Categorization

  • Non-Current Assets: Equipment (€13,000) + Furniture (€1,500) = €14,500.
  • Current Assets: Raw materials inventory (€12,500) + Accounts receivable (€3,500) + Bank (€3,000) + Cash (€500) = €19,500.
  • Stockholders' Equity: Paid-in capital (€12,000) + Retained earnings (€2,000) = €14,000.
  • Non-Current Liabilities: Long-term loan debts = €3,000.
  • Current Liabilities: Short-term loan debts (€15,500) + Accounts payable (€1,500) = €17,000.

Phase 2: Formal Balanced Presentation

ASSETS (Activo) Amount (€) LIABILITIES & EQUITY (Pasivo y PN) Amount (€)
NON-CURRENT ASSETS 14,500 STOCKHOLDERS' EQUITY 14,000
• Equipment 13,000 • Paid-in capital 12,000
• Furniture 1,500 • Retained earnings from the year 2,000
CURRENT ASSETS 19,500 NON-CURRENT LIABILITIES 3,000
• Inventories (Raw materials) 12,500 • Long-term loan debts 3,000
• Accounts receivable 3,500 CURRENT LIABILITIES 17,000
• Cash at bank 3,000 • Short-term loan debts 15,500
• Cash on hand 500 • Accounts payable (Suppliers) 1,500
TOTAL ASSETS 34,000 TOTAL LIABILITIES & EQUITY 34,000
Solvency & Working Capital Audit
$$\text{Working Capital} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities} = 19,500 - 17,000 = \mathbf{+2,500 \text{ \euro}}$$ $$\text{Debt-to-Equity Ratio} = \frac{L}{E} = \frac{3,000 + 17,000}{14,000} = \frac{20,000}{14,000} = \mathbf{1.43}$$
Exercise 4

Hawkins Partners Income Statement & Inventories

Prepare the Income Statement for Hawkins Partners using the following figures and knowing that corporate tax applicable rate is 30% (figures in euros):
1. Initial inventory: 600 | 2. Purchase of goods (sold 100%): 84.000 | 3. Rental costs: 1.000 | 4. Property sold to another company (income): 10.500 | 5. Interests received: 15.200 | 6. Interests from long-term debt: 5.500 | 7. Wages and salaries: 12.700 | 8. Water, gas and electricity: 1.200 | 9. Sales: 130.800 | 10. Inventory at end of period: 500.

Phase 1: Mathematical Step-by-Step Derivation

  1. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): $$\text{COGS} = \text{Initial Inventory} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Inventory} = 600 + 84,000 - 500 = \mathbf{84,100 \text{ \euro}}$$
  2. Gross Profit: $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Sales} - \text{COGS} = 130,800 - 84,100 = \mathbf{46,700 \text{ \euro}}$$
  3. Operating Expenses: $$\text{Total Operating Expenses} = 12,700 \text{ (Wages)} + 1,000 \text{ (Rent)} + 1,200 \text{ (Utilities)} = \mathbf{14,900 \text{ \euro}}$$
  4. Operating Profit (EBIT): $$\text{EBIT} = 46,700 - 14,900 = \mathbf{31,800 \text{ \euro}}$$
  5. Non-Operating & Financial Items: $$\text{Net Non-Operating} = +10,500 \text{ (Property gain)} + 15,200 \text{ (Interest rec.)} - 5,500 \text{ (Debt interest)} = \mathbf{+20,200 \text{ \euro}}$$
  6. Earnings Before Taxes (EBT): $$\text{EBT} = 31,800 + 20,200 = \mathbf{52,000 \text{ \euro}}$$
  7. Corporate Tax & Net Income: $$\text{Corporate Tax } (30\%) = 0.30 \times 52,000 = \mathbf{15,600 \text{ \euro}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Net Income}} = 52,000 - 15,600 = \mathbf{36,400 \text{ \euro}}$$

Phase 2: Multi-Step Income Statement

HAWKINS PARTNERS — INCOME STATEMENT Detail (€) Amount (€)
Sales Revenue 130,800
(-) Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) (84,100)
= GROSS PROFIT 46,700
(-) Operating Expenses (Wages, Rent, Utilities) (14,900)
= OPERATING INCOME (EBIT) 31,800
(+) Non-Operating Items (Property sale €10.5k + Interests net €9.7k) +20,200
= EARNINGS BEFORE TAXES (EBT) 52,000
(-) Corporate Income Tax (30%) (15,600)
NET INCOME (NET PROFIT) 36,400
Exercise 5

Corporate Income Statement, Trade Discounts, Taxes & Dividends

Prepare the Income Statement of a company with the following information from year 2017:
• Gross sales: 2.002 million €. Trade policy gives 3% discount to Type A customers (annual purchases > 120 million €). Type A customers account for 40% of sales. Total sales = Gross sales - Discount.
• Purchases of raw materials: 962 million €. Assume no change in stocks.
• Workforce: 7.800 employees, average salary 30.000 €/year, Social Security spending 30% of salary.
• Other operating expenses: 457 million €.
• In recent years invested 1.200 million € in fixed assets (lifespan 10 years, linear depreciation, no residual value).
• International loan obtained in 2015 of 225 million € at 6% interest along the whole year, principal repayable in 2020.
• Corporate tax rate: 30%. Agreed dividend: 50%.

Phase 1: Step-by-Step Numerical Calculations

  1. Gross Sales & Discount: $$\text{Type A Sales} = 40\% \times 2,002.00 = 800.80 \text{ M\euro} > 120 \text{ M\euro} \implies \text{Qualifies}$$ $$\text{Discount} = 3\% \times 800.80 = \mathbf{24.024 \text{ M\euro}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Net Sales}} = 2,002.00 - 24.024 = \mathbf{1,977.976 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  2. Cost of Goods Sold (Raw Materials): $$\mathbf{\text{COGS}} = \mathbf{962.00 \text{ M\euro}} \implies \mathbf{\text{Gross Profit}} = 1,977.976 - 962.00 = \mathbf{1,015.976 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  3. Operating Expenses: * Workforce: $7,800 \times 30,000 \text{ \euro} = 234.00 \text{ M\euro}$. Social Security ($30\%$): $0.30 \times 234 = 70.20 \text{ M\euro}$. $$\text{Total Personnel} = 234.00 + 70.20 = 304.20 \text{ M\euro}$$ * Other operating expenses: $457.00 \text{ M\euro}$. * Linear Depreciation: $\frac{1,200.00}{10} = 120.00 \text{ M\euro}$. $$\mathbf{\text{Total Operating Expenses}} = 304.20 + 457.00 + 120.00 = \mathbf{881.20 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  4. Operating Income (EBIT): $$\mathbf{\text{EBIT}} = 1,015.976 - 881.20 = \mathbf{134.776 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  5. Financial Interest Expense: $$\text{Interest} = 6\% \times 225.00 \text{ M\euro} = \mathbf{13.50 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  6. Earnings Before Taxes (EBT): $$\mathbf{\text{EBT}} = 134.776 - 13.50 = \mathbf{121.276 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  7. Corporate Taxes ($30\%$) and Net Income: $$\text{Taxes} = 30\% \times 121.276 = \mathbf{36.383 \text{ M\euro}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Net Income}} = 121.276 - 36.383 = \mathbf{84.893 \text{ M\euro}}$$
  8. Profit Distribution ($50\%$ Dividends, $50\%$ Retained): $$\mathbf{\text{Dividends}} = 50\% \times 84.893 = \mathbf{42.447 \text{ M\euro}}, \qquad \mathbf{\text{Retained Earnings}} = \mathbf{42.447 \text{ M\euro}}$$

Phase 2: Formal Income Statement (Year 2017)

(-) Commercial Discounts (3% on Type A) (24.024)

GROSS PROFIT 1,015.976

Exercise 6

Company X Full Accounting Cycle, Balance Sheet & Uncalled Capital

Company X incorporated on 1/1/2017 with 100 m.u. shares fully paid in cash. Purchased on 1/1/2017 with cash: a) land/area for 20 m.u., b) equipment with life of 10 years (straight-line) for 20 m.u., c) raw materials for 20 m.u.
Hires an employee for annual cost of 10 m.u. Employee processes half of raw material and sells it for 50 m.u. Sales are uniform every day, average collection period is 3 months. Corporate tax is 30% (paid on 31/12/2017). Strict self-financing strategy.
1. Prepare the Balance Sheet as on 31/12/2017 with intermediate calculations.
2. Indicate and explain changes if shareholders had paid only 90% of the 100 m.u. subscribed.

Phase 1: Operational Cycle & P&L Statement (Question 1)

  1. Sales Revenue: $50.0 \text{ m.u.}$
  2. Raw Materials Consumed (COGS): Half of $20.0 \text{ m.u.} = \mathbf{10.0 \text{ m.u.}}$ (Remaining inventory in warehouse $= 10.0 \text{ m.u.}$).
  3. Gross Profit: $50.0 - 10.0 = \mathbf{40.0 \text{ m.u.}}$
  4. Operating Expenses: * Employee cost: $10.0 \text{ m.u.}$ * Equipment depreciation ($20 / 10 \text{ yrs}$): $2.0 \text{ m.u.}$ (Land is non-depreciable). $$\text{Total Expenses} = 10.0 + 2.0 = \mathbf{12.0 \text{ m.u.}}$$
  5. Operating Income (EBIT) & EBT: $40.0 - 12.0 = \mathbf{28.0 \text{ m.u.}}$
  6. Corporate Tax ($30\%$ paid in cash on 31/12): $30\% \times 28.0 = \mathbf{8.4 \text{ m.u.}}$
  7. Net Income & Retained Earnings: $\mathbf{19.6 \text{ m.u.}}$ (100% retained under strict self-financing).

Phase 2: Working Capital & Treasury Cash Reconciliation

  • Accounts Receivable (3-month collection period): $$\text{Receivables} = 50.0 \times \frac{3 \text{ months}}{12 \text{ months}} = \mathbf{12.5 \text{ m.u.}} \implies \text{Cash Collected} = 50.0 - 12.5 = 37.5 \text{ m.u.}$$
  • Ending Cash on 31/12/2017: $$\text{Initial Cash (100 - 20 land - 20 equip - 20 materials)} = 40.0 \text{ m.u.}$$ $$\text{Ending Cash} = 40.0 + 37.5 \text{ (Collections)} - 10.0 \text{ (Salaries)} - 8.4 \text{ (Taxes)} = \mathbf{59.1 \text{ m.u.}}$$

Phase 3: Balance Sheet as on 31/12/2017 (100% Paid)

Phase 4: Impact of Uncalled Capital (90% Paid — Question 2)

If shareholders contribute only $90\%$ of subscribed shares ($90 \text{ m.u.}$ in cash), $10 \text{ m.u.}$ represents **Uncalled Capital**:

  1. Income Statement: Completely unaffected ($\text{Net Income} = 19.6 \text{ m.u.}$).
  2. Ending Cash: Drops by exactly $10.0 \text{ m.u.}$ because the starting cash balance was lower: $$\text{New Cash} = 59.1 - 10.0 = \mathbf{49.1 \text{ m.u.}}$$ $$\text{New Current Assets} = 10.0 + 12.5 + 49.1 = \mathbf{71.6 \text{ m.u.}} \implies \mathbf{\text{New Total Assets}} = 38.0 + 71.6 = \mathbf{109.6 \text{ m.u.}}$$
  3. Stockholders' Equity: Uncalled Capital is presented as a negative contra-equity line: $$\text{Subscribed Capital } (100.0) - \text{Uncalled Capital } (10.0) + \text{Retained Earnings } (19.6) = \mathbf{109.6 \text{ m.u.}}$$
Balance Check with Uncalled Capital
$$\mathbf{\text{Total Assets } (109.6 \text{ m.u.}) \equiv \text{Total Liabilities \& Equity } (109.6 \text{ m.u.})}$$
