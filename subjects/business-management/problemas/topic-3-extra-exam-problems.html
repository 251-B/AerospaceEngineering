Exam Problem 1 • High Difficulty

CIRCONSA — Semester Financial Statements & Cash Reconciliation

The financial director of CIRCONSA is working on its financial statements (Balance Sheet and Income Statement) as of 30 June 2015 (30/06/2015). Starting from the Balance Sheet at 31/12/2014 (Current Assets: 5,400 [Cash 1,200, Receivables 1,800, Stock 2,400]; Non-Current Assets: Machinery 500 less Accum Dep 50 = 450; Current Liabilities: 800 [Taxes 350, Suppliers 450]; Owners' Equity: 5,050 [Retained Earnings 850, Capital 4,200]; Total = 5,850 m.u.), evaluate the semester operations from 01/01/2015 to 30/06/2015:
• i. Sales: 1,200 units at 20 mu/u (200 u/month). 30-day payment term. VIP client (30% of sales) receives a 10% commercial discount.
• ii. Purchases: 1,000 units (40% in Q1, 60% in Q2) at 12 mu/u. 90-day supplier payment term.
• iii. Initial Stock: 200 units at 12 mu/u (2,400 mu).
• iv. Prior Year Liabilities: Q1 2015 paid 2014 unpaid taxes (350 mu) and supplier balance (450 mu).
• v. Prior Year Receivables: Q1 2015 collected 2014 customer debt except 300 mu deferred to July 2016.
• vi. Operating Expenses: Personnel 350 mu/mo + Social Security 150 mu/mo (paid in month produced); Overheads 300 mu/mo (paid 2 months after).
• vii. Renting: 1 April 2015 computer renting for 400 mu/month, payment term 120 days.
• viii. Patent: 1 April 2015 purchased patent for 2,400 mu, payable 1 July 2015. 4-year linear amortization.
• ix. Bank Credit: 1 Feb 2015 borrowed 2,000 mu at 12% per annum, principal + interest due 1 Feb 2016.
• x. Machine 1: Bought 1 July 2014, amortized linearly with 20% residual value.
• xi. Capital & Machine 2: 1 Feb 2015 cash capital increase 10,000 mu. Same date bought Machine 2 for 12,000 mu (paid 8,000 mu cash, 4,000 mu payable 1 July 2015; 4-year life, 0 residual value).
• xii. Corporate Tax: 20% tax, recorded as "taxes to pay" on 30 June 2015.
🎯 Exam Day Strategy: Time Horizons & Deferred Payables
  • Semester Duration: $t = 6 \text{ months} = 0.5 \text{ year}$. Multiply monthly figures by 6 (or active months).
  • Payable on 1 July 2015: The patent debt ($2,400 \text{ mu}$) and Machine 2 debt ($4,000 \text{ mu}$) are payable the day after the balance date. Therefore, they are Current Liabilities on 30/06/2015, not cash outflows!
  • Customer Deferral: The $300 \text{ mu}$ deferred to July 2016 is $> 1 \text{ year}$ away $\implies$ Non-Current Account Receivable.
  • Payment Terms: 30-day customer delay means June sales are unpaid on 30 June; 90-day supplier delay means all Q2 purchases are unpaid on 30 June.

Analytical Deductions Step-by-Step

  1. Net Sales: Total gross sales $= 1,200 \times 20 = 24,000 \text{ mu}$. VIP client ($30\% = 7,200 \text{ mu}$) discount of $10\% = 720 \text{ mu}$. $$\mathbf{\text{Net Sales}} = 24,000 - 720 = \mathbf{23,280 \text{ mu}}$$ June sales ($1/6$ of total) $= 3,880 \text{ mu}$ are uncollected on 30/06 $\implies$ Current Accounts Receivable $= \mathbf{3,880 \text{ mu}}$. Collections $= 23,280 - 3,880 = \mathbf{19,400 \text{ mu}}$.
  2. COGS & Ending Inventory: Available units $= 200 \text{ init} + 1,000 \text{ purch} = 1,200 \text{ units}$. Sold $= 1,200 \text{ units} \implies$ Ending Inventory $= \mathbf{0 \text{ mu}}$. $$\mathbf{\text{COGS}} = 1,200 \times 12 = \mathbf{14,400 \text{ mu}}$$ Q2 purchases ($600 \text{ units} \times 12 = 7,200 \text{ mu}$) payable at 90 days are unpaid on 30 June $\implies$ Accounts Payable (Suppliers) $= \mathbf{7,200 \text{ mu}}$.
  3. Personnel, Overheads & Renting:
    • Personnel: $6 \text{ months} \times (350 + 150) = \mathbf{3,000 \text{ mu}}$ (paid in full).
    • Overheads: $6 \text{ months} \times 300 = \mathbf{1,800 \text{ mu}}$. May and June ($600 \text{ mu}$) unpaid $\implies$ Overheads Payable $= \mathbf{600 \text{ mu}}$.
    • Renting (Apr–Jun, 3 months): $3 \times 400 = \mathbf{1,200 \text{ mu}}$. Payable at 120 days $\implies$ Renting Debt $= \mathbf{1,200 \text{ mu}}$.
  4. Depreciation & Amortization:
    • Patent ($2,400 / 48 \text{ mo} \times 3 \text{ mo}$): $\mathbf{150 \text{ mu}}$ (Net BV $= 2,250 \text{ mu}$).
    • Machine 1 ($500 - 100 \text{ res val} = 400 \text{ base}$ over $4 \text{ yrs}$, semester dep): $\mathbf{50 \text{ mu}}$ (Accum dep $= 100 \text{ mu}$). The 4-year life follows from the opening accumulated depreciation: $50 \text{ mu}$ over the $6$ months since 1 July 2014 gives $100 \text{ mu/year}$, so life $= 400/100 = 4$ years.
    • Machine 2 ($12,000 / 48 \text{ mo} \times 5 \text{ mo active}$): $\mathbf{1,250 \text{ mu}}$ (Accum dep $= 1,250 \text{ mu}$).
    • Total Depreciation in P&L: $150 + 50 + 1,250 = \mathbf{1,450 \text{ mu}}$.
  5. Bank Credit Interest: $2,000 \times 12\% \times \frac{5}{12} = \mathbf{100 \text{ mu}}$ (Accrued interest payable $= 100 \text{ mu}$).
  6. EBIT, Tax & Net Income: $$\text{EBIT} = 23,280 - 14,400 - (3,000 + 1,800 + 1,200 + 1,450) = 8,880 - 7,450 = \mathbf{1,430 \text{ mu}}$$ $$\text{EBT} = 1,430 - 100 = \mathbf{1,330 \text{ mu}}$$ $$\text{Corporate Tax } (20\%) = 20\% \times 1,330 = \mathbf{266 \text{ mu}} \implies \mathbf{\text{Net Profit}} = 1,330 - 266 = \mathbf{1,064 \text{ mu}}$$

Exam Problem 2 • Multi-Year Horizon

INFOPYMES — Full Cycle Financial Statements, 2016–2017 & ROA/ROE

Three entrepreneurs founded INFOPYMES on 1 January 2016 with 100,000 € equity capital. A bank granted a 5-year loan of 100,000 € at 10% annual interest, returning 20,000 € principal each 31 December plus due interest. Computer equipment was bought on 01/01/2016 for 100,000 € (paid 01/04/2016; 5-year life, linear, 0 residual value). Online revenues: 300,000 €. Premises rent: 1,000 €/month. Permanent staff: 5,000 €/month. Utilities: 500 €/month. Variable consulting staff: 174,000 €. Results declared at 23:59 on 31 December; corporate tax 30% paid immediately; dividends 40% paid to partners.
1. Construct 2016 Income Statement.
2. Construct 31/12/2016 Balance Sheet at 24:00.
3. Construct 31/12/2017 Balance Sheet at 24:00 (assume no new investments, cash and retained earnings unchanged).
4. Determine ROA and ROE in 2017.
🎯 Exam Day Strategy: Understanding "At 24:00 after payments"

Because the prompt specifies that taxes, loan repayments, and dividends are paid immediately before 24:00 on 31 December:

  • No corporate taxes payable appear on the 2016 balance sheet.
  • No dividends payable appear on the 2016 balance sheet.
  • The bank loan principal drops immediately from €100,000 to €80,000.
  • In 2017, the second repayment drops the loan principal from €80,000 to €60,000, while accumulated depreciation reaches €40,000.

Calculations & Financial Statements

  1. 2016 Income Statement:
    • Operating Expenses: Rent €12,000 + Permanent Staff €60,000 + Utilities €6,000 + Variable Wages €174,000 + Depreciation €20,000 ($100\text{k}/5$) = €272,000.
    • $\text{EBIT} = 300,000 - 272,000 = \mathbf{28,000 \text{ \euro}}$.
    • Financial Interest ($10\%$ of €100,000): $\mathbf{10,000 \text{ \euro}}$.
    • $\text{EBT} = 28,000 - 10,000 = \mathbf{18,000 \text{ \euro}}$.
    • Tax ($30\%$): $0.30 \times 18,000 = \mathbf{5,400 \text{ \euro}}$.
    • $\text{Net Income} = \mathbf{12,600 \text{ \euro}} \implies \text{Dividends } (40\%) = \mathbf{5,040 \text{ \euro}}, \text{ Retained Earnings } (60\%) = \mathbf{7,560 \text{ \euro}}$.
  2. Ending Cash at 31/12/2016: $$\text{Inflows } (100\text{k} + 100\text{k} + 300\text{k}) - \text{Outflows } (100\text{k} + 12\text{k} + 60\text{k} + 6\text{k} + 174\text{k} + 20\text{k} + 10\text{k} + 5.4\text{k} + 5.04\text{k}) = \mathbf{107,560 \text{ \euro}}$$ (In the outflows, the second $20\text{k}$ is the 31 December loan principal repayment, not depreciation.)

Determination of ROA and ROE in 2017

$$\mathbf{\text{ROA}_{2017}} = \frac{\text{EBIT}_{2017}}{\text{Total Assets}_{2017}} = \frac{28,000 \text{ \euro}}{167,560 \text{ \euro}} \approx \mathbf{16.71\%}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{ROE}_{2017}} = \frac{\text{Net Income}_{2017}}{\text{Total Equity}_{2017}} = \frac{14,000 \text{ \euro}}{107,560 \text{ \euro}} \approx \mathbf{13.02\%}$$

(Where $\text{Net Income}_{2017} = (28,000 - 10\% \times 80,000) \times (1 - 0.30) = 20,000 \times 0.70 = 14,000 \text{ \euro}$. This assumes that 2017 revenues and operating costs equal those of 2016 and that interest is $10\%$ of the $80,000$ outstanding at the start of 2017. The statement also fixes retained earnings and cash at their 2016 values, so the 2017 net income of $14,000$ is not reflected as an increase in the balance sheet reserves.)

Exam Problem 3 • Advanced Equity & Dividends

WIFINET S.L. — Telecommunications Accounting Cycle & Dividend Policies

Wifinet S.L. incorporated 1/1/2016 with 6,003 mu registered capital. In 2016 took a 2,000 mu loan at 5% (due 2019), bought equipment for 5,371 mu (20-year life, 26 mu accum dep in 2016), technology licenses for 206 mu (5-year life), and financial shares for 636 mu. 2016 reflected a loss of 340 mu and cash of 1,476 mu.
In 2017: 5,000 clients paying 0.032 mu/mo subscription fees, plus 46 mu online sales (receivables at end of year: 164 mu). Purchased 680 mu materials (560 mu paid immediately, rest deferred; half consumed in sales). Staff costs 559 mu paid in full. Outside services 312 mu paid in full. Administrative costs 300 mu (40% paid, 60% deferred). Corporate tax rate 30% payable Q1 2018. Retain all profits to offset 2016 losses.
1. Prepare 2017 Income Statement.
2. Construct 31/12/2017 Balance Sheet.
3. What changes if a 20% dividend is paid at 24:00 on 31/12/2017?
4. What changes if a 20% dividend is approved before year-end to be paid on 30 June 2018?
🎯 Exam Day Strategy: Dividend Payment vs Dividend Declaration
  • Paid on 31 Dec (Cash Outflow): Cash decreases by dividend amount; Retained Earnings decrease by dividend amount. Total Assets and Total Equity contract.
  • Payable in the Future (Accrued Debt): No cash leaves the company in 2017! Retained Earnings decrease, but a current liability (Dividends Payable) is created. Total Assets remain unchanged!
  • Prior Year Loss: The 340 mu loss sits in Equity as a negative line item, offset by 2017's net profit.

Calculations & Statements

  • Revenues: $5,000 \times 0.032 \times 12 + 46 = 1,920 + 46 = \mathbf{1,966.00 \text{ mu}}$.
  • COGS: Half of $680 = \mathbf{340.00 \text{ mu}}$ (Inventory $= 340.00 \text{ mu}$).
  • Depreciation: Equipment ($5,371 / 20 = 268.55$) + Licenses ($206 / 5 = 41.20$) $= \mathbf{309.75 \text{ mu}}$.
  • Operating Expenses: $340 + 559 + 312 + 300 + 309.75 = \mathbf{1,820.75 \text{ mu}}$.
  • EBIT, EBT & Net Income: $\text{EBIT} = 1,966 - 1,820.75 = 145.25 \text{ mu}$. Interest $= 5\% \times 2,000 = 100 \text{ mu}$. $\text{EBT} = 45.25 \text{ mu}$. Tax ($30\%$) $= 13.575 \text{ mu}$. $\mathbf{\text{Net Income}} = \mathbf{31.675 \text{ mu}}$.
  • Ending Cash: $1,476 + 1,802 \text{ (collections)} - 560 - 559 - 312 - 120 - 100 = \mathbf{1,627.00 \text{ mu}}$.

Dividend Policy Comparison (Questions 3 & 4)

20% Dividend Amount: $20\% \times 31.675 = \mathbf{6.335 \text{ mu}}$.

  • If Paid on 31/12/2017: Cash drops to $1,620.665 \text{ mu}$. Equity drops to $5,688.340 \text{ mu}$. Total Balance $= 8,001.915 \text{ mu}$.
  • If Payable on 30/06/2018: Cash remains $1,627.000 \text{ mu}$. Current Liabilities increase by $+6.335 \text{ mu}$ (Dividends Payable), Equity drops to $5,688.340 \text{ mu}$. Total Balance remains balanced at 8,008.250 mu!
Exam Problem 4 • Industrial Manufacturing

CEMED — Industrial Manufacturing Accounting Cycle & Trade Discount Analysis

CERÁMICAS DEL MEDITERRÁNEO (CEMED) was created 1/1/2016 by 3 partners contributing 7 mu each in cash (total 21 mu).
• 1/2/2016: bought land for 10 mu (50% cash, 50% payable 1 year later); factory building for 6 mu in cash financed by a 10-year mortgage at 5% (20-year linear life, returns 10% principal each 31 Dec + interest).
• 1/4/2016: borrowed 5 mu at 6% from bank, returning 20% each semester for 2.5 years (1st repayment 1/10/2016, 2nd 1/4/2017). Purchased machinery for 3 mu (60-day payment delay; 10-year linear life).
• 1/7/2016: bought 150 tons raw material at 0.03 mu/ton (half used in sales; 60% paid at 90 days, 40% pending).
• Sales: 50 mu (70% collected). Personnel & SS: 0.8 mu paid in full. Tax: 30% paid on 15/01/2017. Reserves: 40% of net profits, 60% dividends payable on 31/12/2016.
1. Prepare 2016 Income Statement and Balance Sheet at 24:00 on 31/12/2016.
2. Analyze impact of 5% raw material purchase discount in exchange for paying the 60% in cash.
🎯 Exam Day Strategy: Semiannual vs Annual Loan Calculations
  • Factory Building (11 months): $D = \frac{6.00}{20} \times \frac{11}{12} = 0.275 \text{ mu}$. Mortgage interest: $6.00 \times 5\% \times \frac{11}{12} = 0.275 \text{ mu}$. Principal returned on 31 Dec $= 0.60 \text{ mu}$.
  • Bank Loan (Repayments & Accruals): On 1 Oct returned $1.00 \text{ mu}$ principal + paid $0.15 \text{ mu}$ interest ($5 \times 6\% \times 0.5$). For Q4 (1 Oct to 31 Dec), remaining principal is $4.00 \text{ mu}$, accruing $4 \times 6\% \times \frac{3}{12} = \mathbf{0.06 \text{ mu}}$ unpaid interest payable in April 2017!
  • Machinery (9 months): $D = \frac{3.00}{10} \times \frac{9}{12} = 0.225 \text{ mu}$.

Statements & Balanced Verification

$\text{COGS} = 2.25 \text{ mu}$. $\text{Operating Expenses} = 2.25 + 0.80 + 0.50 \text{ (dep)} = 3.55 \text{ mu}$. $\text{EBIT} = 50.00 - 3.55 = \mathbf{46.45 \text{ mu}}$. Financial Interest $= 0.275 + 0.210 = \mathbf{0.485 \text{ mu}}$. $\text{EBT} = 45.965 \text{ mu}$. Tax ($30\%$) $= 13.7895 \text{ mu}$. $\mathbf{\text{Net Income}} = \mathbf{32.1755 \text{ mu}}$ (Reserves: $12.8702 \text{ mu}$, Dividends: $19.3053 \text{ mu}$).

Trade Discount Negotiation Verdict (Question 2)

A $5\%$ discount on purchases reduces material cost by $0.225 \text{ mu}$. Because half was consumed, COGS decreases by $0.1125 \text{ mu}$, expanding EBIT by $+0.1125 \text{ mu}$ and Net Profit by $+0.1125 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{+0.07875 \text{ mu}}$. Profit is strictly higher: the income statement does not record the time value of paying the 60% earlier, and both payment dates fall within 2016. The balance sheet also changes: inventories $-0.1125 \text{ mu}$, accounts payable $-0.09 \text{ mu}$, taxes payable $+0.03375 \text{ mu}$, reserves $+0.0315 \text{ mu}$, and year-end cash $+0.08775 \text{ mu}$ ($= 0.135$ saved on the supplier payment $- 0.04725$ extra dividend), so that $\Delta\text{Assets} = \Delta(\text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}) = -0.02475 \text{ mu}$.
