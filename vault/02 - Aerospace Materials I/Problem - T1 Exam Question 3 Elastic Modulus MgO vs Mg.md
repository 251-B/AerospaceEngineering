# ✏️ Problem: T1 Question 3, Elastic Modulus of MgO vs Mg

## 📄 Statement [Slide 39]
> Would you expect MgO or magnesium to have the higher modulus of elasticity? Explain.

Only the content of the slides is used. Theory: [[Topic 1 - Bonding in Solids and Material Properties]].

---

## 1. Setup
- **MgO** is an ionic compound: a metal (Mg) and a nonmetal (O) [Slide 4]. It appears in the lattice-energy tables of Slides 10 and 11.
- **Mg** is a pure metal: metallic bonding [Slide 23].
- The slides give no modulus values, so the answer is a reasoned comparison.

## 2. What the slides say
- A **high binding energy** (steep slope) means a greater force is needed to stretch the bonds, and so a **higher Young's modulus** [Slide 13].
- MgO has a very large lattice energy, 3932 kJ/mol, among the largest in the table, because its ions have valence +2 (valence ↑ → U ↑) [Slide 10].
- Ionic compounds are **hard and brittle** with high melting points [Slide 14]; MgO melts at 2800 °C [Slide 10] and has a Mohs hardness of 6.5 [Slide 11].
- Metals are **malleable**, ductile and workable [Slides 25, 38]; the metallic bond is the range of lowest energy among primary bonds (25-200 kcal/mol vs 150-370 kcal/mol for ionic) [Slide 29].

## 3. Answer
**MgO** should have the higher modulus of elasticity. Its ionic bond between Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ has a very high binding (lattice) energy [Slide 10], so its energy curve is deep and steep and a greater force is needed to stretch the bonds [Slide 13]. Metallic Mg has a weaker, non-directional bond to an electron sea [Slides 23, 29], which gives a softer, more malleable material [Slide 25].

## 4. Interpretation
The same strong ionic bonding that gives MgO its high melting point (2800 °C) and hardness [Slides 10-11] also gives it its stiffness: all of them come from the deep, steep energy curve of a high-binding-energy bond [Slides 11, 13].
