# ✏️ Problem: T1 Question 3, Elastic Modulus of MgO vs Mg

## 📄 Statement [Slide 39]
> Would you expect MgO or magnesium to have the higher modulus of elasticity? Explain.

Theory: [[Topic 1 - Bonding in Solids and Material Properties]].

---

## 1. Setup
- **MgO** is an ionic compound: a metal (Mg) and a nonmetal (O).
- **Mg** is a pure metal: metallic bonding.
- No modulus values are needed: the answer follows from comparing the bonds.

## 2. Key ideas
- A **high binding energy** (steep slope) means a greater force is needed to stretch the bonds, and so a **higher Young's modulus**.
- MgO has a very large lattice energy, 3932 kJ/mol, among the largest in the table, because its ions have valence +2 (valence ↑ → U ↑).
- Ionic compounds are **hard and brittle** with high melting points; MgO melts at 2800 °C and has a Mohs hardness of 6.5.
- Metals are **malleable**, ductile and workable; the metallic bond is the range of lowest energy among primary bonds (25-200 kcal/mol vs 150-370 kcal/mol for ionic).

## 3. Answer
**MgO** should have the higher modulus of elasticity. Its ionic bond between Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ has a very high binding (lattice) energy, so its energy curve is deep and steep and a greater force is needed to stretch the bonds. Metallic Mg has a weaker, non-directional bond to an electron sea, which gives a softer, more malleable material.

## 4. Interpretation
The same strong ionic bonding that gives MgO its high melting point (2800 °C) and hardness also gives it its stiffness: all of them come from the deep, steep energy curve of a high-binding-energy bond.
