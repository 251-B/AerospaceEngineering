# Concept: Solid Solution Strengthening

> **Key idea:** solute atoms of a different size distort the lattice around them, and the distortion blocks the dislocations.

## Definition and formulas
- Strength increases when impurities are introduced as substitutional or interstitial atoms, for example in a solid solution. This is alloying [Session 12 Slide 8].
- A smaller solute puts the solvent atoms in tension; a greater solute puts them in compression. The lattice is distorted and dislocation movement becomes difficult [Session 12 Slide 8].
- Alloying elements improve the yield strength, the UTS and the hardness, and decrease the ductility, the corrosion resistance and the electrical conductivity (printed as $\sigma_{\text{electric}}$) [Session 12 Slide 9].
- Cu-Ni charts: the yield strength rises and the elongation falls as the nickel content goes from 0 to 50 wt% [Session 12 Slide 9].
- If the solubility limit is exceeded, precipitation hardening takes over [Session 12 Slide 9].
