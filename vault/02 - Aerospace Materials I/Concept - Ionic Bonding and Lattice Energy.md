# 📖 Concept: Ionic Bonding and Lattice Energy

> **Key idea in one sentence:** an ionic bond is the complete transfer of valence electrons from a metal to a nonmetal, which creates two oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction.

Full explanation: [[Topic 1 - Bonding in Solids and Material Properties]].

## 1. What it is [Slides 4-6]
- Forms between **metal and nonmetal** elements; electrons go from the less to the more electronegative atom.
- The force is **electrostatic attraction**; the bond is **non-directional**.
- A cation is **smaller** than its atom; an anion is **larger**.
- A pure ionic bond is ideal: there is always some covalent participation.
- Ions are treated as charged, elastic and polarizable spheres.

## 2. Force and energy of an ion pair [Slides 7-8]
$$F_{net} = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a^2} - \frac{n b}{a^{n+1}} \qquad E_{net} = \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a_0} + \frac{b}{a_0^{\,n}}$$
- Far away: no force; at larger distances attraction dominates; at short distances the electron clouds repel.
- The energy is minimum at the equilibrium distance $a_0$, where attraction and repulsion balance.
- $Z_1, Z_2$: electrons removed or added; $e = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ C; $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}$ C²/(N·m²); $b$: constant; $n$: Born exponent (5-9).

## 3. Lattice energy [Slides 9-10]
- **U:** the energy to bring one mole of ions from infinity to their positions in the crystal; alternatively, the energy released when one mole of ionic solid forms from gas-phase ions.
$$U = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2 N_A A}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a_0}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)$$
- The Born-Haber cycle for KCl gives it from measurable steps (sublimation +89, ionization +425, dissociation +244, Cl ionization −355, formation −438 kJ/mol).

## 4. Properties that follow from U [Slides 10-14]
| Property | Rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| Melting point | ion size ↑ → U ↓ → $T_m$ ↓; valence ↑ → U ↑ → $T_m$ ↑ |
| Hardness | $r_{c^+}$ ↓ → U ↑ → hardness ↑ |
| Thermal expansion | $r_{a^-}$ ↑ → U ↓ → α ↑ |
| Young's modulus | high binding energy (steep slope) → higher E |

General properties: high melting and evaporation points, hard and brittle, insulators unless molten or dissolved (ions move slowly), water soluble.

## 5. Arrangement of ions [Slide 15]
Non-directional bonds; arrangements keep the solid electrically neutral (NaCl, CsCl); a smaller cation-to-anion radius ratio means fewer anions around each cation; ionic compounds are crystalline.
