# Concept: Yield Strength and the 0.2 Percent Offset

> **Key idea:** the yield strength is the stress above which the deformation becomes permanent, and for metals it is defined by a 0.2 % offset.

## Definition and formulas
- **Yield strength** $\sigma_y$: the stress above which permanent deformation occurs [Session 11 Slide 28].
- The elastic zone is $\sigma < \sigma_y$, the plastic zone $\sigma > \sigma_y$ [Session 11 Slide 10].
- **Conventional yield stress**: the most used in metals, at a plastic strain of about 0.2 %. The line parallel to the elastic slope from $\varepsilon = 0.002$ cuts the curve at $\sigma_y$ [Session 11 Slide 28].
- **Discontinuous yield** (low-carbon steels, some polymers): $\sigma_y = \dfrac{\sigma_{\max} + \sigma_{\min}}{2}$ [Session 11 Slide 28].
- The criterion depends on the deformation of the material [Session 11 Slide 28].
- **Working stress:** $\sigma_w = \sigma_y/N$, with $N$ from 1.2 to 4 and usually 2 [Session 11 Slide 29].
