# Concept: Slip Systems and Dislocation Glide

> **Key idea:** plastic deformation is the glide of dislocations along close-packed planes and directions, which needs far less force than shearing a perfect crystal.

## Definition and formulas
- Without dislocations, several bonds must break and reform at the same time; with dislocations the bonds break in sequence, which costs less energy [Session 11 Slide 23].
- $E_{\text{theoretical}} > E_{\text{exp}}$ [Session 11 Slide 23].
- A **slip system** is a slip plane together with a slip direction [Session 11 Slide 23].
- The energy to move a dislocation is $E \propto |\vec{b}|^2$. FCC compact direction [110]: $b = 2R$, $E_b \propto 4R^2$. FCC non-compact [100]: $b = 2\sqrt{2}\,R$, $E_b \propto 8R^2$ [Session 11 Slide 24].
- Slip systems: BCC $\{110\}\langle\bar{1}11\rangle$, 12 systems; FCC $\{111\}\langle\bar{1}10\rangle$, 12 systems; HCP $(0001)\langle 110\rangle$, 6 systems (printed direction, to be checked) [Session 11 Slide 25].
- Dislocation motion is parallel to the direction of maximum packing [Session 11 Slide 24].
- The movement of dislocations is the mechanism of plastic deformation [Session 12 Slide 2].
