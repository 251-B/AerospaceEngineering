# 📖 Concept: Point Defects, Thermal Vacancies, Schottky and Frenkel

> **Key idea in one sentence:** point defects are local disruptions of the lattice that always exist because they increase the entropy, and their number grows with temperature.

Full explanation: [[Topic 2 - Structure of Materials and Crystalline Defects]].

## 1. Why defects exist [Session 4 Slides 12-13]
$\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$: a defect forms while $\Delta H < T\Delta S$; equilibrium at $\Delta G = 0$. $S = k_B\ln W$, $W = N!/[(N-n)!\,n!]$. Higher $T$ → more defects.

## 2. Vacancies and interstitials [Session 4 Slides 15-16]
$$n_v = N\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta H_v}{RT}\right), \qquad N = \frac{N_A\rho}{M}, \qquad \frac{n_v}{N} \lesssim 10^{-4}\ \text{(metals)}$$

## 3. Ionic crystals [Session 4 Slides 17-18]
- **Schottky:** cation + anion vacancy pair, $n_S = N\exp(-\Delta H_S/2RT)$.
- **Frenkel:** ion moved to an interstitial site, $n_F = \sqrt{N N_i}\exp(-\Delta H_F/2RT)$.
- The defect with the lower $\Delta H$ forms. $\Delta H_S$(MgO) > $\Delta H_S$(NaCl): far fewer vacancies in MgO.
