# 📖 Concept: The Fe-Fe₃C Diagram and Steel Microstructures

> **Key idea in one sentence:** the metastable Fe-Fe₃C diagram, with its eutectoid at 727 °C and 0.76 % C, tells which mixture of ferrite, cementite, pearlite and ledeburite each steel or cast iron has.

Full explanation: [[Topic 4 - Phase Diagrams and Solidification]].

## Key values [Session 9 Slides 4-10]
| Point | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Max. C in α (727 °C) | 0.022 wt% |
| Eutectoid | 727 °C, 0.76 % C: γ → α + Fe₃C (pearlite) |
| Max. C in γ (1147 °C) | 2.14 wt% (limit steel / cast iron) |
| Eutectic | 1147 °C, 4.30 % C: L → γ + Fe₃C (ledeburite) |
| Peritectic | 1493 °C: δ (0.09) + L (0.53) → γ (0.16) |
| Cementite | 6.67 % C, hard and brittle, metastable |

## Microconstituents of steels just below 727 °C [Session 9 Slides 12-20]
- Eutectoid (0.76 %): 100 % pearlite (89 % α, 11 % Fe₃C).
- Hypoeutectoid: proeutectoid α $= (0.76 - C_0)/(0.76 - 0.022)$, the rest pearlite.
- Hypereutectoid: proeutectoid Fe₃C $= (C_0 - 0.76)/(6.67 - 0.76)$, the rest pearlite.

## Cooling rate and alloying [Session 9 Slides 21-23]
- Faster cooling: eutectoid to lower T and %C, less proeutectoid ferrite, finer and harder pearlite.
- α-stabilisers (Si, Cr, Mo, W, Ti …) raise the eutectoid temperature; γ-stabilisers (Mn, Ni, Co, Cu …) lower it; both lower its %C.
