# 📖 Concept: Invariant Reactions in Binary Systems

> **Key idea in one sentence:** three phases of a binary system can coexist only at one temperature and with fixed compositions, and there the system undergoes one of five invariant reactions.

Full explanation: [[Topic 4 - Phase Diagrams and Solidification]].

## The five reactions on cooling [Session 8 Slide 6]
| Reaction | Equation | Example in the slides |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Eutectic | $L \rightarrow \alpha + \beta$ | Pb-Sn, 183 °C, 61.9 wt% Sn |
| Peritectic | $\alpha + L \rightarrow \beta$ | Fe-Ni, 1517 °C: δ(4.0) + L(5.4) → γ(4.3); Pt-Ag, 1186 °C: α(10.5) + L(66.3) → β(42.4) |
| Monotectic | $L_1 \rightarrow L_2 + \alpha$ | Cu-Pb, 955 °C: L(36) → L₂(87) + α(0) |
| Eutectoid | $\gamma \rightarrow \alpha + \beta$ | Cu-Zn, 560 °C: δ(74) → γ(70) + ε(78) |
| Peritectoid | $\alpha + \beta \rightarrow \gamma$ | Co-W |

The compositions of the examples are in % of the second element, as Session 8 prints them (no basis is given; the Pb-Sn values are wt%).

Eutectoid and peritectoid are solid-state reactions. At 1 atm, $F = 2 - 3 + 1 = 0$.

## Non-equilibrium peritectic [Session 8 Slide 10]
The β formed surrounds the primary α (encasement) and acts as a diffusion barrier, so fast cooling leaves cored, incomplete structures.
