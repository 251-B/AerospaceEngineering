## Q31.1 Adding nickel to copper

- id: B31-Q1
- concept_id: u5-solid-solution-strengthening
- format: what-happens
- asks: application

Nickel is added to copper, raising its content from 0 to 40 wt% in solid solution. What will happen to the alloy:

a) Unchanged yield strength, lower elongation
b) Higher yield strength, lower elongation
c) Higher yield strength, higher elongation
d) Lower yield strength, higher elongation

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 9: the Cu-Ni charts show the yield strength rising (about 70 to 165 MPa) and the elongation falling (about 55 to 30 %) as nickel goes from 0 to 50 wt%; alloying improves the strength and decreases the ductility.

- a) Half right: the elongation falls, but the solute does raise the yield strength.
- c) Half right: the strength rises, but the elongation falls.
- d) Both trends are inverted with respect to the Cu-Ni charts.

## Q31.2 Grain boundaries

Which one of the following statements about grain boundaries is FALSE?

a) They act as barriers to dislocation slip
b) Finer grains give more boundaries per volume
c) They separate crystals with different orientations
d) They are a single interatomic spacing wide

### Answer

d

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 10: the grain boundary width is 2 to 5 interatomic spacings, not one.

- a) This is true: grains act as barriers to slip (Slide 10).
- b) This is true: ↓ grain size → ↑ number of grains → ↑ barriers (Slide 10).
- c) This is true: it is the definition on Slide 10.

## Q31.3 Obstacles in precipitation strengthening

- id: B31-Q3
- concept_id: u5-precipitation-strengthening
- format: identify
- asks: classification

Which obstacles block the dislocations in precipitation strengthening?

a) Particles of a secondary phase in the matrix
b) Tangles of other dislocations
c) Solute atoms dissolved in the lattice
d) Boundaries between grains of different orientation

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 12: particles of a secondary phase precipitate in the alloy and act as obstacles to dislocation motion; they also distort the crystal structure of the matrix. Slide 9 adds that this happens when the solubility limit is exceeded.

- b) Tangled dislocations are the obstacles of strain hardening.
- c) Dissolved solute atoms are the obstacles of solid solution strengthening.
- d) Grain boundaries are the obstacles of grain size reduction strengthening.

## Q31.4 Size and dispersion of precipitates

- id: B31-Q4
- concept_id: u5-precipitation-strengthening
- format: cause-effect
- asks: effect

With smaller, more dispersed and denser precipitates in an alloy, the strengthening:

a) increases, since there are more obstacles to slip
b) does not change, since the amount of solute is fixed
c) increases, since the Young's modulus rises
d) decreases, since each particle is smaller

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 12: "↓ size, ↑ dispersion, ↑ density → ↑ strengthening"; more closely spaced particles leave less room for the dislocation line to bow between them.

- b) The distribution of the particles matters, not only their total amount.
- c) The trend is right but the cause is wrong: precipitates act on dislocation motion, not on the elastic stiffness.
- d) The trend is inverted: the slide gives ↓ size, ↑ dispersion, ↑ density → ↑ strengthening.

## Q31.5 Stages of a dislocation passing particles

- id: B31-Q5
- concept_id: u5-precipitation-strengthening
- format: ordering
- asks: definition

Order the stages by which a dislocation passes a row of secondary-phase precipitate particles:

a) Bending, approach, critical bow-out, escape leaving loops
b) Approach, critical bow-out, bending, escape leaving loops
c) Approach, bending, critical bow-out, escape leaving loops
d) Escape leaving loops, critical bow-out, bending, approach

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 12: (a) approach under a force $\tau b$, (b) sub-critical bending between the particles, (c) critical bow-out under $\tau bL$, (d) escape, leaving a loop around each particle.

- a) The line cannot bend between the particles before it reaches them.
- b) The middle pair is swapped: the line first bends between the particles (sub-critical) and only then reaches the critical bow-out.
- d) This is the sequence reversed.

## Q31.6 Strength of aluminium by strengthening state

- id: B31-Q6
- concept_id: u5-strengthening-aircraft-alloys
- format: ordering
- asks: comparison

Order these aluminium materials from the highest to the lowest typical ultimate tensile strength: pure annealed Al, cold-worked commercial Al, precipitation-strengthened 2xxx (Al-Cu-Mg) alloy, and precipitation-strengthened 7xxx (Al-Cu-Mg-Zn) alloy:

a) 2xxx (Al-Cu-Mg) > 7xxx (Al-Cu-Mg-Zn) > cold-worked Al > pure Al
b) 7xxx (Al-Cu-Mg-Zn) > 2xxx (Al-Cu-Mg) > cold-worked Al > pure Al
c) Cold-worked Al > 7xxx (Al-Cu-Mg-Zn) > 2xxx (Al-Cu-Mg) > pure Al
d) Pure Al > cold-worked Al > 2xxx (Al-Cu-Mg) > 7xxx (Al-Cu-Mg-Zn)

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 13 gives the typical UTS progression: 7xxx Al-Cu-Mg-Zn (572 MPa) > 2xxx Al-Cu-Mg (469 MPa) > cold-worked Al (165 MPa) > pure Al (45 MPa), showing the much higher strength achieved by precipitation hardening in aircraft alloys.

- a) The two precipitation-hardened alloys are swapped: the 7xxx Al-Cu-Mg-Zn alloy reaches higher strength than the 2xxx Al-Cu-Mg alloy.
- c) Cold working alone produces far lower strength than precipitation hardening in aerospace aluminium alloys.
- d) This is the complete ranking reversed, placing pure annealed aluminium first.

## Q31.7 Why precipitation-hardened aircraft alloys are so strong

- id: B31-Q7
- concept_id: u5-strengthening-aircraft-alloys
- format: topic-true
- asks: cause

Referring to precipitation strengthening in 2xxx and 7xxx aerospace aluminium alloys, why are these heat-treated alloys far stronger than pure aluminium?

a) Heat treatment removes all grain boundaries and crystal defects from the matrix
b) Pure aluminium has fewer than six primary slip systems at room temperature
c) Alloying converts the FCC aluminium matrix into a brittle ceramic crystal
d) Fine, densely dispersed intermetallic precipitates distort the lattice and block dislocations

### Answer

d

### Explanation

Session 12, Slides 12-13: in 2xxx (Al-Cu-Mg) and 7xxx (Al-Cu-Mg-Zn) alloys, fine secondary-phase precipitates (such as $\text{MgZn}_2$) form throughout the matrix, distending the lattice and forcing dislocations to bow between closely spaced obstacles at much higher stress.

- a) Fewer defects would make dislocation glide easier, not harder; pure, defect-free crystals are weak.
- b) Aluminium has an FCC crystal structure with 12 slip systems, which is why pure aluminium is very soft and ductile.
- c) Aerospace 2xxx and 7xxx aluminium alloys retain a ductile FCC metallic matrix strengthened by precipitates.

## Q31.9 Definition of hardness

- id: B31-Q9
- concept_id: u5-hardness-definition-methods
- format: complete-sentence
- asks: definition

In materials engineering, hardness is defined as:

a) the energy absorbed by the material up to fracture
b) the plastic strain supported before fracture
c) the surface resistance to permanent deformation
d) the resistance of the material to elastic deformation

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Session 12, Slide 15: hardness is the surface resistance to permanent (plastic) deformation, measured by abrasion or by indentation.

- a) This is toughness (Session 11, Slide 34).
- b) This is ductility (Session 11, Slide 32).
- d) This is stiffness, given by Young's modulus.

## Q31.10 Hardness by abrasion

- id: B31-Q10
- concept_id: u5-hardness-definition-methods
- format: identify
- asks: classification

Which hardness method measures the resistance to abrasion rather than to indentation?

a) Mohs
b) Rockwell
c) Brinell
d) Vickers

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Session 12, Slides 15-16: abrasion methods are Mohs, Martens and the wear test; the Mohs scale ranks materials by which can scratch which. Brinell, Vickers and Rockwell are indentation tests.

- b) Rockwell measures the depth of penetration of a cone or a ball.
- c) Brinell measures the indentation of a ball under a load.
- d) Vickers measures the indentation of a diamond pyramid.
