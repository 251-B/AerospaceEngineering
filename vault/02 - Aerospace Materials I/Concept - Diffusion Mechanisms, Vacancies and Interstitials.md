# 📖 Concept: Diffusion Mechanisms, Vacancies and Interstitials

> **Key idea in one sentence:** atoms diffuse by jumping into vacancies (substitutional diffusion) or between interstitial sites (interstitial diffusion), and each jump needs an activation energy.

Full explanation: [[Topic 3 - Diffusion in Solids and Mass Transport]].

## 1. Requirements [Session 5 Slides 2-6]
- Driving force: a **composition gradient**. Diffusion needs thermal activation.
- An **empty site** (vacancy or interstitial) and **energy** larger than the activation energy $E_a$, which depends on the diffusing atom and on the host.

## 2. Vacancy diffusion [Session 5 Slides 7-12]
- Atoms jump into neighbouring vacancies; the rate depends on the number of vacancies, which rises with $T$.
- **Self-diffusion** (same atoms): $E_{a} = E_{\text{form vacancy}} + E_{\text{move vacancy}}$; $E_a$ rises with $T_m$ (Zn 91.6, Al 165, Cu 196, Ni 293, α-Fe 240, Mo 460 kJ/mol).
- **Interdiffusion** (different atoms): follows the Hume-Rothery rules; forms alloys and intermetallics; diffusion welding. Example: Cu-Ni couple (both FCC, 128 vs 124 pm, electronegativity 1.90 vs 1.91, valence +2).

## 3. Interstitial diffusion [Session 5 Slide 13]
- Small atoms (C, H, O, N in metals) jump between interstitial sites.
- Lower activation energy ($E_i < E_v$), faster, more available sites.
