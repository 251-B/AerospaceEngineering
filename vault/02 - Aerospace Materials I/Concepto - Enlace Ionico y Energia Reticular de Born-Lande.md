# 📖 Concepto: Enlace Iónico y Energía Reticular de Born-Landé

> **Key Idea in One Sentence:** The ionic bond is an isotropic, non-directional electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by the complete transfer of valence electrons from a metallic element to a non-metallic element, whose cohesive lattice energy is quantified by the Born-Landé equation and the Born-Haber thermochemical cycle.

---

## 🎯 1. Physical Foundations and Atomic Principles

### 1.1 Electron Transfer and Ion Formation [Slides 4-6]
* **Mechanism:** Occurs between elements with a large electronegativity difference ($\Delta\chi > 2.0$, e.g., alkali/alkaline-earth metals and halogens/chalcogens). The electropositive metal atom loses its outer valence electron(s) to become a positively charged **cation**, while the electronegative non-metal gains electron(s) to become a negatively charged **anion**.
* **Ionic Radii Evolution:**
  * When a metal atom ionizes to form a cation ($M \to M^{z+} + z e^-$), the loss of valence electrons reduces electron-electron repulsion and often strips the outermost shell, causing the **cation radius to contract significantly** ($r_{c+} < r_{\text{atom}}$).
  * When a non-metal gains electrons ($X + z e^- \to X^{z-}$), the increased electron-electron shielding expands the electron cloud, causing the **anion radius to expand** ($r_{a-} > r_{\text{atom}}$).
* **Fundamental Assumptions of Ionic Crystal Physics [Slide 6]:**
  1. **Charged Spheres:** Ions are treated electrostatically as spherical charge distributions.
  2. **Elastic Spheres:** At short range, electron orbital overlap introduces a steep repulsive resistance to interpenetration.
  3. **Polarizable Spheres:** The electron cloud of an ion (especially large, polarizable anions like $\text{I}^-$ or $\text{S}^{2-}$) undergoes distortion in the strong local electric field generated by neighboring cations. Pure ionic bonding is an idealized limit; a small degree of covalent orbital character is always present [Slide 5].

## 📐 2. Interionic Force and Potential Energy Formulation

### 2.1 Net Interionic Force for an Isolated Ion Pair [Slide 7]
Consider two ions of valences $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ (where $Z_1$ is positive and $Z_2$ is positive in magnitude) separated by an interionic distance $a$. The total force is the superposition of Coulombic electrostatic attraction and short-range Born quantum repulsion:

$$F_{\text{net}}(a) = F_{\text{attractive}}(a) + F_{\text{repulsive}}(a)$$

$$F_{\text{net}}(a) = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a^2} - \frac{n b}{a^{n+1}}$$

Where:
* $Z_1, Z_2$: Formal valence charges (number of electrons transferred, dimensionless).
* $e$: Elementary charge ($1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$).
* $\varepsilon_0$: Permittivity of free space ($8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2)$ or $\text{F/m}$).
* $a$: Interionic separation distance $[\text{m}]$.
* $b$: Born repulsive constant $[\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^{n+1}]$.
* $n$: Born exponent (typically $n \in [5, 9]$, depending on the noble gas electron configuration of the interacting ions).

### 2.2 Net Potential Energy Function $E_{\text{net}}(a)$ [Slide 8]
Integrating the net force with respect to interionic distance $a$ (with the reference state $E(\infty) = 0$ at infinite separation):

$$E_{\text{net}}(a) = -\int_\infty^a F_{\text{net}}(r)\, dr = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a} + \frac{b}{a^n}$$

$$\underbrace{E_{\text{attraction}} = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a}}_{\text{Attractive Energy (Negative, Exothermic)}} \quad + \quad \underbrace{E_{\text{repulsion}} = +\frac{b}{a^n}}_{\text{Repulsive Energy (Positive, Endothermic)}}$$

## 🔍 3. Equilibrium Distance and the Born-Landé Lattice Energy

### 3.1 Determination of Equilibrium Spacing $a_0$ [Slide 8]
At the equilibrium interionic spacing $a = a_0$, the net potential energy reaches a global minimum, and the net force vanishes identically:

$$\left.\frac{\partial E_{\text{net}}}{\partial a}\right|_{a = a_0} = -F_{\text{net}}(a_0) = 0$$

$$\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a_0^2} - \frac{n b}{a_0^{n+1}} = 0 \implies b = \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2 a_0^{n-1}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 n}$$

Substituting the expression for the constant $b$ back into the potential energy equation yields the equilibrium bonding energy for a single isolated ion pair:

$$E(a_0) = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a_0} + \frac{1}{a_0^n}\left(\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2 a_0^{n-1}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 n}\right) = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a_0}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)$$

### 3.2 The Born-Landé Equation for a Three-Dimensional Crystal Lattice [Slide 9]
In a real 3D crystal lattice, each ion interacts not merely with a single partner, but with an infinite geometric array of coordinating cations and anions. Summing all pairwise electrostatic interactions produces the dimensionless geometric factor known as the **Madelung Constant ($A$)**:

$$A = \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{(\pm)}{r_{ij}/a_0}$$

*(For example, $A_{\text{NaCl}} \approx 1.7476$, $A_{\text{CsCl}} \approx 1.7627$, $A_{\text{ZnS}} \approx 1.6381$).*

Multiplying by Avogadro's number $N_A$ ($6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$) yields the **Molar Lattice Energy ($U$)**:

$$U = -\frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2 N_A A}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a_0}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)$$

* **Physical Definition:** $U$ is defined as the internal energy change or energy released when one mole of an ionic crystalline solid is formed from its constituent gaseous ions at infinite separation [Slide 9]:
  $$M^{z+}(g) + X^{z-}(g) \longrightarrow MX(s) \quad \Delta H = U < 0$$
  *(Conversely, the lattice dissociation energy is defined with a positive sign: $+|U|$).*

## 🧪 4. The Born-Haber Thermochemical Cycle for $\text{KCl}$ [Slide 9]

Because the direct reaction $K^+(g) + Cl^-(g) \to KCl(s)$ cannot be isolated in a laboratory calorimeter, the lattice energy $U$ is determined indirectly via Hess's Law through the Born-Haber cycle.

```
                    K⁺(g)  +  Cl(g)
                     ▲          ▲
      IE(K) = +425   │          │  EA(Cl) = -355
                     │          ▼
                    K⁺(g)  +  Cl⁻(g)
                     ▲                 \
                     │                  \  Lattice Energy U
 ΔH_sub(K) = +89     │                   \ (Exothermic)
                     │                    ▼
               K(s) + 1/2 Cl₂(g) ─────────► KCl(s)
                      │
   1/2 D(Cl₂) = +122  │       ΔH_f° = -438 kJ/mol
                      ▼
                 K(s) + Cl(g)
```

### Official UC3M Numerical Values [Slide 9]:
| Thermochemical Step | Reaction Equation | Official $\Delta H$ ($\text{kJ/mol}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **K sublimation** ($\Delta H_{\text{sub}}$) | $\text{K}(s) \to \text{K}(g)$ | $+89$ |
| **$\text{K}(g)$ ionization** ($\text{IE}_1$) | $\text{K}(g) \to \text{K}^+(g) + e^-$ | $+425$ |
| **$\text{Cl}_2(g)$ dissociation** ($D_{\text{Cl-Cl}}$) | $\text{Cl}_2(g) \to 2\text{Cl}(g)$ | $+244$ |
| **$\text{Cl}(g)$ electron affinity** ($\text{EA}$) | $\text{Cl}(g) + e^- \to \text{Cl}^-(g)$ | $-355$ |
| **$\text{KCl}(s)$ standard formation** ($\Delta H_f^\circ$) | $\text{K}(s) + \frac{1}{2}\text{Cl}_2(g) \to \text{KCl}(s)$ | $-438$ |

### Explicit Calculation of $U_{\text{KCl}}$:
Applying the closed thermodynamic enthalpy cycle:

$$\Delta H_f^\circ = \Delta H_{\text{sub}}(\text{K}) + \text{IE}_1(\text{K}) + \frac{1}{2} D_{\text{Cl}_2} + \text{EA}(\text{Cl}) + U$$

*Note on dissociation:* Forming 1 mole of $\text{Cl}(g)$ requires $\frac{1}{2}$ mole of $\text{Cl}_2(g)$:
$$\frac{1}{2}(+244\text{ kJ/mol}) = +122\text{ kJ/mol}$$

$$-438 = (+89) + (+425) + (+122) + (-355) + U$$
$$-438 = (+281) + U \implies U = -438 - 281 = \mathbf{-719\text{ kJ/mol}}$$

*(Note: If the dissociation value $+244\text{ kJ/mol}$ were considered per mole of $\text{Cl}$ atoms produced without the factor $1/2$, the sum would be $+403 + U = -438 \implies U = -841\text{ kJ/mol}$. Standard thermochemical data strictly verifies $U \approx -717\text{ to }-719\text{ kJ/mol}$).*

## 🔬 5. Macroscopic Properties Governed by Lattice Energy

### 5.1 Melting Temperature ($T_m$) [Slide 10]
* **Influence of Ionic Size:** As the ionic radii increase within a given family (e.g., alkali halides: $\text{LiF} \to \text{NaCl} \to \text{KBr} \to \text{CsI}$), the equilibrium distance $a_0 = r_c + r_a$ increases. Because $U \propto 1/a_0$, **lattice energy decreases, leading to a direct drop in melting point $T_m$**.
* **Dominant Influence of Valence ($Z_1 Z_2$):** Divalent ionic compounds ($Z_1 = +2, Z_2 = -2$, such as alkaline-earth oxides $\text{MgO}, \text{CaO}$) exhibit a product $Z_1 Z_2 = 4$, quadrupling the electrostatic attraction compared to monovalent halides ($Z_1 Z_2 = 1$). Consequently, $\text{MgO}$ has a massive lattice energy ($|U| \approx 3790\text{ kJ/mol}$) and a soaring melting temperature ($T_m \approx 2852^\circ\text{C}$), whereas $\text{NaCl}$ melts at $801^\circ\text{C}$.

$$\text{Valence } Z \uparrow \implies U \uparrow \implies T_m \uparrow$$
$$\text{Ion size } a_0 \uparrow \implies U \downarrow \implies T_m \downarrow$$

### 5.2 Hardness on the Mohs Scale [Slide 11]
Hardness measures the resistance of a material to localized plastic indentation or abrasive scratching. In ionic crystals, plastic deformation requires displacing ions past one another against strong electrostatic forces.
As cation radius $r_{c+}$ decreases at constant valence, $a_0$ shrinks and $U$ escalates, dramatically enhancing hardness:

| Compound | Cation / Anion | Interionic Distance $d$ ($\text{\AA}$) | Mohs Hardness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$\text{BeO}$** | $\text{Be}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}$ | $1.65$ | **$9.0$** |
| **$\text{MgO}$** | $\text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}$ | $2.10$ | **$6.5$** |
| **$\text{CaO}$** | $\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}$ | $2.40$ | **$4.5$** |
| **$\text{SrO}$** | $\text{Sr}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}$ | $2.57$ | **$3.5$** |
| **$\text{BaO}$** | $\text{Ba}^{2+}, \text{O}^{2-}$ | $2.77$ | **$3.3$** |

*(Reference benchmarks: Hydroxyapatite $= 5.0$, Keratin fingernail $= 2.5$).*

### 5.3 Coefficient of Volumetric Thermal Expansion ($\alpha_V$) [Slide 12]
Thermal expansion represents the fractional volume change per unit temperature increment: $\alpha_V = \frac{1}{V}\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}$.
As anion radius $r_{a-}$ increases, $a_0$ increases, weakening the lattice energy ($U \downarrow$) and flattening the potential well. The shallower, more asymmetric well facilitates larger anharmonic vibrational excursions:

| Alkali Halide | Interionic Spacing trend | $\alpha_V \times 10^6$ ($^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$\text{NaF}$** | Smallest $r_{a-}$ | **$39$** |
| **$\text{NaCl}$** | Intermediate | **$40$** |
| **$\text{NaBr}$** | Larger | **$43$** |
| **$\text{NaI}$** | Largest $r_{a-}$ | **$48$** |

*(Hydroxyapatite reference: $\alpha_V = 12 \times 10^{-6}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$).*

$$\text{Strong bonding (steep, deep well)} \implies \text{High } U \implies \text{Low thermal expansion } \alpha$$

### 5.4 Brittleness & Fracture Mechanism [Slide 14]
Under shear or tensile stress, mechanical deformation forces adjacent planes of ions to slide past one another. Because ionic lattices are strictly arranged with alternating cations and anions, a shear shift by one atomic spacing brings **ions of identical charge into direct juxtaposition** ($\text{cation}\leftrightarrow\text{cation}$ and $\text{anion}\leftrightarrow\text{anion}$).
The resulting catastrophic Coulombic repulsion tears the crystal apart along cleavage planes, rendering ionic solids inherently **hard and brittle** with negligible room-temperature plasticity.

### 5.5 Electrical Conductivity Disparity [Slide 14]
* **Solid State:** Valence electrons are tightly bound in localized ionic orbitals with enormous band gaps ($> 6\text{ eV}$). Under an applied electric field, electric current can only be carried by physical migration of entire ions through point defects (vacancies/interstitials). Because ionic diffusion in a rigid solid lattice is extremely slow ($\sigma < 10^{-12}\text{ S/m}$), **solid ionic materials are electrical insulators**.
* **Molten or Aqueous Solution:** Upon melting or dissolution in polar solvents ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), the rigid lattice dissociates into mobile cations and anions. Under an electric field, these free ions migrate readily, making molten salts and ionic solutions **excellent ionic conductors**.

### 5.6 Packing Geometry and Radius Ratio Rules [Slide 15]
Because ionic bonds are non-directional, geometric coordination is governed purely by electrostatic packing efficiency and the **radius ratio** $r_{c+}/r_{a-}$ to prevent anion-anion overlap:
* $\frac{r_+}{r_-} \in [0.414, 0.732] \implies \text{Octahedral coordination (CN = 6, e.g., NaCl)}$.
* $\frac{r_+}{r_-} \in [0.732, 1.000] \implies \text{Cubic coordination (CN = 8, e.g., CsCl)}$.
