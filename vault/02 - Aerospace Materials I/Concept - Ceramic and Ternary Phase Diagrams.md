# 📖 Concept: Ceramic and Ternary Phase Diagrams

> **Key idea in one sentence:** ceramic diagrams are read like metallic ones with oxides as components, and ternary diagrams place three-component compositions in a triangle whose corners are the pure components.

Full explanation: [[Topic 4 - Phase Diagrams and Solidification]].

## Ceramic diagrams [Session 10 Slides 2-4]
- Al₂O₃-Cr₂O₃: complete solid solution Al₂₋ₓCrₓO₃ between about 2100 and 950 °C; two solid solutions below 950 °C.
- Al₂O₃-SiO₂: eutectic at 1587 °C, L (7.7 wt% Al₂O₃) → mullite + SiO₂; mullite (3Al₂O₃·2SiO₂) melts incongruently, L (77 wt% Al₂O₃) + Al₂O₃ (1828 °C in the slide text, 1890 ± 10 °C on its diagram).

## Ternary diagrams [Session 10 Slides 5-8]
- Corners: pure components; sides: binary systems; the three percentages add up to 100 %.
- Read each percentage on the lines parallel to the side opposite that component.
- Shown as liquidus and solidus projections and as isotherms (Ag-Cu-Zn; Fe-Cr-Ni at 650 °C: point 1 = 20 Fe, 60 Cr, 20 Ni, α + γ; stainless steel 74 Fe, 18 Cr, 8 Ni).
