# 📖 Concept: Volumetric, Linear and Planar Density in Crystal Lattices

> **Key idea in one sentence:** densities count the atoms that belong to a volume, a line or a plane and divide by its size.

Full explanation: [[Topic 2 - Structure of Materials and Crystalline Defects]].

## Formulas [Session 4 Slides 7-9]
$$\rho = \frac{n\,M}{V_{cell}\,N_A}, \qquad \rho_{[uvw]} = \frac{\text{atoms centred on the line}}{\text{length}}, \qquad \rho_{(hkl)} = \frac{\text{atoms centred on the plane}}{\text{area}}$$

## Copper (FCC, $R = 1.28$ Å, $a = 2\sqrt2\,R$)
- Volume: $4/a^3 = 8.43\times10^{22}$ atoms/cm³; $\rho = 8.89$ g/cm³.
- $[100]$: $2\cdot\tfrac12 = 1$ atom over $a$: $2.76\times10^7$ atoms/cm.
- $[111]$: only the two corners lie on the body diagonal: $1$ atom over $\sqrt3\,a$.
- $(100)$: $1 + 4\cdot\tfrac14 = 2$ atoms over $a^2$: $1.5\times10^{15}$ atoms/cm².

> [!warning] Note on the official material
> The official $[111]$ example counts a central atom, which FCC does not have; the correct count is 1 atom, $\rho_{[111]} = 1/(\sqrt3\,a)$.
