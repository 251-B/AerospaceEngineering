# ✏️ Problem: T1 Question 2, Potential Curves of Titanium vs Aluminium

## 📄 Statement [Slide 39]
> Titanium is stiffer than aluminum, has a lower thermal expansion coefficient than aluminum, and has a higher melting temperature than aluminum. On the same graph, carefully and schematically draw the potential well curves for both metals and showing how the physical properties are manifested in these curves.

Theory: [[Topic 1 - Bonding in Solids and Material Properties]].

---

## 1. Setup
- The statement gives three facts: Ti is **stiffer**, has a **lower thermal expansion coefficient** and a **higher melting temperature** than Al.
- The sketch is **schematic**: no numbers are asked for.

## 2. Key ideas about the curve
The energy curve has its minimum at the equilibrium distance $a_0$. Three features of it are linked to properties:

| Property | Feature of the curve |
| :--- | :--- |
| Melting temperature | **Depth** of the minimum (binding energy): high binding energy → high $T_m$ |
| Thermal expansion | **Width** of the trough: a deep, narrow trough (strong bonding) → low coefficient of thermal expansion |
| Stiffness (Young's modulus) | **Steepness** of the curve: a steep slope means more force is needed to stretch the bonds → higher modulus |

## 3. Answer: the sketch
Draw both curves on the same axes (energy vs distance), with their minima close to each other in distance:

- **Titanium:** a **deeper** minimum (higher melting temperature), a **narrower** trough (lower thermal expansion) and **steeper** walls around $a_0$ (stiffer).
- **Aluminium:** a **shallower** minimum, a **wider** trough and gentler walls.

![[attachments/T1/wells.svg]]

How each property shows up:
1. **Melting temperature:** the Ti minimum lies lower; more energy is needed to break its bonds.
2. **Thermal expansion:** heating adds the same energy to both curves; in the narrow Ti trough the spacing between atoms grows less than in the wide Al trough.
3. **Stiffness:** around $a_0$ the Ti curve is steeper, so a larger force is needed to stretch the bonds.

## 4. Interpretation
All three facts point to the same thing: the bonds in titanium are **stronger** than in aluminium. One curve shape, deeper, narrower and steeper, carries the three properties at once.
