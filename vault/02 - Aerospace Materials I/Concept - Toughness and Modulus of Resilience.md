# Concept: Toughness and Modulus of Resilience

> **Key idea:** toughness is the energy absorbed up to fracture (the whole area under the curve), and resilience is the energy stored in the elastic part (the area up to yield).

## Definition and formulas
- **Toughness:** the ability of a material to absorb energy up to fracture, by elastic and plastic deformation. It is an energy per unit volume (Pa or J/m³) and the area under the stress-strain curve [Session 11 Slide 34].
- Toughness matters in structural applications and in protection and security systems; the slide links it to the fracture type (ductile or brittle) and to the fracture toughness [Session 11 Slide 34].
- **Resilience:** the capacity to absorb elastic energy when the material is deformed elastically [Session 11 Slide 35].
- **Modulus of resilience:** $U_r = \displaystyle\int_0^{\varepsilon_y}\sigma\,d\varepsilon$ [Session 11 Slide 35].
- With a linear elastic region: $U_r = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\sigma_y\,\varepsilon_y = \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\sigma_y^2}{E}$ [Session 11 Slide 35].
- High resilience: high $\sigma_y$ and low $E$. Application: alloys for springs [Session 11 Slide 35].
