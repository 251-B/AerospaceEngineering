# Concept: Strain Hardening and Percent Cold Work

> **Key idea:** plastic deformation at low temperature multiplies the dislocations, which tangle and block each other, so the strength rises and the ductility falls.

## Definition and formulas
- **Strain hardening** (cold work): the increase in strength and hardness when a material is plastically deformed at low temperature [Session 12 Slide 5].
- The cold deformation generates dislocations (density given on the slide as $10^6$ to $10^{12}$ m/m³, as printed); the dislocations entangle and their motion decreases [Session 12 Slide 5].
- **Percent cold work:** $\%\text{CW} = \left(\dfrac{A_0 - A_d}{A_0}\right)\cdot 100$ [Session 12 Slide 6].
- The higher the %CW, the higher $R$ and $\sigma_y$, and the lower the ductility [Session 12 Slide 5].
- Cold work produces a texture: the grains line up in the direction of rolling [Session 12 Slide 6].
- The charts of the slide: yield strength of a 1040 steel, an α-brass and copper against %CW, and their ductility (%EL) against %CW [Session 12 Slide 7].
