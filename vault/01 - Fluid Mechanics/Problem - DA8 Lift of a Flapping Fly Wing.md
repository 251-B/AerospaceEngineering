# Problem DA8: Lift of a Flapping Fly Wing

> Official dimensional-analysis sheet of the course (exam of Dec 16, 2011), statement transcribed from the sheet, solution after its handwritten key. Part of [[Topic 6 - Dimensional Analysis]].
> Method: Π theorem (Notes.pdf, Eq. 6.25), Reynolds similarity with a different fluid (Eqs. 6.57-6.59) and the conversion of a measured table.

## Statement

A paper published in Scientific American in 2001 by Prof. Michael Dickinson describes an experimental facility designed to determine the lift $L$ generated by a fly suspended at a fixed point by the flapping motion of its wings of length $L$ at a frequency $f$. The facility includes a large container filled with mineral oil. A pair of wings, geometrically similar to those of the fly, is placed within the container. Programmable electrical engines move the wings, reproducing the wing motion of the fly at varying frequencies. To mimic the characteristic fly dynamics, the wings in the experiment are made out of a very light material, so that their weight and inertia are entirely negligible. To guide the experiment design and analyze the results follow the steps indicated below.

The sheet uses $L$ both for the lift and for the wing length; from question 1 on the lift is called $F$, as below.

## Hypotheses, Data and Dimensional Table

- The wings have no weight and no inertia of their own, and the fly is held at a fixed point: the lift depends only on the wing motion (frequency and size, the stroke shape fixed by geometric and kinematic similarity) and on the fluid.
- No free surface and negligible weight: no gravity.

| Parameter | Meaning | Dimensions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $F$ | lift (unknown) | $MLT^{-2}$ |
| $f$ | flapping frequency | $T^{-1}$ |
| $L$ | wing length | $L$ |
| $\rho$ | fluid density | $ML^{-3}$ |
| $\mu$ | fluid viscosity | $ML^{-1}T^{-1}$ |

## Application of the Π Theorem

$$ F = F(f, L, \rho, \mu) $$

$n = 4$; $(f, L, \rho)$ have independent dimensions (rows $f(0,0,-1)$, $L(1,0,0)$, $\rho(-3,1,0)$, $\det = -1\cdot(1\cdot 1 - 0\cdot(-3)) = -1 \neq 0$ expanding along the first row), so one group remains.

**Force.** $[F] = [\rho]^a[f]^b[L]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -2 = -b$; $L:\; 1 = -3 + c$; so $\rho f^2 L^4$.

**Viscosity.** $[\mu] = [\rho]^a[f]^b[L]^c$: $M:\; 1 = a$; $T:\; -1 = -b$; $L:\; -1 = -3 + c$; so $\rho f L^2$, and the group is $\rho L^2 f/\mu$, a Reynolds number with the velocity scale $fL$.

$$ \frac{F}{\rho L^4 f^2} = \Pi\left(\frac{\rho L^2 f}{\mu}\right) $$

## Viscosity of the Oil

Physical similarity requires equal Reynolds numbers:

$$ \frac{\rho L^2 f}{\mu} = \frac{\rho_e L_e^2 f_e}{\mu_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mu_e = \mu\,\frac{\rho_e}{\rho}\left(\frac{L_e}{L}\right)^2\frac{f_e}{f} $$

$$ \mu_e = 1.8\times 10^{-5}\times\frac{800}{1.2}\times\left(\frac{250}{2.5}\right)^2\times\frac{0.2}{200} = 1.8\times 10^{-5}\times 666.7\times 10^{4}\times 10^{-3} = 0.12\ \text{kg/(m s)} $$

## Lift Curve of the Fly

Each measured point, with $\mu_e = 0.12$ kg/(m s), is similar to the fly state with the same Reynolds number:

$$ \frac{\rho L^2 f}{\mu} = \frac{\rho_e L_e^2 f_e}{\mu_e} \;\Longrightarrow\; f = \frac{\rho_e}{\rho}\left(\frac{L_e}{L}\right)^2\frac{\mu}{\mu_e}\,f_e = 666.7\times 10^{4}\times 1.5\times 10^{-4}\,f_e = 1000\,f_e $$

and then equal dimensionless lift:

$$ \frac{F}{\rho L^4 f^2} = \frac{F_e}{\rho_e L_e^4 f_e^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; F = \frac{\rho}{\rho_e}\left(\frac{L}{L_e}\right)^4\left(\frac{f}{f_e}\right)^2 F_e = \frac{1.2}{800}\times 10^{-8}\times 10^{6}\,F_e = 1.5\times 10^{-5}\,F_e $$

The same factor follows in the key's form: substituting $f/f_e$ from the first relation, $F = (\rho_e/\rho)(\mu/\mu_e)^2 F_e = 666.7\times(1.5\times 10^{-4})^2 F_e = 1.5\times 10^{-5}F_e$.

## Answers and Checks

- Reduced law: $F/(\rho L^4 f^2) = \Pi(\rho L^2 f/\mu)$.
- Oil viscosity: $\mu_e = 0.12$ kg/(m s).
- Fly: $f = 1000 f_e$ and $F = 1.5\times 10^{-5}F_e$; maximum lift $1.2\times 10^{-4}$ N at 180 Hz.
- Dimensions: $\rho L^4 f^2$ is $ML^{-3}\cdot L^4\cdot T^{-2} = MLT^{-2}$, a force.
- Order of magnitude: a fly of about 10 mg weighs $\sim 10^{-4}$ N, the order of the maximum lift, which is reasonable for a hovering insect.
- Reynolds number of the fly: $\rho L^2 f/\mu = 1.2\times 6.25\times 10^{-6}\times 200/1.8\times 10^{-5} \simeq 83$, neither small nor large: inertia and viscosity both matter, so no limit simplifies the law and the experiment must match $\mathrm{Re}$ exactly.
- Check against the official key: DA8.pdf gives $\mu_e = 0.12$ kg/(m s) and the table $f$ = 140 to 210 Hz with $F$ = $0.2\times 10^{-4}$, $0.5\times 10^{-4}$, $0.72\times 10^{-4}$, $1.07\times 10^{-4}$, $1.2\times 10^{-4}$, $1\times 10^{-4}$, $0.76\times 10^{-4}$, $0.68\times 10^{-4}$ N, the same values.
