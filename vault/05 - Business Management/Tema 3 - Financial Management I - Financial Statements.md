# 💰 Topic 3: Financial Management (I) — Financial Statements & Capital Structure

> **Topic Objective:** Master the analytical tools of corporate financial management. Understand the duality between the firm’s **Economic Structure** (Assets / Investments) and **Financial Structure** (Liabilities + Equity / Sources of Funds) under the value maximization criterion ($\text{Profitability} > \text{Cost}$). Construct, balance, and interpret the two foundational accounting statements: the **Income Statement (Profit & Loss)** and the **Balance Sheet**. Classify all corporate financing alternatives (internal self-financing, equity shares, long-term bonds/loans/leasing, and short-term commercial credit/factoring), calculate financial leverage ($L/E$), and evaluate the **Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)**.

---

## 📑 Core Theoretical Modules

### 1. Introduction: Economic Structure vs Financial Structure
Financial management addresses the collection and allocation of financial capital across two fundamental decision dimensions:
1. **Investing Decisions (What to invest in and when?):** Defines the **Economic Structure** of the enterprise, representing the total resources owned (goods, rights, productive capacity) to generate operational profits.
2. **Financing Decisions (How to obtain the money to invest?):** Defines the **Financial Structure** (Capital Structure), representing the mixture of internal and external funding sources used to acquire those assets.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           ECONOMIC STRUCTURE           │          FINANCIAL STRUCTURE           │
│           ASSETS (What is owned)       │     EQUITY + LIABILITIES (What is owed)│
├────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Productive equipment, land, buildings│ • Shareholders' capital & reserves     │
│ • Inventory, receivables, cash         │ • Bank loans, bonds, supplier credit   │
├────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│        Generates PROFITABILITY         │         Incurs CAPITAL COST            │
└────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### The Golden Rule of Value Creation:
$$\text{Profitability of Assets} > \text{Cost of Capital}$$
A company only creates economic value when the return generated by its productive investments exceeds the aggregate weighted cost of the funds deployed to finance them.

---

### 2. The Income Statement (Profit & Loss Account — P&L)
The Income Statement reports the company's financial performance over a specific accounting period (quarter, half-year, year) based on the **accrual principle** (revenues and expenses are recognized when earned or incurred, regardless of whether cash has actually been received or paid).

#### Extended Multi-Step Format:
$$\begin{aligned}
& \text{Gross Sales Revenue} \\
- & \text{Sales Discounts and Allowances} \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Net Sales Revenue}} \\
- & \text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)} \quad [\text{Initial Stock} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Stock}] \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Gross Profit}} \\
- & \text{Operating Expenses (Wages, Social Security, Utilities, Rent, Depreciation)} \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Operating Income (Operating Profit / EBIT)}} \\
\pm & \text{Non-Operating Items (Financial Interest Revenues/Expenses, Gain/Loss on Asset Sales)} \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Income Before Taxes (Earnings Before Taxes — EBT)}} \\
- & \text{Corporate Income Taxes} \quad [T = t \times \text{EBT}, \text{ with standard } t = 30\%] \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Net Income (Net Profit after Taxes)}} \\
\hline
\hline
\longrightarrow & \begin{cases} \text{Dividends Distributed to Shareholders} \\ \text{Retained Earnings (transferred to Balance Sheet Equity)} \end{cases}
\end{aligned}$$

### 3. The Balance Sheet
The Balance Sheet presents a photographic snapshot of the company's cumulative financial position at a single specific point in time (typically December 31st).

#### The Fundamental Accounting Identity:
$$\mathbf{\text{Assets}} \equiv \mathbf{\text{Liabilities}} + \mathbf{\text{Equity}}$$
$$\mathbf{\text{Total Economic Structure}} \equiv \mathbf{\text{Total Financial Structure}}$$

#### Balance Sheet Structural Classification:
1. **Assets (Use of Funds):**
   * **Non-Current Assets (Fixed / Long-Term Assets, lifespan $> 1$ year):**
     * *Intangible Assets:* Industrial patents, proprietary software, licenses.
     * *Tangible Fixed Assets (Property, Plant & Equipment — PP&E):* Land (non-depreciable), factory buildings, machinery, transport equipment, furniture, office computers.
     * *Accumulated Depreciation:* Negative contra-asset account reflecting cumulative linear depreciation wear-and-tear:
       $$\text{Book Value} = \text{Acquisition Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}$$
     * *Long-Term Financial Investments:* Long-term loans granted, equity holdings in subsidiaries.
   * **Current Assets (Working Capital / Short-Term Assets, turnover $\le 1$ year):**
     * *Inventories / Stocks:* Raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods.
     * *Trade Receivables / Accounts Receivable:* Invoices issued to customers pending collection.
     * *Cash & Cash Equivalents:* Balances in commercial bank checking accounts, petty cash.
2. **Liabilities (External Obligations / Debt):**
   * **Long-Term Liabilities (Non-Current Liabilities, maturity $> 1$ year):**
     * Long-term bank loans, bonds and debt securities payable, financial lease obligations.
   * **Current Liabilities (Short-Term Liabilities, maturity $\le 1$ year):**
     * Trade payables / Accounts payable to suppliers, short-term bank borrowings, accrued unpaid wages, pending corporate taxes.
3. **Stockholders' Equity (Net Worth / Internal Funds):**
   * *Contributed Capital:* Nominal share capital (common stock) subscribed by owners plus additional paid-in capital (share issuance premiums).
   * *Uncalled Share Capital (Negative Equity adjustment):* Portions of subscribed capital committed by shareholders but not yet transferred into company cash.
   * *Retained Earnings / Legal Reserves:* Cumulative undistributed net profits retained inside the business from prior and current periods.

### 4. Sources of Corporate Financing

```
FINANCIAL STRUCTURE
├── COMPANY RESOURCES (Equity / Own Funds)
│   ├── Internal Equity (Self-Financing / Retained Earnings)
│   └── External Equity (Common Stock, Rights Issues)
└── EXTERNAL RESOURCES (Liabilities / Debt)
    ├── Long-Term Debt (Maturity > 1 year)
    │   ├── Debt Securities: Corporate Notes and Bonds
    │   ├── Long-Term Bank Loans & Credit Lines
    │   └── Leasing (Finance Lease vs Operating Lease)
    └── Short-Term Debt (Maturity ≤ 1 year)
        ├── Trade Credit / Accounts Payable (Supplier financing)
        ├── Bank Loans & Commercial Paper Discounts
        └── Factoring (Receivables monetization)
```

#### A. Equity Financing:
* **Internal Equity (Self-Financing):** Resources generated organically by profitable operations:
  $$\text{Pay-out Ratio} = \frac{\text{Dividends}}{\text{Net Profit}}$$
  $$\text{Self-Financing Ratio (Retention Rate)} = \frac{\text{Retained Earnings}}{\text{Net Profit}} = 1 - \text{Pay-out}$$
* **External Equity (Shares / Stocks):** Fractional ownership portions of corporate capital.
  * *Four Valuations of a Share:* Nominal Value ($\frac{\text{Share Capital}}{\text{Number of Shares}}$), Book / Accounting Value ($\frac{\text{Total Equity}}{\text{Number of Shares}}$), Intrinsic Value (discounted future cash flows), and Market Value (stock exchange quote).
  * *Characteristics:* No mandatory repayment date; dividend payments are non-compulsory; junior claim in bankruptcy liquidation.

#### B. Debt Financing:
* **Bonds (Obligaciones y Bonos):** Fixed-income debt securities issued to capital markets. The issuer is the borrower; the bondholder is the lender who receives periodic interest coupons and the principal repayment at maturity.
* **Bank Loans vs Bank Credit Lines (Pólizas de Crédito):**
  * *Bank Loan:* Lump-sum disbursement repaid according to an amortization schedule; interest is charged on the entire initial balance.
  * *Bank Credit Line:* Revolving credit limit where the firm borrows flexibly as needed; interest is charged only on the drawn amount, accompanied by a smaller commitment fee on undrawn balances.
* **Leasing (Arrendamiento Financiero):** Contract granting the use of a fixed asset in exchange for periodic lease payments, with a purchase option at contract end:
  * *Finance Lease:* Long-term, non-cancellable, lessee bears maintenance and obsolescence risk; intent is asset ownership acquisition.
  * *Operating Lease:* Shorter-term, cancellable, lessor covers servicing; equivalent to traditional rental.
* **Short-Term Commercial Financing:**
  * *Supplier Trade Credit:* Postponing payment for raw materials (typically 30, 60, or 90 days) without explicit loan contracts.
  * *Commercial Discount (Descuento Comercial):* Endorsing customer promissory notes or bills of exchange to a bank before maturity in exchange for immediate cash minus discount interest and bank fees.
  * *Factoring:* Selling entire accounts receivable portfolios to a financial factor who assumes administration, collection, and default risk (non-recourse factoring).

### 5. Financial Leverage and Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

#### Financial Leverage and Debt-to-Equity Ratio:
Let $E$ denote Total Equity and $L$ denote Total Liabilities (Total Debt):
$$\% \text{ Equity} = \frac{E}{E + L}, \qquad \% \text{ Debt} = \frac{L}{E + L}$$
$$\text{Debt-to-Equity Ratio} = \frac{L}{E}$$
* The **Debt-to-Equity ratio** measures financial leverage: the degree to which a company finances operations via debt compared to shareholders' funds. Higher leverage magnifies return on equity during economic expansions but amplifies insolvency risk during downturns.

#### Cost of Capital & WACC:
Every financial resource carries a cost:
* **Explicit Cost of Debt ($K_L$):** Contractual interest rate paid to lenders.
* **Implicit Opportunity Cost of Equity ($K_{EQ}$):** The expected rate of return demanded by shareholders given the risk of the business.
* **Weighted Average Cost of Capital ($K_{WACC}$):**
  $$\mathbf{K_{WACC} = K_{EQ} \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E + L}\right) + K_L \cdot \left(\frac{L}{E + L}\right)}$$
  *(Note: In extended corporate finance with tax-deductible interest, the debt component incorporates the tax shield $K_L(1 - t)$).*
