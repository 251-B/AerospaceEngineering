# Bank C15 — LowOrbit: the drivers behind a cost-leadership strategy in satellite buses

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

LowOrbit S.A. sells the "bus" (platform) of small Earth-observation satellites at 40% below its rivals. It builds one standard design with interchangeable modules instead of custom platforms, and has designed every part to be machined in a single setup. Its line now produces 120 buses a year, and the hours per bus have fallen by half since the first 20 units as the teams repeated the same tasks. It bought a solar-cell supplier located next to the plant, keeps the factory running in three shifts, and its managers are paid for removing waste in every process.

## (a) Identify the drivers of cost advantage present in LowOrbit [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Economies of learning, economies of scale, production techniques, product design, input costs, capacity utilization, residual efficiency [Slides 21-22].

### Checkpoint

- Product design: one standard design, interchangeable modules, design for manufacture.
- Economies of scale: 120 buses a year.
- Economies of learning: hours per bus halved with repetition.
- Input costs: ownership of a low-cost input (solar cells), location.
- Capacity utilization: three shifts.
- Residual efficiency: managers paid to remove waste (motivation, culture).

### Solution

| Driver [Slides 21-22] | Evidence at LowOrbit |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Product design** | Standardization of designs and components; parts designed for manufacture (single setup) |
| **Economies of scale** | 120 buses a year: specialization and division of labour |
| **Economies of learning** | Hours per bus halved as routines improved |
| **Input costs** | Ownership of a low-cost input (the solar-cell supplier) and a location advantage |
| **Capacity utilization** | Three shifts spread the fixed costs over more units |
| **Residual efficiency** | Managerial efficiency, motivation and culture of eliminating waste |

## (b) Explain what a cost advantage is and what risk LowOrbit runs with this strategy [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Compare the two sources of competitive advantage [Slide 20].

### Checkpoint

- Cost advantage: offering a similar product at a lower cost than rivals.
- Risk: standardization limits customization; customers who value unique features may prefer differentiated rivals; rivals may copy the cost drivers.

### Solution

A **cost advantage** means offering a **similar product at a lower cost** than competitors, whereas a differentiation advantage obtains a **price premium from a unique product** [Slide 20]. LowOrbit's standard bus is its main cost driver, but it is also its main risk: customers whose missions need a custom platform will go to differentiated rivals, and competitors can copy standardization and scale. To keep the advantage it must keep reducing costs faster than its rivals (learning, scale) while keeping the product "similar enough" in performance to theirs.
