## F09.1 When each efficiency index is measured

- id: F09-S1
- concept_id: u4-efficiency-indexes

To control the efficiency of the production system, productivity is measured a priori and cost analysis a posteriori.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

It is the other way round: productivity is measured a posteriori, on what has already been produced, and cost analysis (break-even point, operating leverage) is done a priori.

## F09.3 Doubling output and every input

- id: F09-S3
- concept_id: u4-total-productivity

A repair shop that doubles the value of its services while the labour, capital, raw materials and energy it uses also double keeps the same total productivity.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Total productivity is goods and services divided by labour + capital + raw materials + energy; doubling the numerator and every term of the denominator leaves the ratio unchanged.

## F09.4 What the break-even point covers

- id: F09-S4
- concept_id: u4-bep-definition

The break-even point is the volume at which revenue covers the variable costs, so it does not depend on the cost structure of the firm.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

At the break-even point revenue covers the total costs, fixed plus variable, and from there the firm starts to make profits; the course states that the break-even point depends on the cost structure.

## F09.5 A break-even volume from the data

- id: F09-S5
- concept_id: u4-bep-calculation

A maker of composite cabin panels with fixed costs of 120,000 € a year, a price of 50 € and a variable cost of 30 € per panel breaks even at 6,000 panels.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The unit margin is $m = 50 - 30 = 20$ €, and $Q_0 = FC/m = 120{,}000/20 = 6{,}000$ panels.

## F09.6 Two cost structures with one revenue line

- id: F09-S6
- concept_id: u4-cost-structure-cases

Of two firms with the same revenue line, the one with higher fixed costs and a lower variable cost per unit has the steeper profit line beyond its break-even point.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The slope of the profit line is the unit margin $m = P - VC$: with the same price, a lower variable cost gives a larger margin and a steeper line, as in case A of the slide, which also carries the higher fixed costs.

## F09.7 How the operating leverage is defined

- id: F09-S7
- concept_id: u4-ol-definition

The operating leverage is the change in operating profit, in euros, divided by the change in the number of units sold.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The operating leverage divides relative changes, $OL = (\Delta\text{Profit}/\text{Profit})/(\Delta Q/Q)$; the ratio of absolute changes in euros per unit is only the unit margin $m$.

### Source

Topic 4, Slide 39.

The course uses the operating leverage as an index of economic (operating) risk to compare businesses of the same sector.

### Answer

T

The comments on the OL say that it captures the sensitivity of operating income to sales, that it is an index of economic risk and that it is used to compare businesses in the same sector.

## F09.9 Margin of safety as a percentage

- id: F09-S9
- concept_id: u4-margin-of-safety

In percentage, the margin of safety of the course divides the units sold above the break-even point by the actual volume of sales.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The slide divides by the break-even volume: $MS = (Q - Q^*)/Q^*$. Dividing by the actual volume $Q$ is not the course's formula.

### Source

Topic 4, Slide 43.

## F09.10 Selling twice the break-even volume

- id: F09-S10
- concept_id: u4-ol-from-bep

A firm that sells twice its break-even volume has an operating leverage of 2, so a 10% rise in its sales raises its operating profit by 5%.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The leverage is right, $OL = Q/(Q - Q^*) = 2Q^*/Q^* = 2$, but its effect is wrong: profit changes by $OL \times 10\% = 20\%$, not 5%, because the OL multiplies the change in sales.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 40 and 42.
