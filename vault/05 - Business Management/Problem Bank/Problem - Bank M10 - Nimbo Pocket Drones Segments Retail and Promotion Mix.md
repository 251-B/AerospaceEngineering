# Bank M10 — Nimbo pocket drones: segmentation, positioning, life cycle, retail distribution and promotion mix

> [!info] Practice item, not an official exercise
> Written for the bank in the style of Exercise 2 of the examples PDF (TechNova Solutions), here for a firm that sells to consumers. The company is invented. Concepts: Topic 5, Slides 9-15 (segmentation, positioning, market share), 25 (product life-cycle), 29-33 (promotion mix) and 34-37 (distribution); Examples PDF, Exercise 2.

## Statement

Nimbo Drones is a company founded in 2021 in Valencia that designs folding camera drones of less than 250 grams for leisure use. Before its first launch it divided the leisure-drone market of Spain and Portugal into groups of buyers, and it found two groups that the large brands served badly: teenagers aged 12 to 16, whose drones are bought by their parents once, as a birthday or Christmas gift, and adults aged 25 to 45 with a medium income who travel several times a year and post videos of their trips on social networks. Nimbo offers each group its own drone: the Nimbo Junior, covered with foam bumpers, sold in bright colours at 89 € and launched in September 2026, and the Nimbo Travel, with a 4K camera, sold at 249 € with the slogan "Your trip, seen from above, fits in your pocket" and advertised as weighing half as much as the best-selling drone of the market leader. An independent consultancy estimates that Nimbo sells 7% of the leisure drones bought in the two countries.

Sales of leisure drones of less than 250 grams in Spain and Portugal rose from 40,000 units in 2023 to 95,000 units in 2026. Nimbo has no shops of its own, and its website does not sell: it only lists the stores where the drones can be bought. Nimbo sells its whole production to a national wholesaler of consumer electronics, which resells the drones to electronics chains, toy shops, large supermarkets and photography shops; the firm wants its drones on the shelves of as many shops as possible, and they are now in more than 1,800 outlets of both countries.

To make itself known, Nimbo pays for short videos shown before YouTube clips and for posters in the arrival halls of the airports of Madrid, Barcelona and Lisbon. Until 31 December every Nimbo Travel comes with a second battery free of charge. The firm sponsors a school drone-racing league and sends press releases to technology magazines. It sends personalised e-mails to the users registered in its flight application, inviting them to book a free online flying lesson, and counts the bookings that each e-mail produces. At weekends Nimbo demonstrators fly the drones inside the large electronics stores, answer the customers' questions and help each one choose a model. Both drones have a two-year warranty and are delivered in a box of recycled cardboard.

a) Which segmentation criteria did Nimbo use, and which segmentation (targeting) strategy does it follow? Justify each with a fact of the text, and say which fact of the first paragraph is not a segmentation criterion. (0.75 points)

b) On what is the positioning of the Nimbo Travel based? At which stage of the product life-cycle are the category of leisure drones of less than 250 grams and the Nimbo Junior? (0.5 points)

c) Which distribution strategy does Nimbo follow? Describe the type and length of the channel, whether it is B2B or B2C and the distribution intensity, and say whether the website is a direct channel. (0.5 points)

d) Identify every tool of the promotion mix that appears in the last paragraph, each with its fact, and say which facts of that paragraph do not belong to promotion. (0.75 points)

## (a) Segmentation criteria and targeting strategy [0.75 pts]

### Hint

The course groups the criteria into geographical, demographic, and psychological and purchase behaviour. Then count the segments the firm serves and ask whether each gets its own product and marketing mix.

### Checkpoint

- Geographical: Spain and Portugal.
- Demographic: age (12 to 16; 25 to 45) and level of income (medium income).
- Psychological and purchase behaviour: lifestyle (travellers who post videos of their trips); frequency of purchase (a single purchase as a gift, temporary rather than regular).
- Strategy: two segments, each with its own drone, price and message, wide differentiated (multisegment) marketing.
- Not a segmentation criterion: the 7% share is a market-share measure of the demand analysis.

### Solution

#### Criteria of the course found in the text

Market segmentation identifies the smaller markets, the segments, that exist within a larger market [Topic 5, Slide 9]. Each fact of the first paragraph matches a group of criteria of Slide 10 [Topic 5, Slide 10]:

| Fact of the text | Criterion of the course |
| :--- | :--- |
| "The leisure-drone market of Spain and Portugal" | Geographical (national, international, regional) |
| "Teenagers aged 12 to 16" and "adults aged 25 to 45 with a medium income" | Demographic: age and level of income |
| "Travel several times a year and post videos of their trips on social networks" | Psychological: lifestyle |
| "Bought by their parents once, as a birthday or Christmas gift" | Purchase behaviour: frequency of purchase (temporary, not regular) |

"Two groups that the large brands served badly" is the first benefit of segmentation named by the course: it identifies the market needs that are not being satisfied [Topic 5, Slide 11].

#### Targeting strategy

Nimbo serves two segments, and "offers each group its own drone": a different product (foam bumpers against a 4K camera), a different price (89 € against 249 €) and a different message. A firm that targets two or more segments with distinct products and a separate marketing mix for each follows wide differentiated (multisegment) marketing [Topic 5, Slide 12]. It is not undifferentiated marketing, which offers one marketing mix to the whole market, nor concentrated (niche) marketing, which aims at a single segment, the case of VectraSky in Bank M03.

#### What is not a segmentation criterion

"Nimbo sells 7% of the leisure drones bought in the two countries" is the brand's market share, brand sales divided by the total sales of the market of reference, a measure of the analysis of the demand [Topic 5, Slide 15]. It describes how well Nimbo sells, not a characteristic used to divide the buyers into groups.

## (b) Positioning and stage of the life-cycle [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Slide 14 lists the bases of a positioning: product characteristics, benefits, user characteristics, comparison with another product and the price-quality relationship. For the life-cycle, the same curve can describe a product category, a product form or a brand.

### Checkpoint

- Positioning of the Nimbo Travel: benefit of the product ("your trip, seen from above, fits in your pocket"), user characteristics (travellers) and comparison with another product (half the weight of the leader's best seller).
- Category: sales from 40,000 to 95,000 units in three years (+137.5%), growth stage (sales take-off).
- Nimbo Junior: launched in September 2026, introductory stage (launching a new product).

### Solution

#### Bases of the positioning

Positioning is the image the firm tries to create for its product in the perception of the target market [Topic 5, Slide 13]. The Nimbo Travel combines three of the bases of Slide 14 [Topic 5, Slide 14]:

- "Your trip, seen from above, fits in your pocket": a benefit of the product, a small drone that films the trip.
- The slogan speaks to people who travel and post their trips, the user characteristics of the segment.
- "Half as much as the best-selling drone of the market leader": placing the product by comparing it with another product.

#### Two curves of the life-cycle

The product life-cycle can apply to a product category, a product form or a brand [Topic 5, Slide 25], so the category and Nimbo's new model can sit at different stages.

- Category of leisure drones of less than 250 grams: sales rose from 40,000 to 95,000 units between 2023 and 2026, a rise of $(95{,}000 - 40{,}000)/40{,}000 = 137.5\%$ in three years. Sales are taking off, the growth stage [Topic 5, Slide 25]. It is not yet maturity, the longest part of the curve, where sales stop growing.
- Nimbo Junior: "launched in September 2026", in the same year in which the category reaches 95,000 units, so the model has just reached the market and is in the introductory stage, the launching of a new product [Topic 5, Slide 25].

## (c) Distribution strategy [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Follow the drone from the factory to the buyer and count the independent firms it passes through. Then ask who the final buyer is and how many outlets the firm wants.

### Checkpoint

- Indirect channel: a wholesaler and retailers are independent intermediaries.
- Two levels of intermediaries: producer, wholesaler, retailer, consumer (longer than a zero- or one-level channel).
- B2C: the final buyers are consumers, for their personal use.
- Intensive distribution: "as many shops as possible", more than 1,800 outlets.
- The website is not a channel: it does not sell, it only informs.

### Solution

#### Type and length of the channel

"Nimbo sells its whole production to a national wholesaler of consumer electronics, which resells the drones to electronics chains, toy shops, large supermarkets and photography shops": the producer entrusts the distribution to independent intermediaries, so the channel is indirect [Topic 5, Slide 35]. The wholesaler sells to other intermediaries and not to the final consumers, and the shops are retailers, which sell consumer goods directly to the final consumers for their personal or household use [Topic 5, Slide 35]. Extension: the level of a channel is the number of intermediaries between the producer and the final buyer; this one has two levels (producer, wholesaler, retailer, consumer), longer than the zero-level channel of TechNova [Examples PDF, Exercise 2 (c)].

#### Market and intensity

The final buyers are parents and travellers who buy for their own use, so the market is business-to-consumer (B2C). "On the shelves of as many shops as possible ... more than 1,800 outlets" is intensive distribution [Topic 5, Slide 37]. Extension (definition not given on the slide): intensive distribution uses as many outlets as possible, against a limited number of chosen outlets (selective) or a single outlet per area (exclusive). The retailers also perform a function of the intermediaries: they offer the consumer a wide product mix from different producers [Topic 5, Slide 36].

#### The website

A direct channel is one in which the producer sells directly to the final buyers [Topic 5, Slide 35]. The website "does not sell: it only lists the stores", so no sale passes through it and it is not a direct channel; it is a source of information for the buyer.

## (d) Tools of the promotion mix [0.75 pts]

### Hint

The five activities of the promotion mix are advertising, sales promotion, public relations, direct marketing and personal selling. Look for the paid mass media, the incentive for a limited period, the image of the firm, the measurable response and the person-to-person contact.

### Checkpoint

- Advertising: paid videos before YouTube clips and posters at three airports.
- Sales promotion: a free second battery with every Nimbo Travel until 31 December.
- Public relations: sponsorship of a school drone-racing league and press releases to technology magazines.
- Direct marketing: personalised e-mails to registered users, with the bookings counted.
- Personal selling: demonstrators in the stores who answer questions and help each customer choose.
- Not promotion: the two-year warranty (a product attribute) and the recycled cardboard box (packaging).

### Solution

#### The five tools in the text

Marketers' efforts to communicate with their target audiences are called promotion, and the promotion mix has five activities [Topic 5, Slide 29].

| Fact of the text | Tool of the course |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Pays for short videos shown before YouTube clips and for posters in the arrival halls of the airports" | Advertising: a paid form of non-personal communication through the mass media (internet, billboards) by an identified sponsor [Topic 5, Slide 30] |
| "Until 31 December every Nimbo Travel comes with a second battery free of charge" | Sales promotion: an incentive for a limited period, which does not ordinarily come with the product [Topic 5, Slide 31] |
| "Sponsors a school drone-racing league and sends press releases to technology magazines" | Public relations: sponsorship and relations with the media, to build a favourable image of the firm [Topic 5, Slide 32] |
| "Personalised e-mails ... and counts the bookings that each e-mail produces" | Direct marketing: an interactive system that uses a medium (internet) to obtain a specific, measurable response [Topic 5, Slide 32] |
| "Demonstrators ... answer the customers' questions and help each one choose a model" | Personal selling: a person-to-person process in which the seller learns the buyer's wants and seeks to satisfy them with a sale [Topic 5, Slide 33] |

The free battery is a sales promotion and not direct marketing: it is an incentive offered to every buyer for a limited period, while the e-mails ask for a response (a booking) and measure it.

#### What does not belong to promotion

"Both drones have a two-year warranty" is a product attribute, the warranty [Topic 5, Slide 23], and "delivered in a box of recycled cardboard" is the packaging of the product [Topic 5, Slide 22]. Both belong to the product policy of the marketing mix, not to communication; the examples PDF draws the same line between product attributes and communication [Examples PDF, Exercise 2 (b)].

## Interpretation

A product for consumers that are spread over two countries and buy on impulse or as a gift needs to be in many shops, so Nimbo leaves the physical distribution to a wholesaler and to retailers and keeps for itself the communication that pulls the consumer to the shelf. Serving two segments explains why the firm runs two marketing mixes at once, with a cheap, robust drone for gifts and a light, well-equipped one for travellers.
