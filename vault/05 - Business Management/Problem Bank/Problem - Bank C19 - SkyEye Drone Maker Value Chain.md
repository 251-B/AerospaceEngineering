# 🏢 Bank C19 — SkyEye Drones: mapping activities onto Porter's value chain

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

SkyEye Drones S.L. makes surveying drones. Its activities include: (1) receiving and storing motors and cameras from suppliers; (2) assembling and testing the drones; (3) packing and shipping them to customers; (4) online advertising and demonstrations to surveying firms; (5) repairs and software updates after sale; (6) negotiating contracts with component suppliers; (7) recruiting and training technicians; (8) developing the flight software and testing new materials; (9) accounting, legal advice, quality assurance and general management.

## (a) Classify each activity as a primary or a support activity of the value chain, naming it [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Primary: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service [Slide 30]. Support: procurement, human resource management, technological development, infrastructure [Slide 31].

### Checkpoint

- Primary: 1 inbound logistics, 2 operations, 3 outbound logistics, 4 marketing and sales, 5 service.
- Support: 6 procurement, 7 HRM, 8 technological development, 9 infrastructure.

### Solution

| Activity | Value-chain activity | Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Receiving and storing components | Inbound logistics | Primary [Slide 30] |
| 2. Assembling and testing | Operations | Primary |
| 3. Packing and shipping | Outbound logistics | Primary |
| 4. Advertising and demonstrations | Marketing and sales | Primary |
| 5. Repairs and updates after sale | Service | Primary |
| 6. Negotiating with suppliers | Procurement | Support [Slide 31] |
| 7. Recruiting and training | Human resource management | Support |
| 8. Flight software and materials | Technological development | Support |
| 9. Accounting, legal, quality, management | Infrastructure | Support |

## (b) Explain what the value chain is for and where SkyEye's margin comes from [0.75 pts]

### Hint

The chain arranges resources into activities customers are willing to pay for; the management of the activities and of their linkages gives the competitive advantage [Slides 28-29].

### Checkpoint

- The activities together produce the margin [Slide 29], read (extension) as what customers pay minus the cost of all activities.
- Competitive advantage comes from managing the activities and their linkages (e.g. software development linked to after-sales updates).

### Solution

The value chain (Porter, 1985) shows that a firm is not a random collection of resources but a set of **activities** that produce goods customers are willing to pay for; together they produce the **margin** shown at the end of the chain [Slide 29], usually read (extension) as the difference between what customers pay and the cost of all the activities. The **management of the activities and of their linkages** is what gives the competitive advantage: for SkyEye, the link between technological development (activity 8) and service (activity 5) allows software updates that keep old drones competitive, a reason for customers to pay more.
