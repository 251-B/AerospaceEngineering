# Bank F11 — Product, price, place and promotion decisions

> [!info] Practice item, not an official exercise
> Ten True/False statements written for the bank in the style of Exercise 4 of the examples PDF (+0.15 right, −0.15 wrong, a short reason for each answer). Content: Topic 5, Slides 19, 22, 25, 28, 30, 32 and 34-35; Examples PDF, Exercise 2.

## F11.1 What the customer actually buys

- id: F11-S1
- concept_id: u5-core-vs-total-product

The basic (core) product is everything the customer pays for; post-sale services and the image of the product lie outside the product.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The basic (core or generic) product is less than the total product people buy: customers also buy benefits such as quality, design, packaging, size, post-sale services and image, which are part of the product.

- concept_id: u5-psychosocial-attributes

Customer satisfaction depends both on the physical-technical characteristics of the product and on psychosocial attributes, which the course lists as brand, design, packaging and quality.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Slide 19 names these two sources of customer satisfaction and lists brand, design, packaging and quality as the psychosocial attributes of the product.

## F11.3 The label of a container

- id: F11-S3
- concept_id: u5-packaging-label

Packaging covers the design and production of the container or wrapper of a product, including its label, which carries the compulsory information about the product.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

This is the course's definition of packaging; besides carrying the label, packaging protects the content, eases transport and can make the product more convenient and attractive.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 22.

## F11.4 Two ways of judging a design

- id: F11-S4
- concept_id: u5-design-perspectives

Product design is judged only from an aesthetic perspective; how easy the product is to use is a matter of its quality, not of its design.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Design combines physical appearance, colour, shape and ease of use, and it is judged from an aesthetic (beauty) and a functional (ease of use) perspective. Ease of use is part of the design.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 22.

## F11.5 The stage in which sales take off

- id: F11-S5
- concept_id: u5-plc-maturity

Maturity is usually the shortest stage of the product life cycle, and it is the stage in which the sales of the product take off.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Sales take off in the growth stage. Maturity is the longest part of the life cycle, between growth and decline.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 25.

## F11.6 Categories, forms and brands

- id: F11-S6
- concept_id: u5-plc-scope

The product life cycle can be applied to a whole product category, to a product form or to a single brand.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Slide 25 states that the life cycle can apply to a product category, a product form or a brand, each going through introduction, growth, maturity and decline.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 25.

## F11.7 What the mark-up measures

- id: F11-S7
- concept_id: u5-cost-plus-markup

In cost-plus pricing the mark-up is the difference between the cost of a good and its selling price.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Cost-plus pricing is one of the two methods of the cost approach; the mark-up added to the cost is, by the course's definition, the gap between the cost of the good and its selling price.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 28.

## F11.8 The approach behind break-even pricing

- id: F11-S8
- concept_id: u5-pricing-approaches

Break-even analysis is a method of the demand approach to pricing, because it starts from the number of units the market will buy.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Break-even analysis belongs to the cost approach, together with cost-plus pricing; it starts from the fixed and variable costs (Topic 4). The other two approaches are the competition and the demand approach.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 28.

## F11.9 Own website and own sales team

- id: F11-S9
- concept_id: u5-direct-channel

An engine maker that sells its spare parts to airlines only through its own website and its own sales team uses a direct distribution channel.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

In a direct channel the producer sells directly to the final buyers with no independent intermediary; the website and the sales team belong to the producer, as in the TechNova case.

### Source

Topic 5, Slides 34-35; Examples PDF, Exercise 2 (c).

## F11.10 A measurable response at any location

- id: F11-S10
- concept_id: u5-direct-marketing

Direct marketing is a paid, non-personal communication through the mass media by an identified sponsor, and it does not seek any specific response from the audience.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

That describes advertising. Direct marketing is an interactive system that uses one or more media (telephone, internet, direct mail, social media) to obtain a specific, measurable response or transaction at any location.

### Source

Topic 5, Slides 30 and 32.
