## B04.1 Steps 3 to 6 of the decision-making process

- id: B04-Q1
- concept_id: u6-decision-process-order
- format: ordering
- asks: definition

Which sequence gives steps 3 to 6 of the decision-making process in the order of the course?

a) Analysing the alternatives, considering alternative courses of action, choosing an alternative, implementing the decision
b) Considering alternative courses of action, analysing the alternatives, choosing an alternative, implementing the decision
c) Considering alternative courses of action, choosing an alternative, analysing the alternatives, implementing the decision
d) Gathering information, considering alternative courses of action, choosing an alternative, evaluating the results

### Answer

b

### Explanation

The eight steps are: recognizing the problem or opportunity, gathering information, considering alternative courses of action, analysing the alternatives, choosing an alternative, implementing the decision, evaluating the results and implementing changes. Steps 3 to 6 are therefore option b.

- a) The alternatives cannot be analysed before they have been considered (generated): step 3 comes before step 4.
- c) Choosing before analysing inverts steps 4 and 5; the choice must rest on the analysis of the alternatives.
- d) Gathering information is step 2 and evaluating the results is step 7, so this list skips the analysis and the implementation.

## B04.2 A known fault with a written procedure

- id: B04-Q2
- concept_id: u6-decision-types
- format: identify
- asks: classification

A hangar supervisor meets a hydraulic leak that is well known to the team, and the maintenance manual gives the approved repair. Which type of decision does the supervisor take?

a) An innovative decision
b) An adaptable decision
c) A strategic decision
d) A routine (planned) decision

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The problem is known and well defined and the alternative solution is known in advance (the manual), which is the definition of a routine, planned decision.

- a) An innovative decision faces an unusual, ambiguous problem with unplanned alternatives, which is not the case of a fault the manual already covers.
- b) An adaptable decision lies between the two extremes, for a problem or alternatives only partly known; here both are fully known.
- c) Strategic classifies a decision by management level (top management), not by the type of problem and alternatives, and a single repair is not a strategic matter.

## B04.3 Presenting the results to the press

- id: B04-Q3
- concept_id: u6-mintzberg-roles
- format: identify
- asks: classification

At an air show, the CEO of a satellite manufacturer explains the firm's yearly results and plans to journalists. Which managerial role of Mintzberg does the CEO play?

a) Spokesperson, an informational role
b) Negotiator, a decisional role
c) Resource allocator, a decisional role
d) Leader, an interpersonal role

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Transmitting information about the firm to people outside it is the spokesperson role, one of the three informational roles (monitor, disseminator, spokesperson). The slide lists the roles by name only; the meaning used here is the usual one of Mintzberg's classification.

- b) The negotiator role means bargaining with another party (a supplier, a union); giving information to the press involves no negotiation.
- c) The resource allocator decides how money, people and time are distributed inside the firm, which is not what a press briefing does.
- d) The leader role concerns motivating and directing the firm's own employees, while here the audience is outside the firm.

## B04.4 Understanding how things operate

- id: B04-Q4
- concept_id: u6-managerial-skills
- format: complete-sentence
- asks: definition

Complete the sentence: "The ability to understand how things operate, which weighs most in low management, is the ______ skill."

a) conceptual
b) interpersonal
c) technical
d) decisional

### Answer

c

### Explanation

The course defines the technical skill as the ability to understand how things operate; its share is largest for first-line supervisors and smallest for top management.

- a) The conceptual skill is the ability to see the firm as a whole, and it weighs most in top management, not in low management.
- b) The interpersonal skill is the ability to interact with people at work successfully, needed at every level of the hierarchy.
- d) Decisional is a group of Mintzberg's managerial roles, not one of the three basic managerial skills.

## B04.5 The professional bureaucracy

- id: B04-Q5
- concept_id: u6-professional-bureaucracy
- format: false-statement
- asks: definition

Which statement about the professional bureaucracy is false?

a) It relies on high horizontal specialization with highly qualified employees.
b) It coordinates through the standardization of outputs, skills and knowledge.
c) It concentrates decision-making power at the strategic apex.
d) It suits big organizations in a stable but complex environment.

### Answer

c

### Explanation

The professional bureaucracy is decentralized: qualified professionals of the operating core decide on their own work, and the staff mainly supports that core. Centralized power belongs to the machine bureaucracy (and direct supervision by the apex to the simple structure).

- a) This statement is true: high horizontal specialization with highly qualified employees is listed as a feature of the configuration.
- b) This statement is true: standardization of outputs, skills and knowledge is how the professionals are coordinated.
- d) This statement is true: the configuration is that of big organizations in a stable but complex environment.

## B04.6 Talking past the organization chart

- id: B04-Q6
- concept_id: u6-formal-informal-structure
- format: what-happens
- asks: application

The organization chart of a drone maker says that problems between the design and the test departments go through the two department heads. In practice, the engineers of both departments solve them by talking directly at the coffee machine. What does this illustrate?

a) An informal structure, which together with the formal structure forms the real structure of the firm
b) A change of the formal structure, because management has redrawn the organization chart
c) Centralization of decision-making in the two department heads
d) The creation of a technostructure that designs new processes

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Informal contacts, communications and ways of doing things that employees develop on their own are the informal structure; combined with the formal structure defined by management, they give the real structure.

- b) The formal structure is defined by management, and in the case management has changed nothing: the chart still prescribes the old route.
- c) The engineers bypass the heads, so decisions are taken lower down, which is the opposite of centralization.
- d) The technostructure is a formal part made of analysts who design systems and processes, not a spontaneous habit of engineers.

## B04.7 A three-person CubeSat start-up

- id: B04-Q7
- concept_id: u6-simple-structure
- format: identify
- asks: classification

A start-up of three people builds CubeSat components. The founder assigns and checks the work of the two engineers directly; there are no middle managers, analysts or support departments. Which basic configuration does it have?

a) Adhocracy
b) Machine bureaucracy
c) Division organization
d) Simple structure

### Answer

d

### Explanation

Only a strategic apex (the founder) and an operating core (the engineers), coordination by direct supervision of the apex, and a start-up or very small business: these are the features of the simple structure.

- a) An adhocracy mixes middle line, operating core and staff in a matrix of experts and is used as a complementary structure for specific projects; here there is no middle line or staff to mix.
- b) A machine bureaucracy needs many rules, many hierarchical layers and a big, mature organization, the opposite of a three-person firm.
- c) A division organization requires autonomous divisions led by a middle line, which a firm with no middle managers cannot have.

## B04.8 Recruitment and selection

- id: B04-Q8
- concept_id: u6-recruitment-selection
- format: complete-sentence
- asks: definition

Complete the sentence: "Within human resources management, ______ attracts candidates to the firm and comes before ______, which chooses among them."

a) selection ... recruitment
b) recruitment ... selection
c) training ... performance appraisal
d) compensation ... recruitment

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Human resources management is the process of recruiting, selecting, training, appraising and compensating employees, in that order. Recruitment attracts candidates and selection then chooses among them.

- a) This inverts the two activities: selection cannot choose among candidates who have not yet been attracted by recruitment.
- c) Training and performance appraisal act on people already hired, so neither attracts or chooses candidates.
- d) Compensation rewards employees already in the firm, and recruitment comes first in the process, not after compensation.

## B04.9 The activities of controlling

- id: B04-Q9
- concept_id: u6-controlling
- format: ordering
- asks: definition

Which list gives the activities of controlling in the order of the course?

a) Establishing standards, measuring actual performance, identifying deviations, taking corrective action if required
b) Measuring actual performance, establishing standards, taking corrective action, identifying deviations
c) Developing forecasts, defining strategic objectives, developing alternative courses of action, deciding on a course of action
d) Recognizing the problem, gathering information, choosing an alternative, evaluating the results

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Controlling ensures that activities provide the planned results: a standard is set first, actual performance is measured against it, deviations are identified and corrective action is taken if required.

- b) Performance cannot be judged before a standard exists, and corrective action presupposes that a deviation has been identified first.
- c) These are the four main steps of planning, the function that sets the goals controlling later checks.
- d) These are steps of the decision-making process, not the activities of controlling.

## B04.10 Why an adhocracy for a prototype project

- id: B04-Q10
- concept_id: u6-adhocracy
- format: cause-effect
- asks: cause

An aircraft manufacturer sets up a team of designers, test engineers and suppliers' specialists to build an eVTOL demonstrator in eighteen months, organized as an adhocracy. Why does that configuration suit the project?

a) Because its high standardization and formalization reduce the errors of a new design
b) Because it concentrates every decision in the strategic apex, which speeds up approvals
c) Because its low standardization and formalization, decentralized decisions and matrix departmentalization give the flexibility an innovative project needs
d) Because its many hierarchical layers and low span of control keep a close check on each specialist

### Answer

c

### Explanation

The adhocracy has high horizontal and low vertical specialization, low standardization and formalization, decentralization as the key to flexibility and matrix departmentalization, and it is used as a complementary structure for specific projects, which is the situation of the demonstrator.

- a) High standardization and formalization describe the machine bureaucracy; in the adhocracy both are low and the technostructure disappears.
- b) Centralization in the apex is the feature of the simple structure and of the machine bureaucracy; the adhocracy is decentralized.
- d) Many layers and a low span of control are features of the machine bureaucracy, which would slow down an innovative project.
