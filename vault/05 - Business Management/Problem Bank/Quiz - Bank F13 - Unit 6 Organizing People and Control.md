## F13.1 The double process of organizing

- id: F13-S1
- concept_id: u6-organizing-differentiation

Organizing is a double process: differentiation divides the work of the firm into distinct tasks, and integration coordinates those tasks towards the firm's goals.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The course states that organizing implies differentiation and integration: its purpose is to give each person a distinct task and to make sure the tasks are coordinated so that the firm accomplishes its goals.

## F13.2 What the real structure is

- id: F13-S2
- concept_id: u6-formal-informal-structure

The real structure of a firm is the formal structure defined by management; the informal contacts that employees develop are left out of it.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The real structure combines the formal structure defined by management and the informal structure (the contacts, communications and ways of doing things that employees develop).

## F13.3 Vertical specialization

- id: F13-S3
- concept_id: u6-specialization

Vertical specialization measures the number of different tasks that each worker performs.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The number of tasks is horizontal specialization. Vertical specialization refers to the autonomy that a worker has to organize his or her own job.

## F13.4 Layers and span of control

- id: F13-S4
- concept_id: u6-structural-configuration

The number of hierarchical layers and the span of control are elements of the structural configuration, one of the factors that determine the organizational structure.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The structural configuration groups the type of departmentalization, the number of layers, the span of control and the size of the administrative component, next to specialization, standardization, formalization and centralization.

Topic 6, Slide 13.

## F13.5 Who forms the middle line

- id: F13-S5
- concept_id: u6-parts-formal-structure

The middle line of a formal structure is formed by the employees who carry out the basic production work of the firm, such as the mechanics who assemble the landing gear.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The employees who do the basic work form the operating core. The middle line is middle management, the managers between the strategic apex and the operating core.

## F13.6 Departmentalization of two configurations

- id: F13-S6
- concept_id: u6-departmentalization

The machine bureaucracy relies on functional departmentalization, whereas the adhocracy relies on matrix departmentalization.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Functional departmentalization is listed among the features of the machine bureaucracy and matrix departmentalization among those of the adhocracy; the professional bureaucracy can use all types.

## F13.7 The staff of a professional bureaucracy

- id: F13-S7
- concept_id: u6-professional-bureaucracy

In a professional bureaucracy, such as an aerospace research centre staffed by qualified engineers, the support staff exists mainly to serve the operating core.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

In this configuration the highly qualified professionals of the operating core do the key work, and the staff mainly supports them.

### Source

Topic 6, Slide 18.

## F13.8 The technostructure in an adhocracy

- id: F13-S8
- concept_id: u6-adhocracy

In an adhocracy the technostructure grows in importance, because its analysts must standardize the work of the many experts.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

In the adhocracy the technostructure disappears and standardization and formalization are low; the middle line, the operating core and the staff are mixed in project teams.

## F13.9 Appraisal and pay in human resources

- id: F13-S9
- concept_id: u6-hr-staffing

Performance appraisal systems and compensation programmes belong to the directing side of human resources management, together with motivation and leadership.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Directing covers motivation, leadership and communication. Performance appraisal, compensation and incentive programmes belong to staffing, together with recruiting, selecting and training.

## F13.10 Reactive, proactive and interactive control

- id: F13-S10
- concept_id: u6-control-types

Reactive, proactive and interactive control are types of control classified according to when the control takes place.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The course classifies control by who is controlled, by what area, by when it takes place (reactive, proactive or interactive) and by frequency (constant, periodic or occasional).
