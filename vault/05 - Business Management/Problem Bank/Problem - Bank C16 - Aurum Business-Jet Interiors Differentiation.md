# Bank C16 — Aurum Interiors: tangible and intangible differentiation in business jets

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

Aurum Interiors S.L. designs cabins for private jets and charges about twice what its competitors charge. Its cabins use sound-absorbing panels that make them the quietest in the market and seats 20% lighter than average, and they are delivered with a personalised maintenance plan and a 24-hour technical hotline. Each cabin is signed by a well-known designer, carries a numbered plaque, and owners are invited to an exclusive yearly event. Aurum's account managers accompany each customer from the first sketch to years after delivery.

## (a) Classify the sources of Aurum's differentiation as tangible or intangible [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Tangible differentiation rests on observable characteristics (features, performance, packaging, complementary services); intangible differentiation appeals to image, status, identity and exclusivity [Slide 24].

### Checkpoint

- Tangible: quietness (performance), lighter seats (materials/performance), maintenance plan and hotline (complementary services).
- Intangible: designer's signature, numbered plaque, exclusive event (status, exclusivity).
- The account managers: the whole supplier–customer relationship.

### Solution

| Source | Type [Slide 24] | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Quietest cabins (sound-absorbing panels) | Tangible | Performance, observable and measurable |
| Seats 20% lighter | Tangible | Materials and performance |
| Maintenance plan and 24-hour hotline | Tangible | Complementary services |
| Designer's signature, numbered plaque | Intangible | Image, status, desire for exclusivity |
| Exclusive yearly event | Intangible | Identity and status of belonging to a select group |
| Account managers from sketch to after-sales | Whole relationship | Total customer responsiveness [Slide 24] |

## (b) Explain how differentiation allows Aurum to charge twice the price, and what it must protect [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Porter: "providing something unique that is valuable to the buyer beyond simply offering a low price" [Slide 23].

### Checkpoint

- Differentiation creates value for the customer, which earns a price premium [Slide 20].
- The premium must exceed the extra cost of differentiating.
- Aurum must protect what is valuable and hard to copy: reputation, design, relationship.

### Solution

Differentiation means **providing something unique that the buyer values** beyond a low price [Slide 23]; because the customer values it, they accept a **price premium** [Slide 20]. Aurum's quiet, light cabins and its exclusive image create that value, so jet owners pay double. The strategy works only while the premium exceeds the extra cost of the panels, designers and services, and while the uniqueness lasts. The tangible features can eventually be copied; the intangible ones (brand, designer, relationship with owners) are harder to imitate and are what Aurum must protect.
