# 🧩 Bank P21 — Orbitron Satellite Components: bonds, a rights issue and factoring in one year

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

**Orbitron Satellite Components S.A.** builds reaction wheels for small satellites. On 1/1/2025 its balance sheet showed (m.u.): plant at cost 400,000 with accumulated depreciation 100,000; inventory 30,000; accounts receivable 50,000; cash 40,000; contributed capital 250,000 (shares of nominal value 10); retained earnings 60,000; long-term bank loan 70,000; accounts payable 40,000.

The finance director tells the story of 2025 like this:

"We sold for 600,000, all on credit. We collected the 50,000 pending from 2024 and 500,000 of this year's sales. To get cash before Christmas, on 1 December we sold 40,000 of our receivables to a factoring company, which paid us at once after keeping a 3% fee. We bought materials for 320,000 on credit; we paid the 40,000 owed from 2024 and 280,000 of this year's purchases. At year end the warehouse holds 35,000 of materials.

Salaries were 140,000 and other operating expenses 25,000, all paid. The plant is depreciated at 10% of its cost per year. On 1 July we bought a thermal-vacuum test chamber for 180,000 in cash; it will last 10 years with no residual value. To pay for it, the same day we issued bonds for 200,000 at par, maturing in 2030, with a 5% coupon paid every 30 June. On 1 October we also issued 5,000 new shares at nominal value, fully paid in cash.

The bank loan costs 5% per year on the balance at the start of the year; on 31 December we paid the interest and repaid 14,000, and we will repay another 14,000 at the end of 2026. The tax rate is 30% and the tax is paid next year. The board paid a dividend of 40% of the net income on 31 December."

## Setup

**Financing operations of the year** and their place in the map of the financial structure [Slides 21-22]: bonds and the bank loan are long-term external liabilities [Slides 29, 31]; the rights issue is external equity [Slides 25-26]; retained earnings are internal equity (self-financing) [Slide 23]; factoring is short-term external financing [Slide 35].

**Factoring** (without recourse, as described in the course): the receivable is sold, so it leaves the balance sheet; the factor's fee is a financial (non-operating) expense.

**Accrued coupon:** the bonds have been outstanding for 6 months at 31/12, so half a year of coupon has accrued but will be paid on 30/06/2026.

## (a) Classify the financing operations of 2025 by nature and by origin [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Nature: equity or liabilities. Origin: company's own resources or external resources [Slides 21-22].

### Checkpoint

- Bonds 200,000 and bank loan: liabilities, external, long-term.
- Rights issue 50,000: equity, external. Retained earnings: equity, internal.
- Factoring: short-term external financing that brings cash forward; no liability is recorded (the receivable is sold).

### Solution

| Operation | Nature | Origin | Term |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bond issue, 200,000 | Liability | External | Long term (2030) |
| Bank loan (remaining) | Liability | External | Long and short term |
| Rights issue, 5,000 shares | Equity | External | Permanent (never repaid) |
| Retained earnings of 2025 | Equity | Internal (self-financing) | Permanent |
| Factoring of 40,000 | Short-term external resource | External | Short term |
| Accounts payable | Liability | External (trade credit) | Short term |

## (b) Prepare the income statement for 2025 [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Three non-operating expenses: bank interest, the accrued bond coupon (6 months) and the factoring fee. The test chamber is depreciated for 6 months.

### Checkpoint

- Cost of goods sold 315,000; depreciation 49,000 m.u.
- Income from operations 71,000; non-operating expenses 9,700 m.u.
- Net income 42,910 m.u.; dividend 17,164 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Computations

$$\text{COGS} = 30{,}000 + 320{,}000 - 35{,}000 = 315{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$D = 0.10 \times 400{,}000 + \frac{180{,}000}{10} \times \frac{6}{12} = 40{,}000 + 9{,}000 = 49{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$I_{\text{bank}} = 0.05 \times 70{,}000 = 3{,}500, \qquad I_{\text{bonds}} = 0.05 \times 200{,}000 \times \frac{6}{12} = 5{,}000, \qquad \text{Fee} = 0.03 \times 40{,}000 = 1{,}200$$

#### Step 2. Income statement

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Sales revenue | | 600,000 |
| − Cost of goods sold | Step 1 | 315,000 |
| **= Gross profit** | | **285,000** |
| − Salaries | | 140,000 |
| − Other operating expenses | | 25,000 |
| − Depreciation | Step 1 | 49,000 |
| **= Income from operations** | $285{,}000 - 214{,}000$ | **71,000** |
| − Bank interest, bond coupon, factoring fee | $3{,}500 + 5{,}000 + 1{,}200$ | 9,700 |
| **= Income before taxes** | | **61,300** |
| − Income taxes (30%) | $0.30 \times 61{,}300$ | 18,390 |
| **= Net income** | | **42,910** |

$$\text{Dividend} = 0.40 \times 42{,}910 = 17{,}164, \qquad \text{Retained} = 42{,}910 - 17{,}164 = 25{,}746 \text{ m.u.}$$

## (c) Compute the cash balance at 31/12/2025 [1.0 pts]

### Hint

The factor pays the receivable minus its fee. The coupon and the tax have not been paid yet; the dividend has.

### Checkpoint

- Factoring inflow 38,800 m.u.
- Inflows 838,800; outflows 699,664 m.u.
- Cash at 31/12/2025: 179,136 m.u.

### Solution

| Inflows | m.u. | Outflows | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Customers (50,000 + 500,000) | 550,000 | Suppliers (40,000 + 280,000) | 320,000 |
| Factoring ($40{,}000 - 1{,}200$) | 38,800 | Salaries | 140,000 |
| Bond issue | 200,000 | Other operating expenses | 25,000 |
| Rights issue ($5{,}000 \times 10$) | 50,000 | Test chamber | 180,000 |
| | | Bank interest | 3,500 |
| | | Loan repayment | 14,000 |
| | | Dividend | 17,164 |
| **Total** | **838,800** | **Total** | **699,664** |

$$\text{Cash}_{31/12} = 40{,}000 + 838{,}800 - 699{,}664 = 179{,}136 \text{ m.u.}$$

## (d) Prepare the balance sheet at 31/12/2025 and compute the financial structure ratios [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Which of this year's sales are neither collected nor sold to the factor? Split the remaining bank loan by the date of each instalment.

### Checkpoint

- Receivables 60,000; non-current assets 431,000 m.u.
- Equity 385,746; long-term liabilities 242,000; current liabilities 77,390 m.u.
- Total 705,136 m.u.; %equity 54.7%; $L/E = 0.83$.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Balance sheet

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Plant and test chamber (400,000 + 180,000) | 580,000 | Contributed capital (250,000 + 50,000) | 300,000 |
| − Accumulated depreciation (100,000 + 49,000) | (149,000) | Retained earnings (60,000 + 25,746) | 85,746 |
| **Non-current assets** | **431,000** | **Equity** | **385,746** |
| Inventory | 35,000 | Bonds (2030) | 200,000 |
| Accounts receivable | 60,000 | Bank loan due after 2026 | 42,000 |
| Cash | 179,136 | **Long-term liabilities** | **242,000** |
| | | Bank loan due in 2026 | 14,000 |
| | | Accounts payable | 40,000 |
| | | Coupon payable | 5,000 |
| | | Income taxes payable | 18,390 |
| **Current assets** | **274,136** | **Current liabilities** | **77,390** |
| **Total assets** | **705,136** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **705,136** |

#### Step 2. Structure ratios [Slide 36]

$$L = 242{,}000 + 77{,}390 = 319{,}390, \qquad \%\,E = \frac{385{,}746}{705{,}136} = 54.7\%, \qquad \frac{L}{E} = \frac{319{,}390}{385{,}746} = 0.83$$

## Interpretation

- **Long-term asset, long-term money:** the 180,000 test chamber was financed with 200,000 of bonds maturing in 2030, matching the life of the investment.
- **Factoring cost:** bringing 40,000 forward by about a month cost 1,200 m.u.; it is an expensive source but it shifts the risk of non-payment to the factor [Slide 35].
- **Dimensional check:** all amounts in m.u.; coupon = rate × nominal × time fraction.
