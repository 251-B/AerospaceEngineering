## B07.1 Pairs of objectives that conflict

- id: B07-Q1
- concept_id: u4-objectives-contradictions
- format: false-statement
- asks: classification

The course names the pairs of objectives of the production function that can contradict each other. Which one of the following pairs is NOT among them:

a) Cost and quality
b) Flexibility and cost
c) Time of delivery and cost
d) Quality and flexibility

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The four objectives are cost, quality, time of delivery and flexibility, and the slide on their contradictions names three pairs, every one of them against cost: cost - quality, flexibility - cost and time of delivery - cost (Topic 4, Slides 5 and 6). Quality and flexibility are not paired there.

- a) Cost and quality is named: better quality usually needs better materials and controls, which raise the cost.
- b) Flexibility and cost is named: many different products in small amounts lose the savings of standard production.
- c) Time of delivery and cost is named: delivering faster needs spare capacity or stocks, which cost money.

## B07.2 Organization of flexible production

- id: B07-Q2
- concept_id: u4-flexible-organization
- format: identify
- asks: classification

A workshop that makes custom interiors for business jets is organized as a flexible (intermittent) production system. Which of the following features belongs to its organization:

a) Job positions assigned by seniority, which bureaucratizes the company
b) Employee rotation and remuneration that depends on qualification
c) A narrow division of labour, so that each worker learns one simple task
d) Big volumes of standard products sold at a low price

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Flexible production organizes the plant around teamwork, gives workers more responsibility, has fewer hierarchical levels, rotates its employees and pays according to qualification (Topic 4, Slide 15), which fits its complex, differentiated products and highly qualified workers (Slide 14).

- a) Assignment by seniority and bureaucratization describe the factory of rigid production, not of flexible production.
- c) A narrow division of labour with simple tasks is the job specialization of rigid production.
- d) Big volumes of standard products at a low price are the characteristics of rigid production.

## B07.3 Volume at which the rigid system becomes cheaper

- id: B07-Q3
- concept_id: u4-flexible-rigid-cost-crossover
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: (800000-200000)/(60-20)

A maker of drone propellers compares two production systems. The flexible one has fixed costs of 200,000 € a year and a variable cost of 60 € per unit; the rigid one has fixed costs of 800,000 € a year and a variable cost of 20 € per unit. Above what volume is the rigid system cheaper:

a) 10,000 units
b) 20,000 units
c) 15,000 units
d) 25,000 units

### Answer

c

### Explanation

With $TC = FC + VC\,Q$ (Topic 4, Slide 37) the two total cost lines cross where $200{,}000 + 60\,Q = 800{,}000 + 20\,Q$, so $40\,Q = 600{,}000$ and $Q = 15{,}000$ units. Above that volume (zone B of Slide 17) the rigid system, with its lower variable cost, is cheaper; below it (zone A) the flexible one is.

- a) 10,000 units divides the difference of fixed costs by the flexible variable cost, 600,000/60, instead of by the difference of variable costs.
- b) 20,000 units divides only the rigid fixed costs by the difference of variable costs, 800,000/40, ignoring the flexible fixed costs.
- d) 25,000 units adds the two fixed costs, 1,000,000/40, instead of taking their difference.

Topic 4, Slides 17 and 37.

## B07.4 How a Gantt chart shows progress

- id: B07-Q4
- concept_id: u4-gantt-chart
- format: complete-sentence
- asks: definition

A Gantt chart shows the current status of the schedule of a project by means of:

a) Arrows that link every activity with the activities that depend on it
b) Percent-complete shading of the bars and a vertical "TODAY" line
c) A network of nodes in which the longest path is marked
d) A table of the productivity of each production step

### Answer

b

### Explanation

The Gantt chart is a bar chart with the scheduled start and stop dates of each step, and it shows the current schedule status with percent-complete shading and a vertical "TODAY" line (Topic 4, Slide 31).

- a) The course states that a Gantt chart normally does not show the dependency relationships between activities.
- c) A network of nodes with a longest path is a different planning tool, not the bar chart of Henry Gantt.
- d) Productivity indexes belong to the control of efficiency, measured a posteriori, not to the Gantt chart.

## B07.5 Volume for a target profit

- id: B07-Q5
- concept_id: u4-target-profit-volume
- format: numeric
- asks: application
- calc: (90000+450000)/(120-75)

A supplier of aircraft seat belts has fixed costs of 450,000 € a year, sells each belt at 120 € and spends 75 € of variable cost per belt. How many belts must it sell to earn an operating profit of 90,000 €:

a) 12,000 belts
b) 10,000 belts
c) 2,000 belts
d) 4,500 belts

### Answer

a

### Explanation

From $\text{Profit} = Q\,m - FC$ (Topic 4, Slides 37 and 39), $Q = (\text{Profit} + FC)/m$, with $m = 120 - 75 = 45$ €: $Q = (90{,}000 + 450{,}000)/45 = 540{,}000/45 = 12{,}000$ belts, the same step as part (b) of Examples PDF, Exercise 1.

- b) 10,000 belts is the break-even point, 450,000/45: it gives zero profit, not 90,000 €.
- c) 2,000 belts covers only the target profit, 90,000/45, and forgets that the fixed costs must be paid first.
- d) 4,500 belts divides profit plus fixed costs by the price, 540,000/120, instead of by the unit margin.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 37 and 39; Examples PDF, Exercise 1.

## B07.6 Operating leverage from the cost data

- id: B07-Q6
- concept_id: u4-ol-calculation
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: 50000*(40-25)/(50000*(40-25)-500000)

A maker of cockpit display brackets sells 50,000 units a year at 40 € each, with a variable cost of 25 € per unit and fixed costs of 500,000 € a year. Its operating leverage is:

a) 1.5
b) 2.0
c) 0.33
d) 3.0

### Answer

d

### Explanation

$Q\,m = 50{,}000 \times (40 - 25) = 750{,}000$ € and the operating profit is $750{,}000 - 500{,}000 = 250{,}000$ €, so $OL = Q\,m/(Q\,m - FC) = 750{,}000/250{,}000 = 3$ (Topic 4, Slide 40). The same value follows from $Q^* = 500{,}000/15 = 33{,}333$ units and $OL = Q/(Q - Q^*)$ (Slide 42).

- a) 1.5 divides the total margin by the fixed costs, 750,000/500,000, instead of by the operating profit.
- b) 2.0 divides the fixed costs by the operating profit, 500,000/250,000, which leaves out the margin of the units.
- c) 0.33 is profit over total margin, 250,000/750,000, the inverse of the operating leverage.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 40 and 42.

## B07.7 Margin of safety in percentage

- id: B07-Q7
- concept_id: u4-margin-of-safety
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: (18000-360000/(90-60))/(360000/(90-60))*100

A maker of satellite antenna mounts has fixed costs of 360,000 € a year, a price of 90 € and a variable cost of 60 € per mount, and it sells 18,000 mounts. Its margin of safety in percentage, as defined by the course, is:

a) 33.3%
b) 66.7%
c) 50.0%
d) 150.0%

### Answer

c

### Explanation

The break-even point is $Q^* = 360{,}000/(90 - 60) = 12{,}000$ mounts, and the course's percentage margin of safety is $MS = (Q - Q^*)/Q^* = (18{,}000 - 12{,}000)/12{,}000 = 50\%$ (Topic 4, Slides 37 and 43): sales are 50% above the break-even volume.

- a) 33.3% divides the 6,000 mounts above the break-even point by the actual volume, 18,000, instead of by the break-even volume of the slide.
- b) 66.7% is $Q^*/Q$, the share of sales needed to break even, not the distance above it.
- d) 150.0% is $Q/Q^*$, which forgets to subtract the break-even volume first.

## B07.8 Role of Jidoka and Poka Yoke

- id: B07-Q8

- asks: classification

In a Just in Time production system, Jidoka and Poka Yoke are:

a) Mechanisms to support quality
b) Mechanisms of visual control
c) The tools of internal organization
d) The forms of plant distribution

### Answer

a

### Explanation

The course lists the elements of Just in Time with their tools: job cells for internal organization, the U shape for plant distribution, Jidoka and Poka Yoke as mechanisms to support quality, and Andon, control panels, work sheets and Kanban as mechanisms of visual control (Topic 4, Slides 20 and 21).

- b) The visual control mechanisms are Andon, control panels, work sheets and Kanban, not Jidoka and Poka Yoke.
- c) The internal organization of Just in Time is based on job cells, a different element of the system.
- d) The plant distribution of Just in Time is the U shape, not a quality mechanism.

## B07.9 From a functional to a product layout

- id: B07-Q9
- concept_id: u4-layout-comparison
- format: what-happens
- asks: effect

An engine overhaul shop that grouped similar machines by function reorganizes its plant into a product layout, with one line that follows the sequence of operations. Which change should it expect:

a) A higher use of each machine and easier replacement of machines
b) More handling wastes when the materials are transported
c) Easier incentive programmes defined for each employee
d) Less work in process and simpler planning, but a less flexible plant

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The product layout has little work in process, less space for transport and storage, simpler planning and control and a flow of materials that is easy to anticipate, but it is inflexible, depends on the slowest machine and needs a high investment (Topic 4, Slide 25).

- a) A high use of the machines and easy replacement of machines are advantages of the functional layout the shop is leaving.
- b) Handling wastes in transport are a disadvantage of the functional layout; the product layout reduces transport.
- c) Incentive programmes per employee are difficult to define in a product layout; a specific incentive system per worker is an advantage of the functional layout.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 24 and 25.

## B07.10 Production control

- id: B07-Q10
- concept_id: u4-planning-vs-control
- format: complete-sentence
- asks: definition

In the course, production control is:

a) The effort to ensure that the production plans or schedules are met
b) The determination of what products to produce and where, when and how to produce them
c) The measurement of the productivity of the factors after production
d) The dispatching of notices to the operators so that they begin work

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Production planning decides what to produce and where, when and how; production control is the effort to ensure that those plans or schedules are met (Topic 4, Slide 27).

- b) Deciding what, where, when and how to produce is the definition of production planning, not of control.
- c) Measuring productivity a posteriori is the control of efficiency of Part IV, not the definition of production control.
- d) Dispatching notices to the operators is only the fifth step of the planning and control process, not its definition.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 27, 28 and 33.
