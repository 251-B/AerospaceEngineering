## F08.1 What production management does

- id: F08-S1
- concept_id: u4-production-management-definition

Production (operations) management is the set of activities aimed at planning, designing, staffing and controlling the production system of a firm.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

This is the definition of the course: a production system converts inputs into outputs, and production management plans, designs, staffs and controls that system.

## F08.2 The factors of production

- id: F08-S2
- concept_id: u4-production-system-elements

In the production system of the course, the factors of production that enter the transformation process are classified as land, labour and capital.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The course classifies the factors of production as creative, basic and managerial. Land, labour and capital is the classical grouping of economics, not the one of the slide.

## F08.3 Moving along the product-process matrix

- id: F08-S3
- concept_id: u4-product-process-matrix

On the product-process matrix, moving from project production towards continuous production raises the volume produced and lowers the variability of the products.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The matrix runs from project, craft and batch to mass and continuous production: variability and flexibility grow towards the project end, and volume grows towards the continuous end.

## F08.4 Replacing a specialized worker

- id: F08-S4
- concept_id: u4-job-specialization

One of the advantages of job specialization in rigid production is that a worker who leaves can be replaced easily, and the task can even be mechanized.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Easy replacement of workers (and mechanization) is listed among the advantages of job specialization, because each job position is a simple task that is quick and cheap to learn.

## F08.5 How a rigid factory assigns its workers

- id: F08-S5
- concept_id: u4-rigid-factory-organization

In the organization of a rigid production factory, workers rotate between job positions and are assigned and paid according to their qualification.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Rotation and remuneration by qualification belong to flexible production. The rigid factory divides labour into different job positions, assigns them by seniority, and this leads to the bureaucratization of the company.

Topic 4, Slides 12 and 15.

## F08.6 Craft and batch production compared

- id: F08-S6
- concept_id: u4-craft-vs-batch

Compared with craft production, batch production makes its products with more uniformity and in a greater quantity.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Both make a low quantity of a great variety of products, but craft production has more adaptability and less quantity, while batch production has more uniformity and more quantity (the batch size).

Topic 4, Slide 23.

## F08.7 Why Just in Time was born

- id: F08-S7
- concept_id: u4-jit-origin-advantages

Just in Time arose from the need to make one standard car model in very large batches, and its competitive advantage is a lower cost of the factory building.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Just in Time arose from the need to manufacture many types of automobiles in small batches under the same production process. Its competitive advantages are to manufacture high-quality products and to reduce the whole production cycle.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 18 and 19.

## F08.8 Balance of the lines in a functional layout

- id: F08-S8
- concept_id: u4-functional-layout

In a functional (process) layout the lines are well balanced, because grouping similar machines together spreads the work evenly among them.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Unbalanced lines are a disadvantage of the functional layout: some are saturated and others free. Grouping similar machines gives a high use of the machines and lets operators train and supervise themselves, but it does not balance the flow.

### Source

Topic 4, Slide 24.

## F08.9 The layout of project production

- id: F08-S9
- concept_id: u4-fixed-layout

Project production uses a fixed layout: the product stays in one place and the machinery, the materials and the workers are brought to it.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The summary of the production types pairs the project type with the fixed layout, and the fixed layout is defined by a product that does not move while the resources come to it.

### Source

Topic 4, Slides 23 and 26.

## F08.10 Order of the planning and control process

- id: F08-S10
- concept_id: u4-planning-control-process

In the basic production planning and control process, the production schedules are drawn up before the list of required materials and the route sheet.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The order is the reverse: receive the customers' orders, develop the list of required materials, develop the route sheet, and only then develop the production schedules, dispatch notices to the operators and follow up.
