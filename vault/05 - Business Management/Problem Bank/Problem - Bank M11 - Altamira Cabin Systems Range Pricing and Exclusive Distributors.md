# Bank M11 — Altamira Cabin Systems: range of products, pricing approaches, a price rise and exclusive distributors

> [!info] Practice item, not an official exercise
> Written for the bank in the style of Exercise 2 of the examples PDF (TechNova Solutions), for a supplier that sells to firms, with one short numeric part on the price elasticity of demand as in Exercise 1 (d). The company is invented. Concepts: Topic 5, Slides 17 (elasticity), 24-25 (range of products and life-cycle), 27-28 (price) and 34-37 (distribution); topic 5.2.2.pdf, pp. 2-3; Examples PDF, Exercises 1 and 2.

## Statement

Altamira Cabin Systems, founded in 2009 in Getafe (Madrid), designs and manufactures galley and lavatory equipment for commercial aircraft. In 2026 its range has two product lines: galley inserts (the ovens G1 and G2 and the coffee maker C1) and lavatory modules (the models L1 and L2). Every product is certified for, and installed in, the cabins of commercial aircraft. For 2027 the board has approved three changes: it will launch a new oven, the G3, for long-haul aircraft; it will stop making the lavatory module L1, whose sales have fallen every year since 2022 because the new aircraft no longer use it; and it will open a third line of food trolleys for high-speed trains (the models R1 and R2), to be sold to railway operators.

The G2 oven costs Altamira 8,000 € per unit, and its price is set by adding 35% to that cost. The trolleys R1 and R2 will be priced 5% below the equivalent trolleys of the market leader. The price of the coffee maker C1 was fixed after asking twenty airlines how much they would be willing to pay for it. The commercial director's objective is to reach 15% of the European market of galley inserts by 2029. Altamira sells 500 G2 ovens a year; very few ovens are certified for the aircraft that use it, and the sales department estimates the price elasticity of demand of the G2 at 0.5 in absolute value. The board is considering a 6% rise in the price of the G2.

Ovens and lavatory modules for new aircraft are sold directly to the two aircraft makers that install them on their assembly lines, by Altamira's own team of key-account engineers, who visit each customer every month. Spare units and the equipment that airlines buy when they refurbish their cabins are sold through one authorised distributor per continent: an independent firm that buys the products from Altamira, stores them and resells them to airlines and maintenance companies. Each distributor holds the sole right to sell Altamira products in its continent.

a) Describe the range of products of Altamira in 2026 and after the three changes of 2027 (extension or width, depth of each line, length and consistency). Which product strategy of the course is each change, and which stage of the product life-cycle explains the withdrawal of the L1? (0.75 points)

b) Which approach to pricing does Altamira use for the G2, for the trolleys and for the coffee maker? Calculate the price and the mark-up of the G2, and say which fact of the second paragraph is a pricing objective and not a pricing approach. (0.5 points)

c) If Altamira raises the price of the G2 by 6%, calculate the new expected sales volume and the sales revenue of the G2 before and after the rise. Is the demand elastic or inelastic, and why? What would the revenue be if the elasticity were 1.6? (0.75 points)

d) Describe the two distribution channels of Altamira: type and length, B2B or B2C, and the distribution intensity of the network of distributors. Is the team of key-account engineers a distribution channel? (0.5 points)

## Setup

| Item | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Unit cost of the G2 | 8,000 € |
| Rule of the G2 price | cost plus 35% |
| Present sales of the G2 | $Q = 500$ ovens a year |
| Price elasticity of demand of the G2 | $\lvert E_d \rvert = 0.5$ |
| Price change of part (c) | $+6\%$ |
| Alternative elasticity of part (c) | $\lvert E_d \rvert = 1.6$ |

Model of the course: the elasticity of the demand is the percentage variation in the demand divided by the percentage variation in the factor [Topic 5, Slide 17]; for the price, the coefficient is negative and its absolute value is used [topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2]. As in Exercise 1 (d) of the examples PDF, the percentage change in the quantity is the elasticity times the percentage change in the price, with the opposite sign [Examples PDF, Exercise 1 (d)]. Sales revenue is the price times the quantity [topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 3].

## (a) The range of products and its changes [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Count the lines, the products of each line and the products in total, before and after. For consistency, ask whether every product still has the same final use.

### Checkpoint

- 2026: extension 2 lines; depth 3 (galley inserts) and 2 (lavatory modules); length 5; consistency high (all for aircraft cabins).
- 2027: extension 3; depth 4, 1 and 2; length 7; consistency lower (the train trolleys have another final use).
- G3: extend a product line (more depth). Train trolleys: add a product line (more range). L1: the opposite of extending a line, depth of the lavatory line from 2 to 1.
- L1: decline stage (obsolescence), sales falling since 2022.

### Solution

#### The range in 2026 and in 2027

A range of products is an offer of product lines and individual products, described by its extension (number of lines), depth (products per line), length (total number of individual products) and consistency (with the final use of the product) [Topic 5, Slide 24]. The examples PDF calls the extension the width (breadth) of the range [Examples PDF, Exercise 4, statement 3].

| Characteristic | 2026 | 2027 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Extension (width) | 2 (galley inserts, lavatory modules) | 3 (plus train trolleys) |
| Depth | galley 3 (G1, G2, C1); lavatory 2 (L1, L2) | galley 4 (G1, G2, G3, C1); lavatory 1 (L2); trolleys 2 (R1, R2) |
| Length | $3 + 2 = 5$ | $4 + 1 + 2 = 7$ |
| Consistency | high: every product is "installed in the cabins of commercial aircraft" | lower: the trolleys are used in high-speed trains, another final use |

#### Strategy behind each change

Slide 24 lists three product strategies: add more product lines (more range), extend the product lines (more depth) and incorporate product variations (more length) [Topic 5, Slide 24].

- New oven G3 in the galley line: extend a product line; the depth of the galley line rises from 3 to 4.
- Line of trolleys R1 and R2: add a product line; the extension rises from 2 to 3. It is also the change that lowers the consistency, because the new products do not share the final use of the others.
- Withdrawal of the L1: the reverse of extending a line; the depth of the lavatory line falls from 2 to 1 and the length falls by one.

The length goes from 5 to $5 + 1 + 2 - 1 = 7$: the G3 and the two trolleys add three products and the L1 removes one.

#### Why the L1 is withdrawn

"Sales have fallen every year since 2022 because the new aircraft no longer use it": the L1 is in the decline stage of its life-cycle, the stage of obsolescence [Topic 5, Slide 25]. It is not maturity, the longest part of the curve, where sales stay high.

## (b) Approaches to pricing [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Slide 28 gives three approaches: competition, demand and cost (cost-plus pricing or break-even analysis). Ask what each price is built from: a cost, the price of a rival or what the customer would pay.

### Checkpoint

- G2: cost approach, cost-plus pricing; price $8{,}000 \times 1.35 = 10{,}800$ €; mark-up 2,800 € per oven.
- Trolleys: competition approach (5% below the leader).
- Coffee maker C1: demand approach (what twenty airlines would pay).
- Pricing objective: 15% of the European market of galley inserts, a market-share objective.

### Solution

#### The approach of each price

Slide 28 distinguishes the competition approach, the demand approach and the cost approach, which uses cost-plus pricing or break-even analysis [Topic 5, Slide 28].

| Fact of the text | Approach of the course |
| :--- | :--- |
| G2: "set by adding 35% to that cost" | Cost approach, cost-plus pricing |
| Trolleys: "5% below the equivalent trolleys of the market leader" | Competition approach |
| C1: "after asking twenty airlines how much they would be willing to pay" | Demand approach |

#### Price and mark-up of the G2

The mark-up is the difference between the cost of a good and its selling price [Topic 5, Slide 28].

$$p_{G2} = 8{,}000 \times (1 + 0.35) = 10{,}800\ \text{€}$$

$$\text{Mark-up} = p_{G2} - \text{cost} = 10{,}800 - 8{,}000 = 2{,}800\ \text{€ per oven}$$

#### Objective, not approach

"Reach 15% of the European market of galley inserts by 2029" says what the prices should achieve, not how they are computed: it is a pricing objective, achieving a certain market share, one of the three objectives of Slide 27 with maximizing profit and maximizing sales [Topic 5, Slide 27].

## (c) A 6% price rise of the G2 [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Write the percentage change of the quantity from the elasticity, then multiply the new price by the new quantity. Compare the revenue before and after, and remember what an elasticity below 1 means.

### Checkpoint

- New price: $10{,}800 \times 1.06 = 11{,}448$ €.
- Change in quantity: $-0.5 \times 6\% = -3\%$; new volume 485 ovens.
- Revenue: 5,400,000 € before; 5,552,280 € after; change +152,280 €.
- Inelastic demand ($\lvert E_d \rvert = 0.5 < 1$): few certified substitutes, so the revenue rises.
- With $\lvert E_d \rvert = 1.6$: $-9.6\%$, 452 ovens, revenue 5,174,496 €, a fall of 225,504 €.

### Solution

#### New price and new quantity

The price rises by 6% from the price of part (b):

$$p_1 = 10{,}800 \times (1 + 0.06) = 11{,}448\ \text{€}$$

From the elasticity, the percentage change in the quantity is the elasticity times the percentage change in the price, with the opposite sign [Examples PDF, Exercise 1 (d)]:

$$\frac{\Delta Q}{Q} = -\lvert E_d \rvert\,\frac{\Delta p}{p} = -0.5 \times 6\% = -3\%$$

$$Q_1 = 500 \times (1 - 0.03) = 485\ \text{ovens}$$

#### Revenue before and after

$$I_0 = p_0\,Q_0 = 10{,}800 \times 500 = 5{,}400{,}000\ \text{€}$$

$$I_1 = p_1\,Q_1 = 11{,}448 \times 485 = 5{,}552{,}280\ \text{€}$$

$$\Delta I = 5{,}552{,}280 - 5{,}400{,}000 = 152{,}280\ \text{€}$$

#### Elastic or inelastic

$\lvert E_d \rvert = 0.5 < 1$, so the demand of the G2 is inelastic: a 1% change in the price moves the quantity by less than 1% [Topic 5, Slide 17]. With an inelastic demand a price rise sells nearly the same quantity at a higher price, and the revenue rises [topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 3], as found above. The reason is in the text: "very few ovens are certified for the aircraft that use it", and the elasticity depends mainly on the availability of substitute goods [topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2].

#### With an elasticity of 1.6

$$\frac{\Delta Q}{Q} = -1.6 \times 6\% = -9.6\%, \qquad Q_1' = 500 \times (1 - 0.096) = 452\ \text{ovens}$$

$$I_1' = 11{,}448 \times 452 = 5{,}174{,}496\ \text{€}, \qquad \Delta I' = 5{,}174{,}496 - 5{,}400{,}000 = -225{,}504\ \text{€}$$

With $\lvert E_d \rvert = 1.6 > 1$ the demand would be elastic: the quantity falls by more than the price rises and the revenue falls [topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 3]. The same 6% rise helps or hurts the revenue depending only on the elasticity.

## (d) The two distribution channels [0.5 pts]

### Hint

For each channel, ask whether an independent firm takes the title of the product between Altamira and the final buyer, who that buyer is, and how many distributors there are in each area.

### Checkpoint

- New aircraft: direct channel, zero levels (Altamira to aircraft maker), B2B.
- Spares and refurbishment: indirect channel, one level (Altamira, distributor, airline or maintenance company), the distributor acts as a wholesaler, B2B.
- Intensity of the distributors: exclusive, one distributor per continent with the sole right to sell.
- The key-account engineers are not a channel: they are Altamira's own sales force (personal selling, a promotion tool) inside the direct channel.

### Solution

#### Channel for new aircraft

"Sold directly to the two aircraft makers": the producer sells directly to the final buyers, a direct channel [Topic 5, Slide 35], with no intermediary, the zero-level channel of the TechNova case [Examples PDF, Exercise 2 (c)]. The buyers are firms, so it is business-to-business (B2B).

#### Channel for spares and refurbishment

"An independent firm that buys the products from Altamira, stores them and resells them to airlines and maintenance companies": an independent intermediary takes the title, so the channel is indirect [Topic 5, Slide 35]. It sells to organizational buyers and not to final consumers, which is what the course calls a wholesaler [Topic 5, Slide 35]; the storage is one of the functions of the intermediaries [Topic 5, Slide 36]. Extension: the level of a channel is the number of intermediaries between the producer and the final buyer; this one has one level (Altamira, distributor, airline). The buyers are again firms: B2B.

"One authorised distributor per continent ... the sole right to sell Altamira products in its continent" is exclusive distribution [Topic 5, Slide 37]. Extension (definition not given on the slide): exclusive distribution grants a single outlet per area, against a limited number of chosen outlets (selective) or as many as possible (intensive).

#### The key-account engineers

A distribution channel is a series of interdependent firms that transfer the title of the product from the producer to the final buyer [Topic 5, Slide 34]. The engineers are Altamira's own employees, so they add no level to the channel: they are the firm's sales force, and their monthly visits are personal selling, a tool of the promotion mix [Topic 5, Slide 33]. The channel of the new aircraft stays direct and zero-level.

## Interpretation

Altamira's buyers are few, large and technical firms, so its marketing rests on the range, the price and a short channel rather than on mass communication. The inelastic demand of a certified oven makes a price rise profitable in revenue, while the train trolleys widen the range at the cost of its consistency and are priced against the leader of a market in which Altamira is a newcomer.
