# 🧩 Bank P20 — Kestrel MRO: a half-year with renting, a credit line and accrued interest

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

**Kestrel MRO S.L.** maintains the light aircraft of three flying schools. Its balance sheet at 31/12/2025 was (m.u.):

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Tools and equipment | 80,000 | Contributed capital | 70,000 |
| − Accumulated depreciation | (16,000) | Retained earnings | 12,000 |
| Spare parts | 10,000 | Long-term loan | 15,000 |
| Accounts receivable | 14,000 | Accounts payable | 9,000 |
| Cash | 22,000 | Taxes payable (2025) | 4,000 |
| **Total** | **110,000** | **Total** | **110,000** |

The manager must present the statements at 30/06/2026. During the first half of 2026:

1. Kestrel billed 30,000 per month for maintenance; the schools pay 30 days after each invoice. The receivables of 2025 were collected in January, except 2,000 from a school that has agreed to pay in September 2026.
2. Spare parts were bought for 8,000 per month, paid at 60 days; the suppliers' balance of 2025 was paid in January. The physical count of 30/06 shows 12,000 of spare parts in stock.
3. The mechanics' salaries are 12,000 per month, paid monthly.
4. From 1 March, Kestrel rents an engine test bench (renting contract) for 1,500 per month, paid at the end of each month.
5. On 1 April a bank opened a credit line of 40,000; Kestrel drew 20,000 that day. Interest is 6% per year on the amount drawn and is paid at the end of each quarter.
6. The tax of 2025 was paid in January.
7. On 1 May Kestrel bought a borescope diagnostic kit for 24,000 in cash, with a useful life of 5 years and no residual value. The old equipment is depreciated at 8,000 per year.
8. The long-term loan bears 4% per year, paid every 31 December.
9. The tax rate is 25%; the tax on the half-year profit will be paid later.

## Setup

**Time window:** 1/1/2026 to 30/06/2026, six months. Expenses are recognised when incurred, even if not paid (the half-year interest of the long-term loan has accrued but is paid only in December) [Slide 8].

**Renting** works like an operating lease [Slide 33] with no purchase option used: the monthly fee is an operating expense and no asset is recorded, as in the CIRCONSA exercise.

## (a) Classify every operation by its effect on the income statement, on cash and on the balance sheet at 30/06 [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Build the three-column table used in the method note of the portal: income statement, cash, and what is still open at 30/06.

### Checkpoint

- June invoices (30,000) and the deferred 2,000 stay as receivables.
- Parts of May and June (16,000) stay as payables; parts consumed 46,000.
- Accrued interest on the long-term loan: 300, current liability.

### Solution

| Operation | Income statement H1 | Cash H1 | Open at 30/06/2026 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Maintenance invoices | Revenue 180,000 | +150,000 (Jan–May) +12,000 (2025 balance) | Receivables 30,000 + 2,000 |
| 2. Spare parts | Consumption 46,000 | −32,000 (Jan–Apr) −9,000 (2025 balance) | Payables 16,000; stock 12,000 |
| 3. Salaries | 72,000 | −72,000 | — |
| 4. Test bench renting | 6,000 (4 months) | −6,000 | — |
| 5. Credit line | Interest 300 | +20,000 −300 | Credit line 20,000 |
| 6. Tax 2025 | — | −4,000 | — |
| 7. Borescope kit | Depreciation 800 | −24,000 | Equipment +24,000 |
| Old equipment | Depreciation 4,000 | — | Accumulated depreciation |
| 8. Long-term loan | Interest 300 | — | Interest payable 300 |
| 9. Tax H1 | 25% of the profit | — | Taxes payable |

## (b) Prepare the income statement of the half-year [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Parts consumed = opening stock + purchases − closing stock. The kit works for two months; the credit line for three months.

### Checkpoint

- Parts consumed 46,000; depreciation 4,800 m.u.
- Income from operations 51,200; interest 600 m.u.
- Net income 37,950 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Computations

$$\text{Parts consumed} = 10{,}000 + 6 \times 8{,}000 - 12{,}000 = 46{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$D = 8{,}000 \times \frac{6}{12} + \frac{24{,}000}{5} \times \frac{2}{12} = 4{,}000 + 800 = 4{,}800 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$I_{\text{credit line}} = 20{,}000 \times 0.06 \times \frac{3}{12} = 300, \qquad I_{\text{loan}} = 15{,}000 \times 0.04 \times \frac{6}{12} = 300 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Income statement 1/1–30/6/2026

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Maintenance revenue | $6 \times 30{,}000$ | 180,000 |
| − Spare parts consumed | Step 1 | 46,000 |
| − Salaries | $6 \times 12{,}000$ | 72,000 |
| − Renting of the test bench | $4 \times 1{,}500$ | 6,000 |
| − Depreciation | Step 1 | 4,800 |
| **= Income from operations** | $180{,}000 - (46{,}000 + 72{,}000 + 6{,}000 + 4{,}800) = 180{,}000 - 128{,}800$ | **51,200** |
| − Interest expense | $300 + 300$ | 600 |
| **= Income before taxes** | $51{,}200 - 600$ | **50,600** |
| − Income taxes (25%) | $0.25 \times 50{,}600$ | 12,650 |
| **= Net income** | $50{,}600 - 12{,}650$ | **37,950** |

## (c) Compute the cash balance at 30/06/2026 [1.0 pts]

### Hint

Start from the 22,000 of the opening balance sheet. Each column of the cash row in part (a) is one line of the cash account.

### Checkpoint

- Inflows 182,000; outflows 147,300 m.u.
- Cash at 30/06/2026: 56,700 m.u.

### Solution

| Inflows | m.u. | Outflows | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| 2025 receivables collected | 12,000 | 2025 suppliers paid | 9,000 |
| Invoices January–May | 150,000 | Parts January–April | 32,000 |
| Credit line drawn | 20,000 | Salaries | 72,000 |
| | | Renting | 6,000 |
| | | Credit-line interest | 300 |
| | | Tax 2025 | 4,000 |
| | | Borescope kit | 24,000 |
| **Total** | **182,000** | **Total** | **147,300** |

$$\text{Cash}_{30/06} = 22{,}000 + 182{,}000 - 147{,}300 = 56{,}700 \text{ m.u.}$$

## (d) Prepare the balance sheet at 30/06/2026 [1.0 pts]

### Hint

The half-year profit is not distributed; add it to equity as the result of the period. Everything open in part (a) appears here.

### Checkpoint

- Non-current assets 83,200; current assets 100,700 m.u.
- Equity 119,950; current liabilities 48,950 m.u.
- Total 183,900 m.u.

### Solution

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Tools and equipment (80,000 + 24,000) | 104,000 | Contributed capital | 70,000 |
| − Accumulated depreciation (16,000 + 4,800) | (20,800) | Retained earnings | 12,000 |
| **Non-current assets** | **83,200** | Profit of the half-year | 37,950 |
| Spare parts | 12,000 | **Equity** | **119,950** |
| Accounts receivable (30,000 + 2,000) | 32,000 | Long-term loan | 15,000 |
| | | **Long-term liabilities** | **15,000** |
| Cash | 56,700 | Accounts payable | 16,000 |
| | | Bank credit line | 20,000 |
| | | Interest payable | 300 |
| | | Taxes payable | 12,650 |
| **Current assets** | **100,700** | **Current liabilities** | **48,950** |
| **Total assets** | **183,900** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **183,900** |

The receivable deferred to September 2026 is still within one year, so it stays current.

## Interpretation

- **Bank credit vs loan:** Kestrel pays interest only on the 20,000 drawn, not on the 40,000 limit [Slide 31].
- **Accrual check:** the 300 m.u. of interest on the long-term loan reduce the profit of the half-year although no money has left the company yet.
- **Dimensional check:** m.u./year × months/12 = m.u. for depreciation and interest.
