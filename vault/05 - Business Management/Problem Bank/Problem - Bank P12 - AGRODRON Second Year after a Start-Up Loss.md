# Bank P12 — AGRODRON: the second year of a drone start-up that closed its first year with a loss

> [!info] Exam-style practice exercise
> Statement supplied by the student in the style of `T3_Additional_EXERCISES.pdf` and solved here step by step. It is not an official exercise and has no official key. Numbers follow the European format of the exam: dots for thousands, commas for decimals.

## Statement

The company **AGRODRON LEVANTE S.L.** was incorporated on 1 January 2017 (01/01/2017) by a group of agricultural engineers with a registered paid-in capital of 4.500 m.u., having an objective of providing precision crop-monitoring services by drone and distributing organic fertilizers in the region of Murcia.

**The following information applies to fiscal year 2017:**

To finance the operations of the company, on 1 January 2017 (01/01/2017) a bank granted the company a loan of 1.500 m.u. at 6% annual interest, with a duration of 5 years, i.e. until 31 December 2021 (31/12/2021). Each 31 December, the company must return to the bank an equal principal amount of 300 m.u. and should also pay the due annual interest (6%) calculated from the loan value at the beginning of each year.

During 2017, the company invested in a fleet of agricultural drones worth 2.400 m.u. The useful life of the drones purchased was 8 years (amortized linearly, with no residual value) and at the end of 2017 the company recorded in accumulated depreciation an amount of 75 m.u. for those assets. Also, a further investment of 400 m.u. in mapping software licenses was made on 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017) to process aerial images. They use these licenses for 4 years (linear amortization with residual value zero). In addition, the company spent 800 m.u. to purchase a plot of rural land for its flight testing base. Most of 2017 was devoted to obtaining civil aviation permits and only the actual business work was started in the final months of the year. At 31 December 2017 (31/12/2017), its balance sheet reflected a loss of 220 m.u., there was no stock (stock in stock) nor pending customer or supplier balances, and the cash in hand and bank account was 1.955 m.u.

**The following information applies to fiscal year 2018:**

- For aerial crop monitoring, the average number of subscribed farms during 2018 was 400 with an average revenue of 0,45 m.u. / month per client in subscription fees. Additionally, the company billed 840 m.u. for the direct sales of organic fertilizers. At the end of 2018, AGRODRON had accounts receivable of 310 m.u. from their customers.
- The company purchased supplies of organic fertilizers during 2018 which amounted to 500 m.u. Of these purchases, 70% was paid immediately in cash and the payment of the remaining 30% was deferred to the following year. Year 2018 sales consumed 80% of the purchased goods.
- AGRODRON operates from rented hangars that have a monthly cost (from 1 January 2018 (01/01/2018)) of 25 m.u. / month, paid in cash. Fixed salaries and Social Security costs (contribution of the company to the Social Security) of the permanent employees amounted to 60 m.u. per month (from 1 January 2018 (01/01/2018)) and were paid in full in 2018. To respond to increased demand during the harvest campaign, the company hired external pilots whose variable wages amounted in 2018 to 192 m.u. (paid in cash).
- Electricity, insurance, and other administrative expenditures totaled 180 m.u. in 2018. The company postponed the payment of 40% of this amount to the following year. Furthermore, on 1 July 2018 (01/07/2018), the company acquired thermal cameras for its drones for a value of 200 m.u., paying 50% in cash on that date and leaving the remainder payable to 9 months, i.e. on 1 April 2019 (01/04/2019). This equipment has a useful life of 5 years and will be amortized linearly with a residual value of 20%.
- The financial results of the company are declared at 23:59 hrs on 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018). The applicable corporation tax rate was 25% on the Profits obtained and is payable in the first quarter of 2019. In 2018, the results were positive: after offsetting in full the loss of the previous year, the company decided to distribute 30% of the remaining net profit after taxes as dividends (paid at 24:00 on 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018)) and retain the rest in the company.

**Required:**
1. Construct the Income Statement (Profit and Loss Account) of AGRODRON LEVANTE S.L. for the period ending 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018), and deduce the exact date in 2017 on which the fleet of agricultural drones was acquired.
2. Construct and justify the balance sheet at 24:00 on 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018), detailing all intermediate cash and accrual calculations.
3. Determine the values of the ROA and ROE ratios in 2018.
4. Indicate which items of the balance sheet at 31 December 2018 (31/12/2018) would be affected and the exact amount of the effect if the decision was made before the end of 2018 to distribute the same dividend, but to pay it on 30 June 2019 (30/06/2019), and assuming simultaneously that the company had decided not to offset the loss of 2017 before calculating the 30% dividend payout.

## Setup

**Hypotheses:**
1. The tax of 25% is applied to the whole profit before taxes of 2018; the loss of 2017 is offset afterwards, against the net profit, as the statement orders ("after offsetting in full the loss of the previous year, ... distribute 30% of the remaining net profit after taxes").
2. The software licences were bought on the last day of 2017, so their amortization starts in 2018 (a full year).
3. The fertilizers not consumed (20% of the purchases) stay in stock at cost.

**The balance at 31/12/2017, rebuilt from the data** (needed to start 2018):

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and owners equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Land | 800 | Paid-in capital | 4.500 |
| Drones ($2.400 - 75$) | 2.325 | Losses of 2017 | (220) |
| Software licences | 400 | **Owners equity** | **4.280** |
| Cash | 1.955 | Bank loan due 2019–2021 | 900 |
| | | Bank loan due 31/12/2018 | 300 |
| **Total assets** | **5.480** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **5.480** |

The loan was 1.500 and 300 were repaid on 31/12/2017, so 1.200 remain at the start of 2018.

**Extension (not defined in the course decks).** $\text{ROA} = \text{Income from operations}/\text{Total assets}$ and $\text{ROE} = \text{Net income}/\text{Owners equity}$, on the balance at 24:00 on 31/12/2018.

## (a) Prepare the 2018 Income Statement and deduce the purchase date of the drones [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Compute the yearly depreciation of the drones: how many months of it are 75 m.u.? In 2018 only 80% of the fertilizers are an expense, and the cameras work for six months.

### Checkpoint

- Drones bought on 1 October 2017 (01/10/2017).
- Revenue 3.000; fertilizers consumed 400; depreciation and amortization 416 m.u.
- Income from operations 792; interest 72; profit before taxes 720; tax 180; net profit 540 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Purchase date of the drones

$$D_{\text{drones}} = \frac{2.400 - 0}{8} = 300 \text{ m.u./year}, \qquad \frac{75}{300} \times 12 = 3 \text{ months} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{bought on 1 October 2017 (01/10/2017)}$$

#### Step 2. Revenue

$$\text{Subscriptions} = 400 \times 0{,}45 \times 12 = 2.160, \qquad \text{Revenue} = 2.160 + 840 = 3.000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 3. Fertilizers consumed

$$\text{Consumed} = 0{,}80 \times 500 = 400, \qquad \text{Stock at } 31/12/2018 = 500 - 400 = 100 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 4. Depreciation and amortization

$$D_{\text{drones}} = 300, \qquad D_{\text{software}} = \frac{400}{4} = 100, \qquad D_{\text{cameras}} = \frac{200 - 0{,}20 \times 200}{5} \times \frac{6}{12} = 32 \times \frac{6}{12} = 16$$

$$D_{2018} = 300 + 100 + 16 = 416 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 5. Interest

$$I_{2018} = 0{,}06 \times 1.200 = 72 \text{ m.u.} \quad \text{(loan value at the beginning of 2018)}$$

#### Step 6. Income Statement 2018

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Subscription fees | $400 \times 0{,}45 \times 12$ | 2.160 |
| Sales of fertilizers | | 840 |
| **Revenue** | | **3.000** |
| − Fertilizers consumed | Step 3 | 400 |
| − Hangar rent | $12 \times 25$ | 300 |
| − Fixed salaries and Social Security | $12 \times 60$ | 720 |
| − Variable wages of external pilots | | 192 |
| − Electricity, insurance and administration | | 180 |
| − Depreciation and amortization | Step 4 | 416 |
| **= Income from operations** | $3.000 - 2.208$ | **792** |
| − Financial expenses (interest) | Step 5 | 72 |
| **= Profit before taxes** | $792 - 72$ | **720** |
| − Corporation tax (25%) | $0{,}25 \times 720$ | 180 |
| **= Net profit** | $720 - 180$ | **540** |

## (b) Prepare the balance sheet at 24:00 on 31 December 2018 [1.5 pts]

### Hint

First the distribution (offset, then 30%), then the cash account starting from 1.955. Every unpaid amount of 2018 is a current liability; the loan keeps one instalment as current.

### Checkpoint

- Remaining profit 320; dividends 96; reserves 224 m.u.
- Cash 2.407 m.u.
- Owners equity 4.724; current liabilities 802; total 6.126 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Distribution

$$\text{Remaining profit} = 540 - 220 = 320, \qquad \text{Dividends} = 0{,}30 \times 320 = 96, \qquad \text{Reserves} = 320 - 96 = 224 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Collections and payments

- Customers: $3.000 - 310 = 2.690$ collected.
- Fertilizers: $0{,}70 \times 500 = 350$ paid, $150$ owed.
- Administration: $0{,}60 \times 180 = 108$ paid, $72$ owed.
- Cameras: 100 paid, 100 owed until 01/04/2019.
- Loan: interest 72 and principal 300 paid on 31/12/2018; it falls to $1.200 - 300 = 900$ (300 due on 31/12/2019, 600 later).

#### Step 3. Cash account 2018

| Inflows | m.u. | Outflows | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Collections from customers | 2.690 | Fertilizers | 350 |
| | | Hangar rent | 300 |
| | | Fixed salaries and Social Security | 720 |
| | | External pilots | 192 |
| | | Administration (60%) | 108 |
| | | Thermal cameras (50%) | 100 |
| | | Loan interest | 72 |
| | | Loan principal | 300 |
| | | Dividends | 96 |
| **Total** | **2.690** | **Total** | **2.238** |

$$\text{Cash}_{24:00,\,31/12/2018} = 1.955 + 2.690 - 2.238 = 2.407 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 4. Balance sheet at 24:00 on 31/12/2018

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and owners equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Land | 800 | Paid-in capital | 4.500 |
| Drones ($2.400 - 375$) | 2.025 | Losses of 2017 (offset) | 0 |
| Software licences ($400 - 100$) | 300 | Reserves | 224 |
| Thermal cameras ($200 - 16$) | 184 | **Owners equity** | **4.724** |
| **Non-current assets** | **3.309** | Bank loan due 2020–2021 | 600 |
| Stock of fertilizers | 100 | **Non-current liabilities** | **600** |
| Accounts receivable | 310 | Bank loan due 31/12/2019 | 300 |
| Cash | 2.407 | Suppliers of fertilizers | 150 |
| | | Creditors for administration expenses | 72 |
| | | Supplier of thermal cameras | 100 |
| | | Taxes payable | 180 |
| **Current assets** | **2.817** | **Current liabilities** | **802** |
| **Total assets** | **6.126** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **6.126** |

Accumulated depreciation of the drones: $75 + 300 = 375$.

## (c) Compute ROA and ROE of 2018 [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Use the income from operations for ROA and the net profit for ROE, on the year-end balance.

### Checkpoint

- ROA = 12,93%; ROE = 11,43% (extension).

### Solution

$$\text{ROA} = \frac{792}{6.126} = 12{,}93\%, \qquad \text{ROE} = \frac{540}{4.724} = 11{,}43\%$$

The ROE is below the ROA because interest and taxes are deducted from the numerator, while the equity base (4.724) is not much smaller than the total assets (6.126).

## (d) Effect of paying the dividend on 30 June 2019 and of not offsetting the 2017 loss before the 30% payout [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Two changes at once: the dividend is now 30% of the whole 540, and it becomes a debt instead of a payment. The 2017 loss stays in equity.

### Checkpoint

- Dividend $0{,}30 \times 540 = 162$ m.u., recorded as "dividends payable" (current).
- Cash +96 (2.503); reserves 378; losses of 2017 (220) remain; owners equity 4.658 (−66).
- Totals 6.222 m.u. (+96).

### Solution

#### Step 1. New distribution

$$\text{Dividend} = 0{,}30 \times 540 = 162, \qquad \text{Reserves} = 540 - 162 = 378 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \text{the loss of 2017 } (-220) \text{ is not offset}$$

#### Step 2. Changes in the balance sheet at 31/12/2018

| Item | Base case | New case | Change |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cash (the 96 m.u. are no longer paid) | 2.407 | 2.503 | +96 |
| **Total assets** | **6.126** | **6.222** | **+96** |
| Losses of 2017 | 0 | (220) | −220 |
| Reserves | 224 | 378 | +154 |
| **Owners equity** | **4.724** | **4.658** | **−66** |
| Dividends payable (current liability) | 0 | 162 | +162 |
| **Total liabilities and equity** | **6.126** | **6.222** | **+96** |

Check: $-66 + 162 = +96$ (checked). The shareholders are promised more (162 instead of 96) because the dividend is computed before absorbing the loss; equity is lower by exactly the extra dividend ($162 - 96 = 66$), and the cash stays in the company until June.

## Interpretation

- **Dimensional check:** farms × m.u./(month·farm) × months gives m.u.; m.u./year × months/12 gives m.u.
- **Accrual vs cash:** net profit 540 m.u., while cash grew by 452 m.u.: $540 + 416$ (depreciation, no cash) $- 310$ (uncollected sales) $- 100$ (stock) $+ 150 + 72 + 180$ (suppliers, administration and tax still owed) $- 100$ (cameras) $- 300$ (principal) $- 96$ (dividends) $= 452$.
- **Typical mistakes:** expensing all 500 m.u. of fertilizers, amortizing the software in 2017, forgetting the 20% residual value of the cameras, and computing the interest on the original 1.500 m.u.
