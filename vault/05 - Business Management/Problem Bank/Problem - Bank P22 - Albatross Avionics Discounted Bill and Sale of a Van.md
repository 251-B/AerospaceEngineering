# 🧩 Bank P22 — Albatross Avionics: a discounted bill of exchange and the sale of a used van

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

**Albatross Avionics S.L.** distributes certified radio altimeters to aircraft workshops. Its balance sheet at 31/12/2024 was (m.u.): warehouse equipment 60,000 (bought 1/1/2021, 10 years of life, no residual value) with accumulated depreciation 24,000; a delivery van bought for 20,000 with accumulated depreciation 12,000 (it is depreciated at 4,000 per year); inventory of 80 altimeters at 500 each; accounts receivable 30,000; cash 25,000; contributed capital 100,000; retained earnings 9,000; accounts payable 30,000.

In 2025 Albatross bought 400 altimeters at 500 each. It paid the 30,000 owed from 2024 and all the purchases of the year except 25,000, still pending at 31 December. It sold 380 altimeters at 800 each. It collected the 30,000 pending from 2024 and 244,000 from the 2025 sales. For the rest of its December sales, a large workshop signed a bill of exchange of 60,000 maturing on 31/3/2026; on 1 December Albatross took the bill to its bank, which advanced the money after deducting 1,200 of interest and commission. If the workshop does not pay at maturity, the bank will claim the money from Albatross.

On 30 June Albatross sold the old van for 11,000 in cash. Staff costs were 50,000 and transport and insurance 12,000, all paid. The tax rate is 30% (paid in 2026), and a dividend of 50% of the net income was paid on 31 December.

## Setup

**Trade discount** [Slide 35]: the bank advances the amount of a bill before it falls due, after deducting interest and commission. Because the bank keeps the right to claim from Albatross if the customer does not pay, the bill stays among Albatross's receivables and the bank's advance is a short-term debt until 31/3/2026; the 1,200 are a financial expense. For simplicity, as in the course exercises, the whole 1,200 is charged to 2025 instead of being spread over the four months of the advance.

**Sale of a fixed asset** [Slide 11]: depreciate it up to the date of sale; the gain (or loss) is the price minus the book value, and it is a non-operating item.

## (a) Compute the gain on the sale of the van and explain where it goes in the income statement [1.0 pts]

### Hint

Book value at sale = cost − accumulated depreciation **at 30/06/2025**, not at 1/1/2025.

### Checkpoint

- Depreciation of the van in 2025: 2,000 m.u.; accumulated at sale 14,000 m.u.
- Book value 6,000 m.u.; gain 5,000 m.u., non-operating.

### Solution

$$D_{\text{van, 2025}} = 4{,}000 \times \frac{6}{12} = 2{,}000, \qquad \text{Accumulated at sale} = 12{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 14{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$\text{Book value} = 20{,}000 - 14{,}000 = 6{,}000, \qquad \text{Gain} = 11{,}000 - 6{,}000 = 5{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

Albatross sells avionics, not vans: the gain does not come from its primary operations, so it is a non-operating item placed after the income from operations [Slide 11]. The 2,000 of depreciation are an operating expense (the van was used for the business until June).

## (b) Prepare the income statement for 2025 [1.0 pts]

### Hint

Inventory moves in units: $80 + 400 - 380 = 100$ altimeters remain, all at 500.

### Checkpoint

- Cost of goods sold 190,000; depreciation 8,000 m.u.
- Income from operations 44,000; non-operating +3,800 m.u.
- Net income 33,460 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Cost of goods sold and depreciation

$$\text{COGS} = 380 \times 500 = 190{,}000 = 40{,}000 + 200{,}000 - 50{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$D = \frac{60{,}000}{10} + 2{,}000 = 6{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 8{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Income statement

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Sales revenue | $380 \times 800$ | 304,000 |
| − Cost of goods sold | Step 1 | 190,000 |
| **= Gross profit** | | **114,000** |
| − Staff | | 50,000 |
| − Transport and insurance | | 12,000 |
| − Depreciation | Step 1 | 8,000 |
| **= Income from operations** | $114{,}000 - 70{,}000$ | **44,000** |
| + Gain on the sale of the van | Part (a) | 5,000 |
| − Discount interest and commission | | 1,200 |
| **= Income before taxes** | | **47,800** |
| − Income taxes (30%) | $0.30 \times 47{,}800$ | 14,340 |
| **= Net income** | | **33,460** |

## (c) Compute the cash at 31/12/2025 and prepare the balance sheet [1.5 pts]

### Hint

Which receivable is still open at year end? The bank's advance is recorded at the amount owed to the bank, not at the cash received.

### Checkpoint

- Cash 85,070 m.u.
- Current assets 195,070; current liabilities 99,340 m.u.
- Total 225,070 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Cash account

$$\text{Dividend} = 0.50 \times 33{,}460 = 16{,}730 \text{ m.u.}$$

| Inflows | m.u. | Outflows | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Receivables of 2024 | 30,000 | Payables of 2024 | 30,000 |
| Collections of 2025 sales | 244,000 | Purchases of 2025 ($200{,}000 - 25{,}000$) | 175,000 |
| Bank advance on the bill ($60{,}000 - 1{,}200$) | 58,800 | Staff | 50,000 |
| Sale of the van | 11,000 | Transport and insurance | 12,000 |
| | | Dividend | 16,730 |
| **Total** | **343,800** | **Total** | **283,730** |

$$\text{Cash}_{31/12} = 25{,}000 + 343{,}800 - 283{,}730 = 85{,}070 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Balance sheet

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Warehouse equipment | 60,000 | Contributed capital | 100,000 |
| − Accumulated depreciation (24,000 + 6,000) | (30,000) | Retained earnings (9,000 + 16,730) | 25,730 |
| **Non-current assets** | **30,000** | **Equity** | **125,730** |
| Inventory (100 × 500) | 50,000 | Accounts payable | 25,000 |
| Bill receivable (discounted) | 60,000 | Bank debt for the discounted bill | 60,000 |
| Cash | 85,070 | Income taxes payable | 14,340 |
| **Current assets** | **195,070** | **Current liabilities** | **99,340** |
| **Total assets** | **225,070** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **225,070** |

The van has left the balance sheet: its cost (20,000) and its accumulated depreciation (14,000) are both removed.

## (d) Compare the trade discount with factoring the same receivable [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Who bears the risk that the workshop does not pay? What happens to the receivable in each case [Slide 35]?

### Checkpoint

- Trade discount: risk stays with Albatross; receivable and bank debt both stay until maturity.
- Factoring: the factor assumes the insolvency risk; the receivable leaves the balance sheet and there is no bank debt.

### Solution

With the **trade discount**, Albatross gets the cash early but keeps the risk: if the workshop fails to pay on 31/3/2026, the bank will charge Albatross. That is why both the bill and the 60,000 debt appear until maturity. With **factoring**, the company sells the credit to the factoring company, which collects it and **assumes the risk of insolvency** [Slide 35]: the receivable would disappear from the balance sheet, no bank debt would be recorded, and the total assets would be 60,000 m.u. smaller. Factoring usually costs more because it includes that risk.

## Interpretation

- **Liquidity:** working capital $= 195{,}070 - 99{,}340 = 95{,}730$ m.u. (extension); about 60,000 of the current assets will cancel against the bank debt in March.
- **Dimensional check:** units × m.u./unit = m.u.
- **Typical mistakes:** computing the van's book value at 1/1/2025, and treating the bank advance as revenue.
