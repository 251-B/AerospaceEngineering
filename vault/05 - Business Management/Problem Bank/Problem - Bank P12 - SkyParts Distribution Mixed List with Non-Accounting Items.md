# 🧩 Bank P12 — SkyParts Distribution: a mixed list with items that are not accounts

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

SkyParts Distribution S.A. sells certified spare parts to aircraft maintenance shops. A trainee has typed into one list everything they found in the files at 31/12/2025 (m.u.). Some lines are not accounting items at all. The tax rate is 30% and the tax of 2025 is paid in 2026. On 31/12/2025 the board declared a dividend equal to 30% of the net income of 2025, to be paid on 15/01/2026.

| Item | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: |
| Sales revenue | 240,000 |
| Order backlog signed with customers for deliveries in 2026 | 80,000 |
| Warehouse at cost | 100,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation of the warehouse | 30,000 |
| Market value of the warehouse according to an appraiser | 150,000 |
| Delivery vans at cost | 40,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation of the vans | 12,000 |
| Private car of the majority shareholder | 20,000 |
| Cost of goods sold | 150,000 |
| Salaries | 38,000 |
| Advertising | 5,000 |
| Depreciation of 2025 (warehouse and vans) | 9,000 |
| Interest expense | 3,000 |
| Interest revenue from a short-term deposit | 1,000 |
| Short-term deposit (matures in March 2026) | 15,000 |
| Inventory of spare parts | 35,000 |
| Accounts receivable | 30,000 |
| Cash | 15,000 |
| Advance received from a customer for a delivery in February 2026 | 5,000 |
| Accounts payable | 22,000 |
| Long-term bank loan (repayable in 2029) | 30,000 |
| Contributed capital | 80,000 |
| Retained earnings at 1/1/2025 | 20,000 |
| Number of employees | 12 |

## Setup

Only economic events that have already happened and that can be measured in money enter the statements. Revenues are recorded **when the sale is made** [Slide 8]; assets are the economic resources **owned by the company** [Slide 13]. The tax and the dividend payable are not in the list: they are computed.

**Extension (not defined in the course decks).** Current ratio $= \text{Current assets}/\text{Current liabilities}$. The pay-out and self-financing ratios are course material [Slide 23].

## (a) Identify the items that must be left out of both statements and justify each one [0.75 pts]

### Hint

For each suspicious line ask three questions: has a sale or purchase already taken place? Does the company own the resource? Is the figure the amount the company actually recorded?

### Checkpoint

- Four lines are left out: order backlog, market value of the warehouse, the shareholder's private car, number of employees.
- The customer advance is **not** left out: it is a current liability.

### Solution

| Item | Decision | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Order backlog 80,000 | Left out | No sale has been made yet; revenue is recorded when the sale is made [Slide 8] |
| Market value of the warehouse 150,000 | Left out | The warehouse stays at cost less accumulated depreciation, as in every course example; recording appraisals is not done (historical-cost principle, extension) |
| Private car of the shareholder 20,000 | Left out | It belongs to the shareholder, not to the company; the corporation is a separate legal entity from its owners [Topic 1, Slide 24] |
| Number of employees 12 | Left out | Not a monetary amount |
| Advance from a customer 5,000 | **Kept** as current liability | The company owes a delivery in February 2026 (unearned revenue) [Slide 13] |

## (b) Prepare the income statement for 2025 [1.0 pts]

### Hint

The interest revenue and the interest expense are both non-operating items [Slide 11]. The 2025 depreciation is the flow; the accumulated depreciation is a stock.

### Checkpoint

- Gross profit 90,000; income from operations 38,000 m.u.
- Income before taxes 36,000; taxes 10,800; net income 25,200 m.u.

### Solution

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Sales revenue | | 240,000 |
| − Cost of goods sold | | 150,000 |
| **= Gross profit** | $240{,}000 - 150{,}000$ | **90,000** |
| − Operating expenses | $38{,}000 + 5{,}000 + 9{,}000$ | 52,000 |
| **= Income from operations** | $90{,}000 - 52{,}000$ | **38,000** |
| ± Non-operating items | $+1{,}000 - 3{,}000$ | −2,000 |
| **= Income before taxes** | $38{,}000 - 2{,}000$ | **36,000** |
| − Income taxes (30%) | $0.30 \times 36{,}000$ | 10,800 |
| **= Net income** | $36{,}000 - 10{,}800$ | **25,200** |

## (c) Prepare the balance sheet at 31/12/2025, after the dividend has been declared [1.25 pts]

### Hint

A dividend declared but not yet paid is a debt to the shareholders due within a few days: it leaves retained earnings and becomes a current liability.

### Checkpoint

- Dividend payable $0.30 \times 25{,}200 = 7{,}560$ m.u.; retained earnings at 31/12 = 37,640 m.u.
- Non-current assets 98,000; current assets 95,000; current liabilities 45,360 m.u.
- Total 193,000 m.u. on both sides.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Distribution of the net income [Slide 23]

$$\text{Dividends} = 0.30 \times 25{,}200 = 7{,}560 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \text{Retained} = 25{,}200 - 7{,}560 = 17{,}640 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$\text{Retained earnings}_{31/12} = 20{,}000 + 17{,}640 = 37{,}640 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Balance sheet

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Warehouse (100,000 − 30,000) | 70,000 | Contributed capital | 80,000 |
| Delivery vans (40,000 − 12,000) | 28,000 | Retained earnings | 37,640 |
| **Non-current assets** | **98,000** | **Equity** | **117,640** |
| Inventory | 35,000 | Long-term bank loan | 30,000 |
| Accounts receivable | 30,000 | **Long-term liabilities** | **30,000** |
| Short-term deposit | 15,000 | Accounts payable | 22,000 |
| Cash | 15,000 | Customer advance | 5,000 |
| | | Dividend payable | 7,560 |
| | | Income taxes payable | 10,800 |
| **Current assets** | **95,000** | **Current liabilities** | **45,360** |
| **Total assets** | **193,000** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **193,000** |

The short-term deposit matures within one year, so it is a current asset (a short-term investment [Slide 14]).

## (d) Compute the pay-out and self-financing ratios and the current ratio, and comment [1.0 pts]

### Hint

The pay-out ratio relates dividends to profits; the self-financing ratio relates retained earnings to profits. Both add up to 100%.

### Checkpoint

- Pay-out 30%; self-financing 70%.
- Current ratio 2.09 (extension).

### Solution

#### Step 1. Distribution ratios [Slide 23]

$$\text{Pay-out} = \frac{7{,}560}{25{,}200} = 30\%, \qquad \text{Self-financing} = \frac{17{,}640}{25{,}200} = 70\%$$

#### Step 2. Current ratio (extension)

$$\text{Current ratio} = \frac{95{,}000}{45{,}360} = 2.09$$

#### Step 3. Comment

SkyParts keeps 70% of its profit inside the company, an internal equity source that needs no repayment and has no explicit cost [Slides 23, 37]. Its current assets cover the short-term debts about twice, so paying the dividend in January does not threaten its liquidity.

## Interpretation

- **Dimensional check:** all amounts in m.u.; the three ratios are dimensionless.
- **Key idea:** the statements record transactions of the company, at the amounts recorded. Expectations (the backlog), opinions (the appraisal) and the owners' private assets stay out.
- **Typical mistakes:** adding the backlog to sales, revaluing the warehouse, and leaving the customer advance out because "no money is owed".
