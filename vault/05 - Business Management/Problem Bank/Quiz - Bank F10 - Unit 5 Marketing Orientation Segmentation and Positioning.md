## F10.1 Who holds the power in the sales era

- id: F10-S1
- concept_id: u5-marketing-eras

In the sales era (organizational marketing) producers are sales-oriented and face a buyer's market, in which supply is greater than demand.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Slide 4 describes organizational marketing as the sales era: producers are sales-oriented, the market is a buyer's market (supply greater than demand) and the result is consumerism, a high consumer power.

Topic 5, Slide 4.

## F10.2 Marketing when demand exceeds supply

- id: F10-S2
- concept_id: u5-production-era

In the production era marketing was a decisive activity of the firm, because demand was greater than supply and producers had to compete for every customer.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

In the production era (passive marketing) demand was greater than supply, a seller's market: producers sold whatever they made, usually through mass production, so marketing was not important.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 4.

## F10.3 What social marketing adds

- id: F10-S3
- concept_id: u5-social-marketing

Social marketing keeps the aim of finding out the needs and wants of the target market and adds the aim of contributing to a better welfare state.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The main objective of social marketing, the last stage of the evolution of the marketing concept, is finding out the needs and wants of the target market and contributing to a better welfare state.

Topic 5, Slides 4-5.

## F10.4 Where the SWOT analysis sits

- id: F10-S4
- concept_id: u5-swot-diagnosis

The SWOT analysis is the opening stage of strategic marketing, the one in which the market, the competitors and the environment are first studied.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The opening stage is the analysis of the situation (market, competitors, environment and internal analysis). The SWOT analysis is the second stage, the diagnosis of the situation, which uses the results of that analysis.

Topic 5, Slide 7.

## F10.5 The money for the marketing mix

- id: F10-S5
- concept_id: u5-marketing-budget

Establishing the marketing budget is part of the action plan for the marketing mix, so it is a task of operative marketing.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

The first stage of operative marketing is the action plan for the marketing mix: identify the sales objectives, define the activities of the 4 Ps and establish the marketing budget.

## F10.6 What a firm gains by segmenting

- id: F10-S6
- concept_id: u5-segmentation-benefits

Segmenting the market helps a firm detect needs that no supplier is satisfying yet, adjust its supply better to the demand and identify its competitors.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

These are the three advantages of segmentation listed in the course: it identifies the market needs not being satisfied, it gives a better adjustment of supply and demand, and it identifies the competitors.

Topic 5, Slide 11.

## F10.7 Grouping buyers by what they earn

- id: F10-S7
- concept_id: u5-demographic-criteria

A business-aviation charter firm that groups its clients by their level of income and type of job is using psychological and purchase-behaviour criteria of segmentation.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Level of income and type of job are demographic criteria, together with age, sex and family size. Psychological and purchase-behaviour criteria are personality, lifestyle, frequency of purchase and type of use of the product.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 10.

## F10.8 Who completes the positioning

- id: F10-S8
- concept_id: u5-positioning-two-stages

Positioning is complete once the marketing manager has chosen the marketing-mix strategy for the product; the perception that buyers form of it is a separate matter.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

Positioning has two stages: the marketing manager uses the marketing-mix strategy to place the product in the mind of the customer, and the customer creates a perception of the product. How buyers see it is part of positioning.

### Source

Topic 5, Slide 13.

- id: F10-S9

A successful positioning strategy is usually based on a sustainable competitive advantage of the company.

### Answer

T

### Reasoning

Slide 14 states this directly. The positioning can then be built on product characteristics, benefits, user characteristics, a comparison with another product or the price-quality relationship.

## F10.10 The denominator of the market share

- id: F10-S10
- concept_id: u5-market-share

A brand's market share is obtained by dividing the brand's sales by the sales of its main competitor.

### Answer

F

### Reasoning

The market share divides the brand's sales by the total sales of the market of reference, the sales of all the brands of that market, not those of one competitor.

Topic 5, Slide 15.
