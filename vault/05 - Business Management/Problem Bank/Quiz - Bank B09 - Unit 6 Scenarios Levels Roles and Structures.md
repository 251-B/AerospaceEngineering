## B09.1 Sharing out the plant budget

- id: B09-Q1
- concept_id: u6-management-levels
- format: identify
- asks: classification

The manager of an aero-engine plant decides how next year's budget of the plant is shared out among its machining, assembly and test departments. Which management level and type of decision is this?

a) Top management, a strategic decision
b) Middle management, a tactical decision
c) Low management, an operational decision
d) Top management, a general decision

### Answer

b

### Explanation

A plant manager sits between the top management of the company and the first-line supervisors of the shop floor, and distributing resources among the departments of one plant is a tactical decision: tactical decisions are taken by middle management.

- a) Strategic decisions concern the firm as a whole (markets, products, growth), not the internal budget of one plant.
- c) Operational decisions of first-line supervisors deal with the daily work of a team, such as shifts or task assignments, not a yearly budget for several departments.
- d) General decisions are those of top management covering the whole firm; this one is limited to one plant and its departments.

## B09.2 A strike on the final assembly line

- id: B09-Q2
- concept_id: u6-mintzberg-roles
- format: identify
- asks: application

A sudden strike stops the final assembly line of a business-jet maker, and the plant director steps in at once to restore production. Which of Mintzberg's managerial roles is the director playing?

a) Liaison, an interpersonal role
b) Monitor, an informational role
c) Disturbance handler, a decisional role
d) Entrepreneur, a decisional role

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Reacting to an unexpected crisis that disrupts the firm is the disturbance handler role, one of the four decisional roles. The slide lists the roles by name only; the meaning used here is the usual one of Mintzberg's classification.

- a) The liaison role builds contacts with people outside the manager's own unit; it is not about solving a crisis inside the plant.
- b) The monitor role gathers information about the firm and its environment; here the director acts on a problem rather than collecting data.
- d) The entrepreneur role starts planned changes and new projects on the manager's initiative, whereas a strike is an unplanned disturbance imposed from outside.

## B09.3 Matching roles and groups

- id: B09-Q3
- concept_id: u6-mintzberg-roles
- format: false-statement
- asks: classification

Which pairing of a managerial role with its group of Mintzberg's classification is false?

a) Liaison: interpersonal roles
b) Monitor: informational roles
c) Entrepreneur: decisional roles
d) Disseminator: decisional roles

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The disseminator is an informational role, with the monitor and the spokesperson; the decisional roles are entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator and negotiator.

- a) This pairing is true: the interpersonal roles are figurehead, leader and liaison.
- b) This pairing is true: the monitor is the first of the three informational roles.
- c) This pairing is true: the entrepreneur heads the list of the four decisional roles.

## B09.4 A fuel that is suddenly banned

- id: B09-Q4
- concept_id: u6-decision-types
- format: identify
- asks: application

A small launch-services firm learns that the propellant its engines use will be banned within a year. The situation has no precedent in the firm, and there is no ready list of alternatives. Which type of decision must its managers take?

a) An innovative (not planned) decision
b) A routine (planned) decision
c) An adaptable decision
d) An operational decision

### Answer

a

### Explanation

The problem is unusual and ambiguous and the alternatives are ambiguous and not planned, which defines the innovative decision.

- b) A routine decision needs a known, well-defined problem with known alternatives, the opposite of a situation with no precedent.
- c) An adaptable decision lies in between, for problems and alternatives that are partly known; here nothing is known in advance.
- d) Operational refers to the level of first-line supervisors, not to the type of problem and alternatives, and a threat to the firm's engines is far beyond daily work.

## B09.5 From supervisor to general manager

- id: B09-Q5
- concept_id: u6-managerial-skills
- format: what-happens
- asks: effect

An engineer who supervised a composite-layup team is promoted, years later, to general manager of the whole company. How does the mix of managerial skills the job demands change?

a) The technical skill weighs more and the conceptual skill less
b) The interpersonal skill stops being needed, because the manager no longer leads a team directly
c) The conceptual skill weighs more and the technical skill less, while the interpersonal skill remains necessary
d) The mix does not change, because the three skills weigh the same at every level

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Moving up the hierarchy, the share of the conceptual skill (seeing the firm as a whole) grows and the share of the technical skill (understanding how things operate) falls; the interpersonal skill is needed at every level.

- a) This inverts the trend: technical skill weighs most in low management and conceptual skill in top management.
- b) Interacting with people at work successfully is needed at every level, including top management.
- d) The slide shows different proportions of the three skills along the hierarchy, so the mix does change with the level.

## B09.6 The four steps of planning

- id: B09-Q6
- concept_id: u6-planning-steps
- format: ordering
- asks: definition

Which list gives the main steps of planning in the order of the course?

a) Defining strategic objectives, developing forecasts, deciding on a course of action, developing alternative courses of action
b) Developing forecasts, defining strategic objectives, developing alternative courses of action, deciding on a course of action
c) Establishing standards, measuring actual performance, identifying deviations, taking corrective action if required
d) Recognizing the problem, gathering information, considering alternatives, analysing the alternatives

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Planning starts from forecasts, sets the strategic objectives, develops the alternative courses of action that could reach them and finally decides on one of them.

- a) The objectives rest on the forecasts, so forecasting comes first, and a course of action cannot be chosen before the alternatives have been developed.
- c) These are the activities of controlling, the function that checks the results against the standards that planning has set.
- d) These are the first four steps of the decision-making process, not the steps of planning.

## B09.7 Part of the structural configuration

- id: B09-Q7
- concept_id: u6-structural-configuration
- format: identify
- asks: classification

Among the factors that determine the organizational structure, which of the following is listed as an element of the structural configuration rather than as a factor of its own?

a) The span of control
b) Standardization
c) Centralization
d) Formalization

### Answer

a

### Explanation

The structural configuration groups the type of departmentalization, the number of layers (hierarchical levels), the span of control and the size of the administrative component.

- b) Standardization is a factor of its own on the slide, next to specialization, formalization, centralization and the structural configuration.
- c) Centralization is a separate factor, which describes whether decision-making is centralized or decentralized.
- d) Formalization is listed as a separate factor, not inside the structural configuration.

## B09.8 The quality office and the legal department

- id: B09-Q8
- concept_id: u6-parts-formal-structure
- format: identify
- asks: application

In a satellite factory, a quality-engineering office designs the inspection procedures that the integration technicians follow, while a legal department and a cafeteria serve the factory without taking part in the production flow. To which parts of the formal structure do the office and the legal department belong?

a) Middle line and operating core
b) Operating core and strategic apex
c) Support staff and technostructure
d) Technostructure and support staff

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The analysts who design systems and processes form the technostructure, and the units outside the operating workflow form the support staff; the office designs procedures and the legal department lies outside the workflow.

- a) The middle line is middle management and the operating core is the technicians who integrate the satellites; neither designs procedures nor serves from outside the flow.
- b) The strategic apex is top management, and the operating core does the production work, which the legal department does not do.
- c) This swaps the two parts: the designers of procedures are the technostructure, and the unit outside the workflow is the support staff.

## B09.9 Two environments, two bureaucracies

- id: B09-Q9
- concept_id: u6-configuration-environment
- format: identify
- asks: comparison

A large, mature maker of standard aircraft fasteners works in a stable and simple environment; a large aerospace engineering consultancy works in a stable but complex one. Which basic configurations fit them, in that order?

a) Simple structure and division organization
b) Machine bureaucracy and professional bureaucracy
c) Professional bureaucracy and machine bureaucracy
d) Adhocracy and simple structure

### Answer

b

### Explanation

The machine bureaucracy is the configuration of mature, big organizations in a stable and simple environment, and the professional bureaucracy that of big organizations in a stable but complex environment.

- a) The simple structure fits start-ups and very small businesses, and the division organization diversified businesses in more dynamic environments; neither matches a large, stable firm.
- c) This inverts the two: the stable but complex environment of qualified professionals is that of the professional bureaucracy, not of the fastener maker.
- d) The adhocracy is a complementary structure for specific projects and the simple structure is for very small firms, so neither suits a large, mature company.

## B09.10 Rating pilots and linking their pay

- id: B09-Q10
- concept_id: u6-hr-staffing
- format: identify
- asks: classification

Six months after hiring a group of pilots, the human resources department of an airline rates the performance of each one and links part of their pay to the rating. Which activities of human resources management are these?

a) Performance appraisal and compensation, both part of staffing
b) Recruitment and selection, both part of staffing
c) Motivation and leadership, both part of directing
d) Training and communication, both part of directing

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Rating employees already in the firm is performance appraisal, and linking pay to it belongs to compensation and incentive programmes; both are listed under staffing.

- b) Recruitment and selection attract and choose candidates before they are hired, while these pilots are already employees.
- c) Motivation and leadership belong to directing and do not consist of rating performance or setting pay.
- d) Training belongs to staffing, not to directing, and neither training nor communication rates performance or sets pay.
