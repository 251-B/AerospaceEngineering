# 🧩 Bank P24 — Halcyon Regional Airline: what a 15% fall in sales does to profit and cash

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key. Margins, ROE and the three-section cash-flow statement are **extensions** of the course.

## Statement

**Halcyon Regional Airline S.A.** flies island routes with turboprops. In 2025 a new ferry service took many of its passengers. Its statements are (m.u.):

| Income statement | 2024 | 2025 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Passenger revenue | 1,200,000 | 1,020,000 |
| Operating expenses except depreciation (fuel, crews, fees, maintenance, leases) | 1,000,000 | 930,000 |
| Depreciation of aircraft | 80,000 | 80,000 |
| Interest expense | 20,000 | 22,000 |
| Tax rate | 30% | 30% |

| Balance sheet at 31/12 | 2024 | 2025 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Aircraft (net) | 800,000 | 720,000 |
| Spare parts | 40,000 | 45,000 |
| Accounts receivable (travel agencies) | 60,000 | 75,000 |
| Cash | 100,000 | 103,000 |
| Contributed capital | 400,000 | 400,000 |
| Retained earnings | 150,000 | ? |
| Long-term loan | 300,000 | 280,000 |
| Short-term bank credit | 0 | 60,000 |
| Accounts payable | 120,000 | 100,000 |
| Taxes payable | 30,000 | 0 |

In 2025 Halcyon paid a dividend of 35,000 m.u. out of the 2024 profit. No aircraft were bought or sold.

## Setup

A company with a loss pays no corporate tax that year; the loss can be offset against future profits (as in the WIFINET exercise of the course), which is not needed here.

**Extensions.** Operating margin $= \text{Income from operations}/\text{Sales}$; net margin $= \text{NI}/\text{Sales}$; $\text{ROE} = \text{NI}/E$; working capital $= \text{CA} - \text{CL}$; indirect cash-flow statement as in Bank P23.

## (a) Prepare both income statements, compute the operating and net margins, and explain why profit fell much more than sales [1.0 pts]

### Hint

Which costs moved with the passengers and which did not? Depreciation, crews on fixed contracts and leases do not shrink when sales fall.

### Checkpoint

- Income from operations 120,000 → 10,000 m.u.; net income 70,000 → −12,000 m.u.
- Operating margin 10.0% → 1.0%; net margin 5.8% → −1.2%.

### Solution

| Line | 2024 | 2025 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Passenger revenue | 1,200,000 | 1,020,000 |
| − Operating expenses except depreciation | 1,000,000 | 930,000 |
| − Depreciation | 80,000 | 80,000 |
| **= Income from operations** | **120,000** | **10,000** |
| − Interest expense | 20,000 | 22,000 |
| **= Income before taxes** | **100,000** | **−12,000** |
| − Income taxes (30%) | 30,000 | 0 |
| **= Net income** | **70,000** | **−12,000** |

$$\text{Operating margin: } \frac{120{,}000}{1{,}200{,}000} = 10.0\% \;\to\; \frac{10{,}000}{1{,}020{,}000} = 1.0\%; \qquad \text{Net margin: } 5.8\% \;\to\; -1.2\%$$

Sales fell by $180{,}000/1{,}200{,}000 = 15\%$, but operating expenses fell by only $70{,}000/1{,}000{,}000 = 7\%$ and depreciation not at all: much of an airline's cost is fixed in the short run. Income from operations therefore fell by $110{,}000/120{,}000 = 92\%$.

## (b) Find the retained earnings of 2025 and prepare the cash-flow statement of 2025 [1.5 pts]

### Hint

Retained earnings: start from 150,000, subtract the loss and the dividend. In the cash-flow statement, the loss is the starting line and the drop in taxes payable absorbs cash.

### Checkpoint

- Retained earnings 2025: 103,000 m.u.
- CFO = −2,000; CFI = 0; CFF = +5,000 m.u.
- Δcash = +3,000 m.u. ✓

### Solution

#### Step 1. Retained earnings

$$\text{RE}_{2025} = 150{,}000 - 12{,}000 - 35{,}000 = 103{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

Check of the 2025 balance: assets $720{,}000 + 45{,}000 + 75{,}000 + 103{,}000 = 943{,}000$; liabilities and equity $400{,}000 + 103{,}000 + 280{,}000 + 60{,}000 + 100{,}000 + 0 = 943{,}000$ ✓.

#### Step 2. Cash-flow statement 2025 (extension)

| Section | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Net income (loss) | | −12,000 |
| + Depreciation | | 80,000 |
| − Increase in spare parts | $45{,}000 - 40{,}000$ | −5,000 |
| − Increase in receivables | $75{,}000 - 60{,}000$ | −15,000 |
| − Decrease in payables | $100{,}000 - 120{,}000$ | −20,000 |
| − Decrease in taxes payable | $0 - 30{,}000$ | −30,000 |
| **= CFO** | | **−2,000** |
| **= CFI** (no aircraft bought or sold) | | **0** |
| − Repayment of the long-term loan | $280{,}000 - 300{,}000$ | −20,000 |
| + Short-term bank credit drawn | | +60,000 |
| − Dividend paid | | −35,000 |
| **= CFF** | | **+5,000** |
| **Change in cash** | $-2{,}000 + 0 + 5{,}000$ | **+3,000** |

$\Delta\text{Cash} = 103{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 3{,}000$ m.u. ✓

## (c) Compute the working capital, the ROE and the debt-to-equity ratio of both years [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Do not be misled by the working capital: ask whether the new short-term credit can change it, and what does.

### Checkpoint

- Working capital 50,000 → 63,000 m.u. (+13,000, explained by the long-term side, not by the credit).
- ROE 12.7% → −2.4%; $L/E$ 0.82 → 0.87.

### Solution

| Ratio | 2024 | 2025 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Working capital | $200{,}000 - 150{,}000 = 50{,}000$ | $223{,}000 - 160{,}000 = 63{,}000$ |
| ROE | $70{,}000/550{,}000 = 12.7\%$ | $-12{,}000/503{,}000 = -2.4\%$ |
| $L/E$ | $450{,}000/550{,}000 = 0.82$ | $440{,}000/503{,}000 = 0.87$ |

The new short-term credit does **not** explain the rise: it adds 60,000 to cash (current assets) and 60,000 to current liabilities, so it leaves the working capital unchanged. The rise comes from the long-term side of the balance sheet:

$$\Delta\text{WC} = \Delta E + \Delta\text{Long-term liabilities} - \Delta\text{Non-current assets} = (-47{,}000) + (-20{,}000) - (-80{,}000) = +13{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

The aircraft lost 80,000 of value through depreciation that was not reinvested, more than the loss, the dividend and the loan repayment together. What the credit does change is the quality of the liquidity: without it, cash would be only 43,000.

## (d) Diagnose the situation and propose two measures based on the course [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Operations no longer generate cash. Which financing sources of the course do not add fixed charges, and which decisions protect cash?

### Checkpoint

- Diagnosis: fixed-cost structure + negative CFO; the dividend and the loan repayment were paid with borrowed short-term money.
- Measures: suspend dividends (self-financing), refinance at long term or via sale and leasing of an aircraft, adjust capacity (variable-cost measures); a rights issue lowers leverage.

### Solution

Halcyon has gone from a profitable year to a small loss because its costs are largely fixed. Its operations consumed 2,000 m.u. of cash, and the 35,000 dividend plus the 20,000 loan repayment were in practice paid with **short-term bank credit**, which is the most fragile source [Slide 35].

Measures consistent with the course:
1. **Suspend the dividend** until profits recover, so that profits (when they return) stay in the company as self-financing [Slides 23-24].
2. **Replace the short-term credit with long-term resources**, a long-term loan or leasing arrangements for the aircraft [Slides 31, 33], or a **rights issue** that reduces leverage [Slide 26].
3. On the operating side, turn fixed costs into variable ones (for example operating leasing of aircraft and seasonal crews) so that costs follow revenue.

## Interpretation

- **Operating leverage (extension):** a 15% fall in sales caused a 92% fall in income from operations because most costs are fixed.
- **Working capital is not liquidity:** it rose by 13,000 m.u. while the operations consumed cash and the cash balance depended on short-term borrowing.
- **Dimensional check:** all figures in m.u.; margins and ROE dimensionless.
