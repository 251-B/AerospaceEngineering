# 🧩 Bank P30 — Polaris Space Hardware: self-financing, bonds and a rights issue in one plan

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key. ROA and ROE are **extensions** of the course.

## Statement

**Polaris Space Hardware S.A.** builds deployable solar arrays. Its net income of 2025 was 375,000 m.u., and the board paid 75,000 m.u. as dividends; the rest was kept in the company as cash. After that distribution, its balance sheet at 31/12/2025 showed (m.u.): total assets 3,000,000; contributed capital 1,000,000 (100,000 shares of nominal value 10); reserves (retained earnings) 800,000; a bank loan of 700,000 at 5% per year; accounts payable 500,000 (no interest).

On 1/1/2026 Polaris starts a new clean-room line costing 1,200,000 m.u., financed as follows:
- the 300,000 m.u. retained from the 2025 profit;
- a bond issue of 500,000 m.u. at par, coupon 6% per year, maturing in 2033;
- a rights issue of 40,000 new shares at nominal value, fully paid in cash.

For 2026 Polaris expects an income from operations of 520,000 m.u.; the tax rate is 25% and it plans to keep a pay-out of 20%. Assume the balance sheet of 2026 stays as it is on 1/1/2026 after the investment.

## Setup

**Map of the financing sources** [Slides 21-22]: self-financing is internal equity [Slide 23]; a rights issue is external equity [Slides 25-26]; bonds are long-term external liabilities [Slide 29].

**Effect on the balance sheet.** The 300,000 of retained profit is already in the balance sheet as cash: using it changes cash into plant and leaves the total unchanged. The bonds and the rights issue bring 900,000 of new money, so the total grows by 900,000.

**Extensions.** $\text{ROA} = \text{Income from operations}/\text{TA}$; $\text{ROE} = \text{NI}/E$.

## (a) Compute the pay-out and the self-financing ratios of 2025 [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Use the definitions of [Slide 23]: both are shares of the profit.

### Checkpoint

- Pay-out 20%; self-financing 80%.

### Solution

$$\text{Pay-out} = \frac{75{,}000}{375{,}000} = 20\%, \qquad \text{Self-financing} = \frac{375{,}000 - 75{,}000}{375{,}000} = \frac{300{,}000}{375{,}000} = 80\%$$

## (b) Classify the three sources of the plan by nature and origin, and give one advantage and one drawback of each [1.0 pts]

### Hint

Think about repayment, cost (explicit or implicit), and who receives future profits.

### Checkpoint

- Self-financing: equity, internal; no repayment, no new owners; limited by the profit and lowers dividends.
- Bonds: liability, external, long-term; owners keep control; fixed coupon and repayment in 2033.
- Rights issue: equity, external; never repaid, no fixed charge; dilutes the current owners.

### Solution

| Source | Nature / origin | Advantage | Drawback |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Retained profit, 300,000 | Equity / internal (self-financing) | No repayment and no explicit cost; no new owners [Slides 23-24] | Limited by the profit; shareholders give up dividends now |
| Bonds, 500,000 | Liability / external, long-term | Finances a long-term investment without diluting control [Slide 29] | Fixed coupon every year and repayment of the principal in 2033 |
| Rights issue, 400,000 | Equity / external | Never repaid; dividends are not compulsory [Slides 25-26] | New shareholders share future profits (dilution); usually the most expensive source (implicit cost) |

## (c) Compare the financial structure before and after the plan [1.0 pts]

### Hint

Before: the balance sheet of 31/12/2025. After: add 500,000 of bonds and 400,000 of capital; the retained 300,000 are already in equity.

### Checkpoint

- Before: $E$ 1,800,000, $L$ 1,200,000, TA 3,000,000; 60% equity; $L/E = 0.67$.
- After: $E$ 2,200,000, $L$ 1,700,000, TA 3,900,000; 56.4% equity; $L/E = 0.77$.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Rights issue

$$40{,}000 \times 10 = 400{,}000 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \text{Capital after} = 1{,}000{,}000 + 400{,}000 = 1{,}400{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Structure

| Item | 31/12/2025 | 1/1/2026 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Contributed capital | 1,000,000 | 1,400,000 |
| Reserves | 800,000 | 800,000 |
| **Equity $E$** | **1,800,000** | **2,200,000** |
| Bonds | 0 | 500,000 |
| Bank loan | 700,000 | 700,000 |
| Accounts payable | 500,000 | 500,000 |
| **Liabilities $L$** | **1,200,000** | **1,700,000** |
| **Total** | **3,000,000** | **3,900,000** |

$$\%E: \frac{1{,}800{,}000}{3{,}000{,}000} = 60\% \;\to\; \frac{2{,}200{,}000}{3{,}900{,}000} = 56.4\%; \qquad \frac{L}{E}: 0.67 \;\to\; \frac{1{,}700{,}000}{2{,}200{,}000} = 0.77$$

The plan increases leverage only slightly, because 700,000 of the 1,200,000 come from equity (internal and external).

## (d) Project the income statement of 2026, the dividend, the ROA and the ROE [1.25 pts]

### Hint

There are two interest-bearing debts: the bank loan and the bonds. The accounts payable bear no interest.

### Checkpoint

- Interest 65,000; income before taxes 455,000; net income 341,250 m.u.
- Dividend 68,250; retained 273,000 m.u.
- ROA 13.3%; ROE 15.5%.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Interest

$$I = 0.05 \times 700{,}000 + 0.06 \times 500{,}000 = 35{,}000 + 30{,}000 = 65{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Income statement 2026 (forecast)

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| **Income from operations** | | **520,000** |
| − Interest expense | Step 1 | 65,000 |
| **= Income before taxes** | | **455,000** |
| − Income taxes (25%) | $0.25 \times 455{,}000$ | 113,750 |
| **= Net income** | | **341,250** |

$$\text{Dividend} = 0.20 \times 341{,}250 = 68{,}250, \qquad \text{Retained} = 341{,}250 - 68{,}250 = 273{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 3. Returns

$$\text{ROA} = \frac{520{,}000}{3{,}900{,}000} = 13.3\%, \qquad \text{ROE} = \frac{341{,}250}{2{,}200{,}000} = 15.5\%$$

Leverage works in the owners' favour because the assets earn more than the liabilities cost. The ROE (after tax) and the ROA (before interest and tax) are not directly comparable; the right test compares the ROA with the average cost of all the liabilities, including the interest-free suppliers:

$$i = \frac{65{,}000}{1{,}700{,}000} = 3.82\% < \text{ROA} = 13.33\%$$

The leverage formula of Bank P27 (extension) confirms the ROE: $[0.1333 + (0.1333 - 0.0382) \times 0.7727] \times 0.75 = [0.1333 + 0.0735] \times 0.75 = 0.155$.

## Interpretation

- **Mixed financing:** combining the three sources keeps leverage moderate (0.77) while limiting the dilution to 40,000 new shares (40% more shares).
- **Self-financing next year:** the 273,000 retained in 2026 will finance part of the next investment, closing the cycle "profits → retained earnings → investment" of Topic 2 (unified vision).
- **Dimensional check:** all amounts in m.u.; ratios dimensionless.
