## B03.1 A phase of the analysis system

- id: B03-Q1
- concept_id: u5-strategic-marketing-phases
- format: identify
- asks: classification

Which one of the following is a phase of strategic marketing, and not of operative marketing:

a) Establishing the marketing budget
b) Diagnosing the situation with a SWOT analysis
c) Controlling and assessing the results of the marketing mix
d) Executing the marketing-mix strategies

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Strategic marketing has four stages: analysis of the situation, diagnosis of the situation with a SWOT analysis, definition of the marketing objective, and formulation of the strategies (segmentation and positioning). The SWOT diagnosis is its second stage.

- a) Establishing the marketing budget is part of the action plan of the marketing mix, the first stage of operative marketing.
- c) Control and assessment is the third stage of operative marketing, which checks the execution of the marketing mix.
- d) Executing the marketing-mix strategies is the second stage of operative marketing, the action system, not the analysis system.

## B03.2 Elasticity from two observations

- id: B03-Q2
- concept_id: u5-pe-initial-base
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: ((1700 - 2000) / 2000) / ((440 - 400) / 400)

A maker of drone batteries raises the price of its main model from 400 m.u. to 440 m.u., and its monthly sales fall from 2,000 to 1,700 units. Taking the percentage changes with respect to the initial values, as on Slide 17, the coefficient of price elasticity of demand (with its sign) is:

a) −0.67
b) −1.70
c) −1.50
d) −1.94

### Answer

c

### Explanation

With the initial values as the base, $\%\Delta Q = (1{,}700 - 2{,}000)/2{,}000 = -15\%$ and $\%\Delta P = (440 - 400)/400 = +10\%$, so $E_d = -15\%/10\% = -1.50$. Its absolute value, 1.5, is above 1: the demand is elastic.

- a) −0.67 is the price change divided by the quantity change, the ratio turned upside down.
- b) −1.70 uses the midpoints of the interval (1,850 units and 420 m.u.) as the base, which the question does not ask for.
- d) −1.94 takes the final values (1,700 units and 440 m.u.) as the base instead of the initial ones.

Topic 5, Slide 17; topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2.

## B03.3 Sales after a price rise

- id: B03-Q3
- concept_id: u5-pe-quantity-forecast
- format: numeric
- asks: application
- calc: 12000 * (1 - 0.8 * 0.15)

A supplier of cabin headsets sells 12,000 units a year. The price elasticity of demand is 0.8 in absolute value, and the firm raises its price by 15%. With the percentage changes taken with respect to the initial values, the expected yearly sales are:

a) $10{,}560$ units
b) $13{,}440$ units
c) $9{,}750$ units
d) $10{,}200$ units

### Answer

a

### Explanation

From $E_d = \%\Delta Q/\%\Delta P$ with the sign of the law of demand, $\%\Delta Q = (-0.8) \times 15\% = -12\%$. The new sales are $12{,}000 \times (1 - 0.12) = 10{,}560$ units: the demand is inelastic, so the quantity falls by less, in percentage, than the price rises.

- b) 13,440 units adds the 12% instead of subtracting it: the negative sign of the elasticity (law of demand) has been ignored.
- c) 9,750 units divides the price change by the elasticity ($15\%/0.8 = 18.75\%$) instead of multiplying them.
- d) 10,200 units applies the 15% price change to the quantity, as if the elasticity were 1 (unit elastic demand).

## B03.4 Midpoint elasticity of an interval

- id: B03-Q4
- concept_id: u5-pe-midpoint
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: ((420 - 300) / ((420 + 300) / 2)) / ((40 - 50) / ((40 + 50) / 2))

On the demand table of a satellite-imagery service, a price of 50 m.u. per image goes with 300 images a week and a price of 40 m.u. with 420 images. The price elasticity of demand at the midpoint of this interval, computed as in Exercise 1 of the course's elasticity sheet, is:

a) −2.00
b) −1.14
c) −0.67
d) −1.50

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The midpoint method divides each change by the average of its two values: $\bar Q = (300 + 420)/2 = 360$ and $\bar P = (50 + 40)/2 = 45$. Then $\Delta Q/\bar Q = 120/360 = 0.3333$ and $\Delta P/\bar P = -10/45 = -0.2222$, so $E_d = 0.3333/(-0.2222) = -1.50$.

- a) −2.00 takes the initial values (300 images, 50 m.u.) as the base, the simple formula of Slide 17, not the midpoint asked for.
- b) −1.14 takes the final values (420 images, 40 m.u.) as the base.
- c) −0.67 is the midpoint ratio turned upside down: price change over quantity change.

PED Exercises SOL.pdf, Exercise 1; topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2.

## B03.5 Cutting the price with inelastic demand

- id: B03-Q5
- concept_id: u5-elasticity-income
- format: cause-effect
- asks: effect

A training-simulator firm faces a price elasticity of demand of 0.6 in absolute value and cuts its price by 10%. What happens to its income from sales:

a) It rises, because the quantity sold rises by more, in percentage, than the price falls
b) It falls, because the quantity sold rises by about 6%, less than the 10% fall of the price
c) It does not change, because the rise in quantity exactly offsets the fall in price
d) It falls, because the quantity sold falls by about 6% together with the price

### Answer

b

### Explanation

With $\lvert E_d \rvert = 0.6 < 1$ the demand is inelastic: a 10% price cut raises the quantity by about $0.6 \times 10\% = 6\%$. Income $I = P \cdot Q$ is multiplied by about $0.90 \times 1.06 = 0.954$, a fall of about 4.6%.

- a) This is what happens with elastic demand ($\lvert E_d \rvert > 1$), not with an elasticity of 0.6.
- c) Income stays the same only with unit elastic demand ($\lvert E_d \rvert = 1$).
- d) The law of demand makes the quantity rise when the price falls; this option has the wrong sign of the elasticity.

topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 3; Topic 5, Slide 17.

## B03.6 A new line in a cabin-interiors range

- id: B03-Q6
- concept_id: u5-range-characteristics
- format: what-happens
- asks: application

A cabin-interiors firm offers three product lines: seats (4 models), galleys (3 models) and lavatories (2 models). It now launches a fourth line, cabin lighting, with 2 models. What happens to its range of products:

a) Only the depth increases, because the firm now offers more individual products
b) Only the length increases; the extension stays at three because lighting is also a cabin product
c) The extension (width) rises from 3 to 4 lines and the length from 9 to 11 products; the depth of the existing lines does not change
d) The consistency of the range is lost, because every new line lowers the consistency with the final use

### Answer

c

### Explanation

The extension, or width (breadth), of the range is the number of product lines (3 to 4); the length is the total number of individual products ($4 + 3 + 2 = 9$ to $9 + 2 = 11$). The depth is the number of products per line, so the existing lines keep 4, 3 and 2 models. Adding a line is the strategy "add more product lines".

- a) Depth counts products per line; the existing lines gain no models, so their depth does not change.
- b) A new line raises the extension whatever its use; lines are counted, not grouped by the market they serve.
- d) Consistency refers to the final use of the products; lighting is still used in the aircraft cabin, so the range stays consistent.

## B03.7 Segmenting by how often customers fly

- id: B03-Q7
- concept_id: u5-segmentation-criteria
- format: identify
- asks: classification

A regional airline divides its passengers into regular flyers and occasional flyers and designs a different offer for each group. Which segmentation criterion is it using:

a) Psychological and purchase behaviour
b) Demographic
c) Geographical
d) Price-quality relationship

### Answer

a

### Explanation

The frequency of purchase (regular or temporary) is listed under the psychological and purchase-behaviour criteria, together with personality, lifestyle and the type of use of the product.

- b) Demographic criteria are age, sex, family size, level of income and type of job, not how often a person buys.
- c) Geographical criteria are national, international, regional or different areas, not the frequency of purchase.
- d) The price-quality relationship is a basis for positioning a product, not a criterion for dividing the market into segments.

## B03.8 Brand options

- id: B03-Q8
- concept_id: u5-brand-types
- format: false-statement
- asks: definition

Which one of the following statements about the brand is FALSE:

a) The name is the part of the brand that can be said
b) The logo includes the design, symbol, graph and distinct colours of the brand
c) A family (umbrella) brand places one name over many products or businesses
d) A dealer brand is owned by the manufacturer that produces the goods

### Answer

d

### Explanation

A dealer brand is owned by a wholesaler or a retailer, not by the manufacturer; the brand owned by the producer is the manufacturer brand.

- a) This is how Slide 20 defines the name, so the statement is true.
- b) Slide 20 defines the logo as design, symbol, graph and distinct colours, so the statement is true.
- c) Slide 21 shows umbrella branding as one name over many businesses, so the statement is true.

## B03.9 One authorised centre per region

- id: B03-Q9
- concept_id: u5-distribution-intensity
- format: identify
- asks: application

A maker of business-jet engines sells its overhaul kits through only one authorised service centre in each region, and that centre may not carry competing brands. Which distribution strategy is this:

a) Intensive distribution
b) Selective distribution
c) Exclusive distribution
d) A direct channel, because the producer controls who sells the kits

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Slide 37 names three distribution strategies: intensive, selective and exclusive. Extension (definitions not given on the slide): intensive distribution uses as many outlets as possible, selective distribution uses a limited number of chosen outlets per area, and exclusive distribution grants one outlet per area the sole right to sell the product, often without competing brands. One centre per region with no competing brands is exclusive.

- a) Intensive distribution would place the kits in as many outlets as possible, the opposite of one centre per region.
- b) Selective distribution chooses several outlets per area; here the producer allows only one.
- d) The service centres are independent intermediaries, so the channel is indirect; a direct channel has the producer selling to the final buyer.

Topic 5, Slides 35 and 37; Examples PDF, Exercise 2 (c).

## B03.10 A limited-time incentive

- id: B03-Q10
- concept_id: u5-promotion-mix
- format: identify
- asks: classification

During the first three months after launching a new agricultural drone, the maker offers 20% off its maintenance kits to every buyer. Which activity of the promotion mix is this:

a) Advertising
b) Sales promotion
c) Public relations
d) Personal selling

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Sales promotion is any activity that offers an incentive for a limited period to induce a desired response from customers; the discount does not ordinarily come with the product.

- a) Advertising is a paid form of non-personal communication through the mass media by an identified sponsor; it carries a message, not an incentive.
- c) Public relations build and keep a favourable image of the firm with its stakeholders, through the media or sponsorship, not through discounts.
- d) Personal selling is a person-to-person process in which a seller learns the buyer's wants; no salesperson is involved here.
