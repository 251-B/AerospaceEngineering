# Bank M14 — Voltaris hybrid-electric regional aircraft

> [!info] Practice item, not an official exercise
> Case written for the bank in the style of Exercises 2 and 3 of the examples PDF, which has no Topic 7 exercise. Voltaris Aero, Brenner Turbines, Litheon Cells, Kestrel Power, Fjordair, Mercator Regional and Alcudia Air are fictitious companies. Concepts and definitions: Topic 7, Slides 10-16.

## Statement

Voltaris Aero, founded by engineers who had designed electric motors for sports cars, is developing the VX-19, a 19-seat regional aircraft for routes of up to 400 km. Instead of two turboprop engines, its wing carries eight electric motors with small propellers, fed by a battery pack and by a turbo-generator bought from Kestrel Power, a maker of ground power units; wing, propellers, batteries and flight-control software had to be designed together, because the air blown by the eight propellers over the wing changes its lift. The cells of the pack come from Litheon Cells, a maker of cells for electric cars. Litheon has just launched a cell with a silicon anode that stores 15% more energy per kilogram, with the same format, connectors and cooling as its previous cell, so it fits the VX-19 pack unchanged. On its existing car-cell line, Litheon has also added an automatic X-ray inspection station before packing, which has cut defective cells from 1 in 2,000 to 1 in 50,000.

Brenner Turbines has built turboprop engines, with their reduction gearboxes and variable-pitch propellers, for forty years; it supplies the engines of most of today's 19-seat aircraft. Its last three engine generations cost 180, 260 and 390 million euros to develop and cut fuel burn per seat-kilometre by 6%, 3% and 1.2% respectively. Litheon's cells, on the other hand, gained little for years (from 160 to 190 Wh/kg between 2010 and 2016) and then quickly (300 Wh/kg in 2024). Today the VX-19 still flies only 400 km, against 1,500 km for a turboprop aircraft of the same size.

The target market has 200 regional airlines. In the first year 4 airlines ordered the VX-19, among them Fjordair, an island-hopping airline whose managers follow every new propulsion technology closely and whose routes are all shorter than 250 km. After three years 30 airlines in total have ordered it. Mercator Regional, a large operator, says it will order "once ten airlines have flown it for two years without trouble"; Alcudia Air, a small family-owned airline with old aircraft, will "only buy when nobody sells turboprops any more".

## (a) Classify the distributed hybrid-electric propulsion of the VX-19, the silicon-anode cell and the X-ray inspection station along the pairs product/process, radical/incremental and architectural/component, with the reason for each [0.75 pts]

### Hint

For each innovation, find in the statement what changes: the output or the way of producing it; how far it moves from prior solutions; whether the way the parts of the system interact changes or only one part.

### Checkpoint

- Distributed hybrid-electric propulsion: product, radical, architectural.
- Silicon-anode cell: product (of Litheon), incremental, component.
- X-ray inspection station: process (fewer defects), incremental, the line keeps its configuration (a component change of the production system).

### Solution

#### Distributed hybrid-electric propulsion

- **Product:** it is embodied in Voltaris's output, the aircraft it will sell [Topic 7, Slide 11].
- **Radical:** "instead of two turboprop engines, its wing carries eight electric motors ... fed by a battery pack and by a turbo-generator": very new and different from the turboprop solutions on which today's 19-seat aircraft rely [Topic 7, Slide 12].
- **Architectural:** "wing, propellers, batteries and flight-control software had to be designed together, because the air blown by the eight propellers over the wing changes its lift": the overall design of the aircraft and the way its components interact change [Topic 7, Slide 13].

#### Silicon-anode cell

- **Product:** the cell is Litheon's output [Topic 7, Slide 11].
- **Incremental:** it "stores 15% more energy per kilogram" with the same format; a relatively minor change to an existing solution [Topic 7, Slide 12].
- **Component:** "the same format, connectors and cooling as its previous cell, so it fits the VX-19 pack unchanged": one component improves without affecting the overall configuration of the pack or of the aircraft [Topic 7, Slide 13].

#### X-ray inspection station

- **Process:** the cells are the same; what changes is the technique of producing them, with the improvement in effectiveness the course names, fewer defects ("from 1 in 2,000 to 1 in 50,000") [Topic 7, Slide 11].
- **Incremental:** one station added "on its existing car-cell line", a minor change to existing practices [Topic 7, Slide 12].
- **Component, not architectural:** the line keeps its configuration and only gains one station before packing [Topic 7, Slide 13].

## (b) For Brenner Turbines, Litheon Cells and Voltaris Aero, is the hybrid-electric propulsion competence-enhancing or competence-destroying? [0.5 pts]

### Hint

The pair is not a property of the innovation alone: ask, firm by firm, whether its existing knowledge and skills are used or made obsolete.

### Checkpoint

- Brenner: competence-destroying (turboprops, reduction gearboxes and variable-pitch propellers are no longer needed; the turbo-generator comes from Kestrel Power, not from Brenner).
- Litheon: competence-enhancing (cells for electric cars).
- Voltaris: competence-enhancing (electric motors for sports cars).
- One innovation, opposite answers: the course says it depends on the perspective.

### Solution

#### Brenner Turbines: competence-destroying

Brenner's forty years of knowledge are in "turboprop engines, with their reduction gearboxes and variable-pitch propellers". In the VX-19 the propellers are driven by electric motors and the only turbine, the turbo-generator, is "bought from Kestrel Power". The innovation renders Brenner's existing skills obsolete for this aircraft [Topic 7, Slide 12].

#### Litheon Cells and Voltaris Aero: competence-enhancing

Litheon makes "cells for electric cars", and the VX-19 needs a battery pack: the innovation builds on its existing knowledge base and opens a new market to it. Voltaris's founders "had designed electric motors for sports cars", the same knowledge the eight motors use [Topic 7, Slide 12].

#### Why the answers differ

The same innovation enhances the competences of two firms and destroys those of a third: whether an innovation is competence-enhancing or competence-destroying depends on whose perspective is taken, so the firm must always be named [Topic 7, Slide 12].

## (c) Where are Brenner's turboprop and Litheon's cell technology on their S-curves, and why is hybrid-electric propulsion a discontinuous technology for regional flights? [0.5 pts]

### Hint

Divide each engine generation's improvement by its development cost, then compare the cell data with the shape of the S-curve of Slide 14 and with the two curves of Slide 15.

### Checkpoint

- Improvement per 100 million euros: 3.33%, 1.15% and 0.31%: the turboprop is in the flat upper part, near the limit of the technology.
- Cells: slow start (160 to 190 Wh/kg in six years), then fast growth (300 Wh/kg in 2024): the steep middle part.
- Discontinuous: same market need (regional passenger transport), entirely new knowledge base (electric propulsion, batteries); starts below (400 km against 1,500 km) and may overtake.

### Solution

#### Brenner's turboprop

The improvement obtained per 100 million euros of development effort falls from $6/1.8 = 3.33\%$ to $3/2.6 = 1.15\%$ and then to $1.2/3.9 = 0.31\%$. Each generation costs more and yields less: on the S-curve of performance against effort, the turboprop is in the flat upper part, approaching the limit of the technology [Topic 7, Slide 14].

#### Litheon's cells

The cells "gained little for years (from 160 to 190 Wh/kg between 2010 and 2016) and then quickly (300 Wh/kg in 2024)": the slow first part of the S-curve is over and the technology is in its steep middle part [Topic 7, Slide 14]. The path of the specific energy through time is the technology trajectory of the cells, measured as its rate of performance improvement [Topic 7, Slide 10].

#### A discontinuous technology

Hybrid-electric propulsion fulfils the same market need, carrying 19 passengers on regional routes, by building on an entirely new knowledge base (electric motors, batteries, distributed propulsion), so it is a discontinuous technology [Topic 7, Slide 15]. As the second technology of Slide 15, it starts below the first one ("400 km, against 1,500 km") but improves fast, while the turboprop is near its limit; if the trend continues, the second curve may overtake the first, which is the threat Brenner faces given part (b).

## (d) Place Fjordair, the airlines that ordered by the end of year 3, Mercator Regional and Alcudia Air in the adopter categories of the course, and say what Voltaris needs to reach the next category [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Turn the orders into cumulative shares of the 200 airlines and compare them with the cumulative boundaries of the S-curve of adopters (2.5%, 16%, 50%, 84%). Then read the profile of each category on Slide 16.

### Checkpoint

- Year 1: 4/200 = 2.0% < 2.5%: the first buyers, Fjordair included, are innovators.
- Year 3: 30/200 = 15.0%, between 2.5% and 16%: diffusion is still in the early adopters; the early majority starts after 32 airlines (16% of 200).
- Number of airlines per category: 5, 27, 68, 68, 32.
- Mercator: early majority (waits to see how it is received). Alcudia: laggard.
- To reach the early majority Voltaris must show the results of the first operators.

### Solution

#### Cumulative shares

The spread of the VX-19 among the 200 airlines is a technology diffusion, which follows the S-curve of cumulative adopters [Topic 7, Slides 15-16]:

- after year 1: $4/200 = 2.0\%$, below the 2.5% boundary of the innovators;
- after year 3: $30/200 = 15.0\%$, between 2.5% and 16% (2.5% + 13.5%), so the buyers of years 2 and 3 are early adopters and the early adopters are not yet complete;
- the boundary of 16% is $0.16 \times 200 = 32$ airlines: the 33rd airline to order would be the first of the early majority. The categories hold $0.025 \times 200 = 5$, $0.135 \times 200 = 27$, $0.34 \times 200 = 68$, $68$ and $0.16 \times 200 = 32$ airlines.

#### The airlines of the case

- **Fjordair: innovator.** It ordered in the first year, its managers "follow every new propulsion technology closely" (risk-oriented, leading-edge, extremely interested in technological developments), and its short routes make the 400 km range enough [Topic 7, Slide 16].
- **Mercator Regional: early majority.** It will order "once ten airlines have flown it for two years without trouble": open to new ideas, but it waits to see how they are received before investing [Topic 7, Slide 16].
- **Alcudia Air: laggard.** A small, conservative firm with old aircraft that will "only buy when nobody sells turboprops any more": it invests only once an idea is extremely well established [Topic 7, Slide 16].

#### What Voltaris needs

The next large group is the early majority (34%, 68 airlines), which waits for evidence: Voltaris must make the experience of the first operators visible (reliability, costs, range in service). Improving range along its S-curve (part c) also widens the set of routes, and so of airlines, for which the aircraft is a real alternative.

## Interpretation

The case links the three patterns of Part II of Topic 7: the four pairs classify each innovation (and the competence pair needs a named firm), the S-curves of performance explain why a radical, discontinuous technology threatens an incumbent near the limit of its technology, and the diffusion curve tells how far adoption has gone. Thirty orders sound like many, but at 15% of the market the VX-19 has not yet reached the early majority.
