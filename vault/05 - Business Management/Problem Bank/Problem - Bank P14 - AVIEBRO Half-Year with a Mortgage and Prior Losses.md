# 🧩 Bank P14 — AVIEBRO: a half-year close with a volume discount, a mortgage and prior losses

> [!info] Exam-style practice exercise
> Written for the problem bank in the style of `T3_Additional_EXERCISES.pdf`. It is not an official exercise and has no official key. Numbers follow the European format of the exam: dots for thousands, commas for decimals.

## Statement

**AVIÓNICA DEL EBRO S.L. (AVIEBRO)**, a Zaragoza workshop dedicated to the repair and calibration of avionics equipment for regional airlines and flying schools, must present to the bank that finances it the financial statements (balance sheet and Income Statement) as of 30 June 2020 (30/06/2020). The financial director has the balance of 31 December 2019 (31/12/2019), included below, and the following operations of the first semester, from 1 January 2020 (01/01/2020) to 30 June 2020 (30/06/2020):

**I.** The company has invoiced repairs for 1.600 m.u. every month. The payment condition agreed with the customers is payable to 60 days. A regional airline, which represents 40% of the invoicing of every month, surpassed in March the annual volume agreed in its contract, and from 1 April 2020 (01/04/2020) it receives a discount of 5% on all its invoices.

**II.** The customer debt outstanding at 31 December 2019 (31/12/2019) was charged in January, except 250 m.u. from a flying school that has been granted to pay it on 30 September 2020 (30/09/2020). The suppliers of the previous year were paid in February. As the company closed 2019 with losses, there were no taxes to pay.

**III.** At 31 December 2019 (31/12/2019) there is a stock (stock in stock) of 300 spare parts valued at 2 m.u. / unit. On 1 February 2020 (01/02/2020) the company purchased 400 parts at 2 m.u., 50% paid in cash and 50% payable to 90 days; on 1 May 2020 (01/05/2020) it purchased 300 parts more at the same price, payable to 120 days. At 30 June 2020 (30/06/2020) there remain 250 parts in the warehouse.

**IV.** The technicians cost 600 m.u. / month, paid in the same month, to which 180 m.u. / month of Social Security must be added, paid the month after. The overheads (electricity, telephone, cleaning) are 100 m.u. / month and are paid two months after being made. On 1 March 2020 (01/03/2020) the company carried out a renting contract for a test bench of 120 m.u. monthly, paid at the end of each month.

**V.** The patent of the balance (a calibration procedure) is amortized linearly in 5 years with residual value zero; the computer equipment was bought on 1 January 2019 (01/01/2019) and is amortized linearly in 3 years with residual value zero.

**VI.** On 1 April 2020 (01/04/2020) AVIEBRO bought a small hangar next to the airport: the building is valued at 2.000 m.u. and the land at 1.000 m.u. It paid 20% in cash and the rest with a mortgage loan at 4% annual interest, to be returned in installments of 120 m.u. every 1 October and every 1 April, the first one on 1 October 2020 (01/10/2020); the same days it pays the interest of the semester calculated on the debt at the beginning of the semester. The building is amortized linearly in 50 years with residual value zero.

**VII.** The long-term loan of the balance bears 5% annual interest, which is paid every 31 December, and it is returned in one single payment on 31 December 2022 (31/12/2022).

**VIII.** The corporation tax is 25%. The taxable base of the semester is the profit before taxes minus the losses from previous years still pending to offset, and the resulting tax will be paid on 20 July 2020 (20/07/2020) (counted in the balance as "taxes to pay"). No dividends are distributed at the half-year.

**BALANCE 31 December 2019 (31/12/2019)**

| ASSETS | m.u. | LIABILITIES | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| **NON-CURRENT ASSETS** | **1.900** | **OWNERS EQUITY** | **2.700** |
| Patent | 1.200 | Paid-in capital | 3.200 |
| Accum. amortization of the patent | (300) | Losses from previous years | (500) |
| Computer equipment | 900 | **NON-CURRENT LIABILITIES** | **1.000** |
| Accum. depreciation of computer equipment | (300) | Long-term loan (31/12/2022) | 1.000 |
| Financial shares | 400 | **CURRENT LIABILITIES** | **500** |
| **CURRENT ASSETS** | **2.300** | Suppliers | 500 |
| Stock of spare parts | 600 | | |
| Accounts receivable | 1.000 | | |
| Cash | 700 | | |
| **Total Assets** | **4.200** | **Total Liabilities + OE** | **4.200** |

**Required:**
1. Deduce the exact date on which the patent was acquired, and construct the Income Statement (Profit and Loss Account) of AVIEBRO for the first semester of 2020, explaining the intermediate calculations.
2. Calculate the cash and construct the balance sheet at 30 June 2020 (30/06/2020).
3. Determine the values of the ROA and ROE ratios of the semester, and indicate how the Income Statement and the balance sheet would change if the tax authority did not allow to offset the losses from previous years.
4. Instead of buying the hangar, the company could have rented it from 1 April 2020 (01/04/2020) for 50 m.u. monthly, paid at the end of each month. Indicate the changes in the Income Statement, the cash and the balance sheet at 30 June 2020 (30/06/2020). In which case is the profit higher, and in which case is the final cash balance higher? Discuss, reasoned, why.

## Setup

**Time window:** six months, 01/01/2020–30/06/2020. Depreciation and interest are prorated by months; expenses are recorded when incurred even if they are paid later [Slide 8].

**Hypotheses:**
1. The airline's discount applies to the invoices of April, May and June only.
2. "Payable to 90 days" from 1 February means payment on 1 May; "payable to 120 days" from 1 May means payment at the end of August, outside the semester.
3. The losses from previous years are offset for tax purposes, but they stay in the balance sheet until the annual meeting distributes the profit of the year; the result of the semester is shown separately in equity.
4. Land is not depreciated.

**Extension (not defined in the course decks).** $\text{ROA} = \text{Income from operations}/\text{Total assets}$ and $\text{ROE} = \text{Net profit}/\text{Owners equity}$, both for the semester and on the balance at 30/06/2020.

## (a) Deduce the purchase date of the patent and prepare the Income Statement of the semester [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Compute the yearly amortization of the patent and compare it with the 300 accumulated. For the parts, compute the consumption from the stocks; for the airline, the discount is 5% of 40% of 1.600 in three months.

### Checkpoint

- Patent bought on 1 October 2018 (01/10/2018).
- Revenue 9.504 (discount 96); parts consumed 1.500; depreciation 280 m.u.
- Income from operations 1.964; interest 49; profit before taxes 1.915 m.u.
- Taxable base 1.415; tax 353,75; net profit 1.561,25 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Purchase date of the patent

$$D_{\text{patent}} = \frac{1.200}{5} = 240 \text{ m.u./year}, \qquad \frac{300}{240} = 1{,}25 \text{ years} = 15 \text{ months before } 31/12/2019 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{1 October 2018 (01/10/2018)}$$

#### Step 2. Revenue

$$\text{Discount per month} = 0{,}05 \times 0{,}40 \times 1.600 = 32 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \text{Revenue} = 6 \times 1.600 - 3 \times 32 = 9.600 - 96 = 9.504 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 3. Parts consumed

$$\text{Consumption} = 600 + 400 \times 2 + 300 \times 2 - 250 \times 2 = 600 + 800 + 600 - 500 = 1.500 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 4. Personnel, overheads and renting

$$\text{Personnel} = 6 \times (600 + 180) = 4.680, \qquad \text{Overheads} = 6 \times 100 = 600, \qquad \text{Renting} = 4 \times 120 = 480 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 5. Depreciation of the semester

$$D_{\text{patent}} = 240 \times \frac{6}{12} = 120, \qquad D_{\text{computer}} = \frac{900}{3} \times \frac{6}{12} = 150, \qquad D_{\text{building}} = \frac{2.000}{50} \times \frac{3}{12} = 10$$

$$D = 120 + 150 + 10 = 280 \text{ m.u.}$$

Check of the computer equipment: one year at 300 m.u. gives the 300 of the 2019 balance ✓.

#### Step 6. Interest of the semester (accrued, nothing paid)

$$I_{\text{mortgage}} = 2.400 \times 0{,}04 \times \frac{3}{12} = 24, \qquad I_{\text{loan}} = 1.000 \times 0{,}05 \times \frac{6}{12} = 25 \text{ m.u.}$$

The mortgage is $3.000 - 0{,}20 \times 3.000 = 2.400$ m.u.

#### Step 7. Tax

$$\text{Taxable base} = 1.915 - 500 = 1.415, \qquad \text{Tax} = 0{,}25 \times 1.415 = 353{,}75 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 8. Income Statement 01/01/2020–30/06/2020

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Repair revenue | Step 2 | 9.504 |
| − Spare parts consumed | Step 3 | 1.500 |
| − Personnel (wages and Social Security) | Step 4 | 4.680 |
| − Overheads | Step 4 | 600 |
| − Renting of the test bench | Step 4 | 480 |
| − Depreciation and amortization | Step 5 | 280 |
| **= Income from operations** | $9.504 - 7.540$ | **1.964** |
| − Financial expenses | $24 + 25$ | 49 |
| **= Profit before taxes** | $1.964 - 49$ | **1.915** |
| − Corporation tax | Step 7 | 353,75 |
| **= Net profit of the semester** | $1.915 - 353{,}75$ | **1.561,25** |

## (b) Compute the cash and prepare the balance sheet at 30 June 2020 [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Collected at 60 days: January to April invoices. Of the overheads, January to April are paid. The hangar is paid in full to the seller: 600 of own cash and 2.400 from the mortgage.

### Checkpoint

- Cash at 30/06/2020: 538 m.u.
- Receivables 3.386; non-current assets 4.620; total 9.044 m.u.
- Owners equity 4.261,25; non-current liabilities 3.160; current liabilities 1.622,75 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Cash account of the semester

| Inflows | m.u. | Outflows | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| 2019 receivables ($1.000 - 250$) | 750 | Suppliers 2019 | 500 |
| Invoices January–March ($3 \times 1.600$) | 4.800 | Parts of 01/02 ($400 + 400$) | 800 |
| Invoice of April ($1.600 - 32$) | 1.568 | Technicians' wages | 3.600 |
| Mortgage loan | 2.400 | Social Security, January–May | 900 |
| | | Overheads, January–April | 400 |
| | | Renting, March–June | 480 |
| | | Hangar (building and land) | 3.000 |
| **Total** | **9.518** | **Total** | **9.680** |

$$\text{Cash}_{30/06/2020} = 700 + 9.518 - 9.680 = 538 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Items still open at 30/06/2020

- Receivables: May and June invoices, $2 \times (1.600 - 32) = 3.136$, plus the 250 deferred to 30/09/2020: 3.386 m.u.
- Suppliers: the parts of 01/05 ($300 \times 2 = 600$), due at the end of August.
- Mortgage: installments of 01/10/2020 and 01/04/2021 ($2 \times 120 = 240$) are current; $2.400 - 240 = 2.160$ non-current.

#### Step 3. Balance sheet at 30/06/2020

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and owners equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Patent ($1.200 - 420$) | 780 | Paid-in capital | 3.200 |
| Computer equipment ($900 - 450$) | 450 | Losses from previous years | (500) |
| Land | 1.000 | Profit of the semester | 1.561,25 |
| Building ($2.000 - 10$) | 1.990 | **Owners equity** | **4.261,25** |
| Financial shares | 400 | Long-term loan (31/12/2022) | 1.000 |
| **Non-current assets** | **4.620** | Mortgage loan, non-current part | 2.160 |
| Stock of spare parts (250 × 2) | 500 | **Non-current liabilities** | **3.160** |
| Accounts receivable | 3.386 | Mortgage loan, installments within one year | 240 |
| Cash | 538 | Interest payable ($24 + 25$) | 49 |
| | | Suppliers | 600 |
| | | Social Security payable | 180 |
| | | Overheads payable | 200 |
| | | Taxes to pay | 353,75 |
| **Current assets** | **4.424** | **Current liabilities** | **1.622,75** |
| **Total assets** | **9.044** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **9.044** |

## (c) Compute ROA and ROE of the semester, and the effect of not offsetting the prior losses [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Without the offset, the whole 1.915 m.u. is taxed. Which items of the balance change, and by how much?

### Checkpoint

- ROA = 21,72%; ROE = 36,64% (semester, extension).
- Without the offset: tax 478,75 (+125); net profit 1.436,25 (−125); taxes to pay +125; equity 4.136,25; totals unchanged; ROE 34,72%.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Ratios of the semester (extension)

$$\text{ROA} = \frac{1.964}{9.044} = 21{,}72\%, \qquad \text{ROE} = \frac{1.561{,}25}{4.261{,}25} = 36{,}64\%$$

#### Step 2. Without the offset of the losses

$$\text{Tax} = 0{,}25 \times 1.915 = 478{,}75 \;(+125), \qquad \text{Net profit} = 1.915 - 478{,}75 = 1.436{,}25 \;(-125)$$

In the balance, "taxes to pay" rises by 125 m.u. and the profit of the semester (equity) falls by 125 m.u.; total assets and cash do not change, because the tax is paid only in July. The ROE falls to $1.436{,}25 / 4.136{,}25 = 34{,}72\%$. The offset is worth exactly $0{,}25 \times 500 = 125$ m.u. of tax saved.

## (d) Renting the hangar instead of buying it; which case gives more profit and which more cash? [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Remove everything the purchase brought (land, building, its depreciation, the mortgage, its interest, the 600 paid) and add three months of rent.

### Checkpoint

- Income from operations 1.824 (−140); interest 25; tax 324,75; net profit 1.474,25 (−87).
- Cash 988 (+450); total assets 6.504; no mortgage.
- Profit higher buying; cash higher renting.

### Solution

#### Step 1. New results

$$\text{Income from operations} = 1.964 + 10 - 3 \times 50 = 1.824, \qquad \text{Profit before taxes} = 1.824 - 25 = 1.799$$

$$\text{Tax} = 0{,}25 \times (1.799 - 500) = 324{,}75, \qquad \text{Net profit} = 1.799 - 324{,}75 = 1.474{,}25$$

#### Step 2. New cash

$$\text{Cash} = 538 + 600 - 3 \times 50 = 988 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 3. Changes in the statements

| Item | Buying | Renting | Change |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Depreciation | 280 | 270 | −10 |
| Rent of the hangar | 0 | 150 | +150 |
| Financial expenses | 49 | 25 | −24 |
| Net profit | 1.561,25 | 1.474,25 | −87 |
| Land and building (net) | 2.990 | 0 | −2.990 |
| Cash | 538 | 988 | +450 |
| **Total assets** | **9.044** | **6.504** | **−2.540** |
| Owners equity | 4.261,25 | 4.174,25 | −87 |
| Mortgage loan (current and non-current) | 2.400 | 0 | −2.400 |
| Interest payable | 49 | 25 | −24 |
| Taxes to pay | 353,75 | 324,75 | −29 |
| **Total liabilities and equity** | **9.044** | **6.504** | **−2.540** |

**Discussion.** The **profit is higher buying the hangar**: three months of rent (150) cost more than the depreciation of the building (10) and the mortgage interest (24) together, because the land is not depreciated and the mortgage is cheap. The **cash is higher renting**: the company keeps the 600 m.u. of down payment and pays only 150 of rent. Buying gives more profit but ties up cash and adds 2.400 m.u. of debt; renting preserves liquidity, which matters for a workshop that ends the semester with only 538 m.u. in the bank.

## Interpretation

- **Dimensional check:** m.u./month × months gives m.u.; rates × m.u. × months/12 give m.u.; ratios dimensionless.
- **Half-year ratios:** ROA and ROE of a semester are not annual returns; annualising them would roughly double them.
- **Profit is not cash:** the company earned 1.561,25 m.u. but its cash fell from 700 to 538 m.u.: the hangar, the uncollected May–June invoices and the growth of receivables absorbed it.
- **Typical mistakes:** depreciating the land, applying the airline discount to the whole semester, paying the mortgage interest in the semester, and taxing the profit without offsetting the losses as the statement allows.
