## B08.1 Income after a price rise with elastic demand

- id: B08-Q1
- concept_id: u5-revenue-after-price-rise
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: 8000 * (1 - 1.5 * 0.08) * 250 * 1.08

Flight-data recorders currently bring their maker an income of 2,000,000 m.u. a year (8,000 recorders at 250 m.u.). Management plans an 8% price increase, knowing that buyers react with an elasticity of 1.5 in absolute value. Measuring every change against the current figures, what income should the maker expect after the increase:

a) $2{,}160{,}000$ m.u.
b) $1{,}900{,}800$ m.u.
c) $1{,}760{,}000$ m.u.
d) $2{,}419{,}200$ m.u.

### Answer

b

### Explanation

With the sign of the law of demand, $\%\Delta Q = (-1.5) \times 8\% = -12\%$, so the quantity falls to $8{,}000 \times 0.88 = 7{,}040$ units while the price rises to $250 \times 1.08 = 270$ m.u. Income is $I = P \cdot Q = 270 \times 7{,}040 = 1{,}900{,}800$ m.u., below the initial $2{,}000{,}000$ m.u.: with elastic demand a price rise lowers income.

- a) 2,160,000 m.u. sells the initial 8,000 units at the new price, ignoring the reaction of the quantity demanded.
- c) 1,760,000 m.u. multiplies the new quantity of 7,040 units by the old price of 250 m.u. instead of the new one.
- d) 2,419,200 m.u. raises the quantity by 12% instead of lowering it: the negative sign of the elasticity has been ignored.

## B08.2 Point elasticity on a linear demand

- id: B08-Q2
- concept_id: u5-point-elasticity-linear
- format: numeric
- asks: calculation
- calc: -30 * 200 / (18000 - 30 * 200)

The yearly demand for an inspection-drone software licence is $Q_d = 18{,}000 - 30\,p$, and the current price is $p = 200$ m.u. The point price elasticity of demand at this price, computed as in Exercise 3 of the course's elasticity sheet, is:

a) −2.00
b) −0.33
c) −1,800
d) −0.50

### Answer

d

### Explanation

At $p = 200$ m.u. the quantity is $q = 18{,}000 - 30 \times 200 = 12{,}000$ licences, and the derivative of the demand is $dq/dp = -30$. Then $E_d = (dq/dp)\,(p/q) = -30 \times 200/12{,}000 = -0.50$: the demand is inelastic at this price.

- a) −2.00 is the inverse of the right value, the ratio $q/p$ divided by the slope: the fraction has been turned upside down.
- b) −0.33 divides by the intercept 18,000 instead of by the quantity actually demanded at 200 m.u.
- c) −1,800 multiplies the slope by $q/p$ instead of $p/q$, which gives a number with units, not a dimensionless elasticity.

topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2; PED Exercises SOL.pdf, Exercise 3.

## B08.3 Income from two observations

- id: B08-Q3
- concept_id: u5-revenue-observed-change
- format: numeric
- asks: application
- calc: 108 * 6000

A pilot-training school cuts the price of its simulator hour from 120 m.u. to 108 m.u., and the hours sold each month rise from 5,000 to 6,000. Its new monthly income from simulator hours is:

a) $648{,}000$ m.u.
b) $540{,}000$ m.u.
c) $720{,}000$ m.u.
d) $594{,}000$ m.u.

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Income is $I = P \cdot Q = 108 \times 6{,}000 = 648{,}000$ m.u., up from $120 \times 5{,}000 = 600{,}000$ m.u. It agrees with the elasticity: with the initial values as the base, $\%\Delta Q = +20\%$ and $\%\Delta P = -10\%$, so $E_d = -2$, an elastic demand, for which a price cut raises income.

- b) 540,000 m.u. keeps the old 5,000 hours at the new price, as if the quantity did not respond to the price cut.
- c) 720,000 m.u. values the new 6,000 hours at the old price of 120 m.u. instead of the new 108 m.u.
- d) 594,000 m.u. lets the quantity rise only by 10%, the size of the price cut, as if the demand were unit elastic.

## B08.4 Fifteen years of steady orders

- id: B08-Q4
- concept_id: u5-plc-stage-identification
- format: identify
- asks: application

A regional turboprop has been on the market for fifteen years. Its yearly orders stopped growing some years ago and now stay at a high, fairly stable level, with no sign yet of obsolescence. In which stage of the product life cycle is it:

a) Introduction
b) Growth
c) Maturity
d) Decline

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Maturity is the stage after growth, when sales no longer take off but stay high; it is the longest part of the life cycle, which fits fifteen years on the market.

- a) Introduction is the launch of a new product, not a model that has been sold for fifteen years.
- b) Growth is the stage in which sales take off; here the orders have stopped growing.
- d) Decline is the stage of obsolescence, with falling sales, and the statement says there is no sign of it.

## B08.5 A curve that rises and falls

- id: B08-Q5
- concept_id: u5-plc-curve-shapes
- format: identify
- asks: classification

On the three sales curves of Slide 26, one curve rises quickly and then falls soon after its peak. Which kind of product does it represent (reading of the figure):

a) A fashion product
b) An immediate failure
c) A product of a dilated life
d) A product still in its introduction stage

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Reading the curves of Slide 26: the curve that rises and then falls (B) is the fashion product; the curve whose sales grow and then stay (A) is the product of a dilated life, and the one that falls almost at once (C) is the immediate failure.

- b) An immediate failure falls almost at once, without the quick rise that this curve shows.
- c) A product of a dilated life keeps its sales after they grow instead of losing them soon after the peak.
- d) A curve that has already peaked and fallen is past introduction, the launching stage of the product.

## B08.6 Pricing objectives and approaches

- id: B08-Q6
- concept_id: u5-pricing-objectives-approaches
- format: false-statement
- asks: classification

Which one of the following statements about the price decisions is FALSE:

a) The price influences the demand and reinforces the image of the product
b) Pricing objectives can be maximizing profit, maximizing sales or achieving a certain market share
c) Cost-plus pricing and break-even analysis are methods of the cost approach
d) In the demand approach the price is set by adding a mark-up to the cost of the good

### Answer

d

### Explanation

Adding a mark-up to the cost of the good is cost-plus pricing, a method of the cost approach. The course names three approaches (competition, demand and cost), and only the cost approach starts from the cost of the good.

- a) Slide 27 gives these two reasons why the price matters, so the statement is true.
- b) Slide 27 lists exactly these pricing objectives, aligned with the marketing objectives, so it is true.
- c) Slide 28 places both methods under the cost approach, so the statement is true.

## B08.7 What intermediaries contribute

- id: B08-Q7
- concept_id: u5-intermediary-functions
- format: true-statement
- asks: definition

Which one of the following is a function of the intermediaries of a distribution channel, according to the course:

a) Manufacturing the goods they distribute, so that the producer can concentrate on marketing
b) Offering a wide product mix from different producers
c) Choosing the target segment and the positioning of the producer's product
d) Designing the brand name and the logo of the products they carry

### Answer

b

### Explanation

Slide 36 lists the functions of intermediaries: improving exchange efficiency, helping match supply and demand, offering a wide product mix from different producers, managing logistics, and other marketing functions such as financing, market information and storage.

- a) Intermediaries operate between the producer and the final buyer; production stays with the producer.
- c) Segmentation and positioning are strategies of the producer's strategic marketing, not tasks of the channel.
- d) Brand and logo are product attributes decided by the owner of the brand, not a function of distribution.

## B08.8 Steps for selecting a channel

- id: B08-Q8
- concept_id: u5-channel-selection-order
- format: ordering
- asks: definition

In what order does the course list the steps for selecting a distribution channel:

a) Define the channel; set the distribution objectives; choose intensive, selective or exclusive distribution
b) Choose intensive, selective or exclusive distribution; define the channel; set the distribution objectives
c) Set the distribution objectives; choose intensive, selective or exclusive distribution; define the channel
d) Set the distribution objectives; define the channel; choose intensive, selective or exclusive distribution

### Answer

c

### Explanation

Slide 37 lists three steps: first the objectives of the distribution (buying habits, customer, product, company and competitors), then the distribution strategy (intensive, selective or exclusive), and finally the definition of the channel, with the characteristics of each intermediary and their coordination.

- a) The channel is defined last, once the objectives and the intensity are known, not at the start.
- b) The intensity is a strategy chosen to meet the objectives, so the objectives cannot come last.
- d) This swaps the last two steps: the intensity is chosen before the channel and its intermediaries are defined.

## B08.9 A light aircraft for first licences

- id: B08-Q9
- concept_id: u5-positioning-bases
- format: identify
- asks: application

A maker of light aircraft presents its two-seater in every campaign as "the aircraft for flying clubs and students taking their first licence". On which basis is this positioning built:

a) The price-quality relationship
b) A comparison with another product
c) A geographical segmentation criterion
d) The characteristics of the user

### Answer

d

### Explanation

The message defines the product by who uses it (flying clubs and students), which is positioning based on user characteristics, one of the bases listed on Slide 14.

- a) The message says nothing about price or quality, so it is not a price-quality positioning.
- b) No other aircraft is named or compared with the two-seater in the message.
- c) Geographical criteria divide a market into segments by area; they are not a basis for positioning.

## B08.10 Elasticity with respect to advertising

- id: B08-Q10
- concept_id: u5-elasticity-other-factor
- format: complete-sentence
- asks: application

A drone-services firm raises its advertising budget by 20%, and the demand for its services rises by 5%, all else equal. Using the elasticity of the demand of Slide 17, the demand with respect to advertising is:

a) Inelastic, since the elasticity is 0.25, below 1
b) Elastic, since the demand responds to the advertising budget
c) Unit elastic, since both changes have the same sign
d) Not measurable, since an elasticity is only defined with respect to the price

### Answer

a

### Explanation

Slide 17 defines the elasticity of the demand with respect to any factor, mainly the marketing-mix variables: $E = 5\%/20\% = 0.25 < 1$, so the demand is inelastic, not very sensitive to the advertising budget.

- b) Any response gives a non-zero elasticity; the demand is elastic only when the ratio is above 1.
- c) Unit elasticity needs equal percentage changes (ratio 1), not merely changes of the same sign.
- d) Slide 17 applies the elasticity to any factor of the demand, not only to the price.

Topic 5, Slide 17.
