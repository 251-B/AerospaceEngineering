# 🏢 Bank C05 — Three engineers choosing a legal form for a composite-repair shop

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

Ana, Bruno and Carla want to open a shop that repairs composite parts of light aircraft. Ana would invest 60,000 m.u. of savings; Bruno and Carla only their work. A single faulty repair could lead to claims far larger than everything they own, so they worry about losing their homes. They want to start quickly and cheaply, but in five years they hope to open three more shops with outside investors, and they want the business to continue if one of them leaves.

## (a) Compare the sole proprietorship, the partnership and the corporation on the features that matter to the three engineers [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Look at the number of owners, liability, ease of starting, permanence and ability to raise funds [Slides 21-24].

### Checkpoint

- Sole proprietorship: one owner only, unlimited liability: ruled out.
- Partnership: easy to start, pooled talents; unlimited and joint liability, relative impermanence.
- Corporation: limited liability, permanence, easy transfer of shares, greater financial capability; complex and costly, double taxation.

### Solution

| Feature | Sole proprietorship | Partnership | Corporation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Owners | One person | Two or more co-owners | Shareholders |
| Liability | Unlimited | **Unlimited and joint** | **Limited** |
| Starting | Simple | Few restrictions | Complex and costly |
| Permanence | Impermanent | Relatively impermanent | Permanent |
| Funds | Hard to raise | Pooled funds and talents | Greater financial capability |
| Taxation | No separate tax | No separate tax | Special and double taxation |

A sole proprietorship belongs to **one** person [Slide 21], so it does not fit three owners. A partnership is easy to set up through a partnership agreement and pools Ana's money with Bruno's and Carla's skills [Slide 22], but each partner answers for the firm's debts with all their personal wealth, jointly [Slide 23]. A corporation is a separate legal entity whose owners have **limited** liability [Slide 24].

## (b) Recommend a legal form and justify it with the engineers' goals [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Weigh their biggest fear (claims) and their plans (outside investors, continuity) against the cost of forming the company.

### Checkpoint

- Recommend a corporation: limited liability protects their homes; permanence; easy entry of investors through shares.
- Accept its drawbacks: cost of formation, regulation and disclosure, double taxation.

### Solution

The **corporation** fits best. Limited liability addresses their main fear: a large claim would affect the company's assets, not their homes. It is **permanent**, so the business continues if one partner leaves, and ownership is **easily transferred through shares**, which is how the outside investors of the expansion would enter; it also has greater financial capability [Slide 24]. The price is a more complex and costly formation, government regulation and disclosure requirements, and double taxation; the engineers should accept these costs in exchange for protection and growth. The course also lists forms of partnership that limit liability: the limited liability partnership (LLP) [Slide 20] and the limited partnership, where some partners answer only up to what they invested [Slide 29]. An LLP would also meet the fear of claims, but the corporation fits better with the plans for outside investors and continuity.
