# 🧩 Bank P11 — AeroFix Composites: statements from an unordered list of balances

> [!info] Practice item
> Written for the problem bank in the style of the course exercises. It is not an official exercise and has no official key.

## Statement

AeroFix Composites S.L. repairs carbon-fibre fairings for regional aircraft. At 31/12/2025, after closing the year, its accountant hands you the following list of balances in alphabetical order (figures in monetary units, m.u.). The corporate income tax rate is 30%; the tax of 2025 has not been paid yet, and no dividends are distributed this year.

| Account | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: |
| Accounts payable (suppliers of resin and fabric) | 18,000 |
| Accounts receivable (airline customers) | 26,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation of equipment | 24,000 |
| Bank loan, instalment due in 2026 | 10,000 |
| Bank loan, instalments due after 2026 | 40,000 |
| Cash | 23,000 |
| Contributed capital | 60,000 |
| Cost of goods sold (materials used in repairs) | 92,000 |
| Depreciation expense of 2025 | 8,000 |
| Equipment (autoclave, curing ovens) at cost | 120,000 |
| Gain on the sale of an old van | 2,000 |
| Interest expense | 4,000 |
| Inventory of resin and carbon fabric | 21,000 |
| Rent expense of the workshop | 12,000 |
| Retained earnings at 1/1/2025 | 14,000 |
| Salaries expense | 46,000 |
| Sales revenue (repair services) | 190,000 |
| Utilities expense | 6,000 |

## Setup

The list mixes **flows** of the year (revenues and expenses, which go to the income statement) and **stocks** at 31/12 (assets, liabilities and equity, which go to the balance sheet). The income statement is prepared first, because its net income is needed to close the equity of the balance sheet [Slide 7].

**Conventions.** Extended format of the income statement [Slides 9-11]; assets split into non-current and current, liabilities into long-term and current [Slides 14-16]; equity = contributed capital + retained earnings [Slide 17].

**Extension (not defined in the course decks).** Working capital $= \text{Current assets} - \text{Current liabilities}$ and current ratio $= \text{Current assets} / \text{Current liabilities}$ measure short-term liquidity. The debt-to-equity ratio $L/E$ is course material [Slide 36].

## (a) Classify every item of the list as income statement or balance sheet, and give its class [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Ask of each item: is it a flow measured over 2025, or an amount that exists at the instant 31/12/2025? For balance-sheet items, ask whether it turns into cash (or is paid) within one year.

### Checkpoint

- 8 income-statement items: sales, cost of goods sold, salaries, rent, utilities, depreciation, interest, gain on the van.
- 10 balance-sheet items; the bank loan splits into 10,000 current and 40,000 long-term.
- The tax of 2025 is not in the list: it must be computed, and it appears twice (expense and current liability).

### Solution

| Item | Statement | Class | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sales revenue | Income statement | Revenue | Earned when the repair is done [Slide 8] |
| Cost of goods sold | Income statement | Expense | Materials used in the repairs sold [Slide 10] |
| Salaries, rent, utilities, depreciation | Income statement | Operating expenses | Usual costs of running the business [Slide 10] |
| Interest expense | Income statement | Non-operating item | Financing cost, not the primary operation [Slide 11] |
| Gain on the sale of the van | Income statement | Non-operating item | Gain on the sale of equipment [Slide 11] |
| Equipment, accumulated depreciation | Balance sheet | Non-current asset (net) | Used for more than one year [Slide 15] |
| Inventory, accounts receivable, cash | Balance sheet | Current assets | Used or turned into cash within one year [Slide 14] |
| Contributed capital, retained earnings | Balance sheet | Equity | Owners' financing [Slide 17] |
| Bank loan due after 2026 | Balance sheet | Long-term liability | Not paid within the year [Slide 16] |
| Bank loan due in 2026, accounts payable | Balance sheet | Current liabilities | Paid within the coming year [Slide 16] |

Accumulated depreciation is not a liability: it is subtracted from the cost of the equipment to give its net book value.

## (b) Prepare the income statement for 2025 [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Follow the cascade gross profit → income from operations → income before taxes → net income. The gain on the van and the interest are both non-operating items, with opposite signs.

### Checkpoint

- Gross profit 98,000 m.u.; income from operations 26,000 m.u.
- Income before taxes 24,000 m.u.; income taxes 7,200 m.u.
- Net income 16,800 m.u.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Operating expenses

$$\text{Operating expenses} = 46{,}000 + 12{,}000 + 6{,}000 + 8{,}000 = 72{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 2. Non-operating items

$$\text{Non-operating items} = +2{,}000 - 4{,}000 = -2{,}000 \text{ m.u.}$$

#### Step 3. Income statement

| Line | Computation | m.u. |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Sales revenue | | 190,000 |
| − Cost of goods sold | | 92,000 |
| **= Gross profit** | $190{,}000 - 92{,}000$ | **98,000** |
| − Operating expenses | Step 1 | 72,000 |
| **= Income from operations** | $98{,}000 - 72{,}000$ | **26,000** |
| ± Non-operating items | Step 2 | −2,000 |
| **= Income before taxes** | $26{,}000 - 2{,}000$ | **24,000** |
| − Income taxes (30%) | $0.30 \times 24{,}000$ | 7,200 |
| **= Net income** | $24{,}000 - 7{,}200$ | **16,800** |

## (c) Prepare the balance sheet at 31/12/2025 [1.25 pts]

### Hint

Two figures are not in the list and come from part (b): the tax payable and the retained earnings at the close of the year. With no dividends, the whole net income is retained [Slide 23].

### Checkpoint

- Non-current assets 96,000 m.u.; current assets 70,000 m.u.
- Equity 90,800 m.u. (retained earnings 30,800 m.u.).
- Current liabilities 35,200 m.u.; total 166,000 m.u. on both sides.

### Solution

#### Step 1. The two computed figures

$$\text{Retained earnings}_{31/12} = 14{,}000 + 16{,}800 - 0 = 30{,}800 \text{ m.u.}$$

$$\text{Income taxes payable} = 7{,}200 \text{ m.u.} \quad \text{(due in 2026, current liability)}$$

#### Step 2. Balance sheet

| Assets | m.u. | Liabilities and equity | m.u. |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| Equipment | 120,000 | Contributed capital | 60,000 |
| − Accumulated depreciation | (24,000) | Retained earnings | 30,800 |
| **Non-current assets** | **96,000** | **Equity** | **90,800** |
| Inventory | 21,000 | Bank loan due after 2026 | 40,000 |
| Accounts receivable | 26,000 | **Long-term liabilities** | **40,000** |
| Cash | 23,000 | Accounts payable | 18,000 |
| | | Bank loan due in 2026 | 10,000 |
| | | Income taxes payable | 7,200 |
| **Current assets** | **70,000** | **Current liabilities** | **35,200** |
| **Total assets** | **166,000** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **166,000** |

Check: $96{,}000 + 70{,}000 = 166{,}000$ and $90{,}800 + 40{,}000 + 35{,}200 = 166{,}000$ [Slide 18].

## (d) Compute the working capital, the current ratio and the debt-to-equity ratio, and comment on liquidity and solvency [0.75 pts]

### Hint

Working capital and the current ratio use only the current blocks; the debt-to-equity ratio uses all the liabilities, current and long-term.

### Checkpoint

- Working capital 34,800 m.u.; current ratio 1.99 (extension).
- Total liabilities 75,200 m.u.; debt-to-equity 0.83; equity 54.7% of the financing.

### Solution

#### Step 1. Liquidity (extension)

$$\text{Working capital} = 70{,}000 - 35{,}200 = 34{,}800 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \text{Current ratio} = \frac{70{,}000}{35{,}200} = 1.99$$

#### Step 2. Solvency [Slide 36]

$$L = 40{,}000 + 35{,}200 = 75{,}200 \text{ m.u.}, \qquad \frac{L}{E} = \frac{75{,}200}{90{,}800} = 0.83, \qquad \%\,\text{Equity} = \frac{90{,}800}{166{,}000} = 54.7\%$$

#### Step 3. Comment

The current assets cover the debts due within one year almost twice, so the company has no short-term liquidity problem. More than half of the financing comes from the owners, and the debt-to-equity ratio below 1 indicates moderate financial leverage: the risk for shareholders is limited [Slide 36].

## Interpretation

- **Dimensional check:** every line is in m.u.; ratios are dimensionless.
- **Why the income statement comes first:** the balance sheet only closes once the net income of the year is added to retained earnings; with the tax payable, these are the two figures that link the statements.
- **Typical mistakes:** treating accumulated depreciation as a liability, putting the whole bank loan in long-term liabilities, and forgetting the tax payable.
