# ✏️ Problem: Exercise 1 — A price equal to the variable cost

## 📄 Statement

A company's annual fixed costs are €2,000. The variable cost per unit equals its selling price, which is €2. What is the break-even point?

## Data and model

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| $FC$ | annual fixed costs | 2,000 € |
| $P$ | selling price per unit | 2 €/unit |
| $VC$ | variable cost per unit | 2 €/unit |

The break-even point is the volume at which the profit is zero [Slide 36], and with the unit margin $m = P - VC$ it is $Q_0 = FC/m$ [Slide 37].

## Solution

### Step 1. Unit margin

$$m = P - VC = 2 - 2 = 0 \ \text{€/unit}$$

### Step 2. Break-even point

$$Q_0 = \frac{FC}{m} = \frac{2{,}000}{0} \to \infty$$

The division has no finite result: no volume makes the profit zero. Writing the profit for any volume shows why,

$$\text{Profit} = Q\,m - FC = Q \times 0 - 2{,}000 = -2{,}000 \ \text{€}$$

Every unit sold brings in exactly what it costs, so nothing is left to cover the fixed costs: the company loses 2,000 € a year whatever it sells.

## Checks and official key

- **Meaning:** a break-even point only exists when $m > 0$. With $m = 0$ the profit line is horizontal at $-FC$ and never reaches zero.
- **Units:** €/(€/unit) = units.
- **Check against the official key** (BEP  OL Statements  Solutions.pdf, p. 3): the key also obtains $Q^* = 2{,}000/(2 - 2) = \infty$ and concludes that the solution makes no economic sense, because the company will never cover its fixed costs. Same result.
