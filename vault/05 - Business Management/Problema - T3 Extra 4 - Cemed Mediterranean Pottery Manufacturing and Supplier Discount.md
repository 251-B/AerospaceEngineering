# ✏️ Exam Problem: CEMED — Industrial Manufacturing Accounting Cycle & Trade Discount Analysis

## 📄 Official Problem Statement (Transcripción Íntegra)

**CERÁMICAS DEL MEDITERRÁNEO (CEMED) MEDITERRANEAN POTTERY** is a company dedicated to the manufacturing and distribution of ceramic tiles for construction, located in Valencia.

* **Incorporation:** Created on **1 January 2016 (01/01/2016)** by three partners, with a contribution of **7 m.u. each** (total **21 m.u.**), fully in cash.
* **Land & Factory:** On **1 February 2016 (01/02/2016)**, purchased land worth **10 m.u.**, paying $50\%$ in cash and $50\%$ to be paid one year later. On the same date, purchased the factory building for **6 m.u.** in cash, financed with a mortgage at **5% annual interest for 10 years**. Factory amortization period is **20 years** (linear, zero residual value). Terms: return each 31 December **10% of mortgage principal** plus interest accrued annually.
* **Additional Bank Loan:** On **1 April 2016 (01/04/2016)**, borrowed **5 m.u.** from a bank, returning **20% of principal each semester for 2.5 years** (first repayment on **1 October 2016**, second on **1 April 2017**, etc.) at **6% annual interest**. Interest is paid semiannually on the amount due in that semester, the day after the end of the semester.
* **Machinery:** With the loan funds, on 1 April 2016 purchased machinery for **3 m.u.** with **60 days payment delay**. Useful life is **10 years** (linear, zero residual value).
* **Raw Materials:** On **1 July 2016 (01/07/2016)**, acquired $150 \text{ tons}$ of raw material at $0.03 \text{ m.u./ton}$. **Half** was used in the production process of products sold. Paid provider $60\%$ of total cost at $90 \text{ days}$, while the remaining $40\%$ remains pending at the end of the year.
* **Sales & Personnel:** Sales reached **50 m.u.**, of which at 31 December 2016 only **70%** had been collected in cash. Personnel plus Social Security costs totaled **0.8 m.u.**, paid in full in 2016.
* **Taxes & Strategy:** Corporate tax rate is **30%**, settled by calendar year and paid on **15 January 2017**. Holds **40% of annual net profit as reserves**; dividends ($60\%$) are payable on 31 December 2016.

**Questions:**
1. Prepare the Income Statement and Balance Sheet at **24:00 on 31 December 2016 (31/12/2016)**, justifying and detailing intermediate calculations.
2. State and explain what changes would occur on the Income Statement and Balance Sheet if the company had negotiated a **5% discount on the total purchase price of raw materials**, in exchange for paying the $60\%$ in cash instead of at 90 days. In which case is profit higher? Discuss reasoned, why.

---

## 🎯 Exam Strategy & Key Analytical Insights

> [!TIP] How to Approach this Problem on Exam Day
> 1. **Time Pro-Rata on Fixed Assets:**
>    - Building bought on 1 Feb $\implies$ active for **11 months** in 2016 ($\text{Depreciation} \times \frac{11}{12}$).
>    - Machinery bought on 1 April $\implies$ active for **9 months** in 2016 ($\text{Depreciation} \times \frac{9}{12}$).
>    - Land is never depreciated.
> 2. **Semiannual vs Annual Loan Repayments:**
>    - **Mortgage (6 m.u. at 5%):** Starts 1 Feb. On 31 Dec, pays $10\%$ principal ($0.6 \text{ m.u.}$) plus 11 months interest ($6 \times 5\% \times \frac{11}{12} = 0.275 \text{ m.u.}$).
>    - **Bank Loan (5 m.u. at 6%):** Starts 1 April. On 1 Oct, returns $20\%$ principal ($1.0 \text{ m.u.}$) plus 6 months interest ($5 \times 6\% \times 0.5 = 0.15 \text{ m.u.}$). From 1 Oct to 31 Dec (3 months), remaining principal is $4.0 \text{ m.u.}$, generating accrued unpaid interest: $4 \times 6\% \times \frac{3}{12} = 0.06 \text{ m.u.}$, payable on 2 April 2017.
> 3. **Taxes Payable on 31/12:** Corporate taxes are paid on 15 January 2017 $\implies$ **Taxes Payable is a Current Liability** on 31/12/2016.

## 🔢 Step-by-Step Resolution: Part 1

### 1. Operations & Cost of Goods Sold
* Raw Materials Purchased: $150 \text{ tons} \times 0.03 \text{ mu/ton} = \mathbf{4.50 \text{ mu}}$.
* Consumed in Production (Half):
  $$\mathbf{\text{COGS}} = \frac{4.50}{2} = \mathbf{2.25 \text{ mu}}$$
  *(The other half, $2.25 \text{ mu}$, remains as Ending Inventory).*
* Raw Materials Payment:
  * $60\%$ of $4.50 = 2.70 \text{ mu}$ paid at 90 days (on 1 Oct 2016) $\implies$ Paid in cash.
  * $40\%$ of $4.50 = 1.80 \text{ mu}$ pending on 31/12 $\implies$ **Accounts Payable (Suppliers)** $= \mathbf{1.80 \text{ mu}}$.

### 2. Operating Expenses & Depreciation
* **Personnel & Social Security:** $\mathbf{0.80 \text{ mu}}$ (paid).
* **Depreciation:**
  * Factory Building ($6.0 \text{ mu}$ over $20 \text{ years}$, active 11 months):
    $$D_{\text{building}} = \frac{6.00}{20} \times \frac{11}{12} = 0.30 \times \frac{11}{12} = \mathbf{0.275 \text{ mu}}$$
  * Machinery ($3.0 \text{ mu}$ over $10 \text{ years}$, active 9 months):
    $$D_{\text{machinery}} = \frac{3.00}{10} \times \frac{9}{12} = 0.30 \times \frac{9}{12} = \mathbf{0.225 \text{ mu}}$$
  * Total Depreciation: $0.275 + 0.225 = \mathbf{0.500 \text{ mu}}$.
* **Total Operating Expenses:**
  $$\mathbf{\text{Operating Expenses}} = 2.25 \text{ (COGS)} + 0.80 \text{ (Labor)} + 0.50 \text{ (Dep)} = \mathbf{3.550 \text{ mu}}$$

### 3. Operating Profit (EBIT)
$$\mathbf{\text{EBIT}} = \text{Sales} (50.00) - \text{Operating Expenses} (3.550) = \mathbf{46.450 \text{ mu}}$$

### 4. Financial Interest Expenses
1. **Mortgage Interest (1 Feb – 31 Dec = 11 months on 6 m.u.):**
   $$\text{Interest}_{\text{mortgage}} = 6.00 \times 5\% \times \frac{11}{12} = \mathbf{0.275 \text{ mu}}$$
2. **Bank Loan Interest:**
   * Semester 1 (1 Apr – 1 Oct on 5 m.u.): $5.00 \times 6\% \times \frac{6}{12} = 0.150 \text{ mu}$.
   * Quarter 4 Accrual (1 Oct – 31 Dec on 4 m.u.): $4.00 \times 6\% \times \frac{3}{12} = 0.060 \text{ mu}$.
   * Total Loan Interest: $0.150 + 0.060 = \mathbf{0.210 \text{ mu}}$.
3. **Total Financial Interest:**
   $$\mathbf{\text{Total Interest}} = 0.275 + 0.210 = \mathbf{0.485 \text{ mu}}$$

### 5. EBT, Corporate Tax & Net Income
$$\mathbf{\text{EBT}} = 46.450 - 0.485 = \mathbf{45.965 \text{ mu}}$$
$$\mathbf{\text{Corporate Income Tax } (30\%)} = 30\% \times 45.965 = \mathbf{13.7895 \text{ mu}}$$
$$\mathbf{\text{Net Income}} = 45.965 - 13.7895 = \mathbf{32.1755 \text{ mu}}$$

* **Profit Distribution:**
  * Reserves ($40\%$ Retained): $0.40 \times 32.1755 = \mathbf{12.8702 \text{ mu}}$.
  * Dividends ($60\%$ Distributed): $0.60 \times 32.1755 = \mathbf{19.3053 \text{ mu}}$ (paid in cash on 31/12).

## 🔍 Step-by-Step Resolution: Part 2 — Supplier Discount Analysis

**Scenario:** The company negotiates a **5% discount on the total purchase price of raw materials**, in exchange for paying the $60\%$ portion in cash on 1 July 2016 instead of at 90 days.

### 1. Quantification of the Discount:
* Original total raw materials purchase: $4.50 \text{ mu}$.
* Commercial Discount ($5\%$):
  $$\text{Discount} = 5\% \times 4.50 = \mathbf{0.225 \text{ mu}}$$
* New Total Purchase Cost: $4.50 - 0.225 = \mathbf{4.275 \text{ mu}}$.

### 2. Impact on the Income Statement:
* **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):**
  Since half of the raw materials was consumed:
  $$\text{New COGS} = \frac{4.275}{2} = \mathbf{2.1375 \text{ mu}} \quad (\text{a cost saving of } 2.25 - 2.1375 = \mathbf{+0.1125 \text{ mu}})$$
* **EBIT and EBT:** Both increase by $+0.1125 \text{ mu}$.
* **Corporate Tax (30%):** Increases by $30\% \times 0.1125 = +0.03375 \text{ mu}$.
* **Net Profit:** Increases by:
  $$\Delta \text{Net Profit} = 0.1125 \times (1 - 0.30) = \mathbf{+0.07875 \text{ mu}}$$
  $$\mathbf{\text{New Net Income}} = 32.1755 + 0.07875 = \mathbf{32.25425 \text{ mu}}$$

### 3. Impact on the Balance Sheet:
1. **Inventories:** The ending stock (other half) is now valued at its lower purchase cost:
   $$\text{New Ending Inventory} = \mathbf{2.1375 \text{ mu}} \quad (-0.1125 \text{ mu})$$
2. **Cash Outflows to Supplier:**
   * $60\%$ paid immediately in cash: $60\% \times 4.275 = \mathbf{2.565 \text{ mu}}$ (compared to $2.70 \text{ mu}$ originally, saving $0.135 \text{ mu}$ in cash).
   * Even though cash is paid earlier (1 July instead of 1 October), both dates fall before 31 December, so the only year-end effect of the payment is the smaller amount paid.
3. **Accounts Payable (Suppliers):** The remaining $40\%$ pending at year end drops to:
   $$\text{New Accounts Payable} = 40\% \times 4.275 = \mathbf{1.710 \text{ mu}} \quad (-0.090 \text{ mu})$$
4. **Taxes Payable:** The extra tax is not paid until 15 January 2017, so it stays in current liabilities: $13.7895 + 0.03375 = \mathbf{13.82325 \text{ mu}}$ ($+0.03375 \text{ mu}$).
5. **Stockholders' Equity:** The extra net profit is split $40\%/60\%$ as in Part 1:
   * Reserves: $+40\% \times 0.07875 = +0.0315 \text{ mu}$ (new reserves $12.9017 \text{ mu}$).
   * Extra dividend: $60\% \times 0.07875 = 0.04725 \text{ mu}$, paid in cash on 31 December (new dividend $19.35255 \text{ mu}$).
6. **Year-end Cash:** Only the extra dividend, not the whole increase in net profit, leaves the company (the extra tax is still unpaid and the extra profit is not cash):
   $$\Delta \text{Cash} = \underbrace{(2.70 - 2.565)}_{\text{smaller supplier payment}} - \underbrace{0.04725}_{\text{extra dividend}} = 0.135 - 0.04725 = \mathbf{+0.08775 \text{ mu}}$$
   $$\text{New Cash} = 28.1697 + 0.08775 = \mathbf{28.25745 \text{ mu}}$$
7. **Balance check** (all changes with respect to Part 1):
   $$\Delta \text{Assets} = \underbrace{-0.1125}_{\text{inventory}} + \underbrace{0.08775}_{\text{cash}} = \mathbf{-0.02475 \text{ mu}}$$
   $$\Delta (\text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}) = \underbrace{-0.09}_{\text{payables}} + \underbrace{0.03375}_{\text{taxes payable}} + \underbrace{0.0315}_{\text{reserves}} = \mathbf{-0.02475 \text{ mu}}$$
   $$\text{New Total Assets} = 63.9197 - 0.02475 = 63.89495 \text{ mu} = \text{New Total Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$$
### 4. Concluding Managerial Discussion:
> **In which case is the profit higher? Why?**
> Profit is strictly **higher under the 5% discount agreement** (Net Profit increases by $+0.07875 \text{ m.u.}$).
> *Reasoning:* A commercial purchase discount reduces the unit acquisition cost of inventory. Through the matching principle, this lowers Cost of Goods Sold in the Income Statement, expanding both Gross Margin and Operating Income. The income statement does not record the time value of paying 90 days earlier (the problem gives no interest on cash), and both payment dates fall within 2016, so the accounting profit is higher by the full after-tax saving. Year-end cash is also higher ($+0.08775 \text{ mu}$ after the extra dividend), as shown in the balance check above.
