# 💰 Topic 3: Financial Management (I) — Financial Statements & Capital Structure

> **Topic Objective:** Master the analytical tools of corporate financial management. Understand the duality between the firm’s **Economic Structure** (Assets / Investments) and **Financial Structure** (Liabilities + Equity / Sources of Funds) under the value maximization criterion ($\text{Profitability} > \text{Cost}$). Construct, balance, and interpret the two foundational accounting statements: the **Income Statement (Profit & Loss)** and the **Balance Sheet**. Classify all corporate financing alternatives (internal self-financing, equity shares, long-term bonds/loans/leasing, and short-term commercial credit/factoring), calculate financial leverage ($L/E$), and evaluate the **Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)**.

## 📑 Core Theoretical Modules

> [!info] Citation convention
> **[Slide N]** refers to the official deck *Topic 3_Financial Management (I).pdf* (37 slides). Items tagged *(standard reference)* are standard accounting or finance material that is **not** on the slides (they are used by the problem sheets and the concept notes).

**Slide-deck outline [Slide 2]:** Introduction (economic and financial structure); Financial statements; Income statement; Balance sheet; The financial structure of the company.

### 1. Introduction: Economic Structure vs Financial Structure
* **Finance area [Slide 3]:** the collection and management of the funds (capital) used by the company. The finance manager faces two decisions: **investing decisions** (what to invest in and when?) and **financing decisions** (how to obtain the money to invest?).
* **Economic structure [Slide 4]:** defined by the investments carried out; it represents the use of the financial resources in terms of goods (assets) and rights: **ASSETS (what is owned)**.
* **Financial structure [Slide 4]:** defined by the sources of financing; it includes all the financial resources used to carry out the investments (debt and equity): **LIABILITIES (what is owed)**.
* **Objective [Slide 5]:** maximize value. Assets generate profitability; equity plus liabilities carry a cost; value is created when
$$\text{Profitability} > \text{Cost}$$
* **Decisions [Slide 6]:**
  * *Financing decisions:* choice of equity or debt. Resources may be internal equity (self-financing: retained earnings), external equity or debt. They include decisions about the capital structure and about dividends.
  * *Investing decisions:* allocation of capital to projects from which future profits are expected; companies invest in projects that create value (Profits > Costs).

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           ECONOMIC STRUCTURE           │          FINANCIAL STRUCTURE           │
│     ASSETS (what is owned)             │   EQUITY + LIABILITIES (what is owed)  │
├────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│        Generates PROFITABILITY         │         Incurs CAPITAL COST            │
└────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. The Income Statement
* **Purpose [Slide 7]:** the first statement prepared; it reports the business performance for a specific period. Simple format: $\text{Revenues} - \text{Expenses} = \text{Net Income}$.
* **Revenues and expenses [Slide 8]:** revenues are earned for the sale of goods or services and occur when the sale is made, whether or not the payment has been received (examples: sales, service revenue, interest revenue). Expenses are incurred when the business receives goods and services, whether or not they have been paid (examples: salaries, utility, interest expense). This is the accrual principle.
* **Extended format [Slide 9]:**
$$\begin{aligned}
& \text{Sales revenue} \\
- & \text{Cost of goods sold} \\
\hline
= & \text{Gross profit} \\
- & \text{Operating expenses} \\
\hline
= & \text{Income from operations} \\
\pm & \text{Non-operating items} \\
\hline
= & \text{Income before taxes} \\
- & \text{Income taxes} \\
\hline
= & \text{Net income}
\end{aligned}$$
* **Cost of goods sold [Slide 10]:** the expense incurred to buy or make a product for resale (book bought for \$25 and sold for \$32: COGS = \$25). **Operating expenses** are the usual expenses of operating the business (salaries, utilities, depreciation).
* **Non-operating items [Slide 11]:** revenues, expenses, gains and losses not related to the primary operations (interest expense, gains and losses on the sale of equipment and investments).
* **Income taxes [Slide 11]:** income before taxes multiplied by the tax rate; example: \$50,000 at 30% gives \$15,000.
* *(standard reference)* Net sales (gross sales less discounts), EBIT/EBT labels, and the stock-flow expression $\text{COGS} = \text{Initial stock} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending stock}$ are used in the problem sheets but not defined on the slides.

### 3. The Balance Sheet
* **Purpose [Slide 12]:** reports the financial position of an accounting entity at a particular point in time. Basic format: $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$.
* **Components [Slide 13]:**
  * *Assets:* economic resources owned by the company (cash, accounts receivable, supplies, buildings, equipment).
  * *Liabilities:* debts or obligations (accounts payable, unearned revenues, bonds payable).
  * *Equity:* the residual balance, $\text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}$. In a corporation it is called stockholders' equity: the financing provided by the stockholders plus the earnings not paid out as dividends (retained earnings).
* **Assets [Slide 14, Slide 15]:** $\text{Current assets} + \text{Non-current assets} = \text{Total assets}$. Current assets will be used or turned into cash within one year (cash, accounts receivable, inventory, short-term investments, supplies, prepaids). Non-current assets are the remainder (long-term investments, land, building, equipment, patents).
* **Liabilities [Slide 16]:** $\text{Current liabilities} + \text{Long-term liabilities} = \text{Total liabilities}$. Current liabilities are paid or satisfied within the coming year; long-term liabilities are not.
* **Stockholders' equity [Slide 17]:** $\text{Contributed capital} + \text{Retained earnings} = \text{Total stockholders' equity}$.
* **Full layout [Slide 18]:** current assets + non-current assets = total assets, equal to current liabilities + long-term liabilities + stockholders' equity (total liabilities and stockholders' equity).
* *(standard reference)* Accumulated depreciation as a contra-asset ($\text{Book value} = \text{Cost} - \text{Accumulated depreciation}$), uncalled share capital and legal reserves appear in the problem sheets (Exercises 1, 2 and 6) but are not defined on the Topic 3 slides.

#### Worked example on the slides [Slide 19]
Given: Cash 5,000; Accounts receivable 10,000; Inventories 45,000; Supplies 4,000; Buildings 65,000; Accounts payable 12,000; Bonds payable 40,000; Common stock 45,000; Additional paid-in capital 20,000; Retained earnings 5,000 (beginning balance); Sales 100,000; Cost of goods sold 58,000; Utility expense 8,000; Supplies expense 3,000; Salaries expense 16,000; Interest expense 5,000; tax rate 30%.

| Step | Computation | Result |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Gross profit | 100,000 - 58,000 | 42,000 |
| Operating expenses | 8,000 + 3,000 + 16,000 | 27,000 |
| Income from operations | 42,000 - 27,000 | 15,000 |
| Income before taxes | 15,000 - 5,000 | 10,000 |
| Income taxes (30%) | 0.30 x 10,000 | 3,000 |
| Net income | 10,000 - 3,000 | 7,000 |
| Retained earnings, end | 5,000 + 7,000 | 12,000 |
| Total assets | 5,000 + 10,000 + 45,000 + 4,000 + 65,000 | 129,000 |
| Total liabilities | 12,000 + 40,000 | 52,000 |
| Total equity | 45,000 + 20,000 + 12,000 | 77,000 |

Check: $129{,}000 = 52{,}000 + 77{,}000$. *(Python recomputation matches; the slide gives the data without a solution, so this is our own computation.)*

### 4. Financing Decisions and the Financial Structure [Slide 20, Slide 21]
Business financing is the collection of the financial resources needed to develop the company's activity. The combination of all possible financial sources is the **capital structure** or **financial structure** [Slide 20]. The slides classify it as follows [Slide 21, Slide 22]:

```
FINANCIAL STRUCTURE
├── COMPANY RESOURCES (Equity)
│   ├── Internal equity: self-financing
│   └── External equity: equity capital and shares
└── EXTERNAL RESOURCES (Liabilities)
    ├── Long term: bonds, long-term loans and bank credits, leasing
    └── Short term: accounts payable (trade discount), short-term loans and bank credits, factoring
```

#### A. Equity financing
* **Self-financing [Slide 23]:** retained profits (resources generated within the company in its normal activity). Profits are either retained (self-financing) or distributed (dividends).
$$\text{Pay-out} = \frac{\text{Dividends}}{\text{Profits}}, \qquad \text{Self-financing (\%)} = \frac{\text{Retained earnings}}{\text{Profits}}$$
  Slide example: profits = 100,000 €, dividends = 75,000 €, retained earnings = 25,000 €, hence pay-out = 75% and self-financing = 25%.
* **External equity [Slide 25]:** equity capital is the funds raised from owners at the start of the business (initial capital) and later rights issues (new shares), used to finance investments. It is not paid back at any expiring date, and a share of capital can only be converted into cash by sale or transmission.
* **Shares [Slide 26]:** a share is one of a finite number of equal portions of a company's capital, entitling the owner to a proportion of distributed, non-reinvested profits (dividends) and to a portion of the company's value in case of liquidation. Share values: nominal, accounting (book), intrinsic and market. Payment of dividends is not compulsory. Capital increase = rights issue.

#### B. Debt financing
* **Bonds [Slide 29]:** a debt security in which the authorized issuer owes the holders a debt and must repay the principal and interest (the coupon) at maturity. Issuer = borrower, bondholder = lender, coupon = interest. Bonds let the issuer finance long-term investments with external funds.
* **Long-term loans and bank credits [Slide 31]:** in a *loan* the company receives an amount of money that must be paid back at a certain time, at a cost (the debt interest). In a *bank credit* the institution makes available a maximum amount; the customer borrows any amount under the limit and pays interest only on the amount borrowed (interest rates are higher).
* **Leasing [Slide 33]:** the firm obtains the use of fixed assets in return for contractual periodic payments; the lessee has the right to buy the asset at the end of the agreed period. Types: finance leasing and operating leasing (no further definition on the slide).
* **Short-term external resources [Slide 35]:**
  * *Accounts payable (trade credit):* payment is made some time after reception of the goods; suppliers offer the possibility to defer payment.
  * *Bank financing:* loans and credits (same characteristics as long term) and **trade discounts**: the financial institution makes a note (bill of exchange, promissory note, ...) available to the customer before its expiry date, after deducting expenses (interest, commission, ...).
  * *Factoring:* the company sells a customer credit to a factoring company before it expires; the factor handles the reimbursement and assumes the insolvency risk.

### 5. Financial Leverage and Weighted Average Cost of Capital
* **Financial structure ratios [Slide 36]:** $\%\,\text{Equity} = E/(E+L)$; $\%\,\text{Liabilities} = L/(E+L)$; $\text{Debt-to-Equity} = L/E$. The debt-to-equity ratio evaluates financial leverage: the degree to which the company finances operations through debt versus wholly-owned funds; it reflects the ability of shareholder equity to cover all outstanding debts in a downturn, and higher leverage ratios tend to indicate higher risk to shareholders.
  * Slide example: total liabilities plus equity = 1,000 M €, $E$ = 700 M €, $L$ = 300 M €, so $\%E = 70\%$, $\%L = 30\%$ and $L/E = 300/700 = 0.4286$.
* **Cost of capital [Slide 37]:** all financial resources have a cost: *implicit* (opportunity) cost for equity, *explicit* cost for liabilities. The capital cost is the weighted average cost of all resources:
$$K_{WACC} = K_{EQ}\cdot\frac{E}{E+L} + K_{L}\cdot\frac{L}{E+L}$$
* *(standard reference, not on the slides)* With tax-deductible interest the debt term becomes $K_L(1-t)$ (tax shield); the slide formula has no tax term and is the one used in the course.
