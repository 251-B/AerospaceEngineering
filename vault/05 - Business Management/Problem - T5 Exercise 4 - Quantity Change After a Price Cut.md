# ✏️ Problem: Exercise 4 — Change in quantity after a price cut

## 📄 Statement

If a producer lowers the prices by 20% in a given good, knowing that the elasticity of demand with respect to price is 1.5, how much do you estimate the quantity demanded will vary (as a percentage)?

a) 15% b) 30% c) 45% d) 5%

## Data and model

Price change $\%\Delta P = -20\%$; price elasticity $\lvert E_d \rvert = 1.5$. From the definition $E_d = \%\Delta Q / \%\Delta P$ (Slide 17; topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 2), $\%\Delta Q = E_d \cdot \%\Delta P$.

## Solution

### Step 1. Percentage change in quantity

With the sign of the law of demand, $E_d = -1.5$:

$$\%\Delta Q = E_d \cdot \%\Delta P = (-1.5) \times (-20\%) = +30\%$$

The quantity demanded rises by **30%**: answer **b)**.

### Step 2. Why the other options are wrong

- a) 15% would need $\lvert E_d \rvert = 0.75$.
- c) 45% would need $\lvert E_d \rvert = 2.25$, or a 30% price cut.
- d) 5% would need $\lvert E_d \rvert = 0.25$, an inelastic demand.

## Checks and official key

- **Meaning:** demand is elastic ($1.5 > 1$), so the quantity changes more, in percentage, than the price.
- **Check against the official key** (PED Exercises SOL.pdf, p. 3): answer b), $20\% \times 1.5 = 30\%$. Same answer.
