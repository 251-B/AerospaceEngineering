# ✏️ Problem: Exercise 6 — Substitute goods from the cross effects

## 📄 Statement

If when the price of a good "X" is reduced, an increase is induced in the quantity demanded of good "X" itself, as well as a decrease in the quantity demanded of good "Y", we say that "X" and "Y" are:

a) Complementary b) Substitutes c) Normal goods d) None of the previous

## Data and model

The price of X falls, the demand for X rises and the demand for Y falls. The **cross elasticity** $E_{YX} = \%\Delta Q_Y / \%\Delta P_X$ is positive for substitutes and negative for complements (topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 5).

## Solution

### Step 1. Sign of the cross elasticity

The price of X falls ($\%\Delta P_X < 0$) and the quantity of Y falls ($\%\Delta Q_Y < 0$):

$$E_{YX} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_Y}{\%\Delta P_X} = \frac{(-)}{(-)} > 0$$

A positive cross elasticity means **substitutes**: answer **b)**. In words, X became cheaper and buyers moved from Y to X (topic 5.2.2.pdf, p. 5).

### Step 2. Why the other options are wrong

- a) Complementary goods would see the demand for **both** rise when the price of X falls.
- c) "Normal good" describes the reaction of a good to the buyers' income, not to the price of another good, and the statement gives no income change.
- d) is wrong because b) is correct.
