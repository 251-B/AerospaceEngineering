# 📖 Concept: Financial Statements — Income Statement (P&L) and Balance Sheet

> **Core Intuition in One Sentence:** The Income Statement acts as a continuous economic film measuring net wealth generation over a period via the accrual principle, while the Balance Sheet serves as an instantaneous balance snapshot proving that everything a firm owns (Assets) is funded by what it owes (Liabilities) or what owners hold (Equity).

---

## 🎯 1. Fundamental Accounting Principles

Corporate accounting is governed by two complementary viewpoints:
1. **The Accrual Principle (Principio de Devengo):**
   * Revenues and expenses are recorded when economic value is created or consumed, **regardless of when cash is physically collected or disbursed**.
   * *Sale on Credit:* Recognized immediately as **Sales Revenue** in the Income Statement, creating an **Account Receivable** asset on the Balance Sheet.
   * *Depreciation:* Recognized as an annual **Operating Expense** to match the wear of long-term assets over their useful life, even though no cash leaves the firm in that year.
2. **The Cash Flow Viewpoint (Principio de Caja):**
   * Records only actual physical transfers of liquid money (**Cash Inflows / Collections** and **Cash Outflows / Payments**).

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ACCRUAL ACCOUNTING (Income Statement) │ CASH FLOW ACCOUNTING (Treasury / Cash)│
├───────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Revenue (Ingreso): Earned from sales  │ Inflow / Collection (Cobro): Cash in  │
│ Expense (Gasto): Consumption of inputs│ Outflow / Payment (Pago): Cash out    │
├───────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Difference: NET INCOME (Profit/Loss)  │ Difference: NET CASH FLOW (Liquidity) │
└───────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 📊 2. The Income Statement (Profit & Loss — P&L)

The Income Statement details operational efficiency and profitability over a fiscal period.

### Extended Multi-Step Architecture:

$$\begin{array}{llr}
& \text{Gross Sales Revenue} & 2,002.00 \\
- & \text{Commercial Discounts & Allowances} & (24.02) \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Net Sales Revenue}} & \mathbf{1,977.98} \\
- & \text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)} & (962.00) \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Gross Profit}} & \mathbf{1,015.98} \\
- & \text{Operating Expenses (Wages, Utilities, Depreciation)} & (881.40) \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Operating Profit (EBIT / Income from Operations)}} & \mathbf{134.58} \\
\pm & \text{Financial & Non-Operating Results (Interest Expense/Revenue)} & (13.50) \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Earnings Before Taxes (EBT / Income Before Taxes)}} & \mathbf{121.08} \\
- & \text{Corporate Income Tax } (t = 30\%) & (36.32) \\
\hline
= & \mathbf{\text{Net Income (Net Profit after Taxes)}} & \mathbf{84.76} \\
\hline
\hline
& \text{Distribution: Dividends } (50\%) & 42.38 \\
& \text{Retention: Retained Earnings to Equity } (50\%) & 42.38
\end{array}$$

#### Key Subtotals and Definitions:
* **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** Direct materials and inventory consumed to manufacture products sold:
  $$\text{COGS} = \text{Initial Inventory} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Inventory}$$
* **EBIT (Earnings Before Interest and Taxes):** Pure operating performance independent of financial structure and tax jurisdictions.
* **EBT (Earnings Before Taxes):** Profit after deducting debt servicing costs ($I = \text{Interest Expense}$).
* **Net Income:** Final residual earnings attributable to equity owners.

## ⚖️ 3. The Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet exhibits the fundamental identity:
$$\mathbf{\text{Total Assets}} \equiv \mathbf{\text{Total Liabilities}} + \mathbf{\text{Total Stockholders' Equity}}$$

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┐
│        ASSETS (Use of Capital)        │   LIABILITIES & EQUITY (Funding)      │
├───────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ NON-CURRENT ASSETS (Fixed Assets)     │ STOCKHOLDERS' EQUITY (Own Funds)      │
│ • Land (Non-depreciable)              │ • Subscribed Share Capital            │
│ • Buildings & Constructions           │ • (-) Uncalled Share Capital          │
│ • Machinery & Tooling                 │ • Additional Paid-in Capital          │
│ • Transport Equipment & Furniture     │ • Retained Earnings (Prior Years)     │
│ • (-) Accumulated Depreciation        │ • Current Year Retained Profit        │
│ • Industrial Patents (Intangible)     │                                       │
│                                       │ LONG-TERM LIABILITIES                 │
│ CURRENT ASSETS (Working Capital)      │ • Long-Term Bank Loans (> 1 year)     │
│ • Raw Materials & Finished Stocks     │ • Bonds Payable (> 1 year)            │
│ • Accounts Receivable (Customers)     │                                       │
│ • Short-Term Financial Investments    │ CURRENT LIABILITIES (Short-Term Debt) │
│ • Cash & Commercial Bank Accounts     │ • Accounts Payable (Suppliers)        │
│                                       │ • Short-Term Bank Loans (≤ 1 year)    │
│                                       │ • Taxes & Unpaid Salaries Payable     │
└───────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Valuation Rules & Depreciation:
* **Linear (Straight-Line) Depreciation:**
  $$\text{Annual Depreciation Expense} = \frac{\text{Acquisition Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Useful Lifespan (years)}}$$
* **Net Book Value (Valor Neto Contable):**
  $$\text{Net Book Value} = \text{Historical Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}$$

---

## 🔗 4. The Interconnection Bridge: Connecting P&L to Balance Sheet

The Income Statement and Balance Sheet do not exist in isolation; they are tied together through **Retained Earnings**:

$$\text{Ending Retained Earnings} = \text{Beginning Retained Earnings} + \text{Net Income} - \text{Dividends}$$

When a company closes its fiscal year, the undistributed portion of Net Income flows into Stockholders' Equity, expanding net worth and financing asset growth.
